# Nondeterministic state complexity of square root 

Sergey Onishchenko *

April 9, 2026 

Abstract 

We investigate the nondeterministic state complexity of the square-root operation pL = { w | w w ∈ L } on regular languages represented by nondeterministic finite automata. For an n-state NFA accepting L, it was previously known that pL can be accepted by an NFA with at most n3 states, while the best lower bound was only (n-1)(n-2)(n-3). In this paper, we close this gap completely and prove that n3 states are sufficient and necessary in the worst case. 

Keywords: state complexity, language operations, nondeterministic finite automata 

# 1 Introduction 

The (non)deterministic state complexity of a regular language L is the number of states in the minimal (non)deterministic finite automaton recognizing L. The square root of a language is defined as pL : = {w ∈ Σ∗|w w ∈ L}. We consider the following problem: NFA A with n states over the alphabet Σ recognizes the language L. How many states might be needed to recognize the language pL : = {w ∈ Σ∗|w w ∈ L}? There has been much interest in the study of state complexity of operations which preserve regularity, or, in other words, in the descriptional complexity of operations on languages represented by finite automata. Yu et al. [6] established classical tight bounds for several basic DFA operations on regular languages. Salomaa et al. [7] gave a detailed study of reversal. They confirmed the worst-case bound and described classes of automata and languages attaining this maximal blow-up. Domaratzki and Okhotin [8] studied powers L k of a regular language. For fixed k Ê 2and an n-state DFA for L, they proved the general asymptotic bound. Also Cui et al. [13] completed the study of combined operations with two basic operations among union, intersection, catenation, star, and reversal. The problem of the state complexity of the square-root operation in the deterministic case was first raised by Maslov [4]. He proved the upper bound nn . Then Caron et al. It is well known that for any language L, which is recognized by an NFA with n states, language pL is recognized by an NFA with n3 states—this exercise is often found in university courses on automata theory. The best lower bound was proved by Okhotin and Salomaa [2] and equal to ( n − 1)( n − 2)( n − 3). In this paper, we are going to prove the exact lower bound n3: for every n we provide an example of NFA with n states, which recognizes language L, such that pL is not recognized by any NFA with fewer than n3 states.
