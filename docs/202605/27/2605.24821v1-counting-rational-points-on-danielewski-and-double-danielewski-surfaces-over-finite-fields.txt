SAKSHI GUPTA, ANIT KUCKIAN, AND INDRANATH SENGUPTA ∗

Abstract. Let Fq be the finite field with q elements. We study the number of Fq -rational points on Danielewski and double Danielewski surfaces. For Danielewski surfaces, the point count is reduced to the number of roots of P (Z) over Fq . For double Danielewski surfaces, one has to count the number of tuples (β, γ ) ∈ F2 

> q

, such that P (0 , γ ) = 0 , Q(0 , β, γ ) = 0 hold simultaneously. We compute these numbers using gcd methods, resultants, character sums, Gauss sums, and the König–Rados theorem. We obtain explicit formulas in several structured cases, derive general bounds, and give a Macaulay2 algorithm for verification and show an intresting connection between the number of Fq -rational points of these surfaces and polygonal numbers. 

1. Introduction 

Let Fq denote the finite field with q elements and let F× 

> q

= Fq \ { 0}. Counting rational points on algebraic varieties over finite fields is a basic and important problem in finite field theory and arithmetic geometry. In general, exact formulas are difficult to obtain. Hence a common approach is to study special classes of affine varieties whose defining equations have enough structure to make exact point-counting possible. In recent years, several authors have obtained exact formulas for structured affine varieties over finite fields, especially for diagonal, triangular, and related systems of equations, using tools such as exponent matrices, Smith normal form, linear congruences, character sums, Jacobi sums, and Gauss sums; see, for example, [2, 12, 13, 16]. The present paper follows this general direction, but for a different class of affine varieties. We study the number of Fq-rational points on Danielewski surfaces and double Danielewski surfaces. Danielewski surfaces arise naturally in affine algebraic geometry through the cancellation problem [5]. For a field k, the cancellation problem asks whether an isomorphism 

A[U ] ∼=k B[U ]

for finitely generated k-algebras A and B implies that 

A ∼=k B. 

In transcendence degree two over C, Danielewski constructed explicit counterexamples by con-sidering the affine surfaces 

Sd : XdY − P (Z) = 0 ,

where P (Z) ∈ C[Z] is a non-constant polynomial with distinct roots. If 

Bd = C[X, Y, Z ]/(XdY − P (Z)) 

is the coordinate ring of Sd, then for suitable d1̸ = d2 one has 

Bd1̸ ∼=C Bd2 but Bd1 [U ] ∼=C Bd2 [U ].

Further developments in different settings, including cancellation results and counterexamples, can be found in [1, 9, 10]. Motivated by this construction, Gupta and Sen introduced a more general class called double Danielewski surfaces [11]. Over a field k, such a surface is defined in the affine 4-space A4 

> k

with coordinates X, Y, Z, T by the system 

Xd1 Y − P (X, Z ) = 0 , Xd2 T − Q(X, Y, Z ) = 0 ,

where d1, d 2 ∈ N, the polynomial P (X, Z ) ∈ k[X, Z ] is monic in Z, the polynomial Q(X, Y, Z ) ∈

k[X, Y, Z ] is monic in Y , deg Z P (X, Z ) ≥ 2, and deg Y Q(X, Y, Z ) ≥ 2. If 

Bd1,d 2 = k[X, Y, Z, T ]/(Xd1 Y − P (X, Z ), X d2 T − Q(X, Y, Z )) 

denotes the corresponding coordinate ring, then under suitable hypotheses one has 

Bd1,d 2̸ ∼= Bd1,d 2+1 but Bd1,d 2 [U ] ∼= Bd1,d 2+1 [U ].

Thus double Danielewski surfaces form a natural higher analogue of the Danielewski surfaces and provide a rich class of affine varieties for which arithmetic point-counting questions can be studied explicitly. A further motivation comes from the appearance of polygonal-number patterns in the number of Fq-rational points on certain double Danielewski surfaces. For suitable choices of the defining polynomials P and Q, these point counts form prime-power-indexed subsequences of certain polygonal-number sequences. This encourages both the computation of exact Fq-point counts and the search for polynomial families producing such patterns. Our main aim is to compute the number of Fq-rational points on these surfaces. For the Danielewski surface

Together, these methods provide exact formulas in monomial, binomial, quadratic, permutation-polynomial, and circulant-rank cases, and show that the Fq-point count is governed by the finite-field behavior of the reduced equations.
