# Diagonal Parity and Loop Toggling for Symmetric Matrices over F2

## Mohsen Aliabadi ∗

Abstract 

Let G be a finite simple graph with adjacency matrix A(G) over F2. The closed neighborhood matrix A(G) + I is central in the theory of odd domination. Sutner proved that every graph has an odd dominating set, equivalently 1 lies in the range of A(G) + I, and Batal proved that every such set has cardinality congruent to rank( A(G) + I) modulo 2. We extend this parity phenomenon from closed neighborhood matrices to partially looped graph matrices A(G) + D, where D is an arbitrary diagonal matrix over F2. Equivalently, we work with arbitrary symmetric matrices M over F2 and the natural right-hand side diag( M ). We include a self-contained proof, attributed by Filmus to Alon, that 

diag( M ) ∈ Im( M ), and we prove that every solution of M x = diag( M ) satisfies 

diag( M )⊤x ≡ rank( M ) (mod 2) .

We also give a complete rank and nullity formula for rank-one diagonal perturbations M 7 → M + uu ⊤,which in the graph setting describes exactly how toggling loops changes the associated solution spaces. Finally, for rooted trees with arbitrary diagonal labels, we develop a finite-state boundary recursion that counts all solutions of M (T, ε )x = ε + αe r with prescribed root value, and we derive explicit nullity formulas for complete rooted d-ary trees. For d ≥ 2, we also prove an eventual-periodicity theorem for complete rooted d-ary trees with depth-dependent eventually periodic diagonal labels. 

Key words. Symmetric matrix over F2, partially looped graph, odd dominating set, diagonal perturbation, rank-one update, rooted tree, nullity. 

AMS subject classifications. 05C50, 05C69, 15A03, 15A18. 

# 1 Introduction 

All matrices and vector spaces in this paper are over the field F2. Let G be a finite simple graph with adjacency matrix A(G). The closed neighborhood matrix of G is 

N (G) = A(G) + I. 

A subset S ⊆ V (G) is an odd dominating set if every vertex v satisfies 

|N [v] ∩ S| ≡ 1 (mod 2) ,

where N [v] denotes the closed neighborhood of v. In matrix language, if xS is the characteristic vector of 

S, this condition is 

N (G)xS = 1.

Sutner proved that such a set always exists [4]. Equivalently, 

1 ∈ Im( N (G)) .

Batal later observed the following parity refinement: for every solution x of N (G)x = 1, one has 

1⊤x ≡ rank( N (G)) (mod 2); 

see [1]. Wagner studied the number of such solutions for trees by a rooted recursion and applied this to parity-dimension questions [5]. The purpose of this paper is to extend these results from closed neighborhood matrices to matrices between the adjacency matrix and the closed neighborhood matrix. More precisely, let D be a diagonal matrix over F2 and consider 

M = A(G) + D. 

Such a matrix may be viewed as the adjacency matrix, over F2, of a graph obtained from G by placing loops on exactly the vertices whose corresponding diagonal entry in D is 1. We call this a partially looped graph .Vertices with diagonal entry 1 are treated with closed-neighborhood parity, while vertices with diagonal entry 0 are treated with open-neighborhood parity. The natural right-hand side is then not necessarily 1,but rather 

diag( M ).

Thus the basic system becomes 

M x = diag( M ).

The corresponding existence statement was proved in an unpublished note of Filmus, who attributes the short proof to Noga Alon [2]. Since the argument is brief and since the note is unpublished, we include the proof. It says that if M is symmetric over F2, then 

diag( M ) ∈ Im( M ).

We then prove a parity refinement: if x is any solution of M x = diag( M ), then 

diag( M )⊤x ≡ rank( M ) (mod 2) .

This recovers Batal’s theorem when M = A(G) + I.The paper also develops two technical extensions. First, we analyze loop toggling. Algebraically, toggling a loop at a vertex corresponds to the rank-one diagonal update 

M 7 −→ M + ev e⊤ 

> v

.

More generally, we determine the nullity of M + uu ⊤ for every symmetric M and every vector u. This gives an exact local rule for how the solution space changes when one moves through the interval of matrices between A(G) and A(G) + I.Second, we extend Wagner’s rooted-tree recursion to arbitrary diagonal labels. For a rooted tree T with root r and binary vertex labeling ε, define 

M (T, ε ) = A(T ) + diag( ε).

2For α, β ∈ F2, we count the solutions of 

M (T, ε )x = ε + αe r , xr = β. 

We prove that the corresponding boundary enumerator has only three possible types. This yields an exact finite-state recursion, a nullity formula for arbitrary labeled rooted trees, explicit formulas for complete rooted 

d-ary trees with uniform diagonal labels, and, for d ≥ 2, an eventual-periodicity theorem for depth-periodic labels.

Definition 3.1. Let G be a finite simple graph with vertex set V (G) = {v1, . . . , v n}, and let ε : V (G) → F2

M (G, ε ) = A(G) + diag( ε).

exactly when ε(v) = 1 . A vector x ∈ FV (G)2 is called an ε-odd dominating pattern if 

M (G, ε )x = ε.

Corollary 3.2. For every graph G and every labeling ε : V (G) → F2, there exists an ε-odd dominating pattern. Moreover, for every such pattern x,

ε⊤x ≡ rank( M (G, ε )) (mod 2) .

Proof. The matrix M (G, ε ) is symmetric and satisfies 

diag( M (G, ε )) = ε.

# 7 Concluding remarks and questions 

The results above extend the Sutner–Batal odd-domination phenomenon from closed neighborhood matrices to partially looped graph matrices. In matrix language, the correct replacement for the all-ones vector is the diagonal vector. Thus, for every symmetric matrix M over F2, the affine space 

S(M ) = {x : M x = diag( M )}

is nonempty, and every vector in it has the same diagonal parity: 

diag( M )⊤x ≡ rank( M ) (mod 2) .

The rank-one update formula shows that the behavior of these solution spaces under loop toggling is completely controlled by whether the toggled coordinate vector lies in the range of the current matrix and, if it does, by one distinguished coordinate of a solution of M y = ev .The rooted-tree recursion shows that Wagner’s three-type framework persists for arbitrary diagonal labels. The uniform complete d-ary formulas and the eventual-periodicity theorem suggest several natural directions. (1) Can Wagner’s asymptotic conclusions for closed-neighborhood matrices be extended to random partially looped graphs, where the diagonal labels are also random? (2) For which graph classes beyond trees does a finite-state boundary recursion survive? Natural candidates include caterpillars, unicyclic graphs, bounded-treewidth graphs, and graphs constructed by rooted products. (3) Can the loop-toggling formula be used to obtain sharp distributional results for the rank of A(G) + D

as D ranges over all diagonal matrices? (4) What additional information is carried by the affine spaces S(A(G) + D) as D varies? In particular, how much of the graph structure is encoded by the family of all generalized odd domination spaces?
