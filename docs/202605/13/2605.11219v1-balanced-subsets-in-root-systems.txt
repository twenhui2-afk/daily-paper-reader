# Balanced subsets in root systems 

# Andrei Moroianu ∗† 

# , Paul Schwahn ‡

Balanced and well-balanced subsets of the set of positive roots of compact Lie algebras arise naturally in problems related to Hermitian and spin geometry. In this paper we compute the maximal and minimal size of well-balanced subsets in all simple root systems. 

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 17B22, 17B25 

Keywords : root systems, balanced sets, strongly orthogonal roots. 

# §1 Introduction 

Throughout this article, let R be a (reduced) root system and R+ ⊂ R a choice of positive roots. 

1.1 Definition. A subset S ⊂ R+ is called: (i) balanced if it has a vanishing signed combination, that is, there exist signs sα = ±1

such that ∑ 

> α∈S

sαα = 0 , or equivalently if S can be partitioned into two subsets of vectors having the same sum, (ii) strongly orthogonal if any two elements α, β ∈ S are strongly orthogonal, that is, neither α + β nor α − β is a root, (iii) well-balanced if S is balanced and R+ \ S is strongly orthogonal. Specifying a balanced subset of R+ is equivalent to picking a subset S of the set of all 

roots with the property that ∑ 

> α∈S

α = 0 and S ∩ (−S) = ∅.Strongly orthogonal sets of roots show up in many representation-theoretic, combi-natorial, or geometric problems, e.g. [8, 13, 12, 10, 9, 1, 3, 7, 6]. Thus they have been widely investigated, in particular by Agaoka–Kaneda [2] who classified the maximal such

Balanced and well-balanced subsets also turn out to be useful in differential geometry. Artacho–Semmelmann [4] classified the simple root systems with the property that the entire set of positive roots is balanced (they call it strongly linearly dependent ) and used this to determine which of the full flag manifolds carry invariant spinors. In [11] we use well-balanced sets of roots to construct left-invariant almost Hermitian structures of Gray-Hervella type W1 ⊕ W 3 (semi-Kähler with skew torsion) on compact Lie groups. Using this construction we classify all complete almost Hermitian mani-folds carrying a Hermitian connection with parallel, skew-symmetric and closed torsion. Of particular interest for our construction are those well-balanced subsets S with co-cardinality |R+ \ S|̸ = 1 . This motivates us to study the possible cardinality of such sets. In this article we determine the minimal and maximal possible cardinality of well-balanced subsets of positive root systems. In a reducible root system, well-balanced subsets are always the union of well-balanced subsets of the factors, so we restrict our attention to simple root systems.

# §3 Balanced subsets of maximal cardinality 

The name “well-balanced” is motivated by the following observation. 

3.1 Lemma. A balanced subset of R+ of maximal cardinality is always well-balanced. Proof. Suppose that S ⊂ R+ is balanced, but not well-balanced. Then there exist 

β1, β 2 ∈ R+ \ S such that either β1 + β2 or β1 − β2, is a positive root. Let γ ∈ R+ be this root, and let s : S → {± 1} be a choice of signs such that ∑ 

> α∈S

sαα = 0 .If γ / ∈ S, we can construct a balanced subset S′ := S ∪ { β1, β 2, γ } by extending s

with suitable signs, namely sβ1 = sβ2 = 1 and sγ = −1 if γ = β1 + β2, and sβ1 = 1 and 

sβ2 = sγ = −1 if γ = β1 − β2.If however γ ∈ S, we can again construct a balanced subset S′ := ( S \ { γ}) ∪ { β1, β 2}

by choosing sβ1 = sβ2 = sγ if γ = β1 + β2, and sβ1 = −sβ2 = sγ if γ = β1 − β2.In any case, we have found a balanced subset S′ ⊂ R+ with |S′| > |S|, so S is not of maximal cardinality. We now proceed to the main theorem of this section. Note that the cases where R+

itself is balanced were discussed in [4]; we include them for the sake of completeness.

A balanced subset of R+ can be very small: of course, the empty set is balanced, and (except for rank 1) one can also always find balanced subsets of cardinality 3. It more interesting to ask about the smallest possible cardinality of a well-balanced subset. Since strongly orthogonal roots are in particular orthogonal, a crude upper bound on the cocardinality of a well-balanced subset of R+ is the rank of R. An improvement over this are the sharp upper bounds on the cardinality of strongly orthogonal subsets in [2, Thm. 3.1, Thm. 5.1]. We will use these facts in the proof of the following result.

Proof. Again, we proceed case by case. 

Case An. Two distinct roots of An are strongly orthogonal if and only if their supports are disjoint, and every α ∈ R+ has |Iα| = 2 . This immediately implies that the maximal cardinality of a strongly orthogonal subset is ⌊(n + 1) /2⌋, cf. [2]. For n odd, this is exactly the cocardinality of the well-balanced subset constructed in Theorem 3.2. Now let n = 2 m be even. For each i = 1 , . . . , 2m + 1 and α ∈ R+, the inner product 

⟨ei, α ⟩ can take values 0 or ±1, and there are exactly 2m many α ∈ R+ with ⟨ei, α ⟩

odd. Thus, if S ⊂ R+ is a well-balanced subset, an even number of such roots must lie outside S. But for each i, a strongly orthogonal subset contains at most one element α

with ⟨ei, α ⟩̸ = 0 , since any two such roots are not strongly orthogonal. Thus the only possibility is that no such element lies outside S for each i, hence S = R+. And indeed, by (2.4), R+ is well-balanced. 

Case Bn. Again, for each i = 1 , . . . , n and α ∈ R+, the inner product ⟨ei, α ⟩ can take values 0 or ±1, and there are exactly 2n + 1 many α ∈ R+ such that ⟨ei, α ⟩ is odd. Therefore, if S is a balanced subset of R+, then for each i the number of roots 

α ∈ R+ \ S such that ⟨ei, α ⟩̸ = 0 is odd. On the other hand, for each i, the maximal cardinality of a strongly orthogonal set of positive roots whose support contains i is 2. Consequently, if S ⊂ R+ is well-balanced, then for every i there is exactly one root α ∈ R+ \ S such that ⟨ei, α ⟩̸ = 0 . Since moreover ei and ej are not strongly orthogonal for i̸ = j, it follows that R+ \ S contains at most one root of this form, and all others have a support of cardinality 2.
