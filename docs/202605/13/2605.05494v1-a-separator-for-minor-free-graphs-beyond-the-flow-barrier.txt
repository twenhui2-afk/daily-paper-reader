# A Separator for Minor-Free Graphs Beyond the Flow Barrier 

## Hung Le University of Massachusetts, Amherst 

Abstract 

In 1990, Alon, Seymour, and Thomas [1] gave the first balanced separator of size O(h3/2pn) for any Kh-minor-free graph, which has had numerous algorithmic applications. They conjectured that the size of the balanced separator can be reduced to O(hpn), which is asymptotically tight. Two decades later, Kawarabayashi and Reed [8] constructed a separator of size O(hpn + f (h)) based on the graph minor structure theorem, where 

f (h) is an extremely fast-growing function typically seen in the structure theorem; their separator’s size is only better than that of Alon, Seymour, and Thomas for a very small value of h. Recently, Spalding-Jamieson [20 ]

constructed a separator of size O(h log h log log hpn); the technique is rooted in concurrent flow-sparsest cut duality. Spalding-Jamieson’s separator comes very close to O(h log hpn), which is the barrier for techniques based on the flow-cut duality. In this work, we first present a simple adaptation of a flow-based algorithm of Korhonen and Lokshtanov [10 ]

to construct a balanced separator of size O(h log hpn), matching the flow barrier. This result motivates the question of whether the flow barrier can be broken, which would be a stepping stone toward resolving the conjecture of Alon, Seymour, and Thomas [1]. The main result of our work is a positive answer to this question: we construct a balanced separator of size O(hplog hpn). Surprisingly, perhaps, our algorithm is still based on the iterative framework of Alon, Seymour, and Thomas [1], although a key component of their algorithm within this framework, called the neighborhood bound, was shown to be tight [12 ]. Our new idea is to incorporate a low-diameter decomposition into the framework, allowing us to reduce the neighborhood bound by a factor of 

h, at the cost of a factor log h, which translates to a reduction from ph to plog h in the final separator’s size. Erasing plog h entirely seems to require a substantially new idea. 

# 1 Introduction 

The two most powerful structural results in minor-free graphs that have numerous algorithmic applications are the graph minor structure theorem [19 ] and the separator theorem [1]. The structure theorem says roughly that one can decompose a Kh-minor-free graph into a tree structure of pieces which are simple, with the simplicity is parameterized by a function f (h), an extremely fast-growing function [19 ]. A recent breakthrough [7] reduces 

f (h) to a polynomial function of h, though the proof remains very complicated, spanning hundreds of pages, and the degree of the polynomial remains impractically large. Specifically, f (h) = O(h2300 ). Determining the exact polynomial dependency of f (h) on h (which could well be very practical) and /or giving a simpler construction remains a long-standing open problem [15 ]. On the other hand, separator theorems often have very small dependence on h, and their proofs are often much simpler. A balanced separator of a graph G = ( V, E) is a subset X ⊆ V such that the largest connected component of 

G \ X has size at most 2 n/3. Alon, Seymour, and Thomas [1] were the first to show that any Kh-minor-free graph admits a balanced separator of size O(h3/2pn), which can be found in polynomial time of O(mpnh ). Regarding the dependence on h of the separator size, they remarked: “We think that the expression h3/2 n1/2 [... ] is not the best possible, and that O(hn 1/2) is the correct answer, but have not been able to decide this. [... ] Every 3-regular expander with n vertices is a graph with no Kh-minor for h = cn 1/2, and with no separator of size dn , for appropriately chosen positive constants c and d; and hence the estimate O(hn 1/2) would be the best possible.” 1

A few years later, Plotkin, Rao, and Smith [16 ] constructed a balanced separator of size O(hn plog n) which has an optimal dependence on h but a larger dependence on n. This separator size is suboptimal in the regime 

h = O(1), which includes well-studied classes, such as planar graphs and graphs with constant genus. Nevertheless, the bound by Plotkin, Rao, and Smith is strong evidence supporting the Alon-Seymour-Thomas conjecture. 

Conjecture 1 (Alon-Seymour-Thomas Conjecture [1]). For any h ≥ 3 and n ≥ 1, any Kh-minor-free graph of n

vertices has a balanced separator of size at most c · hpn for some positive constant c.

This conjecture saw very little progress for almost 20 years, until Kawarabayashi and Reed claimed a solution in a FOCS 2010 conference paper [8]. However, as recently remarked by [23 ], one of the authors later clarified in a talk [9] that the precise bound is O(hn 1/2 + f (h)) where f (h) is the function in the graph minor structure theorem. The full version of their paper remains unpublished. And it is unclear if the recent breakthrough on the structure theorem [7] can be applied to reduce f (h) to a polynomial in h. Even if it does, then f (h) remains a very large polynomial of h, which gives a vacuous bound when h = n1/2300 . Moreover, the construction would not be elementary, which has been regarded as the key advantage of separator theorems [1 , 16 , 23 ] over the structure theorem. In any case, Conjecture 1 remains open in its full generality. In a different direction, Biswal, Lee, and Rao [2] studied spectral partitioning algorithms for Kh-minor-free graphs. Their technique gave a different algorithm for constructing a balanced separator of size O(h3pn log h)

based on the duality between concurrent flow and (uniform) sparsest cut. While this separator bound is worse than that of Alon, Seymour, and Thomas [1] by a factor of h3/2plog h, their framework made an important link between padded decomposition and balanced separators via concurrent flow. As of their writing, the best padding parameter was β(h) = O(h2). Recently, Spalding-Jamieson [20 ] studied reweighted spectral graph partitioning for special classes of graphs, and noticed that using the recent padded decomposition by Conroy and Filtser [4], which has β(h) = O(log h), the separator bound can be improved to O(h log (h) log log hpn) (Corollary 1.6. in [20 ]). We note that the proof by Spalding-Jamieson [20 ] via the spectral technique is somewhat complicated. Here, we give a direct proof relating the padded decomposition parameter and the size of the balanced separator, and on the way, remove the log log (h) factor from the separator bound by Spalding-Jamieson [20 ].

Theorem 1. For any n-vertex Kh-minor-free graph, there exists a separator of size O(h log (h)pn) that can be found in randomized polynomial time. 

Our proof of Theorem 1 is by adapting the proof of the separator theorem for Kh-induced minor-free graphs by Korhonen and Lokshtanov [10 ]. In particular, we use the recent padded decomposition with a padding parameter 

β(h) = O(log h) by Conroy and Filtser [4] to replace O(log n) factor with O(log h) in the duality between concurrent flow and sparsest cut; see Section 4 for details. The log (h) factor in Theorem 1 is precisely the padding parameter, which is also the gap between concurrent flow and (uniform) sparsest cut. Conceivably, if one can improve the gap, one gets a smaller separator. However, the log (h) gap is tight: for a degree-3 expander with at most h2 vertices, which excludes KO(h) as a minor, has the flow-cut gap Ω(log (h)) . Thus, the flow-based technique [2 , 20 , 10 ] has a natural barrier of Ω(h log hpn), and Theorem 1 is optimal for this class of techniques. In this paper, we set out to bypass the flow barrier. Our main result is a new combinatorial 1 algorithm for constructing a balanced separator in the following theorem. 

Theorem 2. For any n-vertex Kh-minor-free graph, there exists a separator of size O(hpn log h) that can be found in randomized polynomial time. 

Theorem 2 cuts the gap between the flow-based techniques and Conjecture 1 quadratically, from log (h) to plog h. See Table 1 for an overview of existing bounds. Our proof is elementary, which we view as a stepping stone towards a full resolution of Conjecture 1. Astute readers might realize that the dependence on h in Theorem 2 is coincidentally the same as the dependence on h in the maximum number of edges of a Kh-minor-free graph, which is O(hplog hn ). ## 1.1 Other related work 

In this paper, we focus solely on the existence of a small balanced separator and make no attempt to minimize the running time. There is a long line of work on the computational aspect of computing a balanced separator [1 , 16 ,18 , 8 , 21 , 22 , 23 ]. The goal is to design a faster algorithm, ideally, in linear time, possibly at the cost of a higher dependence on h in the separator’s size. The recent work by Bonnet, Korhonen, Le, Li, and Masaˇ rík [23 ] gives the first linear-time algorithm when h is a constant: the separator size is O(h13 pn) and the running time is O(h13 n).We refer readers to their paper for an in-depth discussion of existing techniques for fast algorithms. A major open question is to obtain the best of both: a linear-time algorithm for computing a balanced separator of size O(hpn),which remains out of reach. 

## 1.2 Proof overview 

In this section, we give an overview of the proof of Theorem 2. An important concept is a minor model , denoted by 

K, which is a collection of vertex-disjoint subgraphs, called branch sets , such that every two branch sets C, C′ ∈ K

have an edge between them. The size of the minor model is the number of branch sets it contains. A graph G is 

Kh-minor-free if and only if it does not contain a minor model of size h. We first describe an iterative algorithmic template for constructing balanced separators by Alon, Seymour, and Thomas [1] (though our presentation differs from the original by Alon, Seymour, and Thomas [1]). This template captures the algorithm by Alon, Seymour, and Thomas [1] and other variants [16 , 21 ]; see also [13 ]. Some components of the template will be redundant in one algorithm but are necessary in another. And all of them will be necessary in our final algorithm. The algorithm iteratively maintains a minor model K, a set of vertices X ⊆ V , and the largest connected component H if one removes the “current separator” from the graph G. The current separator contains X and some other vertices, denoted by F (K, H); the precise definition of F (K, H) varies across algorithms. The key point is that it depends on the structure of K and H. Every branch set in K has at least one neighbor in H. As long as 

|V (H)| > 2n/3 (which means the current separator is not balanced), the algorithm will shrink H and add more vertices to either X or K, which effectively also updates the current separator. If at any point, the size of the minor model K reaches h, then it is a certificate that G is not Kh-minor-free. Also, the template takes a parameter ℓ > 0 as an input, and finds a separator whose size depends on ℓ. In particular, in the two algorithms most relevant to our work, namely, the Alon-Seymour-Thomas (AST) algorithm [1]

Towards a faster algorithm. While our algorithm described above calls LDD in every iteration, the whole analysis rests on the idea that there exists a vertex v that contains 2 n/3 other vertices of H within distance ∆ = ℓ log (h).Instead of calling LDD to find such a vertex v, we sample a random vertex s, and with probability at least 2 /3, 

v ∈ BH (v, ∆). Then, by the triangle inequality, every vertex in BH (v, ∆) ⊆ BH (s, 2∆), and thus, we could use s and distance threshold 2 ∆ in place of v and ∆ in the above algorithm. Overall, we can replace all but one LDD calls by sampling and running BFS. The running time in each iteration becomes O(m log n), where the log n factor is to get a high probability. Observe that the number of iterations is O(h + n/(hℓ log h) = O(h + pn/ log h) = O(pn). Thus, the total running time is O(mpn log n) plus the running time of exactly one LDD call. The LDD in Lemma 1 is obtained by calling padded decomposition [4], which can be implemented in O(n2) time. Implementing padded decomposition in O(m) time (even in the case of constant h) remains an open problem. The LDD in Conjecture 1 seems simpler than the padded decomposition and hence should be amenable to a fast implementation. We leave this as an open problem for future work.
