AUTOMORPHISM GROUPS OF REDUCTIVE AND ROOT MONOIDS  

> ANTON SHAFAREVICH
> Abstract. We describe the automorphism groups of reductive monoids and of root monoids with active groups of invertible elements introduced in [NZ].

1. Introduction 

An algebraic monoid is an algebraic variety X equipped with an associative multiplication 

X × X → X

given by a morphism of varieties and possessing an identity element. Although algebraic monoids have been studied extensively (see, for example, [Br1, Br2, Pu, Re, Ri1]), compar-atively little attention has been paid to their automorphism groups. One of results can be found in [Pu2]. If X is an alebraic monoid then the group of invertible elements G(X) is an open subset in X. If G(X) is a linear group then X is an affine variety, see [Ri2]. We call such monoids linear. When G(X) is a reductive group the monoid X is called reductive. Reductive monoids were classified in [Ri1, Vin]. In recent years, substantial progress has been made in the classification of non-reductive linear algebraic monoids. In [Bi, DZ], all two-dimensional linear algebraic monoids were classified. In [AAZ] and [ABZ], all monoid structures on the affine three-dimensional space were described. Finally, [Za] contains a classification of algebraic monoids whose unit group is a solvable algebraic group of corank 1. Any connected solvable algebraic group G is isomorphic to a semidirect product G =

T ⋉φ U, where T is an algebraic torus, U is a unipotent group, and φ : T → Aut( U ) is a homomorphism. In [Za], the notion of an active solvable algebraic group was introduced. Namely, the group G is called active if dim Im φ = dim U. If G is active, then every algebraic monoid X with unit group G(X) = G is a toric variety. In [NZ], the authors constructed a procedure that endows affine toric varieties with the structure of an algebraic monoid. The monoids obtained in this way are called root monoids . The same paper proves that every algebraic monoid with an active group of invertible elements is a root monoid. This paper is devoted to the study of automorphism groups of linear algebraic monoids. In Section 2, we describe the automorphism groups of reductive monoids. Our descrip-tion is obtained naturally from the known structure of automorphism groups of connected reductive algebraic groups. In Section 3, we recall the construction of root monoids and describe the automorphism groups of root monoids with active groups of invertible elements.
