A SIMPLE BIVARIATE EXAMPLE OF FAST CONVERGENCE RATES FOR MAXIMUM LIKELIHOOD ESTIMATES 

ANDREY SARANTSEV 

Abstract. We present a one-parameter family of bivariate absolutely continuous distribu-tions based on location-scale family of variance Gaussian mixtures, with continuous densities with the same support (effective domain). The maximum likelihood estimation of the loca-tion parameter converges to the true value faster than the classic square root rate. In fact, we can obtain any convergence rate given by a regularly varying function with index greater than 0.5, and some convergence rates given by regularly varying functions with index 0.5 but faster than the classic square root rate. 

1. Introduction 

1.1. Classic results. Take a (univariate or multivariate) family of absolutely continuous distributions, parameterized by θ ∈ R. Their Lebesgue density is f (x, θ ). Take a sample of independent identically distributed (IID) random variables X1, . . . , X n from this distribution. Our goal is to study the maximum likelihood estimate (MLE) ˆθn. Under classic results by Sir Ronald Fisher and others, this estimate is asymptotically normal: 

√n(ˆθn − θ) d

→ N d(0, Σ−1),

where Σ is the Fisher information , defined as the variance of the score function ∇θ log f (x, θ )with x := X having density f (x, θ ). See for example [4, Theorem 10.1.12]. Therefore, the classic convergence rate is √n: The distance between the MLE and the true value is inversely proportional to the square root of the number of data points. However, there exist cases when the convergence rate is faster: The classic example is the uniform distribution on [0 , θ ], where θ > 0 is an unknown parameter. The MLE is then ˆθn = max( X1, . . . , X n), and converges to the true value with fast rate n instead of √n, see [4, Example 5.5.11]: 

n(θ − ˆθn) d

→ Exp(1) , n → ∞ .

The reason is dependence of the effective domain [0 , θ ] (and not just the likelihood func-tion) upon the unknown parameter θ. One can construct other such examples, with rates intermediate between √n and n. See, for example, leave-one-out likelihood in [10], with super-effective rates nε+1 /2. A case when the Fisher information is infinite and the score function converges with slower-than-standard rate is considered in [9].

We stress that we could have generalized this for Gaussian mean-variance mixtures. This would involve three parameters μ, δ, σ instead of only μ. Moreover, we could have included other unknown parameters in the distribution of W , such as the shape and the scale for the Gamma distribution. However, we deliberately chose to present the simplest possible example in this short note. Generalizations are left for further research. One could connect this to empirical Bayes method. Also, we could consider the case when the mean μ differs from observation to observation: Xi | Wi ∼ N (μi, W i), for i = 1 , . . . , n ,but the vector ( μ1, . . . , μ n) of such means belongs to a certain linear subspace.
