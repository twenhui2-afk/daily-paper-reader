A PROOF OF THE 4, 7 CASES OF SYLVESTER’S CONJECTURE ON CUBE SUMS 

HONGBO YIN 

Abstract. In this paper, we prove that every prime p which is con-gruent to 4 , 7 modulo 9 is the sum of two rational cubes. This is 2 /3of Sylvester’s conjecture which has history nearly 150 years since 1879. In the proof, we use recent progress on Full BSD conjecture of rank 0 elliptic curves in [BF24] to deduce the Manin-Stevens constants of some families of elliptic curves are units. We also use recent solutions of Unbounded Denominators Conjecture in [CDT25] to prove that some cubic roots of modular functions are invariant under some congruence subgroups. Instead of using the Unbounded Denominators Conjecuture, we also give another conditional proof assuming the GRH for number fields or Artin’s primitive root conjecture for arithmetic progressions. 

1. Introduction 

The Sylvester problem, i.e. which integer can be written as the sum of two rational cubes (we will call it cube sum for short) has been investigated inten-sively recently, see [CST17][DV18][HSY19][SSY21][SY23][Yin22][ABS][MS22] [MS23] and many other papers on the arXiv for example. This problem has a long history from Euclid, through Diophantine, Fermat, Euler, Dirichlet, and continued to Sylvester (1879) and Selmer (1951) who studied it dramat-ically. For this interesting history, please see Reyna’s wonderful blog [AdR]. From the modern point of view, this problem is closely related to the elliptic curves y2 = x3 − 432 n2 [DV09]. This is very similar to the famous congruent number problem which is closely related to the elliptic curves y2 = x3 − n2x

[Tia14]. These two families of elliptic curves are also the first examples studied by Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer for their famous conjecture. The classical and typical result in the cube sum problem is Sylvester’s theorem that if p ≡ 2, 5, then p is not a cube sum. Contrary to this, Birch and Stephan [BS66] proposed the conjecture that if p ≡ 4, 7, 8, then p should be a cube sum based on the computation of root numbers of the corresponding elliptic curves and the same story also holds for p2. This conjecture is also indicated (but not written down explicitly) by Selmer in [Sel51], where he proved the upper bound of the algebraic rank of the elliptic curves is one. However, this conjecture is usually called Sylvester’s conjecture, maybe be-cause people believe that Sylvester should have thought this conjecture by his result although he did not write it down.

In 1993, Elkies claimed a proof of this conjecture for primes p ≡ 4, 7 mod 9. But he had never published any details about his proof. He only very briefly sketched his principle of constructing the (mock) Heegner points in [Elk94]. Nobody else knows any further information about his construction. So the only known result to this conjecture is some partial result in all cases, see [DV18][Yin22]. In this paper, inspi d by Elkies’s sketch, we give a detailed construction (but not the same as Elkies’s construction) of CM points which give non-torison rational points on the elliptic curves for p ≡ 4, 7 mod 9 and prove the following theorem: 

Theorem 1.1. Let p ≡ 4, 7 mod 9 be a prime, the both p and p2 are sum of two rational cubes.

9. A conditional proof of the main theorem 

In this section we will show that Artin’s primitive root conjecture for arithmetic progressions or the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) for number fields also imply the main theorem in this paper. In 1927, Artin conjectured that any integer n which is not −1 or a perfect square is a primitive root for infinity many primes. This conjecture is quite open but Hooley prove it under the GRH for some number fields [Hoo67] in 1967. The most well known unconditional result is due to Heath-Brown. He proved there are at most two primes that does not satisfy Artin’s conjecture [HB86] improving Gupta-Murty’s breakthrough result in [GM84]. But we even do not known a concrete number satisfying Artin’s conjecture. We also do not know whether for every primes p there exists at least one another prime q such that p is a primitive root of q. There are many kinds of gen-eralizations of Artin’s conjecture, see Moree’s survey [Mor12]. Here we just mention its generalization to arithmetic progressions by Lenstra [Len77], i.e. very integers which is not −1 or a perfect squares should be a primitive root for infinity many primes in every suitable arithmetic progressions without obvious obstructions. Lenstra also proved that GRH implies Artin’s conjec-ture for arithmetic progressions, see also [Mor08] for an explicit asymptotic formula.
