# A Directivity-Dependent Rician K-Factor Model for Indoor Industrial Channels 

Dimitrios C. Tzarouchis, Senior Member, IEEE 

Abstract —We derive a physics-based, closed-form model link-ing antenna directivity to the root-mean-square (RMS) delay spread and mean excess delay in large reverberant indoor envi-ronments. Starting from the Rician K-factor—the ratio of line-of-sight (LOS) to scattered power—we show that K scales with the total transmit-plus-receive (Tx+Rx) antenna gain through a single reverberance factor that quantifies scatter anisotropy. For an arbitrary scatter power delay profile (PDP), we derive a general identity connecting στ , ¯τ , and K; the exponential scatter model is the physically motivated special case. Ray-tracing simulations over 100 random link placements in a 57 300 m 3

industrial hall at 75 GHz validate the model. Compact design rules map target delay-spread values to the minimum required antenna gain, enabling wideband mmWave industrial links. 

Index Terms —E-band, delay spread, antenna directivity, Rician 

K-factor, reverberance factor, indoor industrial channel, indoor electromagnetics, ray tracing. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# THE E-band (71–86 GHz) offers hundreds of MHz of contiguous spectrum and is already deployed in out-door point-to-point backhaul [1]. Extending this to indoor 

links in large industrial halls—warehouses, assembly lines, hangars—is economically attractive, but such enclosures act as partially reverberant cavities and produce high delay spreads that severely degrade wideband performance [2]. Directive antennas suppress late-arriving multipath and reduce the RMS delay spread στ , which is the dominant impairment metric for wideband link design. This effect has been confirmed empirically at 28 and 73 GHz [3], at 60 GHz [1], at 82 GHz [4], at 140 GHz [5], and in RIS-assisted indoor scenarios at 304 GHz [6], suggesting that the phenomenon persists across sub-THz frequencies. However, all reported results remain empirical fits with no physics-based model predicting στ (G)

or ¯τ (G) from measurable room parameters. Several of the required theoretical ingredients exist in the literature but have not been combined into a unified model. The room-electromagnetics framework [7] predicts an expo-nentially decaying diffuse PDP with a Sabine-derived time constant. The K-factor has been shown proportional to antenna directivity through solid-angle integrals [8], and stochastic in-room models [9] incorporate directive antennas but without closed-form delay-spread expressions. What is missing is a compact model mapping total system gain G to closed-form predictions of both ¯τ and στ , with a single physically interpretable scalar quantifying the departure from the ideal isotropic-cavity limit.

For the FILL hall—with a scatter time constant of 127 ns, a baseline K-factor of 0.32, and a reverberance factor of 0.40—achieving an RMS delay spread below 50 ns requires a minimum total system gain of approximately 40 dBi (co-herence bandwidth Bc ≈ 4 MHz), while achieving στ ≤ 5 ns would require approximately 90 dBi ( Bc ≈ 40 MHz). Using the Sabine formula estimate of 78 ns for the reverberation time relaxes the first requirement to approximately 30 dBi, illustrating the sensitivity of the gain budget to the room’s ab-sorption characteristics. The 5 ns target is effectively unreach-able with passive antennas alone, underscoring the need for complementary equalization or extreme beamforming. Both rules are frequency- and hall-agnostic once {τs, K 0, a K } have been extracted. V. C ONCLUSION 

A three-parameter model {τs, K 0, a K } has been derived linking the Rician K-factor to antenna gain through the rever-berance factor aK . For a general scatter PDP, the pair (¯ τ , σ τ )

traces a semicircle parameterized by K; the exponential scat-ter model is the CV s = 1 special case. Two independent 

K-extraction methods—moment-based and power-ratio—both confirm the monotonic K(G) trend; their quantitative gap is explained by the scatter coefficient of variation CV s departing from unity at directive gains. The key result is that στ decays at half the dBi rate of ¯τ , making the RMS delay spread constraint systematically more demanding. For the validated FILL hall at 75 GHz ( aK = 0 .40 ), reaching στ ≤ 50 ns requires Gtot ≈

40 dBi, while στ ≤ 5 ns would need ≈ 90 dBi—confirming that aggressive delay-spread targets in large reverberant enclo-sures cannot be met by directivity alone. The directivity-driven reduction of στ also benefits the equalization subsystem: in single-carrier modems the equalizer length scales with στ /T s,so the K−1/2 decay of (13) translates directly into fewer taps and reduced computational cost [18], [19]. The closed-form expressions (12)–(13) thus allow joint optimization of antenna gain and equalizer overhead for point-to-point deployments in reverberant industrial halls. Several open directions merit investigation: (i) experimental validation with measured PDPs in furnished industrial halls; (ii) characterizing CV s(G) to extend the model beyond the exponential special case; (iii) leveraging the analytical K0(f )

framework of [8] to predict the baseline K-factor from room geometry and frequency alone, removing the need for acalibration measurement at G = 0 dBi; and (iv) coupling the model with 3GPP-type factory channel models [16], [17] to assess whether the aK parameterization transfers to standard-ized deployment scenarios.
