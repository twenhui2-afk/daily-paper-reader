# The Super Virasoro Minimal String from 3d Supergravity 

Lorenz Eberhardt 

Institute for Theoretical Physics, University of Amsterdam, Amsterdam, 1098XH, NL 

E-mail: l.eberhardt@uva.nl 

Abstract: The super Virasoro minimal string is defined by coupling spacelike and timelike super Liouville theories on the worldsheet. There are four different theories 0A ± and 0B ± depending on discrete choices on the worldsheet. We show that these theories arise naturally from quantization of 3d supergravity, and the amplitudes compute the dimension (+) or superdimension ( −) of the space of N = 1 super-conformal blocks modulo crossing symmetry. Both 0A + and 0B + are perturbatively dual to the same matrix integral as the bosonic Virasoro minimal string, while 0B −

is dual to a matrix integral with an inverse square root singularity. We show that all non-trivial perturbative amplitudes of the 0A − theory vanish.

## 1 Introduction 

Non-critical string theories are a particularly rich and fruitful arena to explore world-sheet string theory and its dualities. In works of the 1990s, it was realized that the (p, q ) minimal string exhibits a dual description in terms of a matrix integral [1–3] and the c = 1 string in terms of matrix quantum mechanics [4–7]. The original motivation for these dualities arises from considering multicritical limits of triangu-lated surfaces. Another route relates the minimal string to topological strings on a non-compact Calabi-Yau 3-fold [8]. From a worldsheet perspective such dualities are typically obscured and amount to seemingly miraculous predictions for moduli space integrals. More recently, further examples of such correspondences were found such as the Virasoro minimal string [9] and the complex Liouville string [10]. Recent years have shown an improved handle on the worldsheet theories, which made it possible to directly derive parts of these dualities from the worldsheet [11– 14] and subject them to stringent numerical tests [9, 13, 15, 16]. In particular, the – 1 – Virasoro minimal string and the complex Liouville string are especially tractable since one can exploit analyticity of their amplitudes. The Virasoro minimal string (VMS) is in a sense the simplest theory among those mentioned above. Its worldsheet theory consists of spacelike Liouville theory at central charge c coupled to timelike Liouville theory at central charge 26 − c. As such, its amplitudes depend continuously on c and the external Liouville momenta 

Pi. It was noted in [9] that this theory counts the number of crossing symmetric linear combinations of conformal blocks on the surface Σ g,n . It can thus be viewed as a gravitational analogue of Verlinde’s formula [17]. This relation arises from quantization of 3d gravity with negative cosmological constant, whose phase space is Teichm¨ uller space [18]. The wavefunctions can be identified with conformal blocks [19, 20] and their inner product involves integration over Teichm¨ uller space, exactly of the kind that appears in the VMS. The worldsheet integral computes the number of such conformal blocks which can be computed from an index theorem (Atiyah’s L2 index theorem [21]). This reduces the worldsheet integral to a cohomological expression on the moduli space Mg,n for which well-established mathematical methods exist. In particular, such expressions are well-known to be reproduced by a dual matrix integral. The density of states of this matrix integral is the chiral half of the Cardy density of states of 2d CFTs 

ρ(E) = 2π sinh(2 πb √E) sinh(2 πb −1√E)

Based on this density of states, one can apply the topological recursion procedure to compute any perturbative amplitude [22]. The VMS amplitudes are called the quantum vol-umes since they recover the Weil-Petersson volumes of moduli space in the c → ∞ 

limit. Thus, the VMS constitutes a deformation of JT gravity. Similar cohomological formulas have been proposed for the amplitudes of the 

c = 1 string [23], the (2 , p ) minimal string [24] and the complex Liouville string [10]. In each of these cases, their amplitudes admit an expansion in Feynman diagrams in which the quantum volumes serve as the vertex factors. Various supersymmetric generalizations of these theories and their dualities have been studied, such as for the ( p, q ) minimal string [25–27] and the c = 1 string [28–30]. Surprisingly, the supersymmetric analogue of these theories is often closely related to their bosonic avatars, a fact that is also obscured from the worldsheet theory. In this paper, we consider the super Virasoro minimal string from a 3d gravity perspective. Some aspects of the theory have already been studied from the world-sheet [31, 32] and the matrix integral perspective [33, 34]. On the worldsheet, the super VMS is defined by coupling N = 1 spacelike Liouville theory of central charge 

c = 32 + 3( b + b−1)2 to N = 1 timelike Liouville theory of central charge 15 − c.– 2 – The super Virasoro minimal string can also be obtained by studying the quanti-zation of N = 1 supergravity in three dimensions. As a consequence, the worldsheet amplitudes count the number of crossing symmetric combinations of N = 1 super-conformal blocks on the (super) surface Σ g,n . There are two discrete signs in this counting. For only NS-sector punctures, the spin structure on Σ g,n can be even or odd and one can choose to weigh the odd spin structures by a sign ( −1) ζ in the counting. The unweighted count corresponds to type 0B string theory which does feature both NS- and R-sector vertex operators, while the weighted count corresponds to type 0A string theory, which only has NS-sector vertex operators. Additionally, we can choose to include a sign ( −1) F into the counting. We denote the theories without ( −1) F by 0A +/0B + and those with ( −1) F counting by 0A −/0B −.The appearance of the same sign was already observed in the worldsheet theory in [31, 32], where it corresponds to a sign flip of a chiral supercharge in one of the Liouville theories. One can count the number of superconformal blocks in terms of an index theorem on super moduli space. We show that the resulting expression can be reduced to its bosonic subspace, the moduli space of spin curves. Standard theorems in algebraic geometry [35] allow us to write down explicit intersection number expressions for the super quantum volumes. We further carry out the sum over spin structures and reduce everything to standard intersection numbers on the moduli space of curves. For the 0A/B + theories, we obtain compact formulas in terms of the basic κ and 

ψ-classes on Mg,n , while the 0A/B − cases additionally lead to the appearance of the so-called Theta class (or a variation thereof) [36]. From these expressions, we show that all these theories can be described via a matrix integral. More precisely, we show that 1. Type 0A + is perturbatively equivalent to bosonic VMS and shares the same matrix integral dual. 2. Type 0B + is also perturbatively equivalent to bosonic VMS. Moreover, ampli-tudes involving Ramond-sector vertex operators are identical (up to normal-ization) to amplitudes with only NS-sector vertex operators. 3. Apart from the sphere two-point function, all perturbative amplitudes of type 0A − vanish identically. This vanishing is non-trivial. We prove the vanishing of the corresponding intersection numbers by employing cohomological field theory techniques in appendix B. As far as we are aware, this vanishing theorem has not appeared in the mathematical literature. 4. Type 0B − with NS-sector vertex operators is dual to a matrix integral with density of states 

ρ(E) = 2π cosh(2 πb √E) cosh(2 πb −1√E)

√E , (1.2) – 3 – which contrary to (1.1) features an inverse square root singularity as E → 0. As a consequence this model behaves quite differently to the 0A/B + theories. It follows the Br´ ezin-Gross-Witten hierarchy [37–40] and all its tree-level am-plitudes vanish (but higher genus amplitudes are non-zero). This paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we discuss 3d supergravity, its quantization and relation to the super VMS and the cohomological reduction via the index theorem. We also discuss the origin of the four different theories. In section 3, we further analyze the resulting cohomological expressions and explain how they can be reduced to intersection numbers on ordinary moduli space, which ultimately allows us to relate them to a matrix integral. We end with a short discussion in section 4. In appendix A, we review topological recursion for density of states with inverse square root singularity and its relation to intersection theory. In appendix B, we prove the vanishing of the intersection numbers of the type 0A − theory. 

## 2 Super VMS amplitudes from 3d supergravity 

We will study chiral 3d N = (1 , 1) supergravity with negative cosmological constant in canonical quantization. Our goal in this section is to give a description of the Hilbert space on a spatial Cauchy slice. The logic is in parallel to the construction of bosonic Virasoro TQFT [18]. Some aspects of the quantization were also worked out in [41]. 

2.1 Chern-Simons theory and phase space Phase space from Chern-Simons theory. At the level of the action and the equations of motion N = (1 , 1) supergravity is equivalent to Chern-Simons theory with gauge group (OSp(1 |2) /Z2) × (OSp(1 |2) /Z2) [42]. Since the gauge group fac-torizes, we can discuss quantization of one factor separately. The two factors should be thought of as holomorphic and antiholomorphic states. We refer to the theory with a single OSp(1 |2) /Z2 factor as chiral N = 1 supergravity. There are various global issues in this identification. We picked the particular global form OSp(1 |2) /Z2 of the gauge group. In the action, only the algebra osp (1 |2) is visible. The bosonic subgroup of OSp(1 |2) is O(1) × Sp(2 , R) ∼= Z2 × SL(2 , R). The Z2 factor acts by a minus sign on the fermionic directions. We thus think of it as ( −1) F. Bosons transform in SL(2 , R) representations that originate from the quotient SO(1 , 2) ∼= PSL(2 , R) ∼= SL(2 , R)/Z2, while fermions transform in spinorial representations of the local Lorentz group SO(1 , 2). ## 3 Intersection theory and topological recursion 

Our main task will now be to evaluate the right hand side further. We will show that it can be reduced to intersection theory on the ordinary moduli space Mg,n . This will allow us to relate it to topological recursion. 

3.1 Reducing to the moduli space of spin curves Split supermanifolds. Let us recall the notion of a split supermanifold. Let M

be a bosonic manifold and F a vector bundle over it. Then one can reverse the parity of the fibers of F and obtain a supermanifold Π F . On Π F , the bosonic co-ordinates x1, . . . , x nb parametrize M , while the directions along the fibers constitute the fermionic coordinates θ1, . . . , θ nf . A supermanifold of this form is said to be split. This structure is not generic, since the coordinate transition functions act separately on the bosonic coordinates and are linear in the fermionic coordinates. For more gen-eral supermanifolds, the transition functions of the bosonic coordinates can involve any superfunction, such as fermion bilinears. For real smooth supermanifolds, splitness is the generic situation: Batchelor’s theorem [68] states that any such supermanifold M with bosonic subspace M is iso-morphic to Π F for some choice of vector bundle F (although not canonically so). The analogous statement fails in the complex-analytic category [53, 69]. In partic-ular, Mg, a is generically not split as a complex supermanifold [70]. ## 4 Discussion 

We conclude by discussing a few future directions and connections to other works. 

Worldsheet analysis. We have studied the super Virasoro minimal string from the point of view of 3d gravity and have said little about actual worldsheet explorations. – 25 – Building on [31, 32], this perspective will be further developed in a forthcoming paper [82]. 

Super JT gravity. In the b → 0 limit, super VMS reduces to super JT gravity studied in [76]. This is manifest from the 3d gravity perspective: b → 0 corresponds to ℏ → 0 in the quantization procedure and the number of quantum states becomes the volume of phase space, i.e. supersymmetric generalizations of the Weil-Petersson volume. Many of the statements made in this paper have an analogous statement in super JT gravity. 

Super topological recursion. Recently a framework of topological recursion on super surfaces was developed [83, 84]. Since we reduced all expressions to ordinary moduli space, we do not make use of this formalism, but it could perhaps constitute an alternative route for supersymmetrizing the VMS dualities. 

Perturbative vanishing of 0A −. As mentioned above, all perturbative amplitudes (apart from the sphere two-point function) vanish identically (and non-trivially) in type 0A −. This exact perturbative vanishing is a rare phenomenon in string theory. Another example with a related property is the N = 2 string/self-dual gravity [85, 86]. It would be interesting to understand whether there is a general underlying mechanism guaranteeing such vanishing theorems. 

Ramond gas for 0B −. Type 0B − features also Ramond punctures. In the world-sheet description, their amplitudes vanish identically. From intersection theory, it is however possible to define non-vanishing amplitudes with Ramond-punctures and we argued that the discrepancy can be traced back to an operator normalization in the worldsheet theory. The intersection expressions for amplitudes with Ramond inser-tions are not naturally described by the matrix integral. Instead it is more natural to consider a ‘gas’ of such punctures, i.e. turn on an R-R background for the model. It is natural to conjecture that such a gas of Ramond punctures deforms the spectral curve, as is also conjectured for JT supergravity in [74, 76]. 

Super complex Liouville string. One can similarly wonder about supersymmetric generalizations of the complex Liouville string. There are also four theories 0A +,0B +, 0A − and 0B − in that case. Based on a worldsheet bootstrap, [87] made a proposal for a (two-) matrix integral dual which bears striking resemblance to the super VMS case discussed in this paper. It would be interesting to understand those dualities also from a more conceptual viewpoint. 

N = 2 super VMS. One can try to further generalize to N = 2 worldsheet supersymmetry. This is technically difficult from the worldsheet since spacelike and timelike N = 2 theories are much less understood than their N = 0 and N = 1 counterparts. Nonetheless, it may be possible to still establish a duality by studying the moduli space of N = 2 super Riemann surfaces. The classical limit is expected to reduce to N = 2 JT supergravity, which was studied in [88] and found to be equivalent to bosonic JT gravity (in a fixed charge sector). 

Heterotic VMS. Another generalization is a variant inspired by [89, 90]. Its world-– 26 – sheet theory is given by coupling timelike N = 1 Liouville theory to spacelike N = 1 Liouville theory with 22 left-moving fermions, right-moving βγ ghosts and the usual 

bc ghosts. Since this worldsheet theory is not left-right symmetric, the relation to 3d gravity does not simply generalize. Thus it constitutes an interesting generalization pushing the boundaries of the minimal string/matrix integral paradigm.
