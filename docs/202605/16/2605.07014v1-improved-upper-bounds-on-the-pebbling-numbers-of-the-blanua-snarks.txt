# Improved Upper Bounds on the Pebbling Numbers of the Blanuša Snarks 

Tong Niu April 2026 

Abstract 

The two Blanuša snarks B1 and B2 are 3-regular graphs on 18 vertices. Dantas, Lordelo, Niedermaier and Nogueira (Discrete Appl. Math. 361, 2025, pp. 336–346) established 23 ≤

π(Bi) ≤ 34 for i = 1 , 2, leaving a gap of 11 in each case. We pull the upper bounds down to π(B1) ≤ 28 and π(B2) ≤ 29 . The route is Hurlbert’s Weight Function Lemma, applied one orbit at a time; the optimal weight functions come from a linear program over a corpus of roughly 30 ,000 rooted-subtree strategies per target. For the lower bound π(Bi) ≥ 23 we re-derive the witnesses of Dantas et al. and re-verify them with two independent oracles: an exhaustive forward state-space search, and a sound-and-complete MILP encoding whose acyclicity constraint is motivated by the Milans–Clark No-Cycle Lemma. The published gaps [23 , 34] thus shrink to [23 , 28] for B1 and [23 , 29] for B2.

# 1 Introduction 

A pebble distribution on a graph G = ( V, E ) is a function C : V → Z≥0. A pebbling move removes two pebbles from a vertex u and places one pebble at a neighbour v. We say a distribution 

C is r-solvable if some sequence of pebbling moves ends with at least one pebble on r, and 

r-unsolvable otherwise. The pebbling number π(G, r ) is then the smallest integer t such that every distribution of size t is r-solvable, and π(G) = max r∈V π(G, r ).Two foundational results: π(Pn) = 2 n−1 for paths [6] and π(Qn) = 2 n for hypercubes [2]. The definition is short, almost combinatorial folklore, yet exact pebbling numbers stay open for most specific small graphs — including, until recently, every snark larger than the Petersen graph. A snark is a connected 3-regular graph with chromatic index 4 and girth at least 5. The two Blanuša snarks B1 and B2 [1] have 18 vertices each. They share diameter 4 and girth 5 but are not isomorphic, and their automorphism groups differ: Aut( B1) ∼= D4 has order 8, while 

Aut( B2) ∼= Z2 ×Z2 has order 4. Dantas, Lordelo, Niedermaier and Nogueira [3] recently gave the first systematic table of pebbling-number bounds for snarks. They established 23 ≤ π(Bi) ≤ 34 

for i = 1 , 2, a gap of 11 in both cases. 

1.1 Contribution 

The route taken here is Hurlbert’s Weight Function Lemma [4]. We apply it one automorphism orbit at a time — the Blanuša snarks are not vertex-transitive, so a single weight function will not do — and combine with a covering linear program over a corpus of about 30 ,000 rooted-subtree strategies per target.

3.1 Strategy enumeration and the LP 

For each orbit representative r in B1 or B2, we enumerate every connected rooted subtree T ⊆ G

containing r with |V (T )| ≤ 16 by edge-by-edge growth: start with T = {r}, then iteratively pick an edge (u, v ) with u ∈ V (T ) and v / ∈ V (T ) and add v. We deduplicate on the weight signature (wT (v1), . . . , w T (vn)) . The result is somewhere between 22 ,000 and 30 ,000 distinct strategies per target. On top of that we throw in all shortest paths from r to every other vertex; each such path is a subtree, but distinct shortest paths give distinct signatures, so we keep them separately. The LP from Corollary 5 is then handed to HiGHS through scipy.optimize.linprog . Its real-valued optimum is the desired LP upper bound; the integer upper bound on π(G, r ) is the floor. A sanity check: pushing the subtree size cap from 14 up to 18 did not move the LP optimum, so the bounds we report are effectively those of strategies of size ≤ 16 .

# 4 Lower bounds and verification 

We re-derive the lower bound π(Bi) ≥ 23 of [3] and verify it independently. The starting point, for each orbit representative r, is the canonical diameter-cone distribution: park 2diam( G) − 1 = 

315 pebbles at one of the four vertices at distance 4 from r. From there we add single pebbles greedily at non-conflicting vertices, iterating until no further addition is safe. Both snarks then admit 22-pebble unsolvable distributions.

The remaining gaps — 5 for B1 and 6 for B2 — are narrower than the gap of 8 that still holds for the Flower snark J5 [3], but they are nowhere near the gap of 0 enjoyed by the Petersen graph and the Flower snark J3. Two routes might close them further. The first is to drop the basic-strategy restriction. Non-basic strategies [4, Section 5] allow strict inequalities 

wT (u) > 2wT (v) at edges that are not tight, and they arise as conic combinations of nested basic strategies; the resulting certificates can be both shorter and sharper. A second route is to use combinatorial structure — the 3-regularity and the class-1 edge decomposition that defines a snark — in the spirit of the Petersen-graph argument that gave π(Petersen) = 10 . We have not pursued either here. All certificates — the strategy weight functions, LP solutions, and witness distributions — are stored as JSON in the supplementary material. The verifier code, written in Python on top of networkx and HiGHS via SciPy, is included as Appendix B.
