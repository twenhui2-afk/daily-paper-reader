The above conjecture is one of the most important conjectures in singularity theory. It remains wide open in general. Conjecture 1.2 (Budur, Mustat ¸˘ a, and Teitler) . If f ∈ C[z1, . . . , z n] is a polyno-mial of degree d such that (f = 0) defines an irreducible, essential, central hyper-plane arrangement, then bf (− nd ) = 0 .

Conjecture 1.2 is known for reduced f with n ≤ 3 [BSY11], for Weyl hyperplane arrangements [BW17], for tame hyperplane arrangements [Wal17] (see also [XY25]), and for f with generic multiplicities [SZ24]. It was claimed by Davis and Yang [DY26] that they proved Conjecture 1.2 by using the theory of Hodge modules. In this paper, we prove Conjecture 1.2 in general, and hence Conjecture 1.1 for hyperplane arrangements, without using Hodge theory. More precisely, we prove: 

Theorem 1.4. Let f be a polynomial of degree d (not necessarily reduced) such that (f = 0) defines an irreducible, essential, central hyperplane arrangement. Then 

−k/d is a root of bf (s) for k = n, n + 1 , . . . , d . In particular, Conjecture 1.2 holds. 

The proof relies on the theory of algebraic relative (holonomic) D-modules. Specifically, we prove the commutativity of duality functors with proper direct images for algebraic relative D-modules (see Proposition 2.1), which serves as an algebraic analogue of a fundamental result by Schapira and Schneiders [SS94] for analytic relative D-modules. Another key ingredient is the Beilinson–Bernstein construction of D-module nearby cycles (see §3). By combining this commutation property, the Beilinson–Bernstein nearby cycles, and resolution of singularities, we first provide an alternative proof of Kashiwara’s rationality theorem for b-functions [Kas76], as well as Lichtin’s refinement (Theorem 4.1). The strategy underlying our proof of Theorem 4.1 establishes the framework for proving Theorem 1.4. Further-more, to better control the section “ f s” before and after passing to a resolution, we must also consider the special direct images of D-modules (cf. [Bj¨ o13, Ch. II.4, 

§2.4.3]). We employ the wonderful model of De Concini and Procesi as the canonical log resolution of the hyperplane arrangement. Exploiting the wonderful model, we prove that the standard direct image and the special direct image of the !-extension of Beilinson-Bernstein on the resolution coincide globally (see Lemma 4.5). This crucial identification allows us to trace the section “ f s” back to the resolution, thereby completing the proof of Theorem 1.4. In the proof, we also utilize the propagation property of cohomology jumping loci for rank one local systems on the hyperplane arrangement complement [DSY17] to establish the non-vanishing result in Lemma 4.4. A broader version of Conjecture 1.2, concerning the zeroes of Bernstein-Sato ideals associated with hyperplane arrangements, is given in [Bud15, Conjecture 1.13]. One can check that the proof of Theorem 1.3 readily extends to this general case.
