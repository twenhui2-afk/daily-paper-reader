ON MODULAR INEQUALITIES FOR PLANE PROJECTIVE CURVES 

JOSE IGNACIO COGOLLUDO-AGUST ´IN ∗ AND ANCA M ˘ACINIC ∗∗ 

Abstract. We introduce modular inequalities for complements of plane curves, based on a Com-binatorial Aomoto complex construction associated with the weak combinatorial type of a curve. We use this as a tool to investigate twisted Alexander polynomials, in particular to study the char-acteristic polynomial of the Milnor fiber associated to a projective plane curve, i.e. the classical Alexander polynomial. We give criteria for the non-triviality of the resonance in degree 1, over a positive characteristic field, for curves with quasi fiber–type structure and we compute lower bounds for the multiplicities of some roots of twisted Alexander polynomials for this type of curves. We apply these results to theoretically compute the multiplicities of certain roots of twisted Alexander polynomials for fiber–type curves.

The purpose of this paper is to give estimates for the multiplicities bk of the classical Alexander polynomial in (1) and bk, ¯m for the twisted version in (2) in case k = ps, for p prime. In order to do that we use two different tools that offer respectively upper and lower bounds to these multiplicities bk and bk, ¯m.Upper bounds are obtained via the weak combinatorial type of a curve C, which can be seen as a generalization to curves of the classical concept of combinatorics of line arrangements, developed by the first author in [4] and jointly with Matei in [5]. We illustrate this result by computing bounds for the multiplicities of roots of the classical Alexander polynomials of order powers of primes, for a series of examples. We also present in §4.1 a triviality criterion for the cohomology in degree 1 of the Combinatorial Aomoto complex with coefficients in a finite field. This in turn enables us to formulate combinato-rial conditions to ensure bk = 0, see Theorem 22. Section §4.2 is devoted to the twisted counterpart of the previous results. In addition, in §4.3 we present some examples. A tool for obtaining lower bounds for bk, and more generally for bk, ¯m, is presented in §5 and applied to (quasi) fiber-type curves , that is, the class of curves that can be obtained as a union of fibers of a pencil. These lower bounds depend on certain properties of the pencil. This class contains as a subclass the fiber–type curves, also known as pencil–type curves, in particular the Halpen pencils of index k ≥ 1 defined in [10], and the projective line arrangements that admit combinatorial pencil/multinet structures as defined in [5, 13]. The lower bounds for bk, ¯m in the (quasi) fiber-type curve case are given precisely by the number and multiplicity of the non-reduced fibers of the pencil C is a union of, but also, and more surpris-ingly, by the extra multiple fibers that are not part of C, see Theorem 37. This generalizes several recent results, for instance [9, Theorems 3.2 – 3.4]. It is worth noticing that this result applies not only to roots of unity of order prime powers as the results in section §4 did. In §6 we present a series of applications to reprove known results by Dimca-Pokora-Sticlaru in [9] on Halphen curves and to provide new examples of calculations of bk when it coincides with the upper and lower bounds obtained above. Finally, in §7 we revisit Yoshinaga’s counterexample to a conjecture by Papadima–Suciu [21]. The conjecture questioned the existence, in the class of arrangements of hyperplanes, of examples for which the modular inequalities are strict.
