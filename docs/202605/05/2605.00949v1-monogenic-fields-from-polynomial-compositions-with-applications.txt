1. Introduction 

A number field K is said to be monogenic if its ring of integers ZK admits a power integral basis; that is, if there exists θ ∈ ZK such that ZK = Z[θ]. The study of monogenic number fields is a classical topic in algebraic number theory, originating in the work of Dedekind. It continues to attract considerable attention due to its deep connections with discriminants, index forms, and Diophantine equations. Let f (x) ∈ Z[x]

be an irreducible monic polynomial and let α be a root of f (x). The polynomial f (x) is called monogenic if 

ZQ(α) = Z[α]. In this case, the number field Q(α) is necessarily monogenic. However, in general, the converse does not hold. This distinction highlights the subtle nature of monogenity at the level of polynomials. In recent decades, explicit criteria for the monogenity of polynomials have played a central role in the construction of monogenic fields. Notable examples include pure fields, trinomials, generalized trinomials, and families related to special recursive polynomials. A particularly subtle situation arises when considering 

compositions of polynomials . Even if a polynomial f (x) is monogenic, the composed polynomial f (g(x)) does not need to be monogenic and the arithmetic of the index [ZK : Z[α]] becomes significantly more involved. A fundamental tool in this context is the relation between the discriminant of a number field and that of a defining polynomial. Let K = Q(θ) and f (x) be the minimal polynomial of θ, then (1.1) Df = [ ZK : Z[θ]] 2dK ,

where dK denotes the discriminant of K and Df is the discriminant of f (x)1. Formula ( 1.1 ) shows that knowledge of Df and the index [ZK : Z[θ]] allows one to determine the field discriminant dK .The study of the irreducibility and arithmetic properties of polynomial compositions has a rich history. The work of Jones has provided deep insights into the irreducibility and Galois-theoretic behavior of composite polynomials. From an arithmetic perspective, Evertse and Győry [ 11 ], and further Gaál [ 12 ] developed general techniques for controlling indices and discriminants, which are particularly effective when combined with congruence conditions in coefficients. These methods have been successfully applied to several families of composed polynomials, although a general theory remains far from complete. Significant progress in the monogenity of polynomial compositions has been achieved in recent years. In particular, Jones [ 22 ]constructed infinite families of monic Eisenstein polynomials f (x) ∈ Z[x] such that the power composition polynomials f (xdn

) are monogenic for all integers n ≥ 0 and any integer d > 1, provided that f (x) is Eisenstein with respect to every prime divisor of d. In 2012, Kedlaya [ 27 ] provided a method to construct, for each integer n ≥ 2, infinitely many degree-n monic irreducible polynomials f (x) ∈ Z[x] such that Df is squarefree. Then these polynomials are necessarily monogenic by ( 1.1 ). However, when Df is not squarefree, this no longer guaranties that f (x) is monogenic, and determining monogenity in such cases can be difficult. In 2020, Jones [ 20 ], using a result of Pasten [ 30 , Theorem 1.1] along with a modification of Kedlaya’s techniques, constructed explicit infinite families of monic irreducible polynomials f (x) ∈ Z[x] of degree p, for every prime p ≥ 3, such that Df is not squarefree but f (x) is monogenic. Furthermore, Jones and White [ 25 ]gave an asymptotic formula for certain special types of monogenic trinomials. In 2022, Bhargava, Shankar and Wang [ 5] proved that at least 30% of polynomials have squarefree discriminant and are therefore monogenic.
