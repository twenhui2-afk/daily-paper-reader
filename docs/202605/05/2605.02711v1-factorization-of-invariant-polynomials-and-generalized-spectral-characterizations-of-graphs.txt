# Factorization of invariant polynomials and generalized spectral characterizations of graphs 

Wei Wang ∗

> School of Mathematics, Physics and Finance, Anhui Polytechnic University, Wuhu 241000, China

Abstract 

The problem of characterizing graphs by their generalized spectra has received significant attention in recent years. This paper provides a complete proof of a conjecture proposed by Wang, Wang, and Zhu (European J. Combin., 2023), which asserts that the square-root polynomial of the invariant polynomial Φ p(G; x) ∈ Fp[x] can replace its square-free part to yield a more effective criterion for a graph to be determined by its generalized spectrum (DGS). A key ingredient of our proof is a novel algebraic factorization: we show that the polynomial Φ p(G; x) is the product of the characteristic polynomials of the adjacency operator restricted to the left null space of the walk matrix and its radical, respectively. Based on this refined DGS-criterion, a broad family of DGS-graphs is constructed via rooted products, significantly generalizing the recent result of Wang, Shen, and Mao (Discrete Appl. Math., 2026). 

Keywords: generalized spectrum, walk matrix, determined by generalized spectrum, rooted product graphs. 

# 1 Introduction 

Let G be a simple graph on n vertices with adjacency matrix A. We often identify a graph G

with its adjacency matrix A. The spectrum of a graph G, denoted by σ(G), is the multiset of eigenvalues of A. The generalized spectrum of G is the ordered pair ( σ(G), σ (G)), where G is the complement of G. Two graphs are generalized cospectral if they share the same generalized spectrum; a graph G is determined by its generalized spectrum (DGS) if any graph generalized cospectral with G is isomorphic to G.The walk matrix of G is defined as 

W = W (G) = [ e, Ae, . . . , A n−1e],

where e is the all-ones vector. This kind of matrices has attracted increasing attention in recent years, as many important properties of graphs, including the DGS-property, are closely related to the corresponding matrices; see, e.g. [3, 6, 10, 12, 19]. It is known [17] that for an n-vertex graph G, the determinant of W (G) is always a multiple of 2 ⌊n/ 2⌋, i.e., 2 −⌊ n/ 2⌋ det W (G) ∈ Z. An integer b is called square-free if p2 ∤ b for any prime 

p. In particular, the number ±1 is square-free, but 0 is not. Wang [16, 17] gave the following arithmetic criterion for a graph to be DGS. 

Theorem 1.1 ([16, 17]) . Let G be an n-vertex graph. If 2−⌊ n/ 2⌋ det W (G) is odd and square-free, then G is DGS.

# 5 Application to DGS-graph construction 

In a recent paper [15], Wang et al. proposed a general method to construct larger DGS-graphs using the rooted product operation. Let H(v) be a rooted graph, where v denotes the root vertex. For a graph G, the rooted product graph G ◦ H(v) is obtained from G and n copies of 

H by identifying the root of the ith copy of H with the ith vertex of G for each i. They [15] focused on a specific family of graphs 

Fn = {an n-vertex graph G : det A(G) = ±1 and 2 −⌊ n

We note that Fn is empty when n is odd as any adjacency matrix of odd order is singular over 

F2 (being skew-symmetric with zero diagonal), whereas Fn is nonempty for all even integers 

n ≥ 6, see [9]. Let F = ∪F n, where n runs through all even integers n ≥ 6. It is known that each graph in F is DGS by Theorem 1.1. In the same paper, Wang et al. obtained a sufficient condition for a rooted graph H(v) to be an F-preserver , that is, to guarantee G ◦ H(v) ∈ F for each G ∈ F .As an application of our main result (Theorem 1.6), we aim to generalize this preservation property to a larger family of graphs where the last invariant factor of W is squarefree but may have odd prime factors.
