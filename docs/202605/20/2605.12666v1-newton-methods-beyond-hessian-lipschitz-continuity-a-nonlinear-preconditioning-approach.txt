# Newton methods beyond Hessian Lipschitz continuity: A nonlinear preconditioning approach 

Alexander Bodard ∗ Panagiotis Patrinos ∗

May 14, 2026 

Abstract 

Newton-type methods are typically analyzed under Lipschitz continuity of the Hessian, an assumption that can fail for objectives with higher-order or polynomial growth. We introduce a class of nonlinearly preconditioned Newton methods that apply Newton’s root-finding scheme to a transformed optimality mapping, thereby extending recent nonlinear preconditioning ideas from first-order methods to the second-order setting. The resulting methods are naturally analyzed under Lipschitz continuity of a preconditioned Hessian, a condition that significantly relaxes the classical Hessian Lipschitz continuity assumption. Under this generalized smoothness model, we establish local superlinear and quadratic convergence guarantees, and develop a globalization strategy for the nonregularized method despite the fact that the preconditioned Newton direction need not be a descent direction. We further propose a regularized variant for isotropic preconditioners, and show that it attains an O(ε−3/2) iteration complexity. An adaptive version removes the need to know the smoothness constant and allows inexact subproblem solutions while preserving the same complexity order. 

Keywords: Newton methods · generalized smoothness · nonlinear preconditioning

In this work, we propose a novel class of Newton methods for solving (P) that serves as a second-order counterpart to (PG) , and we study its properties under anisotropic smoothness and related notions. By designing Newton variants based on generalized smoothness conditions that better reflect the properties of the cost function, we hope to obtain novel algorithms with improved practical performance, and, more generally, to narrow the gap between optimization theory and practice. 

Contributions Our contributions can be summarized as follows. 

• We propose a class of preconditioned Newton methods of which the update directions satisfy (PN) . This scheme is naturally analyzed under a Lipschitz continuity assumption on the preconditioned Hessian (cfr. Assumption 2.2). For appropriate reference functions this may relax the usual Hessian Lipschitz continuity condition. Notably, we show that Assumption 2.2 holds for any 1D polynomial objective. We characterize the region of quadratic convergence of (PN) in terms of the potentially more favorable Lipschitz constant of the preconditioned Hessian . • Restricting ourselves to isotropic reference functions, we then present a regularized variant that attains an O(ε−3/2) complexity under this generalized smoothness condition. 1.1 Related work 

Nonlinear Preconditioning The nonlinearly preconditioned gradient method (PG) was first analyzed in the convex setting by [27], and later in the nonconvex setting [20, 34] under anisotropic smoothness, a notion introduced in [21] and closely related to the concept of Φ-convexity [22, 33]. The method has also been extended to measure spaces [2]. Recent works presented variants with momentum and a stochastic analysis [35], and established saddle point avoidance of the scheme [1]. 

Generalized smoothness Gradient-based methods are traditionally analyzed under restric-tive Lipschitz smoothness assumptions, see e.g. [30]. Bregman relative smoothness is a milder condition that allows for unbounded Hessians [26]. More recently, the ( L0, L 1)-smoothness condition, proposed by [42] based on empirical observations in training LSTMs, has gained traction. Further generalizations include α-symmetric smoothness [10] and ℓ-smoothness [25]. Remark that anisotropic smoothness subsumes ( L0, L 1)-smoothness and ℓ-smoothness for certain reference functions [1]. For Newton methods, Lipschitz continuity of the Hessian or self-concordance are often assumed [31, 17, 11]. 

Newton’s method Following the terminology of [27], the proposed method (PN) is derived by ‘left’-preconditioning ∇f , cfr. (2) . A distinct class of ‘right’-preconditioned Newton methods has been studied in [4, 3]. Various globalization and regularization techniques have been explored for Newton methods. Cubic regularization methods [32] attain an O  ε−3/2 complexity under Lipschitz continuity of the Hessian. This is optimal for this class of problems [5]. Following the works of [6, 7], adaptive variants that attain the optimal rate without knowledge of the actual Lipschitz constant are a popular subject of study [15], and also universal variants receive attention [12]. Quadratic regularization or Levenberg-Marquardt regularization [23, 28] has also been studied for its conceptual simplicity and computational efficiency [24, 36]. Many recent works analyze its iteration complexity, both in the convex [29, 13] and nonconvex setting [16, 40, 18, 44, 43].

2.1 Fast local convergence 

Local convergence of Newton’s method is often analyzed under Lipschitz continuity of the Hessian ∇2f . We assume instead Lipschitz continuity of the preconditioned Hessian , as in [1, Assump. 3.3]. 

Assumption 2.2. The mapping H(x) := ∇2ϕ∗(∇f (x)) ∇2f (x) is LH -Lipschitz continuous, i.e., 

∃LH ≥ 0 : ∥H(x) − H(y)∥ ≤ LH ∥x − y∥, ∀x, y ∈ Rn.

Assumption 2.2 not only subsumes the usual assumption ( ϕ = 12 ∥ · ∥ 2), but also holds for costs with stronger growth. A proof for the following example is given in appendix A.1. 

Example 2.3 ([1, Example 3.4]) . Let f (x) : R → R be a 1D polynomial, and let ϕ(x) = cosh (|x|) − 1. Then there exists an LH > 0 such that Assumption 2.2 holds relative to ϕ with constant LH .

2.2 Globalization

We emphasize that at every iteration, all methods solve a linear system with the same Hessian but a different right-hand side, as noted in Section 2. This yields a similar computational cost per iteration. The preconditioned variants also diverge less, although, in our experience, this depends on the scaling of ¯L.

4.3 Matrix factorization 

Finally, we validate the preconditioned Newton methods on symmetric matrix factorization. Data matrices are synthetically generated with condition numbers ranging from 1 to 10 8 and the variables are matrices in R10 ×5. Figure 3 compares the globalized variant Algorithm 1 against vanilla Newton with Armijo linesearch. For each condition number we report the median number of matrix-vector products. Our implementation uses a standard conjugate 12 gradients (CG) method for solving (N) . The preconditioned systems (PN) involve asymmetric matrices, and are solved using a standard generalized minimal residual (GMRES) method. If the initial iterate is relatively small – in this experiment it is sampled elementwise from a standard normal Gaussian distribution – then all preconditioners perform quite similar. However, if the magnitude of the initial iterate is enlarged by a factor 100, we observe that the preconditioned variants significantly outperform vanilla Newton. It is precisely in this setting where an accurate global smoothness constant is valuable. Also observe that all methods scale similarly with the condition number of the problem. # 5 Conclusion 

In this work, we introduced and studied a novel class of Newton methods that is derived by applying a nonlinear preconditioner to the first-order optimality conditions. Our approach is based on generalized smoothness notions like anisotropic smoothness, which may better describe an objective in the presence of higher-order polynomial growth. We analyzed both the local and global convergence of our scheme under such relaxed smoothness conditions, and discussed practical choices for the preconditioners. We also incorporated a variant of cubic regularization into our approach, and established an O(ε−3/2) iteration complexity. Finally, we qualitatively validated our theoretical results on logistic regression and matrix factorization problems. Directions for future work include (i) extending of our framework to quadratic regularization or even trust-region variants; (ii) applying preconditioned Newton techniques to challenging problems like training Physics-Informed Neural Networks [37]; and (iii) designing novel classes of preconditioners with desirable properties. 13 References
