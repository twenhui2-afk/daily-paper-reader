A PARTIAL DICTIONARY BETWEEN UNIVERSAL CENTRAL EXTENSIONS AND ORTHOGONAL POLYNOMIALS IN THE SUPERELLIPTIC KRICHEVER–NOVIKOV SETTING 

FELIPE ALBINO DOS SANTOS 

Abstract. Let A = C[x±1, u | um = P (x)] be the coordinate ring of a superelliptic curve in the Krichever–Novikov two-point framework. We establish a systematic dictionary between the cocycle data of universal central extensions — both of Der (A) and of g ⊗ A — and families of orthogonal polynomials in the parameter a encoding the branch locus of P . The dictionary has three canonical entries: (1) the reduction relations in the center A/∂A of \Der( A) are exactly the three-term recurrence of the associated orthogonal polynomial family; (2) the generating function of the center satisfies the Sturm–Liouville ODE of that family; (3) the cross-cocycle ψ(e1, f s) equals the Legendre antiderivative ( Pn−1(a) − Pn+1 (a)) /(2 n + 1) in the quadratic case. We prove the dictionary completely for P (x) = x2 − 2ax + 1 (Legendre polynomials) and establish it for the quartic symmetric case P (x) = x4 − 2ax 2 + 1. In the quadratic case, palindromic symmetry forces the unique sector recurrence to be the Legendre three-term recurrence. In the quartic case, the odd-positive sector is governed by the Legendre family, while the even sector satisfies a two-component recurrence with palindromic (symmetric-in-degree) coefficients. We conjecture that this structural pattern — palindromic symmetry forcing symmetric recurrence coefficients — persists for all palindromic P of even degree. 1.1. The phenomenon. The universal central extensions (UCEs) of two infinite-dimensional Lie algebras arise naturally from a superelliptic curve um = P (x): the derivation algebra Der (A) and the current algebra g ⊗ A, where A = C[x±1, u | um = P (x)]. For the simplest case — the loop algebra 

g ⊗ C[x±1] corresponding to the genus-zero curve — the center of each UCE is one-dimensional and no recurrence arises. Once the curve has positive genus or the base ring is replaced by a superelliptic ring, the centers become multi-dimensional and the reduction relations in those centers carry rich structure. The question we address is: what mathematical objects do these recurrences describe? 1.2. Prior work. Universal central extensions of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras were studied systematically by Kassel and Loday [ 4]; the framework extends naturally to the Krichever–Novikov algebras introduced in [ 5] and developed by Schlichenmaier [ 9]. In the superelliptic setting, the algebras Der (A) and g ⊗ A and their UCEs were studied by Cox–Guo–Lu–Zhao [ 2] and Cox–Zhao [ 3 ], who established the dimension formulas above and computed explicit cocycle bases. The connection between the UCE center and orthogonal polynomial families was first observed by Cox, Futorny, and Tirao [ 1] for DJKM algebras, and then developed in the superelliptic current algebra setting in [7 ]; a sequel [ 8 ] extends these results to m ≥ 3, identifying the relevant families as ultraspherical polynomials. The derivation algebra side — the Der (A) half of the dictionary — had not been treated systematically before the present paper. 1.3. The answer. Canonical versus choice-dependent parts. The dictionary has a canonical part and a 

choice-dependent part. The canonical part consists of the data intrinsic to P (x) and m: the three-term recurrence (1) satisfied by the basis elements of A/∂A , the ODE governing the generating function 

F (z), and the functional form of the cross-cocycle formula ψ(e1, f s). These objects are determined entirely by P (x) and m, independently of how one labels or normalises the basis generators. The choice-dependent part is the concrete presentation in terms of explicit basis generators ω1, ω 2:identifying which generator is the positive-sector class and which is the negative-sector class, and fixing the overall normalisation of the cocycle. Concretely, the Legendre reduction [ xk] = Pk(a) ω2

requires fixing ω2 = [1]; the parallel formula [ x−k] = Pk−1(a) ω1 requires ω1 = [ x−1]. Both choices are natural (they are the unique monic choices compatible with the Z/m Z-grading), and the symmetry 

x 7 → x−1 exchanges them without changing the underlying mathematics. 1.5. Why it is structural. The identification is not a computational accident. We believe the key structural point is: the map P (x) 7 → { A/∂A, recurrence } is naturally mediated by the geometry of the branch locus of the superelliptic curve . The branch locus of P (x) = x2 − 2ax + 1 consists of two points α, β (the roots), and the parameter a = ( α + β)/2 is the “position parameter” of the Legendre family. The derivation ∂ = u d/dx acts on A by differentiation, and the quotient A/∂A 

kills all exact forms; the surviving classes are constrained by the residue data at the branch points. In the quadratic case, this constraint is precisely captured by the Legendre recurrence. A general proof that the recurrence for arbitrary P is governed by the branch-locus geometry in this sense remains an open problem. 

Remark 1.1 (Heuristic motivation) . Informally, the Legendre weight w(x) = 1 on [ −1, 1] can be thought of as the flat measure on the branch interval between the two roots of P , up to scaling. This heuristic motivates the appearance of the Legendre family, but a precise identification of the weight measure with the branch-locus data would require further analysis beyond the scope of this paper. For palindromic P (i.e., P (x) = xnP (1 /x )), the symmetry forces the recurrence coefficients in 

A/∂A to be symmetric in the index. In the quadratic and quartic cases, we prove that this yields the Legendre family (and a two-component Legendre recurrence, respectively); we conjecture that the general palindromic case produces an orthogonal family with symmetric recurrence coefficients. For non-palindromic P , a non-classical family (of the type studied in [ 1 ]) arises. We prove these criteria for m = 2 in Sections 4 and 9. 1.6. Contributions of this paper. 3. The dictionary: statement and mechanism 

3.1. Main dictionary table. The core observation of this paper is that the algebraic data of the UCE of Der (A) and the analytic data of a classical orthogonal polynomial family are in precise correspondence. Concretely: the reduction relations in A/∂A — derived from the algebraic identity 

∂(xru) = 0 — are identical to the three-term recurrence of a classical orthogonal polynomial family, with matching initial conditions. By the uniqueness theorem for such recurrences (see [ 11 ]), the algebraic classes [ xk] are forced to equal Legendre polynomials. The dictionary extends this identification to the generating function, the ODE, and the cross-cocycle. It is not a categorical equivalence or a functor; it is the coincidence of a recurrence and initial conditions in two a priori unrelated settings, which forces the two sequences to agree. 3.2. The mechanism. The dictionary is not an analogy; it is a precise algebraic identity. We explain the mechanism in three steps. 

Step 1: The center as a polynomial space. By Theorem 2.5, dim A/∂A = 1 + n(m − 1). For m = 2, P (x) = x2 − 2ax + 1 (so n = 2): dim = 3. A basis is ω0 = [ x−1u], ω1 = [ x−1], ω2 = [1] (proved in Proposition 4.3). The classes [ xk] for k ∈ Z are all expressible in terms of these three generators. 

Step 2: The recurrence is the three-term recurrence. The identity ∂(xru) = 0 in A/∂A 

expands to the linear relation (Lemma 4.1): (r + 1)[ xr+1 ] − a(2 r + 1)[ xr] + r[xr−1] = 0 .

Setting pk := [ xk] and dividing by ω2 (so pk = ckω2), the scalars ck satisfy (r + 1) cr+1 − a(2 r + 1) cr + rc r−1 = 0 with c0 = 1, c1 = a. The unique sequence with these coefficients and initial conditions is the Legendre sequence: ck = Pk(a). DICTIONARY: UCE AND ORTHOGONAL POLYNOMIALS 7

Step 3: Uniqueness is classical. Remark 8.5 . The two-dimensional character of the even-sector recurrence is a direct consequence of the four branch points of P (x) = x4 − 2ax 2 + 1, or equivalently of the increase in genus of the associated curve. For the quadratic case (two branch points, genus 0), the even sector is one-dimensional (generated by ω2 = [1]). For the quartic case (four branch points, genus 1), the even sector requires two generators and the recurrence becomes a two-component system (19) . The dictionary is not breaking; it is becoming a block-Jacobi dictionary : the scalar three-term recurrence is replaced by a matrix recurrence of the same form, and the associated orthogonal polynomial theory is that of matrix-valued orthogonal polynomials (see, e.g., the block-Jacobi framework in the theory of matrix-valued OP). The general principle is that the number of generators in each parity sector of A/∂A equals the number of branch points of P in that parity class. 9.
