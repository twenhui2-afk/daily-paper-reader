COUNTING SOLUTIONS TO THE QUADRATIC DETERMINANT EQUATION 

JONATHAN CHAPMAN AND AKSHAT MUDGAL 

Abstract. Given h, N ∈ N satisfying 1 ⩽ h ⩽ N 2, we prove an asymptotic formula for the number of solutions to the equation x1x2 − x3x4 = h with x1, . . . , x 4 ∈ [−N, N ] ∩ Z. We use a combination of combinatorial and analytic arguments in physical space along with bounds for Kloosterman sums. Our main result concerns the case when h = N 2 + O(N ), wherein we obtain square-root cancellation error terms by bypassing Kloosterman sum bounds and exploiting an additional symmetry available in this setting via Ramanujan sums. This confirms a speculation of Dhanda–Haynes–Prasala in a very general form.

Outline. We use §2 to record various preliminary lemmas from elementary and analytic number theory that we will require throughout our paper. We dedicate §3 to analysing the expected main term contribution in additive-type equations, and we utilise §4 to do the same for difference-type solutions.
