1. Introduction 

Let G be a simple graph with m edges, and let ρ(G) denote the spectral radius of its adjacency matrix. A central problem in fixed-size spectral extremal graph theory asks for the largest possible value of ρ(G) under a forbidden-subgraph condition. In the language now standard in the subject, this is a Brualdi–Hoffman–Turan problem; see, for example, Liu and Wang [ 2 ] for a broad treatment and Rehman and Pirzada [4] for a short survey note. In this paper we study the graph H(4 , 3) obtained by identifying one vertex of a 4-cycle with one vertex of a triangle. The fixed-size H(4 , 3) -free problem fits into a family of recent results around the bowtie 

H(3 , 3) and its neighbors. Li, Lu, and Peng [ 1 ] determined the odd-size extremal graph for H(3 , 3) -free graphs. Pirzada and Rehman [ 3] proved the even-size extremal theorem for {H(3 , 3) , H (4 , 3) }-free graphs, with the extremal value given by the quartic 

pm(x) = x4 − mx 2 − (m − 2) x + m

2 − 1.

Most recently, Zheng and Zhang [ 6] showed that the same quartic still governs the even-size problem when one forbids only H(4 , 3) , but their argument requires the global threshold m ≥ 38 .Lowering that threshold matters for two reasons. First, in this circle of problems the extremal graph and extremal polynomial are often already known, so the natural next question is how far the structural proof can be pushed before new obstructions appear. Second, the even-size H(4 , 3) -free theorem of [ 6]sits between two sharper neighboring statements: the even {H(3 , 3) , H (4 , 3) }-free theorem of Pirzada and Rehman [ 3] begins at m ≥ 10 , while the odd-size H(4 , 3) -free story has recently been pushed further by Wani, Pirzada, and Rehman [ 5]. Thus the numerical gap between 10 and 38 is mathematically meaningful rather than cosmetic. Our contribution is a refinement of a single branch of the Zheng–Zhang proof. We keep their notation and their overall branch decomposition, but we sharpen the only configuration that genuinely forces the threshold 38 .

10. Computational evidence for the sharp threshold 

The proof above yields the unconditional threshold m ≥ 24 . We now record the finite residue that remains if one tries to push the threshold to 18 .By theorem 5.1, the theorem fails for m = 10 , 12 , 14 , 16 , so no threshold below 18 is possible. On the other hand, after the hand reduction in Sections 3–8, the only unresolved cases for m = 18 , 20 , 22 lie inside the K4-branch with e(W ) ∈ { 2, 3}.The residual search space is finite because: 

• every vertex of W has at most one neighbor in A+ and at most one in A0;

• no vertex of W can meet both A+ and A0;

• the vertices of W meeting A+ form an independent set by theorem 4.2;

Further directions. Conjecture 10.1. The sharp threshold in theorem 1.1 is 18 .

By theorem 5.1, no theorem of the same form can hold below 18 . The remaining gap between the unconditional threshold 24 and the obstruction threshold 18 is therefore the only range in which the exact problem remains open. The main obstacle is the e(W ) = 2 branch in the K4-case. Accordingly, the most immediate problem is to determine whether the estimates in section 7 can be sharpened sufficiently to eliminate the residual cases m = 18 , 20 , 22 without recourse to computation. More broadly, it would be interesting to understand whether the branch-reduction method used here can be adapted to other fixed-size spectral extremal problems in which the extremal graph and extremal polynomial are already known, but the proved threshold is not sharp. The present H(4 , 3) -free problem suggests that, once the correct comparison graph has been identified, the main difficulty is often to isolate and analyze the unique threshold-bearing configuration. Finally, it would be worthwhile to turn the finite residual search for m = 18 , 20 , 22 into a fully rigorous computer-assisted argument. Such a result would settle the exact threshold in the present problem and may also provide a useful template for related Brualdi–Hoffman–Turan problems of fixed size. 10 SHREYHAAN SARKAR

Appendix B. Supplementary computational note
