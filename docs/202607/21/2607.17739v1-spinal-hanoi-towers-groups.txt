SPINAL HANOI TOWERS GROUPS 

FRANCESCA CAVALIERI, MIKEL E. GARCIARENA, AND MARIALAURA NOCE 

Abstract. We introduce and study spinal Hanoi towers groups , a family of groups acting on the d-adic tree that contains both the classical Hanoi towers group H(3) and Skipper’s generalizations as extreme cases. Each group is generated by d automorphisms a1, . . . , a d,where ai has a unique non-trivial section, equal to ai itself, at the i-th coordinate, and root permutation σi fixing i. The entire construction is thus encoded by the finite permutation group P = ⟨σ1, . . . , σ d⟩ ≤ Sym( d), and we develop a dictionary between the two: G is fractal and level transitive if and only if P is transitive; every group in the family is amenable and contracting, with explicit nucleus and a word problem solved by a length-halving recursion on syllables; and the abelianizations of G and P together control the first level stabilizer. The branch structure of the family is governed by the subgroup J ≤ Aut( Td), generated by the automorphisms with exactly two non-trivial sections, occupied by an element h ∈ G

and its inverse. We give a criterion for the containment J ≤ G that can be verified in an explicit finite quotient, and we prove that whenever it holds, a level transitive spinal Hanoi towers group is strongly fractal, regular branch over its commutator subgroup, has explicitly described rigid stabilizers at every level, and is just infinite. As an application, we show that the groups of type ( d, m ), whose root permutations are m-cycles, satisfy J ≤ G for all d ≥ 4and are therefore just infinite, in contrast with the classical case of the Hanoi towers group on three pegs. 

1. Introduction 

Groups acting on regular rooted trees provide a rich source of examples and counterexamples in infinite group theory. Since the pioneering work of Grigorchuk [10], who constructed the first finitely generated group of intermediate word growth, self-similar groups have played a central role in geometric and combinatorial group theory, as well as in dynamics, by virtue of their connections to iterated monodromy groups and limit dynamical systems [3, 17]. Important classes of such groups include branch groups (see the survey by Bartholdi, Grigorchuk and ˇSuni´ c [1] and Grigorchuk’s foundational paper [11]), contracting groups, and groups defined by finite-state automata; we refer to the monograph of Nekrashevych [17] for a comprehensive treatment. Among the historically most prominent examples of self-similar groups, beyond the Grig-orchuk group itself [9], are the Gupta-Sidki p-groups [15], providing the first finitely generated infinite p-groups for odd primes, and the Basilica group of Grigorchuk and ˙Zuk [14], whose amenability was established by Bartholdi and Vir´ ag [4]. Closer to the present work is the 

Hanoi towers group H(3) , introduced by Grigorchuk and ˇSuni´ c in [13, 12]. This group acts on the ternary tree and is connected to the classical puzzle on three pegs: its Schreier graphs at level n coincide with the Hanoi graphs on n disks. The group H(3) is self-similar, contracting, regular branch over its commutator subgroup, and amenable. Several generalizations of H(3) have been considered in the literature. Skipper [18] extended the construction to d-ary trees by taking generators whose root permutations are ( d−1)-cycles,

Let X be a finite alphabet of cardinality d ≥ 2. The d-regular rooted tree Td is the tree whose vertex set is the set X∗ of all finite words over X (including the empty word ∅, which is the root), and where a vertex v is connected by an edge to vx for each x ∈ X. The n-th level

3. Definition of the group and first properties 

In this section we define the class of spinal Hanoi towers groups and establish some of its main properties. We recall that the Hanoi towers group H(3) , introduced by Grigorchuk and ˇSuni´ c [12], acts on the ternary tree T3 and is generated by three automorphisms a1, a 2, a 3 where 

ψ(a1) = ( a1, 1, 1)(2 3) ,ψ(a2) = (1 , a 2, 1)(1 3) ,ψ(a3) = (1 , 1, a 3)(1 2) .

The name derives from its connection to the classical Tower of Hanoi puzzle on three pegs: the Schreier graph of the action of H(3) on Xn is isomorphic to the state graph of the Hanoi puzzle with n disks. The group H(3) is self-similar, contracting, and regular branch over its commutator subgroup. A first generalization to d-adic trees, for d ≥ 3, is due to Skipper [18], who introduced the group H(d) = ⟨a1, . . . , a d⟩ ≤ Aut( Td), where each ai has a single non-trivial section, equal to 

ai itself, placed at the i-th coordinate, and root permutation 

σi = ( i + 1 i + 2 · · · i + d − 1) ∈ Sym( d),

i.e. the ( d − 1)-cycle on {1, . . . , d } \ { i} (indices mod d). She studied the branch structure, the congruence subgroup property and the Hausdorff dimension of these groups; for d = 3 one recovers the classical Hanoi towers group H(3) . In [16], Makisumi, Stadnyk and Steinhurst in-troduced a different generalization in which a generator a may have more than one non-trivial section (each equal to a); see [16, Definition 4.1]. They studied the contracting property and obtained necessary and sufficient conditions for such groups to be contracting [16, Proposi-tion 4.4, Theorem 4.5]. Modifying instead the wreath recursion in a Basilica-like fashion leads to the family of p-Basilica groups of [5]. In this paper, we let Td be the d-ary tree. For i ∈ { 1, . . . , d }, consider the elements 

ψ(ai) = (1 , i−1. . ., 1, a i, 1, . . . , 1) σi,

of the group Aut( Td), where σi is an element of the symmetric group Sym( d) that fixes i. We refer to any group generated by elements a1, . . . , a d as a spinal Hanoi towers group. We assume throughout that σi̸ = 1 for every i, in order to exclude the degenerate case ai = 1. Of course, the group H(3) and, more generally, the groups H(d) of Skipper [18] are examples of spinal Hanoi towers groups.
