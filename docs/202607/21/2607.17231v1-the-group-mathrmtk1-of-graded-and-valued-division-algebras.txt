# The group TK 1 of graded and valued division algebras 

Huynh Viet Khanh 1, Nguyen Duc Anh Khoa 1, and Adrian R. Wadsworth 21Department of Mathematics and Informatics, Ho Chi Minh City University of Education, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam, khanhhv@hcmue.edu.vn ; anhkhoa27092002@gmail.com

Department of Mathematics, University of California San Diego, La Jolla, CA 92093-0112, USA, arwadsworth@ucsd.edu 

Abstract 

For a division algebra D, let K1(D) = D∗/[D∗, D ∗] and let TK 1(D) be the torsion subgroup of the abelian group K1(D). We study this torsion group for graded and valued division algebras, in parallel with the known theory of SK 1. For a graded division algebra E finite-dimensional over its center, we give exact sequences describing TK 1(E) in terms of E0, the grade group Γ E , and the conjugation action of E∗ on E0. These yield explicit formulas for TK 1(E) in the unramified, totally ramified, and semiramified cases. For a tame valued division algebra D over its Henselian-valued center K, we identify the obstruction group H to a congruence theorem for TK 1(D). We show that if the residue field K

of the valuation on K has characteristic p > 0, then H ∼= μK [p], the p-primary component of the group μK of roots of unity in K; but if char( K) = 0, then H = 1. We further prove a short exact sequence 1 −→ H −→ TK 1(D) −→ TK 1(gr (D)) −→ 1,

where gr (D) is the associated graded division algebra determined by the valuation on D obtained from the valuation on K.We also prove a stability theorem for a graded division algebra E with quotient division ring q(E), i.e., TK 1(E) ∼= TK 1(q(E)) ,

together with a new proof of the corresponding stability theorem for SK 1. As applications, we obtain graded analogues of Motiee’s primary decomposition and scalar-extension results for torsion Whitehead groups. 

# 1 Introduction 

Let D be a division algebra finite-dimensional over its center K. The reduced Whitehead group SK 1(D) = D(1) /D ′, where D(1) = {d ∈ D∗ | Nrd D(d) = 1 } and D′ = [ D∗, D ∗], has played an important role in the structure theory of division algebras and in applications to algebraic groups. A fundamental tool in its explicit computation in specific cases is the use of valuations. If K is Henselian, the valuation v on K extends uniquely to D, and the associated graded ring gr (D)determined by v is a graded division algebra. The passage from D to gr (D) has been shown to be particularly useful because many computations become simpler in the graded setting. The approach via associated graded rings was developed systematically by Hazrat and Wadsworth in their study in [HaW1] of SK 1 of graded and valued division algebras. They proved, among other things, graded analogues of Ershov’s exact sequences in [E] for SK 1(D) where D is a division 1arXiv:2607.17231v1 [math.RA] 19 Jul 2026 algebra over a Henselian field, and deduced explicit formulas for SK 1 in the unramified, totally ramified, and semiramified cases. They further proved the isomorphism SK 1(D) ∼= SK 1(gr (D)) for tame division algebras D over Henselian fields, thereby obtaining formulas for SK 1(D) from those for SK 1(gr (D)). Additionally, they proved the stability isomorphism SK 1(E) ∼= SK 1(q(E)) for a graded division algebra E, where q(E) is the central quotient division ring of E. These results are also presented and further developed in [TW, Ch. 11]. The purpose of this paper is to study the corresponding questions for the torsion subgroup TK 1(D) = τ (K1(D)) = τ (D∗/D ′) of the Whitehead group K1(D) = D∗/D ′. The congruence theorem for SK 1 says that (1 + MD) ∩ D(1) ⊆ D′, where MD is the maximal ideal for the valuation on D, and it is a key tool for proving the isomorphism SK 1(D) ∼= SK 1(gr (D)). But, unlike SK 1(D), the group TK 1(D) is not controlled solely by reduced norm one elements, and there is no full analogue for TK 1(D) to the congruence theorem for SK 1(D). In [KK], Khanh and Khoa isolated the obstruction to a congruence theorem for TK 1. Namely, if D(μ) is the inverse image of TK 1(D) in D∗, the obstruction group is 

H = ((1 + MD) ∩ D(μ))((1 + MD) ∩ D′).

The congruence theorem for TK 1(D) holds precisely when H = 1. In Prop. 4.1 below we give a complete description of this obstruction group. Namely, 

H ∼= (1 + MK ) ∩ μK .

We deduce from Lemma 4.2 that if the residue field K has characteristic p > 0, then H ∼= μK [p], the p-primary component of the group of roots of unity in K, while if char( K) = 0, then H = 1. Remarkably, the obstruction group thus depends only on the roots of unity in the center K, not on the particular tame division algebra D over K. These results complete and slightly correct the computations for H given in [KK]. Having determined H, our next step is to relate TK 1(D) to TK 1(gr (D)). We prove in Th. 5.1 the short exact sequence 1 −→ H −→ TK 1(D) −→ TK 1(gr (D)) −→ 1. (1.1) This is the TK 1-analogue of [HaW1, Th. 4.8] saying that SK 1(D) ∼= SK 1(gr (D)). It shows that the only obstruction to having an isomorphism TK 1(D) ∼= TK 1(gr (D)) is given by H.Before considering the obstruction group and applications to TK 1(D) we first establish for a graded division ring E analogous formulas for TK 1(E) to formulas given in [HaW1] for SK 1(E). In Th. 3.2 we give a general diagram relating TK 1(E) to data from E0 and Γ E and the conjugation action of E∗ on E0; this diagram is analogous to the one in [HaW1, Th. 3.4]. In Cor. 3.4 we further compress some of the data in this big diagram into a single short exact sequence. We analyze the diagram and its compressed version to obtain in Th. 3.6 formulas for TK 1(E) if E is unramified, totally ramified, or semiramified. After (1.1) is proved, we deduce in Cor. 5.3 formulas for TK 1(D)where D is a tame division algebra over its Henselian center from the corresponding formulas for TK 1(gr (D)). We also prove stability for TK 1 and a new proof of stability for SK 1. For a graded division algebra E with quotient division algebra Q = q(E), we first prove in Prop. 6.2 that the cokernel of the natural map K1(E) → K1(Q), namely Q∗/E ∗Q′, is torsion-free. From this we deduce in Th. 6.4 the stability theorems TK 1(E) ∼= TK 1(Q) and SK 1(E) ∼= SK 1(Q).

2Finally, we record some consequences of the stability theorem for TK 1. By applying results of Motiee in [M] on TK 1 of any finite-dimensional division algebra and invoking the stability theorem, we obtain corresponding results for TK 1 of a graded division algebra. This includes in Th. 7.1 a graded primary decomposition theorem and in Prop. 7.3 an injectivity result under finite graded field extensions of degree relatively prime to the index. The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we fix notation and conventions. In Section 3 we prove exact sequences for TK 1 of a graded division algebra E and derive from them explicit formulas for TK 1(E) in the unramified, totally ramified, and semiramified cases. In Section 4 we determine the obstruction group H. In Section 5 we prove the exact sequence (1.1) and use it to deduce formulas for TK 1(D) from those for TK 1(gr (D)). In Section 6 we prove the stability theorem for SK 1 and TK 1. In Section 7 we give applications to primary decompositions, finite graded field extensions, and tensor products.
