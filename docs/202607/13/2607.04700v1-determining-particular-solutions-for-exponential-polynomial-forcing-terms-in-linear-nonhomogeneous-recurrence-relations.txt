# 1 Introduction 

A recurrence relation defines each term of a sequence in terms of preceding terms. Such equations play a central role in algorithm analysis, combinatorics, and probability. In particular, linear recurrences with constant coefficients have a structure analogous to linear ODEs with constant coefficients, and this corre-spondence provides important mathematical insight [GKP94 , Elaydi05 ]. 

Consider the kth-order linear nonhomogeneous recurrence relation 

an + c1an−1 + c2an−2 + · · · + ckan−k = f (n), n ≥ k (1)

with ck̸ = 0 . Its general solution has the form 

an = xn + qn,

# 2 Preliminaries 

This section summarizes the basic concepts and results needed later. 2Definition 2.1. A recurrence relation of the form 

an + c1an−1 + c2an−2 + · · · + ckan−k = f (n), n ≥ k, 

is called a kth-order linear nonhomogeneous recurrence relation with constant coefficients . Here c1, . . . , c k ∈ C and ck̸ = 0 . If f (n) ≡ 0, it is called a linear homogeneous recurrence relation .Its characteristic polynomial is 

c(t) = tk + c1tk−1 + c2tk−2 + · · · + ck,

and the roots of c(t) = 0 are called characteristic roots .

Define the shift operator E by E[an] = an+1 . Then the recurrence can be written in operator form as 

c(E)[ an−k] = Ek[an−k] + c1Ek−1[an−k] + · · · + ck · 1[ an−k] = f (n).

The forward difference operator ∆ = E − 1 satisfies 

∆[ an] = an+1 − an,

and for any polynomial p(n) of degree at most m, we have ∆m+1 [p(n)] = 0 .
