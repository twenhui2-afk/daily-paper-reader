# Classification of Finite Subquandles in Spherical Quandles 

# Kentaro Yonemura ∗ Naoki Nakamura †

Abstract 

Every finite subquandle of a spherical quandle is accounted for by dihedral quandles and by quandles defined on root systems. 

MSC2020: Primary 20N02; Secondary 57K12, 53C35. 

Keywords: spherical quandle, finite subquandle, root system, dihedral quandle 

## 1 Introduction 

Quandles are algebraic structures introduced independently by Joyce [5] and Matveev [7], and are known to provide powerful invariants in knot theory. Since their introduction, quandles have been studied extensively not only in knot theory but also in topology, algebra, and geometry. For general background on quandles and related topics, see, for example, [6, 8]. Joyce [5] proposed the viewpoint that quandles may be regarded as algebraic analogues of symmetric spaces. Indeed, every Riemannian symmetric space carries a natural quandle structure defined by its point symmetries. From this perspective, there have also been attempts to study quandles as generalizations or discretizations of symmetric spaces. A notable example arising from this perspective is the study of spherical quandles. Azcan–Fenn [1] considered the quandle structure on the n-dimensional unit sphere Sn induced by its structure as a Riemannian symmetric space. More-over, they studied this quandle together with its natural extensions, which they called spherical quandles. Subsequently, ¨Ozdemir–Azcan [9] investigated finite subquandles of spherical quandles and provided a complete classification for S1

and S2. In particular, their classification for S2 essentially relies on the clas-sification of finite subgroups of the real orthogonal group O(3). Although this approach is effective in low dimensions, its complexity increases rapidly when

Theorem 1.1. Let X be a finite subquandle of the spherical quandle Sn 

> R

. If #X is even, then X is a root system in Rn+1 in the sense of Definition 4.1; if #X is odd, then X is isomorphic to the dihedral quandle R#X .This theorem completely classifies the finite subquandles of spherical quan-dles: those of even cardinality are precisely finite root systems consisting of unit vectors, whereas those of odd cardinality are precisely dihedral quandles. In particular, finite subquandles of spherical quandles exhibit a striking rigid-ity: their structure is completely determined by the parity of their cardinality. Note that every finite root system consisting of unit vectors gives rise to a finite subquandle of a spherical quandle. Therefore, every isomorphism class of finite root systems arises in this way. To the best of our knowledge, Theorem 1.1 gives the first concise quandle-theoretic characterization of finite root systems. Our proof does not rely on the classification of finite subgroups of O(n + 1), and thus provides a uniform treatment in all dimensions. The proof is based on the obser-vation that the presence of one antipodal pair forces antipodal closure, whereas the absence of antipodal pairs forces the subquandle to lie in a 2-dimensional subspace, reducing the problem to the classification of finite subquandles of the spherical quandle on S1.The problem studied in [9] and in this paper is a special case of the following, more general question. 

Question 1.2. Let X be a compact Riemannian symmetric space equipped with the natural quandle structure defined by its point symmetries. Classify the finite subquandles of X.Although Question 1.2 remains open in general, Theorem 1.1 provides the first answer in the case of spheres. In the case of spherical quandles, the be-havior of antipodal points plays an essential role. This suggests that the notion of antipodal sets, introduced by Chen–Nagano [2], may be useful in studying Question 1.2. More generally, one can formulate an analogous question for ho-mogeneous quandles. For background on homogeneous quandles, see [4]. We conclude this introduction by describing the organization of the paper. In Section 2, we recall the basic notions of quandle theory needed in this paper. In Section 3, we review the properties of the spherical quandle on S1 relevant to this paper and present ¨Ozdemir–Azcan’s classification of its finite subquandles. In Section 4, we introduce and study a quandle structure on root systems. Finally, in Section 5, we prove Theorem 1.1. 

## 2 Preliminaries 

In this section, we review the terminology on quandles used in this paper. See [6, 8] for more details. , 3

A quandle is a pair ( X, ⊲ ) consisting of a nonempty set X and a binary operation ⊲ : X × X → X satisfying the following three conditions: Q1 For every x ∈ X, one has x ⊲ x = x.Q2 For each y ∈ X, the map sy : X → X defined by x 7 → x ⊲ y is a bijection. Q3 For all x, y, z ∈ X, one has ( x ⊲ y ) ⊲ z = ( x ⊲ z ) ⊲ (y ⊲ z ). The conditions Q1, Q2, and Q3 are consistent with Reidemeister moves, oper-ations for knot diagrams in knot theory, I, II and III respectively. See Figure 1. 

Fig. 1: Geometric interpretation of Q1, Q2 and Q3. We now introduce the quandles considered in this paper. 

Example 2.1 (Trivial quandle) . For any nonempty set X, define a binary operation ⊲ : X × X → X by 

x ⊲ y := x (x, y ∈ X).

Then ( X, ⊲ ) is a quandle. This is called the trivial quandle .

Example 2.2 (Dihedral quandle [10]) . Let M = Z/n Z be the cyclic group of order n. Define a binary operation ⊲ : M × M → M by 

x ⊲ y := 2 y − x (x, y ∈ M ).

Then ( M, ⊲ ) is a quandle. This is called the dihedral quandle and is denoted by 

Rn., 4

Example 2.3 (Spherical quandle [1]) . Consider the unit sphere 

Sn = {(x1, . . . , x n+1 ) ∈ Rn+1 ∣∣ x21 + · · · + x2 

> n+1

= 1 } .

Let 〈− , −〉 denote the Euclidean inner product on Rn+1 . Define a binary oper-ation ⊲ : Sn × Sn → Sn by 

x ⊲ y := 2 〈x, y 〉y − x (x, y ∈ Sn).

Then ( Sn, ⊲ ) is a quandle. This is called the spherical quandle and is denoted by Sn 

> R

.The spherical quandle is an example of the quandle structure on a Rieman-nian symmetric space defined by point symmetries, as observed by Joyce. In this paper, we shall occasionally use without further comment both the fact that Sn is a Riemannian symmetric space and the fact that Sn is naturally embedded in the Euclidean space Rn+1 .Let X and Y be quandles. A map f : X → Y is called a quandle homomor-phism if f (x ⊲ y ) = f (x) ⊲ f (y) for x, y ∈ X. A bijective quandle homomorphism is called a quandle isomorphism . By definition, every quandle gives rise to dis-tinguished quandle automorphisms: for each y ∈ X, one has a map sy : X → X.Condition (Q3) implies that sy is a quandle homomorphism, and by (Q2), it is bijective. Hence sy is a quandle automorphism. This automorphism sy is called the inner automorphism associated with y. We write Inn( X) for the subgroup of the automorphism group of X generated by these inner automorphisms, and call it the inner automorphism group of X. A subquandle of a quandle ( X, ⊲ ) is a subset of X on which the operation ⊲ induces a quandle structure. In this paper, we write # S for the cardinality of a set S.

## 4 Root systems as quandles 

In this section, after recalling the definition of a root system, we show that any root system consisting of unit vectors forms a subquandle of a spherical quandle. For root systems, we refer the reader to [3]. Let n be a positive integer, and consider the Euclidean space Rn. Let 〈− , −〉 :

Rn × Rn → R denote the Euclidean inner product. For α ∈ Rn \ { 0}, define the hyperplane orthogonal to α by Hα = {v ∈ Rn | 〈 v, α 〉 = 0 } . Then the reflection with reflecting hyperplane Hα is defined to be the linear transformation σα :

Rn → Rn given by 

σα(v) := v − 2〈v, α 〉

〈α, α 〉 α (v ∈ Rn).

This reflection is an involution and preserves the Euclidean inner product. 

Definition 4.1. A finite subset X ⊂ Rn \{ 0} is called a root system if it satisfies the following conditions: 1. For every α ∈ X, one has Rα ∩ X = {± α}.2. For every α ∈ X, one has σα(X) = X.

Proposition 4.2 (Root systems as subquandles of the spherical quandle) . Let 

X be a root system in the Euclidean space Rn+1 , and assume that X is a subset of the unit sphere Sn. Then X is a finite subquandle of the spherical quandle 

Sn 

> R

.

Proof. For each α ∈ X, note that the inner automorphism sα of the spherical quandle can be written as −σα in terms of the corresponding reflection. The assertion then follows immediately from the definition of a root system and the basic properties of reflections. 

## 5 Classification of finite subquandles of spherical quandles 

In the preceding sections, we have considered the dihedral quandle Rn and the quandles arising from root systems as examples of finite subquandles of spherical quandles. In this section, we show that, up to isomorphism, these exhaust all finite subquandles of spherical quandles. This proves Theorem 1.1.
