# A note on probabilistic powerdomains, RB-domains, and bc-domains 

Yuxu Chen a

> aSchool of Mathematics, Sichuan University

Abstract 

For a finite nonempty poset F , the normalized probabilistic powerdomain V1(F ) is an RB-domain exactly when F is a finite rooted tree. We extend this classification to arbitrary nonempty dcpos from the viewpoint of forbidden structure. The principal-ideal chain condition is expressed by the absence of a lower fork, i.e. a triple (x, y, t ) with x ≤ t, y ≤ t, and x ∥ y. A useful point is that any dcpo P without lower forks is continuous. For normalized valuations the least element remains necessary, and we prove 

V1(P ) is RB ⇐⇒ V 1(P ) is a pointed bc-domain ⇐⇒ P has a least element and contains no lower fork .

For subprobability and extended valuations, the analogous classifications hold without the pointedness assumption on P .

Keywords: probabilistic powerdomain, RB-domain, bc-domain, Scott-continuous retract, lower fork

1. Introduction 

The probabilistic powerdomain is a central domain-theoretic construction for modelling probabilistic computation, going back to Jones and Plotkin [ 7]. A long-standing structural question in domain theory asks whether probabilistic powerdomains preserve finite-approximation properties of domains, in particular whether they preserve the class of RB-domains. For finite posets this question was recently settled in [2]. For every finite nonempty poset F ,

V1(F ) is an RB-domain ⇐⇒ F is a rooted tree .

Equivalently, F has a least element and every principal ideal is a chain. The proof is finite-dimensional: it uses minimal elements, Hasse graphs, and local stochastic cones. These tools do not directly apply to arbitrary dcpos, which need not have useful Hasse graphs, finite sets of minimal points, or finite-dimensional local order cones. In the present paper, the only result imported from the finite classification is the four-point diamond obstruction stated in Proposition 2.9; all general-dcpo arguments below are independent of the finite-dimensional proof. This paper takes the finite classification as the starting point and proves the corresponding result for arbitrary nonempty dcpos. We call a triple (x, y, t ) a lower fork if 

x ≤ t, y ≤ t, x ∥ y, 

where x ∥ y means x ≰ y and y ≰ x. Thus a poset has no lower fork exactly when every principal ideal ↓ t is a chain. The main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1. Let P be a nonempty dcpo. Then: 

V1(P ) is RB ⇐⇒ V 1(P ) is a pointed bc-domain ⇐⇒ P has a least element and no lower fork ,

V≤1(P ) is RB ⇐⇒ V ≤1(P ) is a pointed bc-domain ⇐⇒ P has no lower fork ,

V(P ) is RB ⇐⇒ V (P ) is a pointed bc-domain ⇐⇒ P has no lower fork .

The proof keeps the same main line as the finite classification, while using the finite paper only through the diamond obstruction stated in Proposition 2.9. First, we prove the obstruction direction: if the relevant powerdomain is RB, then the underlying dcpo must be a tree order, and in the normalized case it must also have a least element. A missing root gives no finite-image deflation on V1(P ). A lower fork gives a finite forbidden retract: a diamond for V1, and a three-point fork for V≤1 and V.Second, we prove the positive direction in the stronger form of bounded completeness. If P has no lower fork, then P is automatically continuous, and bounded families of valuations have explicit least upper bounds, computed from their masses on the basic Scott-open sets ⇑b. Hence the relevant probabilistic powerdomains are pointed bc-domains. By Lemma 2.2, every pointed bc-domain is an RB-domain, so the positive RB direction follows from this stronger statement. 

2. Preliminaries and finite input 

2.1. Continuous dcpos, lower forks, and finite shapes 

We use the standard domain-theoretic terminology of dcpos, continuous dcpos, Scott-continuous maps, and Scott-open sets; see [ 1 , 4]. A directed set is assumed to be nonempty. A dcpo is a poset in which every directed subset has a supremum. A map between dcpos is Scott-continuous if it is monotone and preserves directed suprema. Equivalently, inverse images of Scott-open sets are Scott-open. We write σ(P ) for the Scott topology of P .For elements u, x of a dcpo, u ≪ x means that u is way below x: whenever D is directed and x ≤ sup D,some d ∈ D satisfies u ≤ d. A dcpo P is continuous if, for every x ∈ P , the set {u : u ≪ x} is directed and has supremum x. In a continuous dcpo, the sets 

⇑u = {x ∈ P : u ≪ x}

form a basis of Scott-open sets. More precisely, if U ∈ σ(P ) and x ∈ U , then there exists u ∈ U such that 

u ≪ x, and then x ∈ ⇑ u ⊆ U .A dcpo is pointed if it has a least element, denoted ⊥. For elements x, y ∈ P , we write x ∥ y if x ≰ y and 

y ≰ x. A lower fork in a poset P is a triple (x, y, t ) such that x ≤ t, y ≤ t, and x ∥ y. Equivalently, P has a lower fork exactly when some principal ideal of the form ↓ t is not a chain. A poset with no lower fork will also be called a tree order .

Lemma 2.7. For every dcpo P and every z ∈ P , the set 

P \ ↓ z = {s ∈ P : s ≰ z}

3. Normalized probabilistic powerdomain 

We first prove the classification for V1(P ). This case has two obstructions: a missing root, and a lower fork in the presence of a least element. The latter is detected by the diamond retract.

6. Combined statement 

The three case-by-case classifications give the theorem announced in the introduction. In particular, for these probabilistic powerdomains over nonempty dcpos, the RB property and the pointed bc-domain property coincide. 14 Theorem 6.1. Let P be a nonempty dcpo. Then: 

V1(P ) is RB ⇐⇒ V 1(P ) is a pointed bc-domain ⇐⇒ P has a least element and no lower fork ,

V≤1(P ) is RB ⇐⇒ V ≤1(P ) is a pointed bc-domain ⇐⇒ P has no lower fork ,

V(P ) is RB ⇐⇒ V (P ) is a pointed bc-domain ⇐⇒ P has no lower fork .

Equivalently, “no lower fork” means that every principal ideal of the form ↓ t is a chain. Proof. This is exactly Theorems 3.12, 4.3, and 5.3, together with the equivalence between “no lower fork” and “every principal ideal is a chain”. 

Remark 6.2. The finite-poset paper [ 2 ] solves the finite normalized problem and supplies the diamond obstruction V1(♢). Its proof is finite-dimensional, whereas the present paper proves the dcpo analogue. In the general dcpo setting, the obstruction half is still finite except for the normalized missing-root phenomenon: lower forks in pointed dcpos produce diamond retracts for V1, while arbitrary lower forks produce valuation-level fork retracts for V≤1 and V. The converse direction is stronger than finite approximation: the absence of lower forks gives explicit least upper bounds for bounded families of valuations, hence pointed bc-domains. The RB property then follows from Lemma 2.2. 

Remark 6.3 (Examples) . Every chain, and more generally every dcpo whose principal ideals are chains, satisfies the no-lower-fork condition and is therefore continuous by Lemma 2.1. Hence its subprobability and extended valuation dcpos are pointed bc-domains, and its normalized valuation dcpo is a pointed bc-domain exactly when the underlying dcpo has a least element. A two-point antichain has no lower fork but no least element, so V≤1 and V are RB whereas V1 is not. The finite fork Λ and the diamond ♢ exhibit the lower-fork obstruction: V≤1(Λ) , V(Λ) , and V1(♢) are not RB-domains.
