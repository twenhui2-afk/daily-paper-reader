Abstract 

Classical shuffle products on permutations and binary planar rooted trees (i.e., on the vertices of permutohedra and associahedra) admit descriptions in terms of intervals in the weak Bruhat order and the Tamari order, respectively. Palacios and Ronco extended these products as well as their interval description to surjections and planar rooted trees (i.e. on all faces of permutohedra and associahedra). In this article, we present a broad generalisation of this phenomenon. We show that the shuffle product on faces of certain families of nestohedra admits an interval description with respect to the generalised flip order , a partial order defined on the faces of nestohedra through elementary splitting and fusion operations on the tree-like combinatorial objects encoding them. The generalised flip order extends the flip order of Barnard and McConville from vertices to all faces of nesthedra. We further compare it with the facial weak order of Dermenjian-Hohlweg-Pilaud and the generalised Tamari order of Ronco, and we provide its characterisation in terms of (generalised) inversions. 

In this article we investigate a binary relation on the set of faces of a fixed nestohedron (a.k.a. hypergraph polytope) that was previously introduced by Curien and Laplante-Anfossi in [5]. Our first result is that this relation generates a partial order on the faces of a nestohedron. When restricted to faces of dimension 0 (i.e, to vertices), this order coincides with the flip order of Barnard and McConville [1]. The latter generalises both the classical Tamari order on planar binary trees and the weak Bruhat order on permutations. For this reason, we refer to the order studied here as the generalised flip order . We note that the flip order of [1] was defined for graph associahedra, but its definition and acyclicity proof extend straightforwardly to arbitrary nestohedra. The genuinely new feature of the present work is the extension of this order from vertices to 

all faces. 1arXiv:2607.20132v1 [math.CO] 22 Jul 2026 The generalised flip order is indeed also a generalisation (of a generalisation) of the above classical orders on planar binary trees and on permutations in another direction: not only to other polytopes, but also to all faces of these polytopes (rather than on their vertices only). Such an extension had been proposed already in [9] for permutohedra and in [11] for both associahedra and permutohedra, whose faces are described by planar trees and by surjections, respectively. Our order is defined for any hypergraph polytope and instantiates precisely to those early appearances (see Remark 2.14). Our motivation is partly algebraic. In earlier work [3], we introduced q-tridendriform structures on the faces of certain families of hypergraph polytopes. The operations defining these structures assemble into shuffle products that generalise the classical shuffle products on surjections and planar rooted trees. In the present paper we show that these shuffle products can be characterised as sums over suitable intervals in the generalised flip order. This generalises results [11] of Palacios and Ronco for permutohedra and associahedra. But we also believe that the generalised flip order stands out on its own and deserves further study. In particular, we managed, under a suitable right-filledness condition, to characterise it in terms of generalised inversions, extend-ing in a highly non-trivial way both the condition and the characterisation of the flip order given in [1]. In order to remain as self-contained as possible, we shall recall the neces-sary definitions on nestohedra, as well as the main definitions and results of [3] concerning the shuffle products on faces of nestohedra, in Sections 1 and 3, respectively. The other sections contain our new results. In Section 2, we recall the generalised flip relation from [5] (which was given no name there) and show that it generates a partial order (Proposition 2.12) whose restriction to the 0-dimensional faces coincides with the flip order of Barnard and McConville (Proposition 2.29). In §2.3, we also provide a characterisation of the generalised flip order in terms of generalised inversions. In Section 4, we state and prove our characterisation of the operations of Section 3 in terms of intervals. Section 5 is a discussion section. In particular, we offer some comparisons with other orders that have been introduced on all faces of nestohedra.

At first glance, Definition 2.1 appears perfectly symmetric: the covering re-lation is described either by a fusion or by a split and these two actions are dual to each other. However, once we consider fusion and split as (well-defined) operations to be performed on constructs (in order to produce other constructs), the asymmetry is revealed: there is no side condition associated with a fusion, while there is some precondition to be satisfied for performing a split. Indeed, any edge of a construct can be contracted (provided that the two adjacent ver-tices are appropriately comparable) and the contraction automatically yields another valid construct of the same hypergraph. In contrast, not every node can be arbitrarily split. If we want to split a node Z = U ∪ V , then, in addition 9to requiring that U < V , we must also make sure that V lies in a single con-nected component in the appropriate restricted hypergraph. This connectivity condition is necessary to ensure that the resulting tree is again a construct of the original hypergraph. We formalise it in the following definition.

## 2.3 Generalised flip order in terms of generalised inver-sions 

Our comparison of the vertex-restriction of the generalised flip order with the flip order of Barnard-McConville suggests that we should be able to provide a characterisation of the former in terms of (generalised) inversions. In this section, we give such a characterisation, by generalising the chain of arguments used in [1]. For a construct S : H, a, b ∈ H and the nodes A and B of S such that a ∈ A

and b ∈ B, write 

• b < S a iff the unique path from B to the root of S passes through A, and 

• a =S b iff A = B,and define Inv H(S) := {(a, b ) ∈ H × H | a < b and b < S a}

and fInv H(S) := {(a, b ) ∈ H × H | a < b and a =S b}.

We refer to the elements of Inv H(S) ∪ fInv H(S) as generalised inversions 3. In addition, the elements of Inv H(S) (resp. fInv H(S)) will be called good pairs (resp. 

neutral pairs ) of S. If ( a, b ) are vertices of H such that a < b and a < S b, we say that ( a, b ) is a bad pair of S. Finally, we say that a and b are incomparable in S if neither b ≤S a nor a < S b holds. 

Definition 2.31. An ordered hypergraph H is right-filled if, for any triple of vertices a, b, c ∈ H such that a < b < c and any hyperedge X ∈ H such that 

a, c ∈ X, there exists a hyperedge Y ∈ H such that {b, c } ⊆ Y ⊆ (X\{ a}) ∪ { b}.

Remark 2.32. As a side illustration of the previous definition, we note that if H is right-filled, and if E = {x1 < · · · < x n} is any hyperedge of H, then all sets Ei = {xi < · · · < x n}, for 1 < i < n , are connected in H: indeed, applying the definition to E and to xi, x i+1 , x n, we get hyperedges Yi such that 

{xi+1 , x n} ⊆ Yi ⊆ Ei+1 , from which the conclusion follows easily. So we have that HE as a polytope is at least as truncated as the hypercube C{xn<··· <x 1}.

# 5 Discussion 

We devote this section to positioning our generalised flip order with respect to other partial orders on the faces of certain polytopes proposed in the literature. Specifically, we compare the GFO with the weak order previously introduced by Palacios and Ronco in [11] on the faces of W -permutohedra, and later further studied by Dermenjian, Hohlweg and Pilaud in [7], as well as with the generalised Tamari order on the faces of graph-associahedra, due to Ronco [14]. We shall henceforth denote the generalised flip order explicitly by ≤GF O , in order to distinguish it from the other orders appearing in the discussion. 

## 5.1 Facial weak order 

The weak Bruhat order on permutations was first extended to all the faces of the permutohedra by Krob-Latapy-Novelli-Phan-Schwer [9]. This order was then extended by Palacios-Ronco [11] to all W -permutohedra (which are polytopes associated with finite Coxeter systems), and further studied in this generality by Dermenjian-Hohlweg-Pilaud [7], who called it facial weak order. We present here a version of the facial weak order that applies to all nestohedra, and investigate the link between this order and ours.

## 5.2 Generalised Tamari order 

In [14], Mar´ ıa Ronco introduces a relation on all faces of ordered graph asso-ciahedra, i.e., graph associahedra for which additionally a total order on the set of vertices is given, which she calls generalised Tamari order. She defines a product on some families of graph associahedra satisfying some conditions. We refer to [4] for a comparison of her conditions with the ones considered in our works. Her definition of product is given by means of restrictions of tubings, like we do in Proposition 3.10. She gives a characterisation of this product in terms of intervals like we do in Theorem 4.5. In fact, her work served as inspiration for ours. We note the following differences with respect to our work, though. 

• Our framework is not limited to graph associahedra and takes place in the more general setting of nestohedra. 

• Our characterisation via intervals extends to a tridendriform structure decomposing the product. In doing so (cf. (26)), we were inspired by the work of Palacios and Ronco in which the authors worked out the case of associahedra and permutohedra in detail [11]. 

• Our generalised flip order differs from her general Tamari order, as we show in Figure 7. The discrepancy already appears for linear graphs whose graph-theoretic ordering of vertices is not compatible with the ambient linear order. We expect that Ronco’s covering relations coincide with ours for associahedra equipped with their standard ordering, that is, when the chosen linear order agrees with the order induced by the underlying linear graph. But one cannot hope to induce a preferred total order on the vertices from a graph structure in general.
