# From roots to paths: graphs simultaneously irregular with respect to rooted and ordinary paths 

## Tatiana Dovzhenok ∗

Abstract 

Let 𝑃 𝑛 denote a path on 𝑛 vertices. A simple finite graph 𝐺 is called 𝑃 𝑛 -irregular if any two distinct vertices of 𝐺 belong to a different number of subgraphs of 𝐺 

isomorphic to 𝑃 𝑛 . Alternatively, for a fixed vertex 𝑟 of 𝑃 𝑛 (the root), 𝐺 is called 

(𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 -irregular if any two distinct vertices of 𝐺 act as the root 𝑟 in a different number of subgraphs of 𝐺 isomorphic to 𝑃 𝑛 . This paper proves that for each integer 𝑘 ≥ 4,there exists an infinite family of graphs that are simultaneously 𝑃 𝑛 -irregular and 

(𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 -irregular for every integer 𝑛 satisfying 4 ≤ 𝑛 ≤ 𝑘 and every root 𝑟 of 𝑃 𝑛 . For the path 𝑃 3, we observe that no nontrivial (𝑃 3)𝑟 -irregular graphs exist if 𝑟 is the central vertex. In contrast, if 𝑟 is an end-vertex of 𝑃 3, an infinite collection of graphs is constructed that are both 𝑃 3-irregular and (𝑃 3)𝑟 -irregular. In particular, these results confirm the Strong Conjecture about 𝐹 -irregular graphs for the case where 

𝐹 is a path 𝑃 𝑛 .

Keywords : path 𝑃 𝑛 , root 𝑟 , 𝑃 𝑛 -degree, (𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 -degree, 𝑃 𝑛 -irregular graph, (𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 -irregular graph, Strong Conjecture about 𝐹 -irregular graphs, Strong Conjecture about (𝐹 )𝑟 -irregular graphs.

# 1 Introduction 

Throughout this paper, we consider only finite, simple, and undirected graphs. For a graph 𝐺 , let 𝑉 (𝐺 ) and 𝐸 (𝐺 ) be its vertex and edge sets, respectively. The order of 

𝐺 , denoted by |𝐺 |, is the number of its vertices; we say that 𝐺 is nontrivial if |𝐺 | ≥ 2.The neighborhood of a vertex 𝑣 in 𝐺 , denoted by 𝑁 𝐺 (𝑣 ), is the set of vertices adjacent to 𝑣 , and its closed neighborhood is defined as 𝑁 𝐺 [𝑣 ] = 𝑁 𝐺 (𝑣 ) ∪ { 𝑣 }. The degree of 𝑣 is 

deg 𝐺 (𝑣 ) = |𝑁 𝐺 (𝑣 )|, while 𝛿 (𝐺 ) stands for the minimum degree of 𝐺 .We use standard notation for common graph families of order 𝑛 : 𝑃 𝑛 for the path, 𝐶 𝑛 

for the cycle, 𝐾 𝑛 for the complete graph, and 𝐾 1,𝑛 −1 for the star. Unless otherwise stated, all parameters in this work are assumed to be non-negative integers. To ensure precision, particularly when vertices are labeled numerically, we adopt a specific convention for representing paths.

1arXiv:2607.11700v1 [math.CO] 13 Jul 2026 Formally, for 𝑛 ≥ 1, a path 𝑃 𝑛 is a graph with vertex set {𝑣 1, 𝑣 2, . . . , 𝑣 𝑛 } such that two vertices 𝑣 𝑖 and 𝑣 𝑗 are adjacent if and only if |𝑖 − 𝑗 | = 1 . We denote the path by listing its vertices in the order of their occurrence: 

𝑃 𝑛 = ( 𝑣 1, 𝑣 2, . . . , 𝑣 𝑛 ).

This notation is naturally invariant with respect to the direction of traversal, meaning that 

(𝑣 1, 𝑣 2, . . . , 𝑣 𝑛 ) and (𝑣 𝑛 , 𝑣 𝑛 −1, . . . , 𝑣 1) represent the same object. By convention, we extend the definition to 𝑛 = 0 to account for the empty graph. Under this unified approach, an edge with end-vertices 𝑢 and 𝑣 is viewed as a path of order 2 and written as (𝑢, 𝑣 ). To prevent any ambiguity, ordered tuples are denoted by angle brackets, such as the ordered pair ⟨𝑎, 𝑏 ⟩ of elements 𝑎 and 𝑏 .The primary subject of our study is 𝐹 -irregularity. Introduced by Chartrand, Holbert, Oellermann, and Swart [1] as a generalization of the classical vertex degree, this concept was originally presented alongside an elegant yet deceptively simple conjecture. 

Definition 1. For any two graphs 𝐹 and 𝐺 , the 𝐹 -degree of a vertex 𝑣 in 𝐺 , denoted by 𝐹 - deg 𝐺 (𝑣 ), is the number of subgraphs of 𝐺 isomorphic to 𝐹 that contain 𝑣 . A graph 

𝐺 is called 𝐹 -irregular if all its vertices have pairwise distinct 𝐹 -degrees; that is, 

𝐹 - deg 𝐺 (𝑢 )̸ = 𝐹 - deg 𝐺 (𝑣 ) for all distinct 𝑢, 𝑣 ∈ 𝑉 (𝐺 ).

Conjecture 1 ( [1], 1987) . For every connected graph 𝐹 of order at least 3, there exists a nontrivial 𝐹 -irregular graph. 

Here, a fascinating paradox arises. While no nontrivial graph can be 𝐾 2-irregular (i.e., with pairwise distinct vertex degrees; see, e.g., [2]), this impossibility appears to be a consequence of the structural simplicity of 𝐾 2. Conjecture 1 suggests that once the graph 𝐹 attains sufficient complexity ( |𝐹 | ≥ 3), these classical constraints vanish, rendering 𝐹 -irregularity potentially universal. The validity of Conjecture 1 has been established for several classes of graphs, including stars, complete graphs [1], and paths [3]. For each 2-connected graph 𝐹 with minimum degree 𝛿 (𝐹 ) = 2 (in particular, for cycles), Dovzhenok, Filuta, and Chuhai [4] constructed infinite families of 𝐹 -irregular graphs and proposed a more ambitious conjecture. 

Conjecture 2 (Strong Conjecture about 𝐹 -irregular graphs, [4], 2024) . For each con-nected graph 𝐹 of order |𝐹 | ≥ 3, there exist infinitely many 𝐹 -irregular graphs. 

Conjecture 2 has been fully settled for graphs 𝐹 of diameter 2 by Dovzhenok [5]. It also holds for the paths 𝑃 3 and 𝑃 4 in view of [6] and [3], respectively. In this paper, we confirm Conjecture 2 for the entire family of paths 𝑃 𝑛 with 𝑛 ≥ 3.We also investigate the concept of rooted 𝐹 -degrees introduced in [7], focusing on the case where 𝐹 = 𝑃 𝑛 . In contrast to the classical 𝐹 -degree, which counts all copies of 𝐹 

containing a given vertex, the rooted variant counts only those where the vertex plays a designated role. To our knowledge, this work provides the first systematic study of graphs exhibiting rooted 𝐹 -irregularity. 

Definition 2. Let 𝐹 be a graph with a distinguished vertex 𝑟 ∈ 𝑉 (𝐹 ), referred to as the root. For any vertex 𝑣 ∈ 𝑉 (𝐺 ), its rooted 𝐹 -degree (also termed (𝐹 )𝑟 -degree ), denoted by (𝐹 )𝑟 - deg 𝐺 (𝑣 ), is the number of subgraphs of 𝐺 isomorphic to 𝐹 where 𝑣 corresponds to the root 𝑟 . More formally, this value equals the number of subgraphs 𝐻 ⊆ 𝐺 for which there exists an isomorphism 𝜑 : 𝐹 → 𝐻 satisfying 𝜑 (𝑟 ) = 𝑣 . A graph 𝐺 is (𝐹 )𝑟 -irregular 

if all its vertices have pairwise distinct (𝐹 )𝑟 -degrees; that is, 

(𝐹 )𝑟 - deg 𝐺 (𝑢 )̸ = ( 𝐹 )𝑟 - deg 𝐺 (𝑣 ) for all distinct 𝑢, 𝑣 ∈ 𝑉 (𝐺 ).

2Our central aim is the construction of graphs that are simultaneously 𝑃 𝑛 - and (𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 -irregular across a wide range of distinct pairs ⟨𝑛, 𝑟 ⟩. This phenomenon constitutes a 

super-concentration of irregularities , wherein a single structure satisfies a large system of independent asymmetry constraints. Our first main result establishes both the existence and the abundance of such highly non-uniform graphs. 

Theorem 1. For each integer 𝑘 ≥ 4, there exists an infinite family of graphs that are simultaneously 𝑃 𝑛 -irregular and (𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 -irregular for all integers 𝑛 ∈ { 4, 5, . . . , 𝑘 } and every root 𝑟 of 𝑃 𝑛 .

Regarding the path 𝑃 3, it is straightforward to observe that no nontrivial (𝑃 3)𝑟 -irregular graphs exist if 𝑟 is the central vertex. Conversely, when 𝑟 is an end-vertex, an analogue of Theorem 1 holds. 

Theorem 2. There exists an infinite family of graphs that are simultaneously 𝑃 3-irregular and (𝑃 3)𝑟 -irregular, where 𝑟 is an end-vertex of 𝑃 3.

For brevity, we identify the root 𝑟 = 𝑣 𝑖 of the path 𝑃 𝑛 = ( 𝑣 1, 𝑣 2, . . . , 𝑣 𝑛 ) directly with its index 𝑖 . By exploiting symmetry, we can, without loss of generality, assume that 

𝑟 ∈ { 1, 2, . . . , ⌈𝑛/ 2⌉} .The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces a methodology for comparing 

(𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 -degrees. Section 3 investigates 𝑃 3- and (𝑃 3)𝑟 -irregularity, providing the proof of Theorem 2. Section 4 addresses the case 𝑛 ≥ 4 and presents a constructive proof of Theorem 1. Finally, Section 5 summarizes our findings and proposes an analogue of the Strong Conjecture for (𝐹 )𝑟 -irregular graphs. 

# 2 Methodology for comparing vertex (𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 -degrees 

For a vertex 𝑢 of a graph 𝐺 and a root 𝑟 of the path 𝑃 𝑛 , let 𝒫 𝑛 (𝐺, 𝑢, 𝑟 ) denote the set of all subgraphs of 𝐺 isomorphic to 𝑃 𝑛 containing 𝑢 , in which 𝑢 corresponds to the root 𝑟 . By the definition of (𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 -degree, we have 

(𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 - deg 𝐺 (𝑢 ) = |𝒫 𝑛 (𝐺, 𝑢, 𝑟 )|.

To compare the (𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 -degrees of two distinct vertices 𝑢 1, 𝑢 2 ∈ 𝑉 (𝐺 ), we identify a subset of edges ℰ ⊂ 𝐸 (𝐺 ) such that 𝑢 1 and 𝑢 2 are symmetric in the subgraph 𝐺 1 = 𝐺 ∖ ℰ .This symmetry implies the existence of an automorphism of 𝐺 1 that maps 𝑢 1 to 𝑢 2. In our constructions, this condition is guaranteed by ensuring that 𝑢 1 and 𝑢 2 have either identical neighborhoods, 𝑁 𝐺 1 (𝑢 1) = 𝑁 𝐺 1 (𝑢 2), or identical closed neighborhoods, 𝑁 𝐺 1 [𝑢 1] = 𝑁 𝐺 1 [𝑢 2].Consequently, 

(𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 - deg 𝐺 1 (𝑢 1) = ( 𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 - deg 𝐺 1 (𝑢 2),

and the difference between the (𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 -degrees of 𝑢 1 and 𝑢 2 in 𝐺 satisfies 

(𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 - deg 𝐺 (𝑢 1) − (𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 - deg 𝐺 (𝑢 2) = |𝒜| − |ℬ| , (1) where the sets 𝒜 and ℬ are defined as 

𝒜 = {𝐹 ∈ 𝒫 𝑛 (𝐺, 𝑢 1, 𝑟 ) | 𝐸 (𝐹 ) ∩ ℰ̸ = ∅} ,

ℬ = {𝐻 ∈ 𝒫 𝑛 (𝐺, 𝑢 2, 𝑟 ) | 𝐸 (𝐻 ) ∩ ℰ̸ = ∅} .

Thus, comparing (𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 -degrees reduces to evaluating the relative cardinalities of 𝒜 

and ℬ. For small 𝑛 , this is achieved by direct calculation or simple estimates. For larger 

𝑛 , we employ a hybrid approach by partitioning 𝒜 and ℬ into subfamilies whose sizes are compared either through injective mappings or combinatorial estimates involving binomial coefficients. These combinatorial bounds are valid under certain constraints, primarily when |𝐺 | is sufficiently large. 33 The Case 𝑛 = 3 

We begin our study by considering the simplest case, 𝑛 = 3 . We examine two scenarios: 

𝑟 = 2 , where the root is the central vertex of 𝑃 3, and 𝑟 = 1 , where it is an end-vertex. 

## 3.1 (𝑃 3)2-irregular graphs 

Proposition 1. No nontrivial graph is (𝑃 3)2-irregular. Proof. Let 𝐺 be an arbitrary nontrivial graph and let 𝑣 ∈ 𝑉 (𝐺 ). Every pair of distinct vertices {𝑢 1, 𝑢 2} ⊆ 𝑁 𝐺 (𝑣 ) uniquely determines a subgraph isomorphic to 𝑃 3 centered at 𝑣 , namely the path (𝑢 1, 𝑣, 𝑢 2). Conversely, any such path centered at 𝑣 is uniquely determined by its end-vertices in 𝑁 𝐺 (𝑣 ). This bijective correspondence yields 

(𝑃 3)2- deg 𝐺 (𝑣 ) = (︂ deg 𝐺 (𝑣 )2)︂ . (2) Since 𝐺 contains at least two vertices of the same degree, by (2) their (𝑃 3)2-degrees must also be equal. Consequently, 𝐺 cannot be (𝑃 3)2-irregular. 

## 3.2 (𝑃 3)1-irregular graphs 

In contrast to the case 𝑟 = 2 , the (𝑃 3)1-degree of a vertex 𝑣 in a graph 𝐺 depends not only on deg 𝐺 (𝑣 ) but also on the degrees of its neighbors in 𝐺 . In this subsection, we con-struct an infinite family of graphs that are simultaneously 𝑃 3-irregular and (𝑃 3)1-irregular.

# 5 Conclusion 

This paper initiates the systematic study of (𝐹 )𝑟 -irregular graphs, a concept originally announced in the monograph [7]. At the heart of our work is the construction of graphs that unify multiple forms of irregularity. For any 𝑘 ≥ 4, we explicitly present infinite fam-ilies of graphs that are at once 𝑃 𝑛 -irregular and (𝑃 𝑛 )𝑟 -irregular for each 𝑛 ∈ { 4, 5, . . . , 𝑘 }

and all roots 𝑟 of the path 𝑃 𝑛 . While no nontrivial graph can be (𝑃 3)𝑟 -irregular when 𝑟 is the central vertex of 𝑃 3, we prove the existence of an infinite class of graphs that exhibit both 𝑃 3-irregularity and (𝑃 3)𝑟 -irregularity when 𝑟 is an end-vertex. The present results confirm Conjecture 2 for all paths 𝑃 𝑛 with 𝑛 ≥ 3 (see Corollary 1). By analogy with Proposition 1, one can establish a more general limitation on the pairs ⟨𝐹, 𝑟 ⟩ for which nontrivial (𝐹 )𝑟 -irregular graphs exist: 

Proposition 3. For any integer 𝑛 ≥ 3, if 𝑟 is the center of the star 𝐾 1,𝑛 −1, then no non-trivial graph is (𝐾 1,𝑛 −1)𝑟 -irregular. 

As this appears to be the sole exception, we state the following conjecture. 

Conjecture 3 (Strong Conjecture about (𝐹 )𝑟 -irregular graphs) . For every connected graph 𝐹 of order |𝐹 | ≥ 3 and each root 𝑟 of 𝐹 , unless 𝐹 = 𝐾 1,𝑛 −1 and 𝑟 is the center of 

𝐾 1,𝑛 −1, there exist infinitely many (𝐹 )𝑟 -irregular graphs.

Similarly, this result extends to directed cycles (investigated as “oriented cycles” in [8]). In conclusion, we raise the following open problem: 

Problem. Determine whether analogues of Theorems 1 and 2 hold for any directed path of order 𝑛 ≥ 3.
