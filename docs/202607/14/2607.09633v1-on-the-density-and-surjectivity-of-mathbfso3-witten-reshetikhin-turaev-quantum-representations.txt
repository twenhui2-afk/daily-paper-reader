RENAUD DETCHERRY, PIERRE GODFARD, AND RAMANUJAN SANTHAROUBANE 

Abstract. In this paper, we establish several new fundamental properties of SO(3) -quantum representations ρp,g,λ : PMod(Σ g,n ) −→ PSU dp,g,λ of mapping class groups of surfaces, at prime-order roots of unity. We show that for any surface Σg,n of genus 

g ≥ 3, any number n ≥ 0 of punctures, and any coloration λ of the punctures, ρp,g,λ 

has dense image in the projective unitary group PSU dp,g,λ , extending a landmark result of Larsen and Wang. Moreover, we show that the representations ρp,g,λ are surjective modulo any unramified maximal ideal of Z[ζp], establishing an effective version of strong approximation for these representations. We also give several applications of our main results to residual finite simpleness of PMod(Σ g,n ) (answering a question of Masbaum and Reid); to subnormal cores of some subgroups of PMod(Σ g,n ); to realizability of congruence classes of quantum invariants; to embedding obstructions between 3-manifolds; and to homological sta-bility for mapping class groups with coefficients in SO(3) -quantum representations. 

1. Introduction 

1.1. Mapping class groups of surfaces and their finite quotients. For g, n ≥

0, let us denote by Σng a compact oriented surface with genus g and n boundary components, and by Σg,n an oriented surface of genus g with n punctures. The mapping class group Mod(Σ ng ) is the quotient 

Diff +(Σ ng )/Diff 0(Σ ng ),

of the group of orientation-preserving self-diffeomorphisms by isotopies. We require diffeomorphisms and isotopies to restrict to the identity on ∂Σng . The pure mapping class group PMod(Σ g,n ) is the quotient 

Diff +(Σ g,n )/Diff 0(Σ g,n ),

where diffeomorphisms and isotopies must restrict to the identity on the set of marked points. The groups Mod(Σ ng ) are important in many areas of mathematics, among them low-dimensional topology, geometric group theory and algebraic geometry. Their structure has been studied extensively and in particular, a classical result of Grossman [Gro75] shows that the groups Mod(Σ ng ) are residually finite. Despite Grossman’s result, finite quotients of mapping class groups are not well understood. For instance, three of the problems in Ivanov’s famous problem list on mapping class groups of surfaces [Iva06] revolve around the structure of finite index subgroups of Mod(Σ ng ). Furthermore, very few finite simple groups are known to occur as finite quotients of mapping class groups. From the homology representations, the groups PSp 2g(Z/p Z) are finite quotients of Mod(Σ g) for any prime p. Moreover, results of Dunfield and Thurston [DT06, Theorem 1.3] imply that arbitrarily large alternating groups An occur as quotients of Mod(Σ g) for some g. Finally, in [MR12], Masbaum and Reid used SO(3) -Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev quantum representations at prime levels to show that for each g ≥ 2, the group Mod(Σ g) admits quotients 

PSL d(Fq) where d and q can be arbitrarily large. They deduced that every finite group is a quotient of a finite index subgroup of Mod(Σ g), answering a question of Hamenstädt [MR12, §1]. This result was later recovered by Grunewald, Larsen, Lubotzky and Malestein [GLLM15] using homological representations. The above-mentioned results do not, however, provide explicit finite simple quo-tients of the mapping class groups Mod(Σ g) other than the quotients of Sp 2g(Z). In this paper, we provide many examples of such quotients. Just as an illustration, an immediate corollary of our main results will be: 1

We note that Theorem 1.2 is logically independent of Larsen and Wang’s result and that the method of proof is fairly different. As in [LW05], we start out by proving that the representations ρp,g,λ are tensor-indecomposable and deduce that the closures of their images are simple Lie groups, but whereas Larsen and Wang prove tensor-indecomposability relying on some facts about the representation theory of the groups PSL 2(Z/p Z) and induction on the genus, we prove it without induction for all surfaces by fully exploiting the irreducibility of all the representations ρp,g,λ , the modular functor structure and a saturation property of Λ. Then, we show that the representations ρp,g,λ are weight-multiplicity-free in the sense of Howe [How95] and use a theorem of Howe classifying weight-multiplicity-free representations of simple Lie groups to significantly narrow down the possibilities for the closure of the image. Finally, exploiting again irreducibility of all the representations ρp,g,λ and the modular functor structure to exclude most remaining cases, we show that the ρp,g,λ have dense images. No analog of Theorem 1.3 exists in the literature. In fact, we prove a stronger version, for not necessarily maximal ideals in Theorem 6.4. One way to think about this result is that it determines, away from the prime p, the closure of the image of 

ρp,g,λ in the profinite group PU dp,g,λ ([Z[ζp]) . Note that in the genus 1 and genus 2 cases covered by Lemma 5.1, we prove that the image of ρJ,g,λ is a finite simple group (but do not identify that group; see Lemma 4.10). The proof of Theorem 1.3 stems from the realization that most of the arguments involved in our proof of density for the characteristic zero representations ρp,g,λ may be adapted (sometimes without much additional effort) to prove surjectivity results for the representations ρJ,g,λ . Indeed, most arguments relying on quantum topology (irreducibility, modular functor structure...) stay valid for ρJ,g,λ , and this allows us to jointly prove that the representations ρp,g,λ and ρJ,g,λ are tensor-indecomposable and have simple images. Also, we replace the result of Howe with an analog in positive characteristic due to Zalesskii and Suprunenko [ZS87]. The main additional hurdle is that we have to first establish that the images of ρJ,g,λ are finite simple groups of Lie type in the same characteristic as that of Z[ζp]/J . The notion of a TQFT was introduced by Witten [Wit89], but the first rigorous construction of a TQFT was car-ried out by Reshetikhin and Turaev, using the category of semisimple representations of the quantum group Uqsl 2 (see [RT91b] and [Tur94]). One can define a certain cobordism category C of closed surfaces with colored banded points, in which the cobordisms are decorated by uni-trivalent colored banded graphs. A banded point on a closed oriented surface is an oriented submanifold which is homeomorphic to the unit interval. If a surface has multiple banded points, we will assume that these intervals are disjoint. When one wants to study a surface with boundary from the point of view of TQFTs, one customarily attaches a disk to each boundary component and places a single banded point in the interior of each such disk.
