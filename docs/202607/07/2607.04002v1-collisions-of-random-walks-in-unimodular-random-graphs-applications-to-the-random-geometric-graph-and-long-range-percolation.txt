# Collisions of random walks in unimodular random graphs: applications to the random geometric graph and long-range percolation 

Jhon Astoquillca ∗ Carlos Martinez-Arevalo †

July 7, 2026 

Abstract 

We study collision properties of simple random walks in unimodular random rooted graphs. This work continues the study initiated in [21]: under recurrence and an integrability condition on the root, two independent random walks collide infinitely often a.s. We prove that, under transience and an integrability assumption involving the Green function on the root, two independent random walks collide only finitely often a.s. We apply these results to several random graphs with unbounded degree: the Gilbert graph, the Delaunay graph, the Gabriel graph; and the long-range percolation model. We use these collision properties to characterize stationary measures of the voter model on these graphs. 

# 1 Introduction 

Collision properties of random walks serve as an important tool in the study of systems of random walks, including coalescing, annihilating, and branching random walks, which arise naturally in the study of interacting particle systems. We say that a locally finite graph G = ( V, E ) has the finite (resp. infinite) collision property if two independent discrete-time random walks with transition function 

p(x, y ) = deg( x)−1 · 1{{ x, y } ∈ E}, x, y ∈ V, (1) started from the same vertex collide finitely (resp. infinitely) many times almost surely, where a collision occurs whenever the two walks occupy the same vertex at the same time. The corre-sponding definition for continuous-time random walks with jump rate (1) is analogous; we then speak of the discrete or continuous collision property. Precise definitions and relations between these notions are given in Section 2.2. Collision properties are closely connected to recurrence and transience of the underlying graph. In fact, on vertex-transitive graphs, the infinite collision property is equivalent to recurrence, while the finite collision property is equivalent to transience. However, on more general graphs, these equivalences break down: certain implications continue to hold, whereas others fail completely. For bounded-degree graphs, the infinite collision property implies recurrence, while transience implies the finite collision property. The converses, however, do not hold in general. In partic-ular, the Comb lattice is recurrent and possesses the finite collision property as proved in [22]. Moreover, the same article provides, in the Introduction, an example of a transient graph with the infinite collision property once the bounded-degree assumption is removed, showing that, in general, no implication holds in either direction. Rather than attempting a comprehensive overview of the existing literature, we refer the reader to [3, Related work], which contains an extensive account of previous results on collision properties

The voter model. The voter model on a locally finite graph G, denoted by ( ηt)t≥0, is a Markov process with configuration space {0, 1}V and stochastic dynamics described informally as follows: each vertex (voter) x ∈ V updates its current state (opinion) ηt(x) ∈ { 0, 1} at rate 1 by uniformly choosing a neighbouring vertex y and adopting its state ηt(y). For a formal definition and comprehensive introduction, we refer the reader to [24, 32]. In the voter model literature, it is well known that the evolution of the opinions of a group of voters is dual to a system of coalescing random walks. This duality implies the following. For α ∈ [0 , 1], if the initial configuration η0 is distributed according to the Bernoulli( α) product measure on {0, 1}V , the law of ηt converges weakly, as t → ∞ , to a limiting probability measure μα

which in turn is stationary for the voter model dynamics. We then have a one-parameter family of distinct stationary distributions {μα : α ∈ [0 , 1] }. The measures μ0 and μ1 correspond to the Dirac masses on the all-0 and all-1 configurations, respectively, which we refer to as the consensus measures. A stationary distribution is called extremal if it cannot be expressed as a nontrivial convex combination of other stationary distributions. We denote by Ie(G) the set of extremal stationary distributions. It is known [24, Proposition 1.8.c] that the set of stationary distributions of the voter model coincides with the closed convex hull of Ie(G). Consequently, the structure of the stationary distributions of the voter model is completely characterized once the set Ie(G) is described. The structure of Ie(G) depends strongly on the collision properties of G, as reflected in the following result. This statement follows from a stronger theorem proved in [24, Theorem 1.8. 1.1 Organization of the paper and proof strategies 

We prove Theorem 2 in Section 2.3.2. The proof relies on a mass transport argument that allows us to show that the expected number of collisions is bounded above by the expression appearing in the assumption, whose expectation is finite. Consequently, the expected number of collisions is finite, which implies the finite collision property. We prove Theorem 3 in Section 3. For this, we transfer the graph to its “unimodular version”, introduced in Section 3.1. The transfer lemma, Lemma 2, shows that proving the result for the “unimodular version” is equivalent to proving it for the original version. We also prove that the degree of the root is sufficiently controlled, Lemma 3, which allows us to apply the unimodular results. We treat dimension 2 in Section 3.2, the result is a direct consequence of Theorem 1. To verify the assumptions, we use recurrence results for GDel and GGab from the literature and prove recurrence of GSGil in Section 3.2.2. In the high-dimensional case, Section 3.3, the main tool is the application of Theorem 2. The control obtained on the degree of the root reduces the verification of the assumptions of the unimodular result to the computation of effective resistances in the graph, Lemma 6. To do so, in Section 3.3.2, we exploit the deep relation between the geometric graphs considered and Bernoulli percolation. We introduce a general scheme to compare the effective resistance of a well-behaved random graph with vertices in Rd and the effective resistance in the infinite connected cluster Bernoulli percolation, Lemma 9. Then, we only need control over the tails of the effective resistance in Bernoulli percolation, for which we apply already known heat kernel estimates of [6]. Finally, we prove Theorem 4 in Section 4. We consider the regimes in which an infinite cluster exists and show that, conditioned on the origin belonging to the infinite cluster, the infinite cluster rooted at the origin is unimodular. In Section 4.1, we treat the recurrent regimes and verify the assumptions of Theorem 1. In Section 4.2, we consider the regimes for which heat kernel estimates from [15] are available, allowing us to verify the assumptions of Theorem 2. It is worth noting that Gaussian heat kernel estimates alone are not sufficient to establish the finite or the infinite collision property. Additional assumptions on volume growth allow one to derive stronger conclusions and, in particular, obtain information about recurrence and transience. Beyond the transitive setting, [13] established the equivalence for quasi-transitive graphs with subexponential growth. The subex-ponential growth assumption was later removed in Chapter 3.4 of [27].
