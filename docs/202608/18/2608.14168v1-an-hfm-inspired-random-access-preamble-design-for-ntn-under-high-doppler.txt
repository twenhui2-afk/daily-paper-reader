# An HFM-Inspired Random Access Preamble Design for NTN under High Doppler

Abstract— Non-terrestrial networks (NTNs) are a key enabler of ubiquitous 6G connectivity, but the high orbital velocity and long propagation distances in low-Earth orbit (LEO)-NTN operation introduce large Doppler shifts and substantial delay uncertainty that challenge New Radio (NR) physical random access channel (PRACH) design. Conventional ZadoffChu (ZC) and linear fre-quency modulated (LFM) preambles are particularly vulnerable, as Doppler-induced ambiguity and delayDoppler coupling degrade timing estimation and preamble identification. This paper proposes a hyperbolic frequency modulation (HFM)-inspired PRACH pream-ble for robust synchronization and reliable identification under uncompensated or unknown Doppler, detected with a conventional matched-filter receiver so that the gains reflect the preamble design. A unified delayDoppler ambiguity function framework character-izes the self- and cross-ambiguity behavior of ZC, LFM, and HFM-inspired preambles, and a scaling-factor-based codebook ensures multi-user separability. Simulation results under NTN channel conditions confirm higher detection probability, lower timing root-mean-square error (RMSE), and improved peak-to-sidelobe and integrated sidelobe levels compared with ZC and frequency-domain superposed LFM baselines. 

Index Terms— Delay–Doppler ambiguity function, Doppler accuracy, hyperbolic frequency modulation, low earth orbit, non-terrestrial networks, physical random access channel, uncompen-sated Doppler.

I. Introduction 

In beyond-5G and 6G systems, non-terrestrial net-works (NTNs) complement terrestrial infrastructure with low earth orbit (LEO) satellites, high-altitude platforms, and aerial nodes to deliver ubiquitous connectivity. LEO constellations at altitudes of approximately 300600 km offer a favorable balance between coverage, latency, and deployment cost [1], extending broadband, remote-area, maritime, aeronautical, and critical-infrastructure links to regions where terrestrial coverage is sparse or econom-ically infeasible [2]–[4]. Accordingly, NTNs are intro-duced in the 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) New Radio (NR) framework in Release 17, and require-ments of physical-layer procedures, including synchro-nization, random access, and timing advance under large delay and Doppler uncertainty, are discussed in subse-quent releases [5], [6]. The physical random access chan-nel (PRACH) is central to these procedures, providing initial access [7], uplink timing alignment, and preamble identification under significant channel impairments. In LEO-based NTN, the PRACH procedure is chal-lenged by large Doppler offsets, high Doppler rates from satellite motion, and long propagation delays [8], [9], which violate the quasi-static channel assumptions underlying terrestrial NR PRACH design. The standard Zadoff-Chu (ZC) preambles used in long term evolution (LTE) PRACH rely on cyclic autocorrelation properties preserved only in the absence of frequency offset; under residual Doppler, ZC sequences suffer correlation-peak displacement that degrades timing accuracy and produces ambiguous preamble identification [10], [11]. This degra-dation worsens in multi-user random access, where si-multaneous transmissions increase cross-correlation leak-age [12], [13], and in wide LEO beams, whose large footprint aggregates many access attempts and intensifies preamble collisions and congestion [14]. Reliable ZC-based PRACH in NTN relies on user equipment (UE)-side Doppler and timing pre-compensation from global navigation satellite system (GNSS) and satellite ephemeris data [5], [6]. However, GNSS is unavailable in indoor or obstructed environments and impractical for terminals lacking reliable positioning or processing resources. Even with pre-compensation, residual Doppler can remain large enough to invalidate the ZC correlation properties, motivating GNSS-resilient waveforms and receivers that do not depend on accurate UE-side pre-compensation [15]. Several works address ZC Doppler sensitivity through modified ZC-based PRACH preambles [16]–[19], using root selection, block-cyclic constructions, extended cyclic prefixes, and zero-correlation-zone designs to enlarge the preamble pool and improve detection under Doppler and delay spread [20]. These approaches nonetheless inherit the lattice-like ZC ambiguity function (AF), whose mul-tiple comparable cross-ambiguity peaks in the delay– Doppler plane limit preamble ID (PID) separability under residual Doppler and multi-user interference.

Although LFM preserves strong matched-filter responses under frequency offset, its deter-ministic delay–Doppler coupling yields ridge-shaped AFs; slope mismatch alone therefore does not guarantee PID separability, since cross-ambiguity peaks can approach the self-ambiguity peak along the affine ridge [24]. To improve LFM robustness at higher Doppler, frequency-domain superposed LFM (FDS-LFM) pream-bles superpose multiple short LFM subsequences via frequency-domain phase rotations combined through an inverse DFT (IDFT), embedding PID through structured phase offsets and detecting via subsequence-level corre-lation combining [25]. This improves missed-detection performance over ZC-based PRACH under large residual Doppler, but the underlying LFM structure still produces extended cross-ambiguity ridges that constrain multi-user PID reliability. Hyperbolic frequency modulation (HFM) waveforms offer a fundamentally different chirp structure, widely studied in radar, sonar, and underwater acoustics for their Doppler accuracy [24], [26]–[28]. Their nonlinear instan-taneous frequency reshapes the AF geometry, suppressing the ridge and lattice formations of LFM and ZC while sharpening mainlobe localization and timing accuracy under severe Doppler [24], [26], making HFM well-suited to the unavoidable large Doppler and delay uncertainty of the NTN-PRACH procedure. Despite these favorable characteristics, HFM has not been systematically applied to NR-compatible PRACH design; its implications for PRACH cross-ambiguity be-havior, preamble identification, multi-user separability, and detection-theoretic metrics such as false-alarm and detection probability remain largely unexplored. This paper addresses these gaps through an HFM-inspired PRACH design for LEO NTN under large uncompensated or unknown Doppler.  

> A. Contributions

This paper addresses the PRACH preamble design 

problem for LEO-NTN random access under large un-known or uncompensated Doppler. We propose an HFM-motivated preamble that exploits the inherent Doppler tolerance of HFM signals within an NR-compatible frame structure, and we evaluate it using a conventional matched-filter PRACH receiver so that the observed gains are attributable to the preamble design itself rather than to any receiver-side processing. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:  

> •

We propose a novel HFM-inspired PRACH preamble for LEO-NTN operation under uncompensated or unknown Doppler. The design replaces only the frequency-domain sequence of the NR PRACH, leaving the numerology, framing, and receiver chain unchanged, and directly addresses the timing-bias and PID separability limitations of conventional ZC-and LFM-based preambles without UE-side Doppler pre-compensation.  

> •

A scaling-factor-parameterized HFM codebook is introduced to support multiple UE identities, with a derived minimum-spacing condition that keeps cross-ambiguity sidelobes strictly below the self-ambiguity peak across the delay–Doppler plane.  

> •

We analytically characterize the self- and cross-ambiguity properties of ZC, LFM, and HFM pream-bles within a unified framework, establishing that the HFM-inspired preamble suppresses the lattice and ridge structures inherent to ZC and LFM and yields timing estimation free of the deterministic Doppler-dependent bias exhibited by ZC and LFM.  

> •

Using the conventional receiver, we validate the anal-ysis under 3GPP-compliant NTN conditions, show-ing that the proposed preamble achieves higher de-tection probability, lower timing root mean squared error (RMSE), and improved peak-to-sidelobe level ratio (PSLR)/integrated sidelobe ratio (ISLR) than ZC and FDS-LFM baselines in both single- and multi-user scenarios, confirming that the improve-ments originate from the preamble design alone.  

> B. Paper Structure

The remainder of this paper is organized as fol-lows. Section II presents the LEO-NTN system model, alongside the propagation impairments and the proposed HFM-inspired PRACH preamble. Simulation results are presented in Section V, where the proposed scheme is compared against ZC and FDS-LFM baselines, and Section VI concludes the paper.

Each satellite is equipped with a regenerative payload that supports full NR radio access network (RAN) functionality, enabling uplink signals transmitted by the UEs to be received and processed directly onboard. The considered system focuses on the uplink RAP, where UEs initiate network access by transmitting PRACH preambles toward the serving satellite, as illustrated in Fig. 1. C. Proposed HFM-Inspired PRACH Preamble 

In LEO NTN scenarios, the large uncompensated fD

destroys the cyclic orthogonality of conventional ZC se-quences employed in NR PRACH. Specifically, a carrier-frequency offset applied to a ZC sequence displaces the matched-filter correlation peak away from the true propagation delay, thereby coupling timing estimation with frequency uncertainty and degrading both preamble detection and identification. A preamble waveform whose correlation peak remains at the true delay without the deterministic Doppler-induced shift of ZC is therefore required. The HFM waveform satisfies this requirement. Owing to its nonlinear logarithmic phase evolution, a carrier frequency offset applied to an HFM signal does not dis-place the correlation peak from the true delay, attenuating it instead. Consequently, timing estimation is decoupled from Doppler uncertainty without any UE-side frequency pre-compensation. This property is established formally in the performance analysis of Section IV. The proposed preamble retains the standard NR PRACH transmission structure, comprising a cyclic prefix (CP) of length NCP , a preamble sequence of length NSEQ ,and a guard period (GP) of length NGP , with total duration 

NPRACH = NCP + NSEQ + NGP . (5) Only the preamble sequence itself is replaced; the PRACH numerology, subcarrier allocation, and framing remain unchanged.

We adopt the conventional matched-filter receiver, so that the resulting performance can be attributed solely to the design of the proposed preamble. D. Computational Complexity 

The proposed HFM receiver has total complexity 

O(M ·|V HFM |·|D|· NSEQ ), one matched filter per preamble per Doppler hypothesis. The ZC receiver shares this struc-ture but demands a denser grid ( |V ZC | ≫ |V HFM |) to avoid timing bias, raising its cost proportionally. The FDS-LFM receiver [25] removes the Doppler search but replaces it with Msub subsequence correlations per preamble, each of length βFDS N (βFDS being the frequency-shifting param-eter), followed by delay-shifted non-coherent combining, yielding O(M · Msub · |D| · βFDS N ); the Msub -fold repe-tition and combining overhead make it costlier than the single-correlation HFM design. On the transmitter side, HFM costs O(NSEQ ) for one frequency-domain sequence, whereas FDS-LFM superposes Msub phase-rotated subse-quences before the IDFT, incurring O(Msub · NSEQ ).

VI. Conclusion 

This paper addressed reliable random access in LEO-based NTNs, where large uncompensated Doppler and significant delay uncertainty undermine the timing esti-mation and preamble identification of conventional NR PRACH designs. Through a unified delayDoppler ambi-guity function framework, we showed that ZC and LFM preambles are fundamentally limited by lattice and ridge structures that couple delays with Doppler. To overcome these limitations, an HFM-inspired PRACH preamble with a scaling-factor-parameterized codebook was pro-posed, fully compatible with the existing NR PRACH structure. Analytical and simulation results confirmed that the proposed design suppresses lattice and ridge structures, thereby enabling timing estimation free of de-terministic Doppler-dependent bias and reliable preamble identification at large uncompensated fD while consis-tently outperforming the ZC and FDS-LFM baselines under severe Doppler.
