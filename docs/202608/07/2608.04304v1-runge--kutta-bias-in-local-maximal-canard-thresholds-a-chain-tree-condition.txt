# Runge–Kutta Bias in Local Maximal-Canard Thresholds: A Chain-Tree Condition 

Haibo Lu ∗

Extended version. This extended manuscript is self-contained and includes the expanded technical proofs and reproducibility material. 

Abstract 

A fixed-step Runge–Kutta discretization may approximate ordinary trajectories accurately while displacing the local parameter at which selected attracting and repelling slow manifolds form a maximal canard. For a minimal polynomial fold model, we compare the simple roots of their direct splitting on the fixed section x = 0 for the flow and its exact Runge–Kutta map—the one-step map defined by the Runge–Kutta stage equations, not by a modified equation. The threshold comparison is uniform over all admissible parameter-family selections of the actual side manifolds, as defined below. After a two-stage demonstration, we prove this comparison on compact domains in self-adjoint two- and three-stage families. The threshold functional annihilates the bushy-tree direction of the order-three B-series defect and detects only the chain-tree defect β = bT Ac − 1/6. Hence class-wide leading-bias cancellation is equivalent to 

bT Ac = 16 ,

while the independent bushy-tree condition may fail; the fitted methods can therefore retain strict classical order two. Uniformly as ε = r2 → 0 in the joint wedge r2 ≤

h ≤ r3/4, the corresponding coefficient multiplies h2ε2 in the displacement between the nearby actual map and flow roots. The theorem concerns a local fixed-section threshold on the declared compact method domains, not a global canard explosion or a universal condition for arbitrary Runge–Kutta methods and folds. 

Keywords. maximal canard; fast–slow system; Runge–Kutta method; geometric numerical integration; invariant manifold; B-series order condition.

1.4 Relation to earlier results 

Continuous canard geometry originates with Benoît–Callot–Diener–Diener [ 1] and was developed through geometric and blow-up methods [ 4, 17 , 2]. Boundary-value continuation and bifurcation diagnostics locate continuous-flow canards [ 3 , 18 ]. Our observable instead subtracts the continuous root from the root of the exact fixed-step map on the same local section. Discrete fast–slow theory supplies the surrounding map framework [ 7 , 14 , 15 ]; earlier analytic work on discrete canards and delay includes [ 11 , 12 , 13 , 5 , 6]. Engel–Kuehn– Petrera–Suris [ 8 , Theorem 3.11] provide the closest exact-map precedent: an actual Kahan maximal-canard relation for a quadratic four-parameter family, supported by an explicit invariant orbit and a discrete Melnikov calculation. Their absolute Kahan threshold and the smaller map-minus-flow displacement resolved here are different quantities. On normally hyperbolic side collars we use established invariant-graph architecture from Fenichel, Nipp, Nipp–Stoffer, and Fontich–Simó [ 9 , 21 , 22 , 10 ]. Those sources do not automatically supply the present joint-(r, h ) closing-gap uniformity, fold bridge, selection derivatives, or actual-root transfer; those verifications remain in the proof. Standard B-series and rooted-tree algebra supplies the finite local identities [ 24 , 16 ]. Symbolic scripts certify only those finite identities, never the invariant manifolds, shielding, or root theorem. The fixed-chart restriction is essential. B-series methods are affine-equivariant, but a nonlinear normal-form change followed by rediscretization generally produces a different map [ 19 ]. Likewise, A-stability alone does not imply the fold Gaussian bounds used here, and self-adjointness alone does not provide the required drift, positive weights, stage branch, or selected slow manifolds. 41.5 Proof architecture and reading order

# 11 Consequences, limitations, and outlook 

Purpose, inputs, and output. This section uses only the master theorem, its method-family verifications, and the two application corollaries. It separates consequences that follow at the proved scope from stronger statements that would require new proofs. No estimate established earlier is strengthened here. 46 11.1 A threshold-bias law across method families 

Since r2 = ε, the master theorem identifies the leading threshold bias as the observable functional (1.9) multiplied by h2ε2, uniformly in the joint wedge and over the full product of admissible flow and map selections on each declared compact method domain. Here 

βRK = 1 /12 − ρ for the two-stage family and βRK = (1 − 24 ν)/48 for the three-stage family. This form remains meaningful when the displayed coefficient vanishes. This is a local diagnostic for the systematic movement of a fold-canard parameter under fixed-step discretization. Once the physical germ coefficients are estimated, (3.13) suggests three uses: 1. subtract the predicted leading bias from a computed threshold; 2. combine computations at two step sizes to extrapolate the flow threshold; 3. compare method parameters within either family to separate a method-dependent shift from a germ-dependent one. These are asymptotic consequences, not performance claims. A canard transition may be exponentially narrow, whereas the leading bias is polynomial in h and ε. This explains why a small trajectory error can coexist with a dynamically significant threshold error. Whether the correction is useful at a particular finite parameter value depends on the remainder constants and must be tested separately. 

11.2 Model consequences and their limits 

Corollaries 10.1 and 10.2 state the two model consequences and their coefficients once. The van der Pol calculation uses only a physical affine chart. The Rosenzweig–MacArthur calculation uses an affine state–parameter map and a positive constant clock factor, so it concerns the fixed-step RK discretization of the original rational source field, not of a state-time polynomialization. The independent computation in Figure 3 checks the finite-parameter sign, size, and cancellation; it does not establish the analytic corollary. Both model consequences remain local fixed-section statements. They do not by themselves locate a global relaxation cycle, Hopf bifurcation, or numerically continued canard-explosion branch; such a result would also require a return map and control of its discretization. Moreover, the Rosenzweig–MacArthur theorem does not cover the εs = 0 .02 

parameter displayed in Figure 6 of [ 23 ]. The numerical grid here uses the smaller asymptotic range 25 165888 ≤ εs ≤ 25 23328 .

No dimensional ecological parameters were calibrated. Finally, the finite-order side traces in Figure 3 make that computation a finite-boundary proxy; the actual selected-manifold conclusion comes from the analytic corollary, not from the plotted shooting roots. 

11.3 The mathematical content of the order condition 

At order three, the principal B-series defect space of the complete symmetric two-stage family is two-dimensional. The fixed-node theorem first isolates the chain direction; Corollary 3.4 then varies both defect coordinates on K2 and proves that the actual threshold functional annihilates the bushy-tree direction and detects only the chain-tree defect. The independent three-stage theorem reaches the same functional with a different tableau, a different bushy defect, and an irreducible cubic-over-cubic stability function. The conclusion is therefore not inferred from a single one-parameter fit. The distinction between algebraic and dynamical scope remains essential. The parame-terization (9.1) describes every real, consistent, self-adjoint two-stage tableau, including 47 degenerate and noncompact parts. Uniform actual slow-manifold selection and root transfer are proved only on K2. No theorem is claimed across the coincident-node chart boundary or over the whole noncompact tableau plane. The geometry responsible for this bushy-tree cancellation has two layers. The exact canonical parabola removes a side-independent offset. The remaining forcing is then averaged against the Gaussian Green kernel of the fold passage. Continuous compatibility and the first two Gaussian moments collapse the local polynomial Fρ to the chain coefficient. Thus the observable selects the tree only after actual invariant-manifold geometry has been taken into account. 

11.4 Boundaries of the theorem 

Four limitations are structural. 

The chart is part of the problem. The theorem is formulated in a fixed physical affine chart and time gauge. An arbitrary nonlinear normal-form transformation followed by rediscretization changes the RK map and can change the coefficient. 

The wedge is joint. The estimate is uniform only for 

r2 ≤ h ≤ r3/4.

It is not a statement about taking h → 0 at fixed r, nor about a single-parameter limit with a hidden relation between h and r. The separate remainders h2r5 and h4r3 should not be merged into a one-variable big-O.

Analyticity is used with room to spare. The common complex halo provides the uniform Cauchy bounds needed by the stage branch, the order-seven graph estimates, and the finite mixed-jet recurrences. The argument should extend to a sufficiently high finite 

Ck class, but this paper does not optimize the minimal regularity. 

The fitted methods are observable witnesses. On the base slice, the method at 

ρ = 1 /12 is A-stable and has the same scalar stability function as the two-stage Gauss method, but it remains strict order two and is not symplectic. On the compact two-stage extension, the Gauss method is only one point of the fitted locus. The fitted three-stage method is also strict order two. The theorems say that these methods cancel the leading local canard-threshold bias on this class; they do not say that the methods are globally more accurate, cheaper, or more robust. 

11.5 Directions opened by the proof 

The mechanism suggests several concrete extensions. 

• One can seek uniform actual-threshold theorems beyond K2, including a regular treatment of the coincident-node boundary, or classify higher-stage families for which the same bushy-tree annihilation persists or fails. 

• The same common-target recurrence may be useful for other nonhyperbolic section observables whose invariant manifolds are exponentially close and whose one-step gap collapses. 

• A posteriori numerical work can test the effective range of the master estimate, compare correction and extrapolation strategies, and determine whether the fitted condition has practical value away from the asymptotic wedge. 48 • Mechanical enumeration of the finite Faà di Bruno and stage polynomials would strengthen reproducibility, although it would not change the human geometric proof. 

• A transport theorem with free constants p0 and κ0, an arbitrary-order divided-residual theorem, or a theorem for general folds and higher-dimensional fast–slow systems would require new proofs. They are not consequences of the normalized reference calculation here. The central conclusion is geometric: across two genuinely different RK families, a two-coordinate local defect is filtered to the same chain-tree functional after selection of actual slow manifolds, fold passage, and root transfer. The resulting necessary-and-sufficient conditions are uniform on the declared compact method domains. This final section exports no additional theorem. Its diagnostic notation may be forgotten; the citable mathematical content is the definition package, the named transport results, the master theorem, and the observable-specific B-series corollary. 49 A The complete theorem data
