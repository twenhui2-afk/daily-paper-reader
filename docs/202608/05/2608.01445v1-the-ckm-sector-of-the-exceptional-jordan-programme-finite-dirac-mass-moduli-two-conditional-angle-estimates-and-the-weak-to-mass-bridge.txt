Abstract 

The standard model does not determine quark masses or the CKM matrix. In the exceptional-Jordan programme, charged-fermion square-root masses occupy short Sym 3(3) chains. Compressing the multiplicative symmetric-cube lift onto the occupied nodes gives a root-mass operator Rf whose positive square has exactly the proposed mass-ratio spectrum. This packages the proposed mass spectrum and the relative left frames in one finite Dirac operator; it does not derive the latter from singular-value decomposition alone. Conditional on the occupied-node transport, virtual-node amplitude, real (2 , 3) blocks and balanced-quadrature choices, the no-fit layer gives |Vus | = 0 .2371 (5 .3% high) and |Vcb | = 0 .0422 (0 .8% high) against the 2026 Particle Data Group global fit; the minimal identity |Vub |/|Vcb | = pmu/m c

is a factor two low, and one complex long edge is fitted to the remaining CKM angle and phase. The scale-dependent parent mass-ratio inputs are interpreted at the common matching baseline μ∗ = MZ .For an adopted F1–F2 family embedding, we construct an exact Peirce-changing lift of the local Cabibbo bridge. The question sits in a tradition nearly as old as the CKM matrix itself [ 5, 6 ]. Ever since Gatto, Sartori and Tonin, and then Fritzsch, Weinberg, and Wilczek and Zee, tied the Cabibbo angle to a square-root mass ratio [ 7 , 8, 9, 10 ], the recurring hope has been that the mixing matrix is not fundamental data but arithmetic of the mass spectrum. That hope produced GUT-scale mass relations [ 11 ], the texture-zero programme and its systematic surveys [ 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 , 16 ], the Froggatt–Nielsen mechanism [ 17 ], and careful analyses of which texture predictions are general and which are artefacts of a basis choice [ 18 , 19 ]. The present programme belongs to this lineage and differs from it in one decisive respect: conditional on the parent construction’s Jordan spread, minimal chains and occupied-node assignments, the mass ratios are predicted rather than refitted for the CKM analysis. The node values and hence every rung angle are therefore fixed before mixing is addressed, and nothing can be re-chosen for the CKM matrix’s benefit. For the broader family of division-algebra approaches to standard-model structure, see [20, 21, 22, 23, 24]. Experimental values throughout are global-fit results [25, 26, 27]. This paper completes that analysis as far as the present structure permits, and states precisely what remains. 3.8 Relation to almost-commutative noncommutative geometry and novelty 

The placement of masses and mixing in one finite Dirac operator has an important precedent. In the almost-commutative construction of Chamseddine, Connes and Marcolli, the admissible finite Dirac operators form a moduli space whose physical coordinates include quark and lepton masses, CKM and PMNS angles, and Majorana data [ 31 ]. Thus neither the statement that masses and mixing reside in DF , nor their separation into singular values and relative flavour frames, is claimed here as new. That construction also gives genuine aggregate restrictions, including gauge- and Higgs-sector boundary conditions and a unification-scale sum rule for squared Yukawa masses. It would therefore be incorrect to say that conventional noncommutative geometry makes no particle-physics predictions. The distinction concerns the individual flavour data. In the standard model, the complex matrices Yu, Yd and Ye are independent renormalizable couplings. Choosing them appropriately reproduces the observed masses and CKM matrix, but the standard-model field content and symmetries do not select their hierarchical singular values or relative left frame. Recasting the same data as coordinates of a finite geometry gives them a geometric home but, by itself, does not calculate their observed numerical values. Later Hodge-duality work shows that distinct nonzero masses and nontrivial mixing can be required for a refined property of the finite geometry, but does not derive the measured hierarchies or CKM entries [35]. # 4 Two extensions of the lift, and the one that survives 

4.1 Adjacency from minimality 

The minimality principle that selects the chains [ 1 ] also constrains the mixing structure: every physical step is a move along one edge SU (2) of Sym 3(3), so whatever operator connects generations acts only between adjacent chain nodes . Two readings of “the mass matrix is built from adjacent steps” are then available: 

(i) Hamiltonian-texture reading : the mass matrix in the flavour basis is a tridiagonal (nearest-neighbour) texture on the chain, with eigenvalues matched to the physical masses; diagonalize it. (ii) Transport reading : the flavour-to-mass misalignment is the ordered product of two-state rotations, one per rung, each given exactly by the lift (1) ; mass eigenstates are the chain nodes themselves, as the ladder normalization dictates (each generation’s square-root mass is its node monomial). 15 4.2 The Hamiltonian-texture extension fails 

Reading (i) can be implemented in closed form: for the up chain the 3 × 3 zero-diagonal-plus-endpoint texture reproduces the classic Fritzsch results, but for the down chain the eigenvalue-matched 4 × 4 textures (the fourth eigenvalue assigned to the virtual node, all sign patterns and several auxiliary-eigenvalue candidates scanned) produce non-unitarity of 20–69% across the ten admissible sign assignments, with |Vus | ranging over 0 .05–0 .66 and the assignment closest to the Cabibbo value carrying 31% non-unitarity; no viable case exists among the tested sign patterns and auxiliary-eigenvalue choices (Appendix E) [ D]. The failure is structural: matching four hierarchical eigenvalues with zero diagonals forces large hops and 

O(1) eigenvector rotations. We record this prominently, with the caveat that it excludes this specific (Fritzsch-rigid) texture family, not reading (i) in general: nearest-neighbour textures with free diagonals can always be made diagonal-dominant and mild. The adoption of reading (ii) is thus a modelling choice supported by the exclusion and by the transport character of the lift itself [8, 7], not a forced conclusion.
