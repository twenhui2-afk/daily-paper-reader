CYCLIC DELETION RIGIDITY AND MACAULAY SHADOWS IN THE TU–DENG PROBLEM 

KAIMIN CHENG 

Abstract. We determine all equality cases in the Tu–Deng bound |St,k | ≤ 2k−1. If the k-bit cyclic word of t has R ones, Z zeros, and cyclic one-gap lengths g1, . . . , g Z ,then equality holds if and only if gi ≥ Z − 1 for every i. This resolves Conjecture 3.20 of Flori, Randriambololona, Cohen and Mesnager, and we also enumerate all equality parameters. For R ≥ Z we determine the sharp first stability gap and all extremal words, while for R < Z we obtain an exact quantization of the deficit and an explicit run-sensitive lower bound. The proofs are structural: an explicit matrix conjugation identifies the auxiliary enumerators in the two recent complete proofs of the Tu– Deng conjecture. We then develop a rooted coarsening model for all coefficients, prove one-sided deletion rigidity and an exact Macaulay-flux identity, and derive a Macaulay–M¨ obius formula from the bounded simplex at the highest cyclic level.

4. One-sided deletion rigidity and Macaulay flux 

We now develop the one-sided deletion argument used for L(v). Let {0, 1}∗ denote the set of all finite binary words, including the empty word. 

Definition 4.1. A finite binary language L ⊆ { 0, 1}∗ is 1 -deletion-closed if, whenever 

w ∈ L and w′ is obtained from w by deleting a single 1, then w′ ∈ L .For a, b ≥ 0, put

Proposition 4.2. If L is 1-deletion-closed and b ≥ 1, then 

b|L a,b | ≤ (a + b)|L a,b −1|. (4.1) 

Proof. Mark one of the b ones of a word in La,b and delete it. This gives b|L a,b | marked deletion objects. The image consists of a word z ∈ L a,b −1 together with one of the a + b

insertion slots for a marked 1. The map is injective, since the target word and the marked slot recover the source word and the marked occurrence. This proves (4.1). □

8. Concluding remarks 

The rooted coarsening theorem gives a combinatorial interpretation of every coeffi-cient hq,j (W ) of Cusick’s polynomial, not only those needed for the equality problem. Thus each cyclic defect is a fixed-content defect of a rooted coarsening complex, and the Macaulay flux of Section 4 applies level by level. At the highest cyclic level, Theorem 6.3 makes this connection explicit: shortening a cyclic one-gap produces the correspond-ing Macaulay barrier, with higher-order corrections arising only from overlaps among deficient gaps. The remaining sharp stability problem lies in the complementary range R < Z . The-orem 7.2 shows that the deficit has the exact form 2 Z−RM−(W ) − 1 and gives an explicit lower bound in terms of cyclic runs. Determining the minimum possible value of M−(W )for fixed R and Z, together with all minimizing cyclic words, requires a genuinely multi-level optimization of the defects ∆ 1, . . . , ∆R. The coarsening model provides a finite CYCLIC DELETION RIGIDITY AND MACAULAY SHADOWS 21 

combinatorial formulation of this problem, but no sharp general classification is asserted here.
