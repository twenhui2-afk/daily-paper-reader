> Abstract. Let b=h⊕nbe a Borel subalgebra of a basic classical Lie superalgebra over
> Cwith ha Cartan subalgebra. We give an upper bound for the index ind( b) of b; in some instances this bound is 0, in which case ind( b) = 0. 1. Introduction 

In the 1970s, the index of a Lie algebra was defined by Dixmier in [Di]. Let g be a Lie algebra over C, then its index is (1.1) ind( g) := min  

> ξ∈g∗

dim( gξ),

where gξ := {x ∈ g : ξ([ x, y ]) = 0 for all y ∈ g} is the centraliser in g of ξ ∈ g∗ for the coadjoint action. There has been great interest in the index of Lie algebras due to its relevance in invariant theory and in representation theory, for example from A. Joseph, [Jo2], and D. Panyushev, [Pa1]. We refer to the introduction of [CMo] and the references therein for an overview of results about the index of Lie algebras. The index of a Lie algebra appears to be difficult to calculate in general, although substan-tial progress has been made in specific cases. If g is reductive, then it well known that ind( g)equals rank( g). The index of Borel subalgebras is known and was first established in [Tr, Theorem 4.1] with another approach given in [TY, Th´ eor` eme 2.9]. Subsequently, the index of parabolic and more generally seaweed subalgebras has been studied, see for example [Jo2] and [CCH]. In this paper we initiate the study of the index of a Lie superalgebra g over C by considering the definition of the index as given by (1.1). It can be expected that the index is an important invariant of Lie superalgebras so it is of interest to determine in key examples. In the case that g is a basic classical Lie superalgebra it is straightforward to show that ind( g) ≤ rank( g) by considering regular semisimple elements in g0. The proof of equality from the non-super case does not extend easily, though it may be expected that there is equality. Our main result concerns the index of Borel subalgebras of basic classical Lie superalgebras, and we provide an upper bound for this index. We state this in the below theorem and explain the notation and terminology used later in the introduction.

3. Generalized Root Systems 

3.1. Definitions and Preliminary Results. In 2024, Dimitrov and Fioresi introduced the theory of generalized root systems (GRSs) in [DF]. We give an overview of GRSs and use 

loc. cit. as our general reference for all below. 

Definition 3.1. Let V be a finite-dimensional Euclidean space with inner product ⟨ , ⟩ and let ∆ ⊆ V be a non-empty finite subset of V . Then (∆ , V ) is a generalized root system if 

V = R∆, and if the following holds for all α, β ∈ ∆: (GRS1) If ⟨α, β ⟩ > 0, then α − β ∈ ∆; (GRS2) If ⟨α, β ⟩ < 0, then α + β ∈ ∆; (GRS3) If ⟨α, β ⟩ = 0, then α + β ∈ ∆ if and only if α − β ∈ ∆. The rank of (∆ , V ) is defined as the dimension of V . We tend to omit the vector space and inner product and just write ∆ as the GRS. Note that ⟨α, α ⟩ > 0 for all nonzero α ∈ ∆, so 0 ∈ ∆. Then ⟨α, 0⟩ = 0 and 0 + α ∈ ∆, so −α ∈ ∆. We call ∆ = {0} the trivial GRS. We can view Lie algebra root systems as GRSs, with the minor caveat that these root systems do not contain 0 whereas GRSs do contain 0 by convention. It follows from [DF, Theorem 4.7] that the root systems of the basic classical Lie superalgebras can also be viewed as GRSs, as we explain next. Let R be the root system of a basic classical Lie superalgebra 

g and assume g̸ = psl (n|n). Then ∆ can be viewed as subset of a real form hR of the Cartan subalgebra of g0. The proof of [DF, Theorem 4.7] implies that there is a vector space isomorphism hR → V to an inner product space V such that the image of R in V is a GRS. Note that the inner product ⟨ , ⟩ on V does not correspond to the restriction of ( , ) to hR

as the latter is not positive definite in general; as this does not impact on the methods and results of this paper we do not consider it further here. In the case g = psl (n|n) we need to consider ∆ to be in the 2 n − 1 dimensional subspace of the space diagonal matrices in 

gl (n|n) spanned by the elements of ∆. We remark that GRSs were classified in 2024 by Cuntz and M¨ uhlherr in [CM¨ u]; they found that each GRS of rank at least 2 is the quotient of a Lie algebra root system, although we will not employ this result. This extended the classification of rank 2 GRSs by Dimitrov and Fioresi from [DF, Section 5]. Much of the theory of root systems extends naturally to GRSs. This allows for the defini-tion of irreducible GRSs, and then it can be shown every GRS can be uniquely decomposed
