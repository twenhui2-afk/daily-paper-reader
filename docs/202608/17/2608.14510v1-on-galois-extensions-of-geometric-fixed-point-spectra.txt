# On Galois extensions of geometric fixed point spectra 

Jack Morgan Davies August 17, 2026 

Abstract 

In this article, the cyclotomic Galois action on topological K-theory adjoined with a primitive nth root of unity and the famous GL 1(Z/n )- and GL 2(Z/n )-Galois actions on topological modular forms with Γ1(n)- and Γ( n)-level structures are unified and generalised. This is done by defining a quotient stack in derived algebraic geometry parametrising constant étale subgroups of P-divisible groups, and studying how these quotient stacks and various notions of torsors interact with Galois extensions formed by taking algebraic and categorical invariants. Taking global sections then yields the titular Galois extensions on K-geometric fixed points, recovering and refining the well-known examples above and providing new ones. As an application, the ∞-category of perfect modules over a variety of H-equivariant ring spectra R are decomposed into simple pullbacks of nonequivariant categories, lead-ing to Mayer–Vietoris sequences for localising invariants of R. For example, this occurs for equivariant topological K-theory for all p-groups as well as any finite nonabelian simple group of order less than 500.

Even our understanding of universal invariants such as algebraic K-theory is strongly influenced by Galois descent. In fact, the failure of Ravenel’s famous telescope conjecture [Rav84], disproven in [BHLS23], is a result of K(n + 1) -local algebraic K-theory satisfying Galois hyperdescent and the failure of T (n+1) -local algebraic K-theory to satisfy Galois hyperdescent in certain instances, for n ≥ 1.The goal of this article is to explore new Galois actions on E∞-rings from both an algebro-geometric and an equivariant homotopy-theoretic perspective. First, we show that for a finite abelian group K and a P-divisible group G, the natural Aut( bK)-action on the stack of injections Inj( bK, G) defines a flat affine torsor of spectral stacks over its quotient (Thm.A). By applying global sections or ∞-categories of quasi-coherent sheaves to these torsors, we show that the associated extensions of E∞-rings or presentably symmetric monoidal sta-ble ∞-categories are Galois (Cor.2.37). These results are then translated into equivariant homotopy theory using Lurie’s tempered cohomology theories of [Lur19] and their refine-ment to globally equivariant E∞-rings of [BDL26b] (Thms.B and C). As an application, we study when the ∞-categories of perfect modules of these equivariant tempered cohomology theories decompose into simple pullbacks of nonequivariant categories (§4.1). This gives a valuable inroad to computing invariants of genuinely equivariant E∞-rings. 2The geometric statement 

One classical way to characterise a well-behaved G-action on a scheme Y is to ask that the quotient stack Y/G be represented by a scheme or spectral algebraic space. In the 

∞-category of spectral stacks, we define an affine flat G-torsor over a fixed stack M as a spectral M-stack Y with G-action such that the quotient stack X = Y/G is relatively affine and flat over M. These conditions on the structure morphism of X restrict the potential actions on G. For instance, we show in Pr. 2.14 that such G actions are precisely those such that when pulled back to an affine over M, the associated map between affine stacks induces a classical Galois extension of commutative rings on π0.The affine flat torsors at the heart of this article is constructed out of P-divisible groups G,such as the torsion of an abelian variety, over a spectral stack M. For each finite abelian group K, one defines a homomorphism stack Hom( bK, G) from the constant group stack on the Pontryagin dual of K into G together with its natural Aut( bK)-action. This does not define an affine torsor though: the zero homomorphism bK → G has stabiliser Aut( bK),and consequently, the quotient stack Hom( bK, G)/ Aut( bK) is not affine over the base M.In [BDL26b], together with William Balderrama and Sil Linskens, we define an open sub-stack of Hom( bK, G) classifying injective homomorphisms Inj( bK, G). The fundamental geometric observation of this article is that the Aut( bK)-action on the homomorphism stack lifts Inj( bK, G) and defines an affine flat torsor over M:

Tempered cohomology theories 

In [Lur19], Lurie describes how a P-divisible group G over a stack M gives rise to an equivariant cohomology theory, simultaneously for all finite groups, called the associated 

tempered cohomology theory . Roughly speaking, the K-fixed points of this theory are the global sections of the homomorphism stack Hom( bK, G) for a finite abelian group K. In [BDL26b, GLP24], these tempered cohomology theories are shown to be represented by 

global E∞-rings Γ( OG), so a compatible system of genuinely H-equivariant E∞-rings for all finite groups H. The notation Γ( OG) emphasises this object as an enhancement of the usual global sections Γ( OM) of M courtesy of G. Indeed, the underlying E∞-ring of Γ( OG)

This article is broken up into three sections: we begin with pure spectral algebraic geometry to prove Thm. A (§ 2), then translate this result into equivariant homotopy theory using Lurie’s tempered cohomology theories and prove Thms. B and C (§ 3), and finally, we review KNPR-decompositions and give a catalogue of simplifications of these categorical decompositions as an application of Thm.B (§4). In a short appendix (§A), we summarise some permanence properties for morphisms in spectral algebraic geometry used in this article. In more detail, after setting up the basic theory of torsors, the main theme of § 2.1 is the interaction between torsors and various notions of Galois extensions. We begin by showing that π0-Galois extensions yield affine torsors (Prs. 2.11 and 2.12) as do some Galois extensions of Rognes (Pr.2.18). Then we explore the Galois extensions in various 

∞-categories induced by various flavours of torsor in §2.1.6. In §2.2, we define the stack of subgroups and prove Thm.A that the stack of injections equipped with its tautological action defines a flat affine torsor. Putting these two subsections together, we obtain many examples of Galois extensions in various ∞-categories courtesy of Thm.A (Cor.2.37). The passage from oriented P-divisible groups in spectral algebraic geometry to equivari-ant homotopy theory via Lurie’s tempered cohomology theories is reviewed in §3.1; many more details can be found in [BDL26b], however, the focus here is on the canonical resid-ual actions on genuine and geometric fixed points and their relationship with tempered cohomology theories. This is mostly expository. The proof of Thm.B then follows straight from Cor.2.37, and Thm.C is an exercise in global homotopy theory. In §3.1.3, we detail when the above residual actions on fixed points agree with residual Weyl group actions classically studied in equivariant homotopy theory. Examples of the Galois extensions from Thm.B are given in §3.2, focusing on those involving equivariant K-theory and equivariant elliptic cohomology. Lastly, §4 is concerned with decompositions of ∞-categories of modules over equivariant ring spectra. We begin by reviewing KNPR-decompositions (Thm.4.1), setting the scene for our decomposition (Cor.4.5), and proving some simple algebraicity statements (Cor.4.6). # 2 Torsors and the moduli of subgroups 

One of the main focuses of this article is the systematic study of various notions of torsor in spectral algebraic geometry. We begin with the most general set-up to highlight which phenomenæ occur under which precise hypotheses. 10 2.1 Basics of P-G-torsors 

Recall the notions of a group object [Lur09b, Df.7.2.2.1], groupoid object [Lur09b, Df.6.1.2.7], and an action of a group object [NSS15, Df.3.1]. This latter reference takes place in a topos, but it also makes perfect sense in the category Stk and its slices Stk /M.

Definition 2.1. Let G be a group object in Stk /M and P be a property of morphisms of stacks. A P-G-action on an M-stack Y is a G-action such that the structure map from the quotient Y// G → M satisfies P. A P-G-torsor (over M) is a map φ : Y → X in Stk /M

together with a P-G-action on Y inducing an equivalence Y// G ≃ X. If P is the vacuous property, ie, all morphisms of stacks are P, then we abbreviate these notions to G-action 

and G-torsor .The variables P and M can be used to force the quotient stack Y// G to be well-behaved, such as be representable by an affine or nonconnective spectral scheme. For instance, the universal G-torsor BG = M// G is not an affine torsor if G is not trivial. 

Remark 2.2 . The discussion of torsors in § 2.1.1 and 2.1.2 also applies in the context of 

Stk τ (C) of [BDL25, Def.2.1.1.5] with only cosmetic changes; § 2.1.6 should also broadly apply to generic quasi-coherent sheaf contexts ([BDL25, Df.2.1.3.2]). For readability, we simply write and refer to Stk .
