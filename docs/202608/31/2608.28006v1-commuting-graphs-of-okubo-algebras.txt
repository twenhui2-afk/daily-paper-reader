Abstract 

Commuting graphs of Okubo algebras are considered, and the problem of their connectivity is studied. The commuting graph of a pseudo-octonion algebra P8(F)

over a field F, char F̸ = 3 , that contains a primitive cubic root of unity is shown to be isomorphic to the commuting graph of the matrix algebra M3(F). As a consequence, if the field F is algebraically closed, then the diameter of the commuting graph for the unique Okubo algebra over F equals 4. It is shown that the commuting graph of the real division Okubo algebra is connected, and its diameter also equals 4. The proof of this result relies on the fact that, given any two idempotents in an arbitrary Okubo algebra, the intersection of their centralizers is always nonzero. 

Keywords: Okubo algebras, composition algebras, pseudo-octonions, relation graphs, commuting graph. 

MSC 2020: 05C25, 17A75. 

# 1 Introduction 

The study of binary relations on algebraic structures leads naturally to consideration of the corresponding relation graphs. In the case of algebras over a field, the most widely studied graphs by now are commuting graphs, orthogonality graphs, and zero divisor graphs. The current paper is devoted to the commuting graphs of an important class of non-associative algebras, namely, Okubo algebras. The definition of the commuting graph of an arbitrary algebra A over a field F

(possibly noncommutative or non-associative) is closely related to the definition of the commutative center. The commutative center of an algebra A is defined as the set of those elements in A which commute with all elements: 

CA = c ∈ A | cb = bc ∀ b ∈ A .

char F̸ = 3 , containing a primitive cube root of unity, and also on the real division Okubo algebra — the so-called real pseudo-octonions. In particular, if the field F is algebraically closed and char F̸ = 3 , then the pseudo-octonion algebra is the unique Okubo algebra over F up to isomorphism. The main distinguishing feature of the above cases is a close connection with matrix algebras, whose commuting graphs have been actively studied by various authors [1–3, 5, 24, 25]. At present, the main results on the diameter of ΓC (Mn(F)) — the commuting graph of the algebra of n × n matrices over F — are as follows. Over any field F, the graph ΓC (M2(F)) is disconnected [3, Remark 8]. By [2, Theorems 3 and 17], if for some 

n ≥ 3 the graph ΓC (Mn(F)) is connected, then 4 ≤ diam Γ C (Mn(F)) ≤ 6. If the field F is algebraically closed and n ≥ 3, then ΓC (Mn(F)) is connected, and its diameter equals 4,see [2, Corollary 7]. A similar statement holds in the case when F = R [25]. The first example of an algebra Mn(F) such that the largest possible diameter of the commuting graph is achieved was given in [24]. In [18], the graphs ΓC (Mn(Qp)) were considered, where Qp denotes the field of p-adic numbers, and it was shown that, for a prime q ≥ 7,the diameter of ΓC (M2q(Q2)) equals 6. This provides the first example of a commuting graph with the largest possible diameter that does not use the axiom of choice. Still, it cannot be guaranteed that the graph ΓC (Mn(F)) is connected in general; for example, for every n ≥ 3 the graph ΓC (Mn(Q)) of the matrix algebra over the rational numbers is disconnected, see [1, Remark 8]. This result follows from Theorem 6 of [1], which states that the graph ΓC (Mn(F)) is connected if and only if n ≥ 3 and every field extension of F of degree n contains a proper intermediate field. The structure of this paper is as follows: In Section 2 we present the construction of Okubo algebras over fields of characteristic not three via pseudo-octonion algebras. 2This section also contains the main statements about Okubo algebras, their commutative center, and nonzero idempotents that are used throughout the text. In Section 3 we consider the commuting graph of the pseudo-octonion algebra P8(F)

over a field F, char F̸ = 3 , which contains a primitive cube root of unity. By Proposi-tion 3.2, the graph ΓC (P8(F)) is isomorphic to the commuting graph ΓC (M3(F)) of the algebra of 3 × 3 matrices over the field F. It follows that the diameter of the commut-ing graph of the unique Okubo algebra over an algebraically closed field F, char F̸ = 3 ,equals 4. However, if the field F is not algebraically closed, then the commuting graph 

ΓC (P8(F)) can be disconnected, see Corollary 3.8. In Section 4 we study paths between nonzero idempotents in the commuting graph of an Okubo algebra O over an arbitrary field F. By Corollary 4.6, the distance between any two idempotents in ΓC (O) is at most 2, i.e., the intersection of their centralizers is nonzero. Section 5 is devoted to the study of the commuting graph of the real pseudo-octonion algebra eP8(R). By Lemma 5.10, in ΓC ( eP8(R)) any nonzero element is adjacent to an idempotent. Combining this statement with the results of Section 4, we obtain The-orem 5.11, which states that the commuting graph ΓC ( eP8(R)) is connected, and its diameter equals 4, as in the case of an Okubo algebra over an algebraically closed field.

We use the definition of an Okubo algebra given in [7, Section 1]. Assume first that the characteristic of the field F is distinct from 3, and F contains a primitive cube root of unity ω. In particular, the second condition is satisfied if F is algebraically closed. We set

Definition 2.1. Let (A, +, ∗) be an arbitrary (possibly non-unital and non-associative) algebra over a field F. Assume that A is equipped with a strictly nondegenerate quadratic form n( ·), i.e., the associated symmetric bilinear form n( a, b ) = n( a + b) − n( a) − n( b) is nondegenerate on A. Then A is called a composition algebra if the quadratic form n( ·)

admits composition, i.e., n( a∗b) = n( a) n( b) for all a, b ∈ A . Here n( ·) is called the norm .

Definition 2.2. A composition algebra (A, ∗, n) is called symmetric if 

n( x ∗ y, z ) = n( x, y ∗ z)

for all x, y, z ∈ A .By [12, Lemma 1.1], the algebra P8(F) is a symmetric composition algebra. 

Theorem 2.3 ([20, Theorem 1], [17, (34.1)]) . Let (A, ∗, n) be a composition algebra. The following conditions are equivalent: 

(1) A is symmetric; 

(2) (x ∗ y) ∗ x = x ∗ (y ∗ x) = n( x) y for all x, y ∈ A .

Any algebra A satisfying condition (2) of the theorem above is a flexible algebra .Namely, for all x, y ∈ A the flexibility identity holds: 

x ∗ (y ∗ x) = ( x ∗ y) ∗ x. 

By linearizing the equality in Theorem 2.3(2), we obtain 

(x ∗ y) ∗ z + ( z ∗ y) ∗ x = x ∗ (y ∗ z) + z ∗ (y ∗ x) = n( x, z )y. (2.5) Now assume that the field F is not necessarily algebraically closed, and let F be its algebraic closure. An algebra A over F, char F̸ = 3 , is called an Okubo algebra if it is an F-form of P8(F), i.e., A ⊗ F F ∼= P8(F).

Example 2.4. The first Okubo algebras to be constructed [19] were the complex algebra 

P8(C) and its real form eP8(R), the latter being defined as the set of traceless Hermitian matrices: eP8(R) = x ∈ M3(C) | x∗ = x, tr x = 0 . (2.6) The algebra eP8(R) is called the real pseudo-octonion algebra .4The norm on eP8(R) is positive definite [21, p. 47], and hence is anisotropic , i.e., if for some a ∈ eP8(R) we have n( a) = 0 , then a = 0 .

Remark 2.5. As shown in [11, Theorem 6.6], there are exactly two Okubo algebras over R up to isomorphism, namely, eP8(R) and the split Okubo algebra. The norm on 

eP8(R) is anisotropic, so it is a division algebra, whereas the norm on the split Okubo algebra is isotropic, and thus it contains zero divisors, see [11, Lemma 2.1]. If char F = 3 , then a different approach is needed to define an Okubo algebra. It was first defined in [22] by presenting an explicit multiplication table, and in [13] a more general approach is offered, based on the ideas of Petersson’s construction [23]. In any case, it is universally true that any Okubo algebra over an arbitrary field F is a symmetric composition algebra. We will use O to denote an arbitrary Okubo algebra.

# 3 Commuting graph of the pseudo-octonion algebra 

In this section we consider the commuting graph of the pseudo-octonion algebra P8(F)

over a field F, char F̸ = 3 and ω ∈ F. In particular, if the field F is algebraically closed, then, by definition, P8(F) is the unique Okubo algebra over F (see also [6, Theorem 5.1], [8, p. 4, Table 1] and [10, pp. 3–4]). We show that the commuting graph ΓC (P8(F)) is isomorphic to the graph ΓC (M3(F)) .

Remark 3.1. Given any a, b ∈ P8(F), consider the operations 

[a, b ] = ab − ba, [a, b ]∗ = a ∗ b − b ∗ a. 

Then, as shown in [19, (2.11)], [a, b ]∗ = (2 μ − 1) [ a, b ]. It follows that a and b commute with respect to the matrix product if and only if they commute with respect to the “ ∗”multiplication. 

Proposition 3.2. Let char F̸ = 3 and ω ∈ F. The graphs ΓC (P8(F)) and ΓC (M3(F)) are isomorphic. Proof. If a ∈ M3(F), then a−tr( a)/3·I ∈ P8(F). It is well known that the central elements of the matrix algebra Mn(F) are precisely the scalar matrices. Moreover, adding a scalar matrix does not affect the commutativity relation. Therefore, the map 

φ : P(P8(F)/C P8(F)) → P(M3(F)/C M3(F)),

Fa 7 → Fa + CM3(F),

induces an isomorphism of the commuting graphs. Akbari, Mohammadian, Radjavi, and Raja proved [2, Corollary 7] that if the field F

is algebraically closed and n ≥ 3, then the commuting graph ΓC (Mn(F)) is connected, and its diameter equals 4.

Remark 3.3. In [2], the commuting graph is defined so that its vertices are all non-central elements of the algebra A, whereas in our case the vertices correspond to the classes of elements P(A/C A) = {[a] = Fa + CA | a ∈ A \ CA}. Still, the above-mentioned result on the diameter of ΓC (Mn(F)) remains valid. Indeed, 6• each path [v0] ↔ · · · ↔ [vm] in our graph corresponds to the path v0 ↔ · · · ↔ vm

in the graph from [2]; • any shortest path v0 ↔ · · · ↔ vm in the graph from [2] induces a path [v0] ↔· · · ↔ [vm] in our graph. Note that the loops are prohibited in our definition of the commuting graph. Thus, if [vi] = [ vi+1 ], then we “glue together” this pair of adjacent vertices, and the length of the path decreases by 1. However, if m ≥ 3,then all the classes [vi] are pairwise distinct, as otherwise the original path could be shortened. Thus, passing to elements of P(A/C A) as vertices of ΓC (A) does not affect the diameter of the commuting graph if its value is at least 3.

Corollary 3.4. Let F be an algebraically closed field, char F̸ = 3 . Then the commuting graph ΓC (P8(F)) is connected, and its diameter equals 4.Proof. Follows immediately from Proposition 3.2 and [2, Corollary 7]. In the case when the field F is not algebraically closed but contains a primitive cube root of unity ω, the connectivity of ΓC (P8(F)) may depend on F.

Theorem 3.5 ([2, Theorem 19]) . Let F be an arbitrary field, and p ≥ 3 be a prime number. If the graph ΓC (Mp(F)) is connected, then its diameter equals 4.

Corollary 3.6. Let char F̸ = 3 and ω ∈ F. If the graph ΓC (P8(F)) is connected, then its diameter equals 4.Proof. Follows immediately from Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 3.5. We now show that ΓC (P8(F)) can be disconnected. 

Theorem 3.7 ([1, Theorem 6]) . Let F be a field, and n ≥ 3. Then the graph ΓC (Mn(F)) 

is connected if and only if every field extension of F of degree n contains a proper inter-mediate field. 

Corollary 3.8. Let F = Q[ω] be the field obtained by adjoining an element ω to Q. Then the graph ΓC (M3(F)) is disconnected. Consequently, ΓC (P8(F)) is also disconnected. Proof. Let ζ ∈ C be a primitive ninth root of unity. Then 

[Q[ζ] : Q] = φ(9) = 6 ,

where φ is Euler’s totient function. The minimal polynomial of ζ over Q is the ninth cyclotomic polynomial 

Φ9(x) = x6 + x3 + 1 .

Note that ζ3 is a primitive cube root of unity. Without loss of generality we may assume that ω = ζ3. The minimal polynomial of ω over Q is x2 + x + 1 , so 

[Q[ω] : Q] = 2 .

7Since ω ∈ Q[ζ], the field Q[ω] is a subfield of Q[ζ]. Then, by the tower law, 

[Q[ζ] : Q[ω]] = [Q[ζ] : Q][Q[ω] : Q] = 62 = 3 .

Thus Q[ζ] is an extension of Q[ω] of degree 3.Now assume that the extension Q[ζ]/Q[ω] contains an intermediate field E. Then, again by the tower law, 

[Q[ζ] : E] · [E : Q[ω]] = [ Q[ζ] : Q[ω]] = 3 .

Since 3 is a prime number, the field E must coincide either with Q[ζ] or with Q[ω]. There-fore, there are no proper intermediate fields. Applying Theorem 3.7 and Proposition 3.2, we obtain the required result. 

# 4 Path of length 2 between idempotents
