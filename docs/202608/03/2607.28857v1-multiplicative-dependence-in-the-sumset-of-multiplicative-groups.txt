# Multiplicative dependence in the sumset of multiplicative groups 

## Yuri Bilu, Florian Luca August 3, 2026 

Abstract 

Let Γ and ∆ be finitely generated multiplicative groups of algebraic numbers such that Γ ∩ ∆ is a finite group. We show that, up to finitely many exceptions, non-zero sums x1 + y1 and x2 + y2, with x1, x 2 ∈ Γand y1, y 2 ∈ ∆, are multiplicatively dependent only if x1/x 2 = y1/y 2 is a root of unity. For m ≥ 3, we discuss possible shapes of m multiplica-tively dependent sums x1 + y1, . . . , x m + ym with x1, . . . , x m ∈ Γ and 

y1, . . . , y m ∈ ∆. For m = 3 we classify such sums, up to finitely many exceptions, assuming the abc -conjecture.

# 1 Introduction 

Let Γ and ∆ be finitely generated subgroups of ¯Q×. Call them almost disjoint 

if the intersection Γ ∩ ∆ is finite. We are interested in the following question: how often non-zero elements of the sumset Γ + ∆ := {x + y : x ∈ Γ, y ∈ ∆}

can be multiplicatively dependent? To make it more precise, for every pos-itive integer m we want to classify, up to finitely many exceptions, possible 

x1, . . . , x m ∈ Γ and y1, . . . , y m ∈ ∆ such that the m sums x1 + y1, . . . , x m + ym

are all non-zero and multiplicatively dependent. For m = 1 this reduces to the following question: find all x ∈ Γ and y ∈ ∆such that x + y is a root of unity. This is the classical binary unit equation , and the result (essentially, due to Baker) is well-known. 

Theorem 1.1. There exist at most finitely many couples (x, y ) ∈ Γ × ∆ such that x + y is a root of unity, and all of them can be effectively determined. 

Note that in this case we do not need Γ and ∆ to be almost disjoint. We recall the proof in Subsection 3.3, see Theorem 3.6 therein. 

## 1.1 Results 

Already for m = 2 there is an infinite family of multiplicatively dependent cases. Let x ∈ Γ and y ∈ ∆ be such that x + y̸ = 0, and let ξ be a root of unity be-longing to both Γ and ∆. Then the sums x + y and ξx + ξy are multiplicatively dependent. Our first principal result is the following theorem, which confirms that, up to finitely many exceptions, these are the only examples. 

Theorem 1.2. Let Γ and ∆ be almost disjoint finitely generated multiplicative groups of algebraic numbers. Let x1, x 2 ∈ Γ and y1, y 2 ∈ ∆ be such that the sums x1 + y1 and x2 + y2 are non-zero and multiplicatively dependent. Then, with finitely many exceptions, x1/x 2 = y1/y 2 is a root of unity. 

Unlike Theorem 1.1, in Theorem 1.2 the hypothesis of almost disjointness cannot be dropped, as the following example shows. 

Example 1.3. Set Γ := ⟨− 1, 3⟩ and ∆ := ⟨2, 3⟩. Then Γ + ∆ contains all the powers of 3.

## Plan of the paper 

In Section 3 we collect necessary facts about heights, recall some results from Baker’s theory and state the abc -conjecture. In Sections 4 and 5 we obtain auxiliary results used in the proof of Theorem 1.2. In particular, in Section 4 we show that the sumset of two almost disjoint finitely generated multiplicative groups may contain at most finitely many perfect powers (in a given number field). Theorem 1.2 is proved in Section 6. Section 7 is the technical heart of the article. In it we state and proof The-orem 7.3, which classifies (conditionally to abc ) the triples ( x, y, z ) of elements of a multiplicative group Λ such that x − 1, y − 1, z − 1 are multiplicatively dependent modulo Λ. Theorem 1.5 is deduced from Theorem 7.3 in Section 8.

## 3.4 The abc -conjecture 

Let K be a number field. For every finite v ∈ MK we denote by N v the absolute norm of the corresponding prime of K; for an infinite v we set N v := 1.
