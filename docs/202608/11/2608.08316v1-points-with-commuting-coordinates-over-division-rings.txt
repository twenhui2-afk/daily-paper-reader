# Points with Commuting Coordinates over Division Rings 

Adam Chapman 

Department of Computer Science, Academic College of Tel-Aviv-Ya ff o, Rabenu Yeruham St., P.O.B 8401 Ya ff o, 6818211, Israel

Abstract 

We study properties concerting polynomials in D[x1, . . . , xn] and the set Dnc of points over a division ring D with commuting coordinates. Alon and Paran showed that for D = H, this set parametrizes the set of maximal left ideals of D[x1, . . . , xn]. Here we show that any root of f in Dnc is also a root of its (reduced) norm Norm( f ), and that if a point in Dnc is a fixed point of an n-tuple T = ( f1, . . . , fn) of polynomials in n variables, then it is a fixed point of T ◦m for any positive integer m.

Keywords: Division Rings, Octonion Algebras, Noncommutative Polynomials, Polynomial Rings, Noncommutative Algebraic Geometry, Fixed Points, Noncommutative Discrete Algebraic Dynamics

In Section 2 we prove that if f ∈ D[x1, . . . , xn] vanishes at ⃗ a ∈ Dnc then h = g f vanishes at ⃗ a for any g ∈ D[x1, . . . , xn]. We show in Section 3 that the last statement also extends to octonion algebras, generalizing a result from [5] that covers the case of n = 1. Motivated by classical algebraic geometry and generalizations of the nullstel-lensatz to the quaternions in [2, 3, 1], we look at the zero sets of systems of poly-nomials in Section 4. While the classical property that a zero set of a system of polynomials equals the zero set of the ideal it generates fails for general division rings, we prove that this equality holds if we restrict our evaluation to points in Dnc

and consider left ideals. Finally, in Section 5, we apply these algebraic evaluation properties to discrete algebraic dynamics over division algebras, generalizing results from [7] to the mul-tivariate case. 4. Zero sets of polynomials 

In classical algebraic geometry (e.g., see [11, Chapter I, §1]), for a given set of polynomials S ⊆ k[x1, . . . , xn] for an algebraically closed field k, we define 

V(S ) = {P ∈ Ank | f (P) = 0 for all f ∈ S },

5where Ank is the n-dimensional a ffi ne space over the field k. A basic property of this operation is that 

V(S ) = V(I) (1) where I is the ideal generated by S in k[x1, . . . , xn]. We cannot expect property (1) to hold for general division rings. For example this fails for the real quaternions H already for n = 1; indeed, i is a root of x − i but is not a root of ( x − i)( x − j), despite the latter belonging to the (two-sided) ideal generated by the former.
