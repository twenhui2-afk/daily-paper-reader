# Power series for roots of a trinomial and Kummer-like identities for higher order hypergeometric series 

S. R. Mane a

Abstract 

We study the trinomial equation xn + px + q = 0 . Here p and q are both real and nonzero. For n ≥ 3,expressions for the roots have been published as hypergeometric series in powers of the parameter qn−1/p n.For the special case of the cubic ( n = 3 ), we employ Kummer’s identities to derive alternative series solutions in powers of the discriminant D, and also series in powers of 1/D . We next derive new series, in powers of 

D and also in powers of 1/D , for all n ≥ 3. The resulting series suggest identities analogous to Kummer’s identities, for higher order hypergeometric series. 

Keywords: Trinomial, roots of polynomials, power series, hypergeometric series, discriminant

1. Introduction 

We derive series solutions for the roots of the trinomial equation 

xn + px + q = 0 . (1.1) Here p and q are both real and nonzero. The reason for this constraint is simple. If p = 0 then xn + q = 0 

and the roots are the nth roots of −q. If q = 0 then xn + px = 0 and the origin is a root and the remaining roots are the (n − 1) th roots of −p. In principle, our series below (as well as those published in [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]) are also valid for complex nonzero p and q, but branch cuts in the complex plane are required to obtained well-defined expressions. We shall assume p and q are real in this note. We note the following general symmetries of the roots of eq. (1.1). 1. Suppose n is even. If we reverse the sign of p, eq. (1.1) is unchanged if we reverse the sign of x.2. Suppose n is odd. If we reverse the sign of q, eq. (1.1) is unchanged if we reverse the sign of x.Birkeland [1] derived hypergeometric series solutions for the roots of the more general trinomial equation 

xn = gx s + β. For the case n = 3 , Birkeland published the explicit series solutions; see Appendix B. The

6. Comments 

We note the following general observation about series in powers of the discriminant or inverse discrim-inant. Consider Kummer’s identity in eq. (A.1d). It relates F (a, b ; c; z) to F (. . . ; 1 − z). We can interpret 

F (. . . ; 1 − z) as a Taylor series in z expanded around z = 1 . Here z ∝ qn−1/p n (for example) and 1 − z is then proportional to the discriminant D. Consider Kummer’s identity in eq. (A.1a) or eq. (A.1b). It relates 

F (a, b ; c; z) to F (. . . ; z/ (z − 1)) = F (. . . ; 1 + 1 /(z − 1)) . Then 1/(z − 1) ∝ 1/D . One can visualize a series 14 in powers of the discriminant D (and also 1/D ) as a Taylor series in qn−1/p n expanded around a base point which is not the origin. Kummer derived identities for 2F1 hypergeometric functions, some of which are listed in Appendix A.
