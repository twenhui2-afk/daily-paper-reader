Let g be a simple complex Lie algebra and h be a Cartan subalgebra. One of the classical problems about highest weight modules of g is to compute their associated varieties of annihilator ideals. For a highest weight U (g)-module M , let I(M ) = Ann( M ) be its annihilator ideal in U (g), and let J(M ) be the corresponding graded ideal in S(g) = gr( U (g)). The zero set of J(M ) in the dual vector space g∗

of g is known as the annihilator variety of M , also referred to as the associated variety of I(M ). This is usually denoted by V (Ann( M )). Borho–Brylinski [9] demonstrated that the annihilator variety of a highest weight module with an integral infinitesimal character is irreducible, i.e., it is equal to the Zariski closure of a nilpotent orbit in g∗ via the Springer correspondence. Joseph [21] extended this result to the general infinitesimal character case by utilizing the truncated induction ( j-induction) for representations of Weyl groups. The study of annihilator varieties of highest weight module is closely related to many research fields. Duflo [12] proved that a primitive ideal (the annihilator ideal of an irreducible representation of g) of U (g)can be realized as the annihilator ideal of a highest weight U (g)-module. Barbasch–Vogan [8] established a one-to-one correspondence among the two-sided cells of the Weyl group W , special representations of W , and the special nilpotent orbits of g∗; the closures of the corresponding special nilpotent orbits

Joseph [18] found that the dimension of some irreducible module of a finite W -algebra is equal to the Goldie rank polynomial of a highest weight U (g)-module; these polynomials were used earlier by Joseph [21] to give a basis for the special representations of an integral Weyl group. Further examples and applications can be found in [9, 7, 15, 16, 13, 23]. The computation of this invariant is generally very complicated. For the trivial infinitesimal character this has been done in type A by Joseph [20], in types B and C by Garfinkle [14], and in type D by McGovern and Pietraho [24]. Furthermore, Bai–Ma–Wang [3] gave a simple description of the nilpotent orbit associated with the annihilator variety of a highest weight module for classical Lie algebras by using the Robinson–Schensted insertion algorithm utilized in [5, 4]. For exceptional cases, Bai–Gao–Wang–Xie [2] found that the annihilator variety of a highest weight module can be described by using Sommers duality [28]. In this paper, we present a new proof of the partition algorithm found by Bai–Ma–Wang [3] by using Sommers duality.
