> n, C n) from the per-spective of deformation theory. First, we provide a zeros-and-residues realization of this algebra, extending the construction of Ginzburg, Kapranov, and Vasserot to the non-reduced affine root system setting. 1. Introduction 

Affine Hecke algebras were introduced by Iwahori and Matsumoto as deformations of the group algebras of affine Weyl groups. There is a Hecke algebra attached to each finite root system, and its parameters are indexed by orbits of the finite Weyl group on the root system. Their structure and representation theory were developed extensively by Lusztig[Lus85]. Cherednik subsequently introduced double affine Hecke algebras (DAHAs), which may be viewed as the Hecke algebras attached to affine root systems. Their parameters are indexed by orbits of the affine Weyl group on the affine root system. Cherednik used DAHAs to prove the Macdonald conjectures for reduced root systems. Sahi later extended the construction to the non-reduced affine root system of type (C∨ 

> n

, C n). This DAHA has five parameters for n = 1 and six parameters for n > 1, and it governs the Koornwinder polynomials, which are the most general family of Macdonald polynomials. Sahi used it to prove the remaining Macdonald conjectures in this setting [Sah99]. In this paper, we study the DAHA of type ( C∨ 

> n

, C n) from the perspective of deformation theory to establish two new results about it. First, in Theorems 2.3.3 and 2.4.3, we give a zeros-and-residues construction of the DAHA of type ( C∨ 

> n

, C n), extending the constructions of [GKV97] and [BEG00] from reduced to non-reduced affine root systems. These realizations describe the DAHA as a subalgebra of rational q-difference-reflection operators cut out by explicit vanishing and residue conditions. Such realizations of algebras have proved to be quite versatile. For example, in [GKV97], this result was used to con-struct degenerate and elliptic analogues of affine and double affine Hecke algebras. More recently, analogous descriptions have appeared for Coulomb branches [SS26, Kly] and, more generally, con-volution algebras [Cri26]. Our result places the non-reduced DAHA in the same framework. In view of the known rank-one case [Yos25], it also suggests a higher-rank Coulomb-branch realiza-tion of this DAHA: it would suffice to identify the corresponding Coulomb branch with the same zeros-and-residues subalgebra. Second, in Theorem 3.2.1, we show that the completed DAHA of type ( C∨ 

> n

, C n) is the formal universal deformation of the crossed product of a quantum torus algebra with the finite Weyl group of type Cn. Previously, Oblomkov proved the result for n = 1 [Obl04]. Etingof and Oblomkov subsequently computed the Hochschild cohomology of the crossed product algebra for arbitrary n

[EO06]. In particular, their computation shows the number of parameters of the DAHA equals the

The same construction thus applies more generally to DAHAs admitting a PBW basis compatible with specialization. The main difficulty, however, is showing that the resulting cocycles span the entire second Hochschild cohomology.
