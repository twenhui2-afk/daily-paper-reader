Abstract 

The largest matching root μ(G) of a graph G is that of its matching polynomial. In this paper, all limit points of the largest matching roots of graphs are determined. More precisely, we identify the limit points of the largest matching roots of graphs less than τ 12 + τ − 12 . For any γ ≥ τ 12 + τ − 12 with τ = √5+1 2 , there exists a graph sequence {Gi | i ∈ N} such that 

lim  

> i→∞

μ(Gi) = γ.

AMS classification: 05C31, 05C70, 05C50 

Keywords : Matching polynomial; Largest matching root; Limit point; Adjacency matrix. 

# 1 Introduction 

In this paper, all graphs considered are simple and undirected. Let G = ( V (G), E (G)) be a graph with order n = |V (G)| and size m = |E(G)|. An edge subset M ⊆ E(G) is called a matching of 

G if no two distinct edges in it share a common vertex, and let M(G) be the set of matchings of G. As a celebrated graph polynomial, the matching polynomial of G is defined as 

M (G, x ) = X

In early period, the matching polynomial was investigated as a mathematical model in statistical physics [12, 17, 22] and theoretical chemistry [1, 15, 16] as well as appeared in the first mathe-matical literature [7]. Since then, the study of matching polynomials has garnered considerable attention. For its combinatorial properties, one of amazing results is that infinitely many bipar-tite Ramanujan graphs were constructed via matching polynomial [24]. For more results, see the monographs [9, 26] and some old and new papers [2, 4, 5, 10, 13, 14, 23, 27, e.g.]. The matching roots of a graph G are those of M (G, x ). It has been proved that all the matching roots of G are real and lie in the interval (−2√∆ − 1, 2√∆ − 1) with ∆ = ∆( G) being the maximum degree of G [17]. Since the roots of graph polynomials are closely related to the structures of graphs, of particular interest here is the largest matching root of G, denoted by 

μ(G). Let A(G) = ( aij ) be the adjacency matrix of G with aij = 1 if vivj ∈ E(G) and othewise 

aij = 0 . The characteristic polynomial of G is denoted by ϕ(G, λ ) = det( λI − A(G)) . As usual, the spectral radius ρA(G) of G is the largest absolute value of all roots of ϕ(G, λ ). It is well-known

In this paper, we determine all limit points of the largest matching roots of graphs. Note that in Sections 2 we have shown that the graph sequence {Gi | i ∈ N} is composed of trees. To obtain the limit points of adjacency spectral radii of graphs less than τ 12 + τ − 12 , Hoffman [18] utilized the same tree sequence as the one considered in this paper. Due to (1), we can get the limit points of the largest matching roots of graphs less than τ 12 + τ − 12 from Hoffman’s result. For the completeness of this paper, we provide a detailed proof in Sections 3 and 4; moreover, the newly discovered properties enrich the theory of matching polynomials.
