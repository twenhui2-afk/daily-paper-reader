Background 

The lateral ventricle choroid plexus (LVCP) is gaining recogni Ɵon as a key imaging biomarker 

for mul Ɵple sclerosis (MS) related to physical disability and neuroinflamma Ɵon. Yet, manual 

segmenta Ɵon of the LVCP is highly tedious, restric Ɵng its use in b road clinical trials and 

longitudinal assessments. 

Purpose 

This research aims to develop and test a SwinUNETR-driven pipeline that leverages targeted 

intra- and peri-ventricular small patch sampling to automa Ɵcally segment the LVCP in MS 

pa Ɵents from both standalone and mul Ɵ-modal MRI inputs. 

Methods 

We retrospec Ɵvely assessed 3T MRI scans across three sets of data stemming from two 

separate MS-dominant cohorts (Dataset 1: n=177; Dataset 2: n=177; expanded test set: 

n=388). Our method employed a SwinUNETR architecture trained on 32×32×32 voxel patches, 

benchmarking it against the 3D UXNET model. The primary metric for evalua Ɵon was the Dice 

Similarity Coefficient (DSC), supplemented by computa Ɵonal demand (GFLOPs) and the 95th 

percen Ɵle Hausdorff Distance (HD95). 

Results 

On the extended test set of 388 pa Ɵents, the SwinUNETR model secured a mean DSC of 0.868 

(95% CI: 0.863-0.872) with MPRAGE and FLAIR combined, showing a sta ƟsƟcally significant 

gain over UXNET (DSC: 0.858 [95% CI: 0.853-0.862], p<0.0001). When restricted to standalone 

FLAIR inputs, the transformer-based approach sustained a high DSC of 0.863 (95% CI: 0.858-

0.867), while the spa Ɵal localiza Ɵon of UXNET worsened considerably (HD95: 1.86 vs. 3.00 

mm). Importantly, the proposed framework lowered computa Ɵonal load by 99% (91.8 vs. 

22,080 GFLOPs) and operated with significantly fewer parameters (4.08M vs. 53.02M). 

Conclusion 

By integra Ɵng localized patch sampling with a SwinUNETR architecture, this methodology 

offers an accurate, robust, and sta ƟsƟcally superior alterna Ɵve to current leading models for 

LVCP segmenta Ɵon. Its vast reduc Ɵon in computa Ɵonal cost makes it ideal for widespread 

implementa Ɵon in clinical and resear ch environments. Introduc Ɵon 

The choroid plexus (CP), a highly vascularized network situated in the ventricular system, plays a crucial 

role in cerebrospinal fluid genera Ɵon and acts as a gateway for immune cells entering the central 

nervous system (1,2). Emerging MRI research highlights no Ɵceable CP expansion in individuals with 

mul Ɵple sclerosis (MS), as well as Parkinson's and Alzheimer's diseases (2–4). Specifically in MS, 

histopathological studies reveal inflammatory cells within the CP (5) and the volume of the lateral 

ventricle CP (LVCP) is linked to diffuse neurodegenera Ɵon and both physical and cogni Ɵve impairment 

(6–10), making it a promising imaging biomarker. Nevertheless, CP structure naturally shi Ōs with age, 

beginning around the third decade of life (11). Because manual segmenta Ɵon of the LVCP is highly 

Ɵme -consuming, evalua Ɵng CP metrics in extensive prospec Ɵve trials and longitudinal studies remains 

a major challenge. 

Various deep learning frameworks have been developed for LVCP segmenta Ɵon . ASCHOPLEX (12) is an 

U-Net-based (13) ensemble, achieving a mean test Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) of 0.80 on 

Magne Ɵza Ɵon Prepared RApid Gradient Echo (MPRAGE) images from healthy and MS cohorts, much 

be Ʃer than FreeSurfer (14). Another UNETR-based approach, uƟlizing intra - and peri-ventricular patch 

sampling, outperformed ASCHOPLEX (test DSC: 0.77 vs 0.70) in MS cohorts and demonstrated 

improved performance with mul Ɵ-contrast integra Ɵon (e.g., MPRAGE/FLAIR) (DSC: 0.84) compared 

with single-modality MPRAGE (15). While these models u Ɵlize standard transformer backbones ,

SwinUNETR has demonstrated superior performance in brain tumor segmenta Ɵon by leveraging mul Ɵ-

scale feature extrac Ɵon (16). Recently, the convolu Ɵon -based UXNET (17) reported a test DSC of 0.875 

on two-Ɵmepoint MPRAGE images from people with MS (18); however, its valida Ɵon was limited to a 

rela Ɵvely young cohort (31.1±9.4 years). Addi Ɵonally , UXNET’s reliance on large patch sizes imposes 

substan Ɵal GPU memory overhead, poten Ɵally limi Ɵng its clinical deployment. Beyond MS, U-Net-

based ChpSeg (19), was trained on single-modality T1-weighted, T2-weighted, or FLAIR images from 

Parkinson’s and Alzheimer’s cohorts (DSC: 0.74). 

To address these challenges, this work introduces an automated LVCP segmenta Ɵon pipeline that 

combines a SwinUNETR backbone with a targeted intra- and peri-ventricular small patch sampling 

strategy. We trained these models from scratch leveraging two separate MS-predominant cohorts 

(which included related demyelina Ɵng and neuroinflammatory condi Ɵons): one u Ɵlizing FLAIR and 

MPRAGE, and the second u Ɵlizing FLAIR and MP2RAGE. We assessed the framework's efficacy across 

both mul Ɵ-modal and single-modality inputs, benchmarking the results against the UXNET 

architecture. Finally, the generalizability and robustness of the methodology were comprehensively 

validated using a large, independent extended test set. 

Materials and Methods 

Par Ɵcipants and Imaging Protocol 

The study retrospec Ɵvely included 1-mm isotropic 3T 3D MRI from two prospec Ɵve MS-predominant

provided wri Ʃen consent prior to the studies. Dataset sizes derived from the two cohorts were 

matched. Dataset 1 included 177 par Ɵcipants with FLAIR and MPRAGE images randomly sourced from Cohort 1. 

The inclusion criteria were: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) ability to provide informed consent; (3) MS diagnosis 

according to the 2017 McDonald Criteria, with no psychiatric or neurologic comorbidi Ɵes, 

contraindica Ɵons for MRI, and pregnancy ; (4) MRI acquired at the University Hospital Basel; (5) not 

being in Cohort 2. The included par Ɵcipants were 150 relapsing r emi ƫng MS , 14 secondary

syndrome, and 1 n euromyeli Ɵs opƟca spectrum disorder (age: 48.8±11.8; 124 females; median[IQR]

Dataset 2 consisted of 177 par Ɵcipants from Cohort 2 (age: 46.6±14.0; 106 females; median[IQR] 

EDSS:3.0[2.0;4.5]; 98 relapsing remi ƫng MS , 58 secondary progressive MS, and 21 primary progressive 

MS) undergoing an MRI protocol including FLAIR and MP2RAGE. Par Ɵcipants met the same inclusion 

criteria (1-4) as Dataset 1. 

To evaluate model robustness on a larger scale, an independent subset of 388 par Ɵcipants from Cohort 

1 was u Ɵlized as an extended test dataset, following the same inclusion criteria of Dataset 1 (age: 

44.1±12.7; 252 females; median[IQR] EDSS: 2.0[1.5;3.5]; 338 relapsing remi ƫng MS , 18 secondary

Detailed MRI acquisi Ɵon parameters are given in the Supplementary Material. 

Image Preprocessing 

The Cohort 1 image processing pipeline included skull-stripping through HD-BET (20), rigid co-

registra Ɵon of FLAIR and MPRAGE images using ANTs (21) , and segmenta Ɵon using SAMSEG (22). Skull-

stripped, registered FLAIR and MPRAGE images, as well as ventricle mask, were obtained. 

For Cohort 2, HD-BET skull-stripping, rigid co-registra Ɵon of FLAIR and MP2RAGE images using FSL (23) 

and SAMSEG brain segmenta Ɵon were used. 

Dataset Prepara Ɵon 

Dataset 1 (n=177) was randomly divided into a held-out test dataset (n=36) and a development dataset 

(n=141) for five-fold cross-valida Ɵon. Dataset 2 followed the iden Ɵcal protocol. 

Choroid Plexus Manual Segmenta Ɵon 

For Datasets 1 and 2, i ni Ɵal CP mask s were created using SAMSEG. Two raters manually corrected 10 

CP masks on FLAIR/MPRAGE images using ITK-Snap (24), achieving high inter-rater agreement (DSC: 

0.877), and corrected the rest of masks accordingly and independently. 

To facilitate manual correc Ɵon for the extended test dataset, a UNETR model was trained on Dataset 

1 following the approach described in (15). This model achieved a DSC>0.8. The resul Ɵng masks were 

subsequently manually corrected by the raters following the same criteria applied to Dataset 1. 

Model Training 

For the SwinUNETR, t he number of a Ʃen Ɵon heads was set to (3,6,12,24), with an ini Ɵal embedding 

dimension of 12. Instance normaliza Ɵon was omi Ʃed from the final encoder stage to accommodate a 

minimal input patch size of 32×32×32 voxels. At this stage of the original design, the dimension of the hidden feature representa Ɵon wa s 1x1x1, resul Ɵng in a zero -variance denominator for instance 

normaliza Ɵon. 

Voxel intensi Ɵes were normalized within the brain using z-scores. Intra- and peri-ventricular patches 

were sampled within the bounding box of the ventricle zero-padded by 8 voxels in all direc Ɵons .

Stochas Ɵc data augmenta Ɵon included cropping, flipping, intensity shi Ō of 0.1 , and affine 

transforma Ɵon s with up to 5% scaling and up to 12° rota Ɵon. The loss func Ɵon was a combina Ɵon of 

the Focal loss (25) and the Dice loss. The model was op Ɵmized by AdamW (26) with DSC as the 

evalua Ɵon metric .

The code was implemented with Python (3.10; pytorch v2.0) and R (4.5.1). 

Sta ƟsƟcal Analysis 

Segmenta Ɵon performance was evaluated using the DSC as the primary outcome measure. To provide 

a comprehensive morphological assessment, the 95th percen Ɵle Hausdorff Distance (HD95), precision, 

recall, and volume similarity (27) were calculated as secondary measures. Compara Ɵve analyses 

evaluated the impact of single-modality versus mul Ɵ-modal MRI input (MPRAGE, FLAIR, and 

MPRAGE+FLAIR), patch size selec Ɵon , and bias-field correc Ɵon. In addi Ɵon, the impact of different 

ventricle masks on the inference was also examined (SAMSEG vs FastSurfer (28)). 

UXNET was also trained across iden Ɵcal contrast combina Ɵons of full-brain images. Notably, the 

minimal patch size acceptable for UXNET was 96x96x96 voxels. C omputa Ɵonal efficiency was 

quan Ɵfied through total giga floa Ɵng -point opera Ɵon counts (GFLOPs). S ta ƟsƟcal significance for 

performance differences in the primary measure across the models and MR contrasts, was assessed 

using a two-tailed Wilcoxon signed-rank test. The 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for both absolute 

measures and paired performance differences were computed using 10000-itera Ɵon bootstrapping.

Independent and Extended Test Set Valida Ɵon 

SwinUNETR (MPRAGE+FLAIR) maintained the highest mean DSC of 0.877 (95% CI: 0.863- 0.888) on the 

held-out test set and 0.868 (95% CI: 0.863-0.872) on the extended test dataset (Table 1). Based on the

UXNET deteriorated from 1.77±1.73 mm to 3.0±6.93 mm when only FLAIR was used as the input.

Discussion 

This study demonstrates that a SwinUNETR-based framework with localized small-patch sampling 

provides a highly efficient and robust solu Ɵon for automated LV CP segmenta Ɵon in people with MS 

and related neuroinflammatory and demyelina Ɵng disorders . In addi Ɵon, our findings indicate that 

mul Ɵmodal MRI inputs are beneficial for achieving high, stable segmenta Ɵon performance for both 

transformer-based and convolu Ɵon -based neural networks. 

Interes Ɵngly, SwinUNETR exhibited a minor performance decline when FLAIR was used as the sole 

input modality. This robustness supports its use in prac Ɵcal scenarios where only FLAIR is available. In 

contrast, UXNET’s spa Ɵal localiza Ɵon accuracy, as measured by HD95, greatly deteriorated under this 

single-modality configura Ɵon. This suggests that the transformer’s global self-aƩen Ɵon mechanism 

combined with small patches may be be Ʃer suited for capturing LVCP morphology. 

Our results also demonstrate that specialized sampling strategies can help bridge the gap between 

model complexity and clinical feasibility. By u Ɵlizing intra - and peri-ventricular patch sampling, we 

focused the model's learning capacity on anatomically relevant regions. This allowed for the use of 

smaller 32x32x32 voxel patches, which, in combina Ɵon with the SwinUNETR architecture, resulted in 

a framework with over 99% fewer GFLOPs than the convolu Ɵon -based UXNET did . This reduc Ɵon in 

computa Ɵonal overhead is essen Ɵal for deploying such tools in standard clinical worksta Ɵons , where 

GPU memory may be limited. 

The minimal performance difference observed with bias-field correc Ɵon for preprocessing further 

supports the robustness of the proposed framework. Moreover, the finding that SwinUNETR inference 

performance was independent of the ventricle mask source suggests no need for re-training when 

alterna Ɵve ventricle segmenta Ɵon methods are used, thereby enhancing the generalizability of the 

approach.

Conclusion 

To summarize, this work presents and validates a highly efficient pipeline based on SwinUNETR for the 

automated segmenta Ɵon of the LVCP. The results establish that combining T1 -weighted and FLAIR MRI 

provides op Ɵmal accuracy, yet relying solely on FLAIR imaging is s Ɵll a highly viable op Ɵon. By 

dras Ɵcally cu ƫng down computa Ɵonal overhead while outperforming state -of-the-art techniques on 

a large-scale test set, the proposed method enables extensive volumetric analysis of the LVCP as a 

poten Ɵal biomarker for neurodegenera Ɵon and neuroinflamma Ɵon in clinical se ƫngs .
