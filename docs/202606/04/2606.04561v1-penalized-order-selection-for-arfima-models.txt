# Penalized Order Selection for ARFIMA Models 

Chunhao Cai 

School of Mathematics (Zhuhai), Sun Yat-Sen University 

caichh9@mail.sysu.edu.cn 

Abstract 

We study order selection for a finite-order stationary ARFIMA process by minimizing a nested folded-concave penalization of the integrated Toeplitz profile Whittle criterion. The AR and MA orders are encoded through suffix groups, so that exact recovery of the zero tails is equivalent to exact recovery of the orders. The analysis is carried out on a weighted sieve neighborhood whose geometric weights keep growing-lag perturbations from pushing AR or MA roots toward the unit circle. Under finite true orders, coprime root-separated short-memory polynomials, an interior stationary memory parameter, growing sieve orders, suitable tuning, and independent sub-Gaussian innovations, we prove existence of an oracle local minimizer, rather than global optimality of the nonconvex criterion. This local minimizer selects the true AR and MA orders with probability tending to one, sets all inactive coefficients exactly to zero, and achieves the active-coordinate rate Op(Lnn−1/2), where the stochastic scale Ln is governed by the local mismatch ∆. The proof combines weighted root-separation, matched finite-section Toeplitz control, finite-section trace-bias control, a sub-Gaussian quadratic-form argument for the stationary observed vector, and a local domination step for folded-concave penalties. 

Keywords. ARFIMA model; long memory; order selection; folded-concave penalty; integrated Whittle criterion; growing sieve.

# 1 Introduction 

1.1 Background and related literature 

The ARFIMA model, introduced by Granger and Joyeux [10] and Hosking [12], provides a parsimonious parametric description of time series with fractional memory. It has become a standard model class for slowly decaying dependence, and the long-memory literature now spans both probabilistic foundations and statistical methodology; see Beran [1] for a classical entry point. For parametric inference, the main likelihood-based routes include Gaussian or Whittle-type frequency-domain estimation [9, 5] and exact or pseudo-maximum-likelihood procedures [16, 13]. In all of these approaches, the practical difficulty is not only estimation of the memory parameter d, but also separation of the long-memory component from the short-memory AR and MA dynamics. This separation is delicate because a misspecified short-memory order can contaminate the estimate of d, while an incorrect memory parameter can in turn distort the fitted AR and MA polynomials. Early model-selection work for long-memory models therefore relied mostly on information criteria combined with repeated refitting over candidate orders; see, for example, Bisaglia [2]. More recently, Huang, Chan, Chen, and Ing [14] established consistency of a BIC-type selector for ARFIMA models. Their procedure minimizes (bpn, bqn) = argmin

1.2 Why a penalized local theorem 

Our objective is different. We want a one-step penalized order-selection theorem in which the AR and MA orders are recovered from a single criterion on a growing sieve, rather than by repeated optimization over a rectangular family of candidate models. From this viewpoint, the discrete-search route is less satisfactory for the present problem for three reasons. First, it treats order selection as 

{(p, q ) : 0 ≤ p ≤ P, 0 ≤ q ≤ Q}-search ,

whereas our target is a single penalized optimization over the embedded sieve parameter 

η = ( α1, . . . , α Pn , β 1, . . . , β Qn , d ).

Second, its proof must analyze overfitted candidate manifolds model by model, while our method imposes the canonical zero-tail representation directly through nested suffix penalties. Third, the relevant objective in the present paper is not conditional sum of squares but the integrated Toeplitz profile Whittle criterion, which is better aligned with the Toeplitz-operator calculations used below. The individual ingredients of this construction have important antecedents. Folded-concave penalization and its oracle behavior go back to SCAD and related nonconcave likelihood methods [6], with MCP developed in [19]. Group-level sparsity is represented by the group lasso of Yuan and Lin [18], while overlapping and hierarchical group structures are formalized, for example, by the CAP family of Zhao, Rocha, and Yu [20]. Closely related nested lag penalties have also been used for interpretable high-dimensional VAR lag selection [15]. Thus the novelty claimed here is not the invention of nested or folded-concave penalties in isolation. Rather, it is the coupling of nested suffix folded-concave penalties with the integrated Toeplitz profile Whittle criterion for simultaneous AR and MA order recovery in finite-order ARFIMA models, together with a weighted root-separated growing-sieve local oracle analysis.
