# 1 Introduction 

Vaught’s conjecture, stated by Robert Vaught [15], says that the number I(T , ω ) of non-isomorphic countable models of a complete countable first-order theory T behaves as the cardinality of “nicely definable” (closed, Borel, analytic) subsets of Polish spaces: I(T , ω ) ≤ ω or I(T , ω ) = c. The class of partial orders has a significant role in this context, because by a result of Arnold Miller (see [14]) the conjecture follows from its restriction to that class. Regarding the class of partial orders we recall that Vaught’s conjecture was confirmed for the theories of linear orders (Matatyahu Rubin [12]), model-theoretic trees (John Steel [14]), reticles (James Schmerl [13]), Boolean algebras (Paul Iverson [5]). Following the concept exploited in [6, 7, 8, 9, 10], we deal with the question whether Vaught’s conjecture holds for (the theories of) the structures from ⟨C⟩ , if it holds for the structures from C, where 

C is a class of structures of a countable language and ⟨C⟩ is a closure of C obtained in some reasonable way. For example, Rubin in [12] confirmed the “sharp” version of Vaught’s conjecture, VC ♯ (saying that 

I(T , ω ) ∈ { 1, c}), for linear orders with finitely many unary predicates, while by [8] VC ♯ holds for all relational structures definable by quantifier free formulas in labeled linear orders (i.e. for all structures from the closure ⟨C lo lab ⟩def , called FMD structures 2). In addition, by [9] VC ♯ holds for all partial orders from the closure of the class of linear orders Clo under finite direct products and disjoint unions, ⟨C lo ⟩ ˙∪Π.In [9] VC ♯ was confirmed for the partial orders from the class ⟨C⟩ ˙∪Π, where C is either the class of rooted FMD trees or the class of initially finite rooted trees satisfying VC ♯. The key assumption providing these results was that deleting the root from a tree we obtain a tree having finitely many connected components, which is a strong restriction leaving a large part of the class Crt of rooted trees unexplored (e.g. one can ask what is going on with the product ω<ω × ω<ω ).
