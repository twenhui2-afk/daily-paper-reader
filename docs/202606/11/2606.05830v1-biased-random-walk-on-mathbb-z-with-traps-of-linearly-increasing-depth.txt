# 1 Introduction 

## 1.1 Background and motivation 

For any transient random walk on Z with stationary independent increments, the law of large numbers forces a positive speed. However, when the walk is spatially inhomogeneous or placed in a random environment, new phenomena arise: it may be transient yet have zero speed. Such walks are commonly said to be sub-ballistic. For random walks in random environments, Solomon [19] showed that sub-ballistic behavior does occur. For further studies of sub-ballistic random walks in random environments, we refer to Kesten, Kozlov and Spitzer [11], Sznitman [20], Zeitouni [23] and references therein. In the spatially inhomogeneous setting, Harris [8] initiated the study of near-critical random walks on Z, providing recurrence /transience criteria. Later, Lamperti [12, 13] extended the analysis to more general near-critical processes (not necessarily Markov chains), giving similar criteria as well as a central limit theorem. Problems concerning processes with asymptotically zero drift are often referred to as Lamperti’s problem; see Denisov, Korshunov and Wachtel [3], and Menshikov, Popov and Wade [17]. For nearest-neighbor spatially inhomogeneous random walks on Z+ where the drift at i decays as ciα with α ∈ (0 , 1) and c > 0, Voit [21] proved that the walk is sub-ballistic and grows like c(1 + α)n1/(1 +α). Additional examples of birth–death chains with zero speed can be found in Cs´ aki, F¨ oldes and R´ ev´ esz [2] and James, Lyons and Peres [9] et al. Finally, we recall that symmetric simple random walks on Z or Z2 are recurrent, whereas on Zd (d ≥ 3) they are transient but sub-ballistic (see, e.g., Durrett [5, Chap. 5]). In this paper, we study a spatially homogeneous λ-biased random walk on Z+, where each vertex k ≥ 0 is equipped with a trap of depth k. Although the transition mechanism is ho-mogeneous, the walk spends substantial time exploring traps, leading to asymptotic behavior markedly di ff erent from that of simple random walks. We show that in the transient regime (λ < 1), the walk is sub-ballistic and its position grows only logarithmically in time. A concept intimately related to the speed is that of cutpoints and cut times, which reflect the spatial and temporal regeneration of the walk, respectively. 

Definition 1. Let ( Xn)n≥0 be a Markov chain on a countable state space. For n ≥ 1, if 

{X0, . . . , Xn−1} ∩ { Xn, Xn+1, . . . } = ∅,

then n is called a cut time and Xn is called a cutpoint of the chain. These notions have been extensively studied. Consider simple random walk on Zd and let Rn

be the number of cut times before time n. For d ≥ 5, Erd˝ os and Taylor [6] proved that Rn ∼ cdn

a.s., while for d = 3, 4, Lawler [14, 15] showed that log Rn ∼ cd log n almost surely, where cd

are positive constants. James and Peres [10] proved that every transient random walk with bounded step size on an integer lattice has infinitely many cutpoints almost surely. They further showed that for any Markov chain that has infinitely many cutpoints almost surely, the eventual occupation numbers generate the exchangeable σ-field, partially resolving a conjecture of Diaconis and Freedman [4]. However, James, Lyons and Peres [9] gave an example of a transient birth–death chain that has only finitely many cutpoints. Cs´ aki, F¨ oldes and R´ ev´ esz [2] provided a criterion for 2determining whether the number of cutpoints (or strong cutpoints) is finite or infinite. Later, Benjamini, Gurel-Gurevich and Schramm [1] proved that every transient Markov chain has infinite expected number of cutpoints, but examples of chains with only finitely many cutpoints show that the expectation can be infinite while the actual number is finite. Recently, Halberstam and Hutchcroft [7] gave a condition in terms of Green’s function decay that guarantees infinitely many cut times for general Markov chains. For more general adapted processes with bounded increments, Lo, Menshikov and Wade [16] gave conditions to determine whether the number of cutpoints is finite or not, and further provided lower bounds for the expected number of cutpoints in [0 , n] for su ffi ciently large n. For a spatially inhomogeneous nearest-neighbor random walk on Z+, Wang and Wang [22] showed that if the local drift at n decays like Υ 

> 2n

with a constant Υ > 1, then the number of cutpoints in [0 , n], normalized by log n, converges in distribution to a Γ(Υ − 1, 1) random variable. In our λ-biased random walk on Z+ equipped with traps, the walk spends most of its time wandering inside the traps, so its advance along Z+ is extremely slow. Indeed, we show that the distance from the root grows as log n. For cut times and cutpoints, our model exhibits novel limit behaviors. We prove that the number of cut times up to time N grows only logarithmically, whereas the number of cutpoints among {0, 1, . . . , n} grows linearly with n. This reflects the fact that the walk rarely experiences genuine regeneration events because most of its time is consumed in the traps.
