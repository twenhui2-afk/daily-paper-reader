PERFECT MATCHINGS, FANO PLANES, AND ORTHOGONAL BASES OF TYPE E8

R.M. GREEN AND TIANYUAN XU 

Abstract. We use perfect matchings and labelled Fano planes to construct and study the 2025 orthogonal bases of positive roots in the E8 root system. The set of these bases forms a highly structured, Bruhat-like graded poset (Ω , ≤Q) whose rank function can be computed from the car-dinalities of so-called generalized Rothe diagrams. We give combinatorial characterizations of these diagrams in terms of matchings and Fano planes, and we explain how to compute the ranks of the elements of Ω using suitable combinatorial statistics such as the weights of perfect matchings. We establish simple formulas for the rank generating functions of Ω and of its 50 congruence classes under a natural order congruence relation. Our derivation of the generating functions contains some intermediate results on general perfect matchings and labelled Fano planes that can be stated without mentioning root systems and may be of independent interest. 

1. Introduction 

The purpose of this paper is to use the combinatorics of perfect matchings and Fano planes to gain insight into certain combinatorial structures associated with the E8 root system Φ = Φ( E8). Recall that the E8 lattice gives the densest possible packing of spheres in 8-dimensional Euclidean space [16], and that the E8 root system consists of the 240 elements of the lattice with minimal nonzero norm. The roots can be partitioned into 120 positive and 120 negative roots by choosing a vector space total order on the ambient vector space V . There are 2025 orthogonal bases for V that consist entirely of positive roots, and the set Ω of all such bases appears in the physics literature, where it has been used to give parity proofs of the Kochen–Specker theorem in quantum mechanics [18]. The set Ω is naturally a W -set for the Weyl group W = W (E8) associated to Φ (Section 2.2). In previous work [5], we showed that Ω can be identified with a natural spanning set of a certain Macdonald representation of W [5, Section 1]. We defined a certain function λ : Ω → Z that equips Ω with the structure of a quasiparabolic W -set, which means that Ω forms a special type of 

W -set defined by Rains–Vazirani [10] that carries a graded, Bruhat-like partial order whose rank function is given by λ. The function λ is called the level function in the context of quasiparabolic sets, and it can be used to deform the W -action on Ω in a suitable way to create a module for the Iwahori–Hecke algebra associated to W [10, Theorem 7.1]. As we will explain in Section 2.3, the 50 equivalence classes form a distributive lattice that can be understood using the natural partial order on the root lattice, or the left weak Bruhat order on the Weyl group W of Φ, or the heap poset of a fully commutative element in W , or a certain set S of length-4 integer sequences with small positive entries (Equation (2.9)). In addition, the 50 equivalence classes of ∼σ are highly compatible with the matching–Fano dichotomy, with ΩM being the union of 42 of the equivalence classes and Ω F the other 8. The dichotomy also propagates in natural ways to the contexts of the aforementioned weak Bruhat order, heap poset, and integer sequences, in ways that we will detail in Section 5.1. The 42 equivalence classes that comprise Ω M are in bijection with the nonnesting perfect matchings of the set 10 , and the details of the related Catalan combinatorics also play an important role in the arguments of Section 5.1. For each sequence d ∈ S , we will prove that the level generating function of the corresponding equivalence class in Ω can be expressed in a remarkably simple formula in terms of d (Theorem 2.15). Remark 6.1 . The results of this paper have direct analogues for the root system Φ( E7) of type E7

and the collection Ω ′ of the positive orthogonal bases of type E7, i.e., the collection of size-7 subsets of Φ( E7) that consist of mutually orthogonal positive roots. The set Ω ′ is a quasiparabolic W (E7)-set of size 135 by the results of [5, 6], and we can mimic the methods of this paper to decompose Ω ′ as the disjoint union of two subsets Ω ′ 

> M

and Ω ′ 

> F

, where Ω ′ 

> M

contains 105 orthogonal bases modelled by the perfect matchings of the set {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7} and Ω ′ 

> F

contains 30 orthogonal bases modelled by the 30 inequivalent labellings of the Fano plane using labels from the set {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7}. The map σ has 15 fibres, with Ω ′ 

> F

forming a single fibre and Ω ′ 

> M

being the union of 14 fibres that correspond naturally to the 14 Dyck paths of semilength 4. More generally, we can uniformly index all the 15 fibres of σ by 15 suitable length-3 sequences obtained via a chain decomposition of the heap of a fully commutative element, namely, the so-called “nonnesting element” of type E7 from [5, Section 6.2]. This indexing scheme and the decomposition Ω ′ = Ω ′ 

> M

∪ Ω′ 

> F

are new, and they can be used to prove that P S Ω′ (q) = [3] q[5] q[9] q.

Remark 6.2 . As mentioned in the introduction, there are many interesting examples of quasi-parabolic sets that can be constructed from the orthogonal sets of roots of type En, including certain configurations of curves in del Pezzo surfaces of degrees 2 and 3. The poset (Ω , ≤) is the largest of these exceptional configurations, and the results of this paper can be used to gain insight into these smaller examples by restriction. For example, instead of mimicking the methods of this paper to prove the facts about the set Ω 7 in Remark 6.1 from scratch, one may also deduce them from the results of this paper by identifying Φ( E7) as the set of roots α = P8 

> i=1

For another example, one can use the matching and Fano models for Ω to study the set T (E8, 8) from [6, Section 5.1]. This is a set of 630 quadruples of pairwise orthogonal roots that are closely related to the aforementioned curve configurations and that form another quasiparabolic set for W (E8). We can use the matching and Fano models to obtain the level generating function for T (E8, 8) established in [6, Remark 5.10 (i)]. 

Remark 6.3 . Corollary 5.6 and its proof can be readily generalized to show that for any positive integer k and any nonnesting perfect matching of the set {1, 2, . . . , 2k} with left endpoints 1 < l 1 <l2 < · · · < l k−1 and right endpoints r1 < r 2 < · · · < r k−1 < 2k, we have the multiset equality

This generalization may be of independent interest in that it is a simple but non-obvious equality concerning the endpoints of perfect matchings.
