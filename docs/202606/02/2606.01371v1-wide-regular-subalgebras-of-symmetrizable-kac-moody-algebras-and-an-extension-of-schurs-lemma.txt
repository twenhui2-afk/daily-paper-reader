1. Introduction 

The behavior of representations under restriction is a central theme in Lie theory. Afundamental question is to determine when an irreducible module remains indecomposable upon restriction to a subalgebra. We examine wide regular subalgebras of symmetrizable Kac–Moody algebras, extending work of Douglas and Repka on semisimple Lie algebras [5]. Let g be a symmetrizable Kac–Moody algebra with Cartan subalgebra h, root system Φ, simple roots Π, and root space decomposition 

g = h ⊕ M

> α∈Φ

gα.

Denote by Φ re the set of real roots. A subalgebra is wide if every irreducible integrable highest weight g-module remains indecomposable upon restriction. The theory of regular subalgebras goes back to the foundational work of Dynkin [6]. The study of wide subalgebras dates at least to the work of Douglas and Premat [4], and is closely connected to classical topics such as branching rules. The term wide subalgebra 

was introduced by Panyushev [18] in 2014. Most relevant to the present work, Douglas and Repka [5] established necessary and sufficient conditions for regular subalgebras of semisimple Lie algebras to be wide.

In this article, we extend aspects of this theory to the symmetrizable Kac–Moody setting. To a regular subalgebra s normalized by h, we associate a closed subset T ⊆ Φ by declaring that α ∈ T if s ∩ gα̸ = {0}.Our main result is an extension of Schur’s lemma: if h ⊆ s and the real closure of (T ∪ (−T )) ∩ Φre contains Π, then (End V )s = C Id V , where (End V )s := { F ∈ End( V ) | x · F = 0 for all x ∈ s},

and V is an irreducible integrable highest weight g-module. As a consequence, this real-root closure condition yields a sufficient condition for wideness. In the affine case, we establish a converse: if s is wide, then the closure of T ∪ (−T ) in Φ is all of Φ, and notably this implication does not require the assumption that h ⊆ s. This relies on a structural result showing that closed subsets of affine root systems are closed under arbitrary finite root sums that remain roots. In the finite-dimensional semisimple case, these closure conditions coincide, yielding a necessary and sufficient criterion for wideness, which does not require h ⊆ s (cf. [5, Corollary 3.10]). Extending the results of Douglas and Repka to the symmetrizable Kac–Moody setting requires a different conceptual approach. In contrast to the finite-dimensional case, the root system contains both real and imaginary roots, there are infinitely many root spaces, and integrable highest weight modules typically have infinitely many weights. Moreover, End( V ) is no longer completely reducible, and the classical arguments fail. We overcome these challenges by developing a new approach, centered on an extension of Schur’s lemma that replaces complete reducibility and yields a sufficient condition for wideness. In the affine case, we establish a structural result on closed subsets of root systems that supports the proof of a necessary condition for wideness without requiring h ⊆ s.The article is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews background material on symmetriz-able Kac–Moody algebras, their regular subalgebras, and their representations. In Section 3, we study wide regular subalgebras, prove a sufficient condition for wideness for Cartan-regular subalgebras h ⊆ s via the extension of Schur’s lemma, and establish, in the affine case, a necessary condition for wideness. In this section, we also present the affine finite-sum closure result that supports the proof of the necessary direction. 2.4. Closed subsets of root systems and regular subalgebras. Let Φ be the root system of g with respect to a Cartan subalgebra h. A subset T ⊆ Φ is closed if α, β ∈ T

and α + β ∈ Φ imply α + β ∈ T . For any S ⊆ Φ, the closure of S, denoted [ S], is defined by [S] := the smallest closed subset of Φ containing S.

Lemma 3.1 (Idempotent criterion lemma) . Let s be a subalgebra of a symmetrizable Kac– Moody algebra g, and let V be an irreducible integrable highest weight module. Then V is 

s-indecomposable if and only if (End V )s contains no non-trivial idempotents. Proof. (= ⇒) Assume V is s-indecomposable. Let F ∈ (End V )s be idempotent, so that 

F 2 = F . Since x · F = x ◦ F − F ◦ x = 0 for all x ∈ s, we have F ◦ x = x ◦ F , that is, F

commutes with every element of s.We then have a vector space decomposition 

V = im( F ) ⊕ ker( F ).

Indeed, let v ∈ V , then v = F (v) + ( v − F (v)), where F (v) ∈ im( F ), and v − F (v) ∈ ker( F ), since F is idempotent. This implies V = im( F ) + ker( F ). Further, if v ∈ im( F ) ∩ ker( F ), then F (v) = 0 and F (u) = v for some u ∈ V . Since F is idempotent, we have v = F (u) = 

F 2(u) = F (v) = 0, so that im( F ) ∩ ker( F ) = {0}. Thus V = im( F ) ⊕ ker( F ) as vector spaces. directly, so F is surjective. Combined with ker( F ) = {0}, F is bijective, and multiplying

4. Conclusion 

In this article, we studied wide regular subalgebras of symmetrizable Kac–Moody algebras, extending aspects of the theory from the finite-dimensional semisimple setting in [5]. In the Cartan-regular case h ⊆ s, we proved that Π ⊆ cl re (T ∪ (−T )) =⇒ s is wide .

In the affine case, we established the converse implication 

s is wide =⇒ [T ∪ (−T )] = Φ ,

which does not require the assumption that h ⊆ s.A main ingredient is an extension of Schur’s lemma, which replaces complete reducibility in this setting. In particular, under the same real-root closure condition, the invariant en-domorphisms satisfy (End V )s = C Id V for any irreducible integrable highest weight module 

V , whenever h ⊆ s.The approach differs fundamentally from that in the finite-dimensional semisimple setting of [5]. In the semisimple case, the argument relies on the complete reducibility of End( V ) and the finiteness of its weight decomposition, neither of which holds in the Kac–Moody setting. The presence of imaginary roots introduces additional complications and necessitates new structural tools, such as the affine finite-sum closure property established here. Several natural questions remain open. First, it would be desirable to determine necessary and sufficient conditions for an arbitrary regular subalgebra to be wide, including those that do not necessarily contain a Cartan subalgebra. In the present work, we obtain a sufficient condition under the assumption h ⊆ s, and, in the affine case, a necessary condition that does not require this assumption. Bridging this gap in the general symmetrizable Kac–Moody setting remains an open problem and may require additional conditions on the Cartan component s ∩ h or on imaginary roots. In particular, further extensions of Schur’s lemma may be needed. It is natural to conjecture that requiring s to contain a complement of 

h ∩ [g, g] in h, rather than all of h, may suffice. In the finite-dimensional semisimple case [5], no condition on the Cartan component beyond closure under the Lie bracket is required; however, extending these results to the Kac–Moody setting presents additional difficulties. A second direction for future work is the study of narrow subalgebras (i.e., those for which every nontrivial irreducible integrable highest weight module becomes decomposable upon restriction) and of regular extreme algebras, that is, algebras for which every regular 14 A.
