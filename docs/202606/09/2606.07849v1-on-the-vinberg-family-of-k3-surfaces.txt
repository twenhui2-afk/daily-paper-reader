ON THE VINBERG FAMILY OF K3 SURFACES 

ADRIAN CLINGHER, ANDREAS MALMENDIER, AND BRANDON WILLIAMS 

Abstract. We study orthogonal modular forms associated with moduli spaces of lattice-polarized K3 surfaces whose generic transcendental lattices are of the form T = H ⊕H ⊕L(−1)

where L is a root lattice of type An or Dn. In Picard numbers 10 through 17, we use explicit Jacobian elliptic fibrations to construct modular forms on type IV domains associated with orthogonal groups O +(T ). We show that the coefficients of suitable Weierstrass models naturally realize generators for the corresponding graded rings of orthogonal modular forms. 

1. Introduction 

Let X be a smooth complex projective K3 surface. The intersection form gives the second cohomology group H2(X , Z) the structure of an even integral lattice. This lattice is the unique (up to isometry) unimodular even lattice of signature (3, 19 ), which we denote Λ K3 ≅

H⊕3 ⊕ E8(−1)⊕2. Denote by NS (X ) the N´ eron–Severi lattice. The rank of NS (X ) is called the Picard number and ranges between 1 and 20. In this article, we shall study some specific lattice polarizations on X . Let S be an even lattice of signature (1, ρ S − 1) admitting a primitive embedding S ↪ ΛK3. Following the definition in [2,12,32], an S-polarization on X is a primitive lattice embedding i ∶ S ↪ NS (X )

whose image contains a big and nef class 1. Isomorphism classes of S-polarized K3 surfaces form a quasi-projective moduli space MS of dimension 20 − ρS ; see [12]. Let T be the orthogonal complement of S in Λ K3. Hodge structures (periods) for S-polarized K3 surfaces are known (see [32], as well as [3, 12]) to be classified, up to isomor-phism, by the quotient space O +(T )/ D+(T ), where (1.1) D+(T ) = {[ Z] ∈ P(T ⊗ C)∶ Z ⋅ Z = 0, Z ⋅ Z > 0}

is the Hermitian symmetric domain of type IV associated with T . Here, the index + indicates a choice of connected component and O +(T ) < O(T ) denotes the spinor kernel subgroup of integral isometries that preserve the connected component D+(T ).An appropriate version of the Torelli Theorem (see [31, 32], as well as [3]) allows one to identify the quasi-projective moduli space MS with the quotient O +(T )/ D+(T ). It should also be noted that O +(T ; R) = SO (2, 20 − ρS ) acts transitively on D+(T ), which leads to an identification between D+(T ) and the bounded symmetric domain SO (2, 20 − ρS )/ SO (2) × O(20 − ρS ) .

The classifying period space O +(T )/ D+(T ) may then be seen, from this point of view, as a quotient of a bounded symmetric domain by the action of a (modular) discrete group.

2.1. Facts about Jacobian elliptic K3 surfaces. We begin by recalling standard notions from lattice theory that will be used throughout. By a lattice, we always mean an even integral lattice, i.e. a free Z-module L of finite rank equipped with a nondegenerate integer-valued quadratic form QL. For lattices L1 and L2, we denote by L1 ⊕ L2 their orthogonal direct sum. For λ ∈ Z, the lattice L(λ) is obtained from L by scaling its quadratic form by λ. Given a symmetric matrix m, we write ⟨m⟩ for the lattice having m as Gram matrix in a suitable basis. The symbols An, Dn, and En refer to the positive-definite root lattices associated with the corresponding root systems, while H denotes the unique even unimodular lattice of signature (1, 1).Let L be a lattice of rank ρ. Its discriminant group is defined as D(L) = L∨/L, equipped with the discriminant quadratic form QL∶ D(L) → Q/Z. The lattice L is called 2-elementary 

if D(L) is an elementary abelian 2-group, i.e. D(L) ≅ (Z/2Z)ℓ, where ℓ is the length of 

L, defined as the minimal number of generators of D(L). In this case one also defines the parity δ ∈ {0, 1} by setting δ = 0 if QL takes values in 12 Z/Z for all elements of D(L), and 

δ = 1 otherwise. A theorem of Nikulin asserts that an even, hyperbolic, 2-elementary lattice admitting a primitive embedding into the K3 lattice Λ K3 is uniquely determined by (ρ, ℓ, δ ).

S ≃ H ⊕ K(−1),

where K is an even positive-definite lattice of rank ρS − 2. The lattice K is usually referred to as the frame of the fibration π and carries important geometric information about π. The 

root sub-lattice Kroot ⊂ K decomposes canonically as a sum of ADE-type lattices, and hence it encodes information about the singular fibers of the fibration π. Second, a theorem of Shioda proved that the factor group W = K/Kroot is canonically isomorphic to the Mordell–Weil group W ≃ MW (X , π ), providing information about the sections of π.The arguments above may also be reversed, see [6]: a primitive lattice embedding H ↪ S

is induced by a Jacobian elliptic fibration if and only if the image of H in S contains a quasi-ample class. Furthermore, one can prove that there is a one-to-one correspondence between Jacobian elliptic fibrations on X, up to action of the group of automorphisms Aut (X), and the primitive lattice embeddings H ↪ S, up to the action of the isometry group O (S).A related question in the above context is the following: given a fixed choice of frame, how many non-isomorphic Jacobian elliptic fibrations are there on X with orthogonal complement 

K(−1) = j(H)⊥. Festi and Veniani [14] have proved that the answer is always a finite number mult (K), referred to as the multiplicity of the frame. The number can be computed lattice-theoretically, by counting the elements of a particular coset space of isometries. To describe this space, note that one has the following group homomorphisms: (2.1) Oh(TX ) ↪ O(TX ) → O (QTX ) , O(S) → O (QS ) .

Here, the groups on the right are the automorphism groups preserving the associated dis-criminant quadratic forms, and O h(TX ) is the subgroup of isometries in O (TX ) that preserve the Hodge decomposition. Moreover, due to the fact that TX is the orthogonal complement of 

S ≃ H ⊕ K(−1) within an overall unimodular lattice, one has (non-canonical) isomorphisms: 

D(TX ) ≃ D(S) ≃ D(K),

with the discriminant quadratic forms on D(TX ) and D(K) only differing by a minus sign. Denote by G1 and G2 the abelian groups representing the images of the morphisms (2.1). Up to an overall conjugation, one may then regard G1 and G2 as subgroups within the isometry group O (QK ), i.e., the orthogonal group of the discriminant form on D(K). The double coset space: (2.2) G1/O (QK ) / G2

is then well-defined, and Festi and Veniani [14, Thm 2.8] proved mult (K) = ∣ G1/O (QK ) / G2 ∣.

In particular, the index can be computed using the positive definite lattice K. In geometric terms, this quantity determines the number of Jacobian elliptic fibrations with given singular fibers and Mordell-Weil group, up to automorphisms. By [28], one has O h(TX ) = {±id } whenever the Picard number ρS is odd, and also for a very general K3 surface when ρS is even. In this situation, the image G1 of O h(TX ) in O(QK ) is trivial or consists only of the classes induced by ±id, and the double-coset count simplifies considerably. We identify 

G2 ≃ Im (O(K) → O(QK )) 

with the image of the natural homomorphism induced by the action of lattice isometries on the discriminant form. Then the multiplicity is determined entirely by the failure of isometries of the discriminant form to lift to isometries of K. In particular, when the action of G1 is trivial, one obtains mult (K) = [O(QK ) ∶ G2]. Since O (QK ) is finite, Lagrange’s theorem yields (2.3) mult (K) = [O(QK ) ∶ Im (O(K))] = ∣O(QK )∣ ∣ Im (O(K))∣ .

Equivalently, the multiplicity is the cardinality of the quotient of O (QK ) by the subgroup of automorphisms of the discriminant form induced by lattice isometries of K. We will restrict our attention to Sn-polarized K3 surfaces of Picard number ρSn ≥ 10. This assumption is motivated by general constraints arising from the theory of arithmetic quotients of type IV domains. Indeed, by a result of Shvartsman and Vinberg [33], a necessary condition for the freeness of the algebra of automorphic forms of a non-cocompact arithmetic group ∆ acting on a symmetric domain of type IV of dimension d is that d ≤ 10. Since the dimension of the period domain is 20 − ρSn , this condition translates into ρSn ≥ 10. We call the family of lattice polarized K3 surfaces with polarizing lattice Sn of rank 10 ≤ ρSn ≤ 16 the Vinberg family of rank ρSn .
