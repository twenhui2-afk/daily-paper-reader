SMITH NORMAL FORMS FOR COALESCENCES AT COSPECTRAL VERTICES 

YI-ZHENG FAN*, KUO ZHANG, AND WEI WANG ♯

Abstract. Let Lμ(G) = A(G) − μD (G) be the generalized μ-adjacency matrix of a finite graph G. Fan, Xing, Zhang, and Wang constructed pairs of non-degree-similar trees for which the Smith normal forms of the matrices tI −Lμ(G) over Q(μ)[ t] coincide, and conjectured that their construction remains valid when the attached rooted path is replaced by an arbitrary rooted tree. We prove this conjecture as a consequence of a more general coalescence theorem: if a finite graph H has two vertices u and v that are cospectral for Lμ(H), then, for every finite rooted graph R with root r, the matrices 

tI − Lμ(R(r) ⊙ H(u)) and tI − Lμ(R(r) ⊙ H(v)) have the same Smith normal form over Q(μ)[ t], where ⊙ denotes coalescence of rooted graphs. The proof uses an orthogonal intertwiner over a real closed extension field. 

1. Introduction 

Let G be a finite simple graph with adjacency matrix A(G) and degree matrix D(G). Following Godsil and Sun [5], two graphs G1 and G2 are called degree-similar if there exists an invertible matrix M such that 

M −1A(G1)M = A(G2), M −1D(G1)M = D(G2).

This condition implies similarity for several standard graph matrices, including the adjacency matrix, the Laplacian, the signless Laplacian and, when there are no isolated vertices, the normalized Laplacian. The generalized μ-adjacency matrix of G is 

Lμ(G) := A(G) − μD (G),

and the corresponding μ-polynomial is 

ψ(G, t, μ ) := det( tI − Lμ(G)) .

Wang et al. [10] asked whether two graphs with the same μ-polynomial must be degree-similar via an orthogonal matrix. Godsil and Sun [5] gave a negative answer by constructing pairs of graphs that have the same μ-polynomial but are not degree-similar.

If G1 and G2 are degree-similar, then Lμ(G1) and Lμ(G2) are similar over Q(μ). Godsil and Sun proved that, over a field F , two matrices B1 and B2 are similar if and only if the matrices tI − B1 and tI − B2 have the same Smith normal form over F [t] [5, Theorem 9.1]. Consequently, degree-similar graphs have the same Smith normal form for tI − Lμ(G) over 

Q(μ)[ t]. Godsil and Sun asked whether the converse holds [5, Problem 10.2]. Fan, Xing, Zhang and Wang gave a negative answer by constructing pairs of trees that are not degree-similar but whose matrices tI − Lμ(G) have the same Smith normal form over 

Q(μ)[ t] [1]. Their construction is based on McKay’s rooted tree pair [8]. Let T be the tree shown in Figure 1.1, which was introduced by McKay in his construction of non-isomorphic cospectral trees. In [1, Lemma 11], the authors proved that if a rooted path is attached to T

at vertex 4 and at vertex 7, then the resulting two trees have the same Smith normal form over Q(μ)[ t]. They then proposed the following conjecture. 

Conjecture 1.1 ([1]) . Let T be the tree in Figure 1.1, and let R be any nontrivial rooted tree with root r. Define 

G4 := R(r) ⊙ T(4) , G7 := R(r) ⊙ T(7) ,

where ⊙ denotes coalescence of rooted graphs. Then 

tI − Lμ(G4) and tI − Lμ(G7)

have the same Smith normal form over Q(μ)[ t].

The purpose of this paper is to prove Conjecture 1.1. We prove a more general statement. Let H be any finite graph, and let u, v ∈ V (H) be cospectral for Lμ(H). Then attaching an arbitrary finite rooted graph R at u or at v gives two coalescences whose generalized 

μ-adjacency matrices have the same Smith normal form over Q(μ)[ t]. Conjecture 1.1 follows by taking H = T and ( u, v ) = (4 , 7). The main linear-algebraic ingredient is an orthogonal involution, over a real closed extension field of Q(μ), that commutes with Lμ(H) and sends

Definition 2.1. Let M be a square matrix over a field. Two indices u and v are called 

cospectral for M if det( tI − M (u)) = det( tI − M (v)) .

The idea of cospectral vertices goes back to Schwenk [9], who used it to construct non-isomorphic cospectral trees. The characterization of cospectral vertices for adjacency matrix of a graph was established in [3, 4] over the real field. We shall use real closed fields only as a technical device. Recall that a field F is real closed 

if it is orderable, every positive element of F is a square, and every polynomial over F of odd degree has a root in F ; equivalently, F (√−1) is algebraically closed; see, for example, [7, Chapter XIII]. We fix an ordering of K = Q(μ), for instance the ordering obtained by identifying K with 

Q(t) and declaring t to be a positive infinite transcendental element. Let F be a real closure of K with respect to this ordering. The particular choice of ordering plays no role in what follows. We only use the fact that symmetric matrices over the real closed field F admit an orthogonal spectral decomposition. After the required similarity is obtained over F , it is descended back to K = Q(μ) by using rational canonical form. For a graph G and a vertex v ∈ V (G), or more generally for a square matrix M and an index v, let ev denote the standard basis column vector indexed by V (G), or by the index set of M , respectively. Thus ev has its only nonzero entry, equal to 1, in position v.

Corollary 2.6 (Conjecture 1.1) . Let T be the tree in Figure 1.1, with vertices labelled as in that figure. Let R be any finite rooted graph with root r. Define 

G4 := R(r) ⊙ T(4) , G7 := R(r) ⊙ T(7) .

Then 

tI − Lμ(G4) and tI − Lμ(G7)

have the same Smith normal form over Q(μ)[ t]. In particular, Conjecture 1.1 holds when R

is any nontrivial rooted tree. Proof. Let B := Lμ(T). By the computation in the proof of [5, Lemma 3.1], the two distin-guished vertices 4 and 7 of T are cospectral for B, that is, det( tI − B(4)) = det( tI − B(7)) .

Therefore the assertion follows from Theorem 2.5, applied with H = T, u = 4, and v = 7. □

Corollary 2.7. Let R be any nontrivial rooted tree with root r, and let 

G4 := R(r) ⊙ T(4) , G7 := R(r) ⊙ T(7) .

Then G4 and G7 have the same Smith normal form for tI − Lμ over Q(μ)[ t], but they are not degree-similar. Proof. The equality of Smith normal forms follows from Corollary 2.6. By McKay’s theorem, as used in [1], the two trees G4 and G7 are non-isomorphic whenever R is a nontrivial rooted tree. Also, two trees are degree-similar if and only if they are isomorphic [5, Theorem 3.2]. Therefore G4 and G7 are not degree-similar. □SMITH NORMAL FORMS FOR COALESCENCES 7

Remark 2.8 . Lemma 11 of [1] proves the path case by showing that the ( n−1)-st determinant divisor equals 1, and hence the Smith normal form has the special form 1, . . . , 1, ψ (t, μ ).

For a general rooted graph R, and a fortiori in the general setting of Theorem 2.5, this special form need not hold. The argument above proves the stronger and more natural conclusion needed for Conjecture 1.1: the two complete Smith normal forms are equal.
