COUNTEREXAMPLES TO STANLEY’S CONJECTURE ON DIMER COVERINGS 

XUEJUN GUO AND ZHENGYU TAO 

Abstract. Let Qk(x) be Stanley’s explicit denominator for the dimer-covering generating function Fk(x) = ∑ 

> n≥0

Ak,n xn of k × n rectangles. Stanley conjectured in 1985 that Qk(x) has only simple roots; this longstanding conjecture was recently recorded in Lai’s list of open prob-lems on tilings (see [6, Problem 33]). We disprove the conjecture by proving that Q14 h−1(x)

and Q30 h−1(x) have repeated roots for every h ≥ 1; in particular, k = 13 is the smallest counterexample. The construction comes from two exceptional multiplicative identities among trigonometric algebraic units. We further propose a conjecture concerning this class of trigono-metric identities, which appears to be related to Robinson’s problem on primitive Pell factors.

Remark 2. Stanley pointed out in [10] that Conjecture 1 is equivalent to the assertion that 

2⌊(k+1) /2⌋ is the minimum order of a linear recurrence satisfied by Ak,n . This is also equivalent to the assertion that the polynomials Pk(x) and Qk(x) are relatively prime. Stanley also proved in [10] that if Fk(x) = Pk(x)/Q k(x) is reduced to lowest term, then the denominator has distinct roots. Conjecture 1 is also collected in [6] as Problem 33. The purpose of this note is to show that Stanley’s conjecture, in the form stated above, is false. The smallest counterexample is k = 13 . In fact, we can construct two infinite families of counterexamples to Conjecture 1 (see Theorem 4). This note is organized as follows. In Section 2, we first establish a simple criterion (Lemma 3) for the polynomial Qk(x) to have repeated roots. Then, with the help of two elegant trigonometric identities, we prove Theorem 4. In Section 3, we revise Stanley’s original conjecture to Conjecture 10. Some connections with Robinson’s conjecture are also discussed. 2. Main results
