Structure of the paper. Section 2 collects the notation on polynomials, empirical root distri-butions, and finite free convolutions, and recalls the approximation of free additive and multi-plicative convolutions by their finite counterparts. Section 3 presents a motivating family of examples based on Jacobi polynomials. There we solve an explicit polynomial fixed-point equation and identify the weak limit of the empirical root distribution with a free perpetuity studied in [4]. Next, in Section 4 we introduce normalized derivatives and the U -transform, which provide a convenient probabilistic representation of finite free convolutions and allow us to reformulate polynomial fixed-point equations in moment form. In Section 5 we define finite free perpetuities in general, prove existence and uniqueness under natural assumptions, and establish criteria ensuring that all roots are real and nonnegative. Finally, we study the large-degree limit in Section 6. For admissible sequences of parameters, we prove weak convergence of the empirical root distributions of finite free perpetuities to the law of a free perpetuity, thereby making precise the connection between the finite and free theories.
