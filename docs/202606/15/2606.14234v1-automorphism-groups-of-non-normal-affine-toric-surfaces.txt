> Abstract. We investigate the automorphism groups of non-normal affine toric surfaces. While the connected component of the automorphism group of a normal toric surface is always generated by the acting torus and root subgroups, we prove that this fails for non-normal surfaces in general. Specifically, we show that the property holds if the singular locus contains a one-dimensional torus orbit, but can fail if the singular locus is an isolated point. To illustrate this, we construct an explicit non-normal affine toric surface in A4
> whose connected component of the identity is strictly larger than the subgroup generated by the torus and root automorphisms of the surface.

1. Introduction 

The study of automorphism groups of affine algebraic varieties is a classical subject in algebraic geometry. For affine toric varieties, these groups can be described in terms of the combinatorial data of the underlying cones and lattices. In the case of normal affine toric surfaces, this group is well understood. As shown by Arzhantsev and Zaidenberg [1], this group is an amalgamated free product. In the normal case the connected component of the automorphism group is generated by the acting torus and the root subgroups associated to Demazure roots. Non-normal toric varieties correspond to affine monoids that are not saturated. The “holes” in the character monoid restrict the set of Demazure roots. While the automorphism groups of certain non-normal toric varieties have been addressed in the literature (e.g., [2], [4]), the general case remains poorly understood. In this paper, we consider the automorphism groups of non-normal affine toric surfaces. Our approach relies on lifting automorphisms from a non-normal surface X to its normal-ization eX, which allows us to view Aut( X) as a subgroup of Aut( eX).Recall that under the action of the torus T , an affine toric surface consists of four T -orbits: a dense open orbit, a unique closed orbit consisting of a fixed point, and two one-dimensional orbits corresponding to the two rays of the cone. The singular locus of a non-normal surface is a union of non-open orbits. The type of this locus dictates the existence of Demazure roots associated to the one-dimensional rays. Based on this, we divide our study into three cases: (1) Both one-dimensional torus orbits are singular. The surface admits no Demazure roots, and the automorphism group is isomorphic to the torus T or the semidirect product Z/2Z ⋉ T .

3.1. Toric varieties and affine monoids. A commutative finitely generated monoid S

is called affine if it can be embedded into a free abelian group of finite rank. Every affine monoid defines an irreducible affine variety XS = Spec k[S]. Throughout this paper, we assume all monoids to be affine. Let M ∼= Zn denote the lattice generated by an affine monoid S. The saturation Ssat 

of S is the set of all m ∈ M with lm ∈ S for some positive integer l. We say that S is saturated if S = Ssat . It is a well-known fact that the variety XS is normal if and only if S

is saturated [3, Theorem 1.3.5]. There is an action of the torus T = N ⊗Z Gm ∼= Gnm on k[S], where N = Hom( M, Z).This action is given by 

(u ⊗ t) · χm = t⟨m,u ⟩χm.

This induces a torus action on XS with a dense open orbit, because the coordinate ring 

k[XS ] = k[S] embeds into k[T ] = k[M ] as a finitely generated subalgebra [3]. Thus, XS is a toric variety. Conversely, every affine toric variety X is isomorphic to XS(X), where S(X)

is the monoid of T -characters that are regular on X.If X is a normal toric variety, the monoid S(X) is determined by the associated polyhedral cone 

σ(X) = {u ∈ NQ : ⟨m, u ⟩ ⩾ 0 ∀m ∈ S(X)}

via S(X) = σ(X)∨ ∩ M . For a non-normal toric variety, the monoid S(X) can be described in terms of the cone σ(X) and a set A = S(X)sat \ S(X) of "holes" (see also [2]). 3.3. Torus orbits. Since the singular locus of a toric variety is torus invariant, it is the union of torus orbits. These orbits can be described combinatorially in terms of the faces of the cone σ, see [3]. 

Theorem 2. There is a one-to-one correspondence between torus orbits of a toric variety 

X and faces of its cone σ(X).

For an affine toric surface associated with a non-degenerate cone σ, there exist exactly four orbits corresponding to four cone faces: the maximal two-dimensional face σ, which corresponds to an open torus orbit, two rays associated with two one-dimensional obits, and the origin with the closed torus orbit consisting of single point. Furthermore, the union of a one-dimensional orbit and a closed orbit is closed.
