# How Divergence Becomes Decision Flips in Compressed Language Models 

Beatriz Almeida Felicio 

Instituto de Informática (INF) Universidade Federal de Goiás (UFG) 

beatrizfelicio@discente.ufg.br 

Abstract 

Compression reports summarize how far a com-pressed language model moved from the dense one, usually by a KL divergence; a deployment that relies on the dense model’s outputs needs to know how many of its decisions changed. We show that total variation, not KL, answers this directly. Across 802 compressed and per-turbed copies of 19 open models on five corpora and nine mechanically unrelated perturbation families, the rate at which the arg-max token changes (the flip rate ) tracks total variation at a ratio with median 1.05 , with no fitted constant. KL converts into flips only through its square root and a factor that varies fourfold across models and corpora, because KL averages over tokens before the root is taken; first-order statis-tics averaged per token, such as Hellinger dis-tance, avoid this, but reports rarely give them. As a result, of two compressors reported on different models and corpora whose flip rates differ by at least 10% , KL assigns the smaller divergence to the one that changes more deci-sions in 11% of cases, total variation in 1% .Two pre-registered tests mark the limits: on a held-out code corpus the ratio held for all eight models while three predictions about KL each failed for half of them or more, and on three new models with real kernels it stayed in its band for 37 of 38 checkpoints but fell below one on code for two models. In vLLM spec-ulative decoding, total variation measured un-der teacher forcing predicts greedy draft accep-tance with a mean relative error of 1.1–2.4% ,without the task-specific calibration that KL needs. 

1 Introduction 

Post-training compression is the standard way to reduce the memory and bandwidth cost of Trans-former language models (Vaswani et al., 2017; Gho-lami et al., 2021). Weight quantization (Dettmers et al., 2022; Frantar et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2023), pruning (Frantar and Alistarh, 2023; Sun et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2023), KV-cache quantization (Liu et al., 2024; Hooper et al., 2024) and layer removal (Men et al., 2024; Gromov et al., 2024) are typically assessed end to end: apply the method, run benchmarks, report the change in accuracy (Li et al., 2024a; Jaiswal et al., 2024). End-to-end accuracy mixes (i) how far the method moves the model’s predictive distribution, (ii) how that movement turns into changed token-level de-cisions, and (iii) how changed decisions propagate through generation into changed answers. For sev-eral consumers only stage (ii) matters: systems that need exact agreement with the dense model, such as cached or deduplicated outputs and regres-sion tests across model versions, depend on how many token decisions change. Speculative decod-ing is the clearest case: it accepts a draft token only when it agrees with the target; under sampling, the acceptance probability is exactly one minus the total variation between the two next-token distri-butions (Leviathan et al., 2023), and under greedy decoding a token is accepted exactly when the two arg-max decisions agree. The sampling identity is known; whether a teacher-forced statistic predicts greedy agreement has, to our knowledge, not been tested, and §7 tests it. Stage (iii) is outside this paper, and it bounds everything else: fidelity to the dense model and task accuracy come apart (§8), so the statistics studied here describe decisions, not benchmark quality. A line of work already argues that accuracy hides compression damage and proposes distri-butional statistics instead: Dutta et al. (2024) re-port flips alongside KL divergence, Qamar et al. (2026) evaluates quantized models by total varia-tion, and Kishino et al. (2026) calibrates the scale of KL. Theory does not set-tle it: total variation bounds the flip rate (Lemma 1, §5), but the bound allows several times more flips than compressors produce. The practical question is how to read a reported number. Papers, leader-boards and tools report KL to the dense model and compare it across methods and evaluation sets; someone choosing between two quantized releases, or deciding whether a lower KL is worth a slower kernel, needs to know how many decisions each number implies and whether two numbers mea-sured on different text can be compared at all. Counting flips answers this only for the model and corpus at hand; the divergences that exist in reports, usually KL averaged over tokens, are what must be read. We therefore isolate stage (ii). Under teacher forcing we measure, at the same positions and on the same prefixes, the divergence between a dense model and a perturbed copy and the rate at which the arg-max token changes, and ask three questions: 

RQ1 : how does the flip rate scale with divergence, and does one conversion serve all perturbation fami-lies? RQ2 : what sets the conversion, and why does it depend on the corpus? RQ3 : does the answer predict greedy speculative decoding, the consumer that prices decisions directly? Our contributions are: 1. Total variation reads as decisions. Flips per unit of total variation have median 1.05 (95% 

of configurations within 0.89 –1.29 ) across nine families, with no fitted constant; an isotropic small-noise limit that depends on the dense dis-tributions alone accounts for the value near one. The ratio held in two pre-registered tests, except on code for two new models (§5, §6). 2. KL reads as decisions only within a corpus. 

Within one reference (a model on a corpus), nine families share one square-root curve; across models and corpora its coefficient moves four-fold ( 1.6–2.6× across corpora of one model), so KL misorders compressors compared across reports eight times as often as total variation, and on a held-out code corpus the shared family curve failed its registered test (§4–§6). 3. Why: averaging. An exact decomposition at-tributes most of KL’s corpus dependence to its averaging over tokens (a Jensen factor); first-order statistics averaged per token, such as Hellinger distance, share total variation’s sta-bility, but reports give aggregate KL (§5). 4. Greedy speculative decoding. Total varia-tion measured under teacher forcing predicts a draft’s greedy agreement without task-specific calibration, where KL must be calibrated on the task and still errs two to three and a half times more; in vLLM it predicts measured acceptance within 1.1–2.4% (§7). 

2 Related Work 

Compression and its evaluation. Post-training quantization (Frantar et al., 2022, 2023; Lin et al., 2024; Yao et al., 2022; Chee et al., 2023; Ashkboos et al., 2024b; Egiazarian et al., 2024; Dettmers et al., 2023; Shao et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2024) and pruning (Han et al., 2015; Frantar and Alistarh, 2023; Sun et al., 2024; Ashkboos et al., 2024a; Men et al., 2024) are usually compared by accu-racy or perplexity. Several of these methods al-ready minimize the second-order expansion of the loss that we use descriptively to explain the ex-ponent (Nagel et al., 2020; Li et al., 2021; Dong et al., 2019). Accuracy and perplexity are known to hide differences between methods, bit widths and tasks (Jaiswal et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024a; Kur-tic et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2024; Dettmers and Zettlemoyer, 2023), including instance-level dis-agreement at unchanged accuracy (Rababah et al., 2026). 

Divergence and flips as evaluation statistics. 

Dutta et al. (2024) propose flips and KL as com-plementary metrics for compressed models, and Xu et al. (2021) earlier defined label loyalty (a flip rate) and probability loyalty (a distributional distance) for compressed BERT models; Hooker et al. (2019) ask which examples compression for-gets, and KL and flips are also reported side by side for learned mixed precision (Ogilvy, 2026). More recent work evaluates quantized models by total variation and Jensen–Shannon divergence (Qa-mar et al., 2026), sets an empirical scale for KL across settings (Kishino et al., 2026), and finds that KL tracks the volume of disagreement rather than its direction (Nikoli´ c et al., 2026). These works treat divergence as the endpoint; we take it as the input and ask what it implies for decisions, how the answer depends on the divergence chosen, and why it varies with the corpus. The square-root form follows from standard information geometry (Amari, 1998; Martens, 2020). Table 1 lists, point by point, what the closest studies measure: Qamar et al. (2026) already evaluate by total variation and Jensen–Shannon divergence, but none of these stud-ies fits a functional relation between a divergence and the flip rate, none accounts for why that rela-tion depends on the corpus, and none tests it on a held-out domain.

Churn, negative flips and margins. Disagree-ment induced by retraining is studied as churn (Mi-lani Fard et al., 2016; Bhojanapalli et al., 2021) and as negative flips (Yan et al., 2021); we instead control the perturbation of a fixed model. Mar-gins govern robustness to input noise in random-ized smoothing (Cohen et al., 2019); ours is the parameter-space counterpart, where the density of margins near zero sets how many decisions change. 

Speculative decoding. Speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023; Stern et al., 2018; Cai et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024b; Miao et al., 2023) accepts a draft token when it agrees with the target, and self-speculative variants draft with a compressed copy of the target (Zhang et al., 2024; Tiwari et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025); for sampling, acceptance equals one minus the total variation between the two distributions (Leviathan et al., 2023), KL has been argued to be the wrong training objective for drafts (Zhou et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2025), and total variation has been used di-rectly as a training loss for generation and for drafts (Ji et al., 2023; Goel et al., 2024); we study it as an evaluation statistic. 

3 Setup and Definitions 

Models, perturbations, corpora. We use 19 checkpoints from six families: Gemma-3 (1B, 4B, 12B, 27B) (Gemma Team, 2025); Qwen3 (4B, 8B, 14B) and Qwen2.5 (7B, 72B) (Yang et al., 2025; Qwen Team, 2024); Mistral-7B and Mistral-Small-24B (Jiang et al., 2023); Phi-3.5-mini and phi-4 (Abdin et al., 2024); OLMo-2 (1B, 7B, 13B), with base and instruction-tuned versions of the 1B and 7B models (Team OLMo, 2024); and Llama-3-8B-Instruct (Grattafiori et al., 2024), loaded from a pub-lic mirror whose weight files have the same SHA-256 hashes as the official release. Perturbations cover nine families: round-to-nearest quantization (RTN, 3–8 bits, group 128), GPTQ (Frantar et al., 2023), AWQ (Lin et al., 2024), SparseGPT (Fran-tar and Alistarh, 2023), Wanda (Sun et al., 2024), magnitude pruning (Han et al., 2015), KV-cache quantization (Liu et al., 2024), layer removal (Men et al., 2024) and isotropic Gaussian weight noise, plus two controls (single-layer RTN, and four third-party GPTQ/AWQ checkpoints that check our com-pression code). The four calibrated methods use 128 windows of 512 tokens from the WikiText-2 training split; because two of our corpora are drawn from that split, §4 repeats them with C4 calibration. Statistical protocol. Configurations within a ref-erence share the corpus, the model and its margins, so the 54 references, not the 802 configurations, are the independent units: we fit exponents per reference, treat pooled R2 as descriptive, and com-pute every interval across references or models, never across configurations nested in them. In-sample fits are weak evidence here: with a dozen configurations over two or three decades of KL, a per-reference R2 near one is nearly automatic for any smooth monotone relation, so our claims rest on held-out families, held-out configurations and a held-out corpus instead. Logits in bf16 fall on a grid of 0.0625 –0.25 nats, so 0.15 –6.21% of greedy steps (median 1.16% ) have an exact top-1/top-2 tie; ties dominate flips at the smallest perturbations, so every analysis of margins uses 42 references rescored with an fp32 output head. Apart from the two tests of §6, no analysis was pre-registered; the design grew as results raised questions. 

4 A Square-Root Regularity Shared Across Families 

In brief: within a reference, flips grow almost ex-actly as the square root of KL, for a reason that is a matter of orders, and nine mechanically unrelated families fall on the same curve. 

Why a square root. The intuition is a matter of orders. A small perturbation moves every logit by a small amount ϵ. The gap between the two leading tokens, which decides whether the arg-max changes, moves in proportion to ϵ. KL, which com-pares whole distributions and is smallest when noth-ing changes, can only grow as ϵ2. Alignment with the flip directions. Equation (6) reduces the question to whether A is comparable across families. Answering RQ1. Flips grow as a power of KL with exponents near, but not equal to, 1/2, and within each reference nine families share one curve to within 5.5% ; they are interchangeable for how many decisions change, not necessarily for what those changes cost (§8).

Comparing the two statistics fairly means giving each a per-reference constant: total variation then predicts a held-out configuration’s flip rate with a median error of 1.6% , against 2.4% for √KL , so within one reference the two are nearly interchangeable. Across references, where a constant cannot be car-ried over, the split is by order, not by statistic: the per-reference ratio spans 1.33 × for total variation and 1.54 × for Hellinger distance, both first order at every token, against 3.6× for √JS and 4.0× for √KL ; total variation is the tightest and the only one near one. Per-token √KL t, averaged after the root, escapes the Jensen factor by construction: by Eq. (7), flip /Et[√KL t] = (flip /TV) · c. For sam-pling, total variation is exactly the disagreement probability of the optimal coupling; the arg-max is not a coupling, so the near-unit ratio is not that identity.

δt/2, where δt = pt(i1) − pt(i2) is the dense model’s probability margin. Hence, for every

where F (h) is the fraction of positions with δt < h .

The mass i1 loses and the mass the new arg-max gains must together cover the margin, and neither exceeds TV t (proof in App. A.2). The bound uses only the dense model and the mean total variation, and it permits far more than compressors produce: if F (h) ≤ ρh near zero it gives flip ≤ 2√2ρ TV ,a square root of total variation rather than a pro-portion. 8 Discussion and Scope 

What to report. 

• The consumer needs exact agreement (spec-ulative drafts, cached or deduplicated outputs, regression tests): report the flip rate itself on the task prompts, and screen drafts by total variation, which predicted acceptance in vLLM with mean relative errors of 1.1–2.4% on GSM8K and code, where KL erred by 2.5–5.1% and 17 .8–22 .3% 

(Table B2); picking the fastest draft also needs a cost model of the kernels. • Results are compared across corpora or re-ports : report total variation, per corpus. KL mis-orders compressors in 8.1% of such pairs across corpora of one model and 10 .8% across models and corpora, total variation in 0.7% and 1.3% 

(§5). • Methods are compared within one corpus and one dense reference : KL ranks them correctly (0.2% of pairs misordered); total variation next to it gives the scale in decisions. When both models can be run on the task, counting flips costs no more than a divergence and should be preferred; total variation matters when a number is read rather than measured, as across reports. Both divergences cost the same teacher-forced pass; a top-k truncation with aggregated tail can only un-derstate total variation (for k = 64 , median 0.1% ,95th percentile 7% ), and bf16 logits need an fp32 output head against ties. The released code reports all three for any dense–compressed pair. 

Decisions are not accuracy. None of these statis-tics measures what a changed decision costs: on Gemma-3-4B at a matched GSM8K divergence of 

0.10 nats and nearly the same flip rate, magnitude pruning costs 33 .3 accuracy points while Wanda gains 4.8. A flip rate or a divergence describes fidelity to the dense model, not benchmark qual-ity. Nor do per-position statistics say how changed decisions compound over free generation. 

Scope. The main study uses dense decoder-only transformers and post-training changes of fixed weights or of the KV cache. Mixture-of-experts models and quantization-aware training enter only as a post hoc extension with two MoE models and one QAT family (App. A.6); larger MoE models, quantized routers, other QAT recipes, fine-tuning in general, distillation and multimodal inputs are untested. Every statistic is measured under teacher forcing on greedy references. What should carry over is what Proposition 1 needs: perturbations that move logits like isotropic noise; where they do not, as partly for layer removal, departures should be ex-pected, so adapters or fine-tuning must be checked, not assumed. All statements hold inside the mea-sured window ( 10 −3 to about 2 nats; 8 to 3 bits per weight; 1B–72B parameters; greedy decoding; five corpora in two languages and one held-out code corpus), within the ranges of §4. 

Limitations 

FP8 was not tested (its real kernel produced non-finite logits in most runs). The number of indepen-dent units is modest (54 references, several corpora sharing models); the speculative analyses rest on ten targets with 43 drafts, and the serving test on two targets. p-values other than the Holm-corrected family tests are descriptive. Two statements are proved, the bound of Lemma 1 and the isotropic small-noise limit of Proposition 1; real compres-sors are neither isotropic nor infinitesimal, so the near-unit ratio is explained, not guaranteed, and the budget of the exponent locates its departure from 1/2 in third-token crossings without mod-elling their rate (App. A.2).

A.1 Evaluation hygiene bfloat16 ties. In bf16 the extrapolation that de-fines ρc(0) fails structurally: between the atom of exact ties and the first grid step there are no mar-gins, so the density is zero out to h = 0 .1 and the estimate is negative for 50 of the 54 bf16 references (Figure B4); every margin analysis therefore uses an fp32 output head. Degenerate reference corpora. Greedy continu-ation of raw text makes instruction-tuned models loop: in a diagnostic run, 99–100% of Gemma sequences and 51% of Qwen3-4B sequences re-peated more than half of their 4-grams, κ fell by an order of magnitude and the exponent rose to 

0.85 (R2 = 0 .56 ); those corpora were discarded. With the excerpt inside the chat template the ex-ponent returned to 0.465 , and the pipeline now refuses corpora with more than 10% degenerate sequences; stored generated corpora are 0–0.4% 

degenerate for instruction-tuned models and 31% 

for the one base model generated greedily, which is why WIKITEXT -NAT exists.

A.3 Temperature 

Dividing the logits of both models by T changes no arg-max, so the flips are fixed and only total variation moves. As T → 0 both distributions be-come one-hot and TV t becomes exactly the flip indicator, so flip /TV → 1 for any perturbation; 

Rflat and Ppair also tend to one, since the margin density is flat on a vanishing scale and only the top pair carries mass. Measured on the top-64 tokens of the 42 fp32 references (Figure B5), the median ratio is 1.01 at T = 0 .25 (range 0.99 –1.07 ) and 

1.07 at T = 1 , and it drifts upward with temper-ature on high-entropy corpora. At T = 2 , Rflat 

reaches 13 .5 and Ppair falls to 0.14 , yet the ratio stays within 0.82 –1.63 (median 1.11 ): the same property of the output distribution—how many to-kens compete near the top—inflates one correction and deflates the other. The first-order formula of Eq. (9) follows the rise with T but overestimates it, by +5% at T = 0 .25 to +14% at T = 2 .
