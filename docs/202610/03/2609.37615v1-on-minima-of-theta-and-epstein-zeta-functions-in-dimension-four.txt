Our second main theorem concerns the Epstein zeta function in dimension four and follows from Theorem 1.1 via the Mellin transform:

Poisson summation and the Mellin transform give the meromorphic continuation and func-tional equation of the Epstein zeta function [34]. After rescaling time, Θ( α, L ) is the heat trace of the flat torus R4/L ∗, where L∗ is the Euclidean dual. The derivative of (4 π2)−sE(L, s )at zero determines the regularized Laplacian determinant and spectral height [57]. In dimension four, D4 and A4 are the only perfect forms up to integral equivalence and scaling [37, 2, 1, 58]. Sarnak–Str¨ ombergsson [57] proved strict local minimality of D4 for theta at every positive scale and for the analytically continued Epstein zeta function at every positive real parameter other than its pole. Spherical 4-designs in every nonzero shell give strict local zeta minimality in the convergence range (Coulangeon [30]). Global Gaussian optimality of D4

has numerical support (Cohn–Kumar–Sch¨ urmann [28]); earlier dimension reductions concern specified layered, translated, and orthorhombic families (B´ etermin–Petrache [15]). In our proof, Theorem 1.1 implies Theorem 1.2 through the positive-superposition principle for lattice energies; see the work of B´ etermin [8], inspired by Cohn–Kumar [26]. Historically, the Epstein zeta minimization problem has attracted more attention than its theta counter-part. In dimension two, Rankin [53] initiated the study of Epstein zeta minima for s ≥ 1.035 and proved that the hexagonal lattice is optimal. Cassels [22], Ennola [33], and Diananda [31] subsequently extended the parameter range and developed further methods. Montgomery [50] proved that the hexagonal lattice minimizes theta for every α > 0, thereby implying Rankin’s theorem. This is now a celebrated result in lattice minimization; see B´ etermin [3]. For interesting progress in dimension three, we refer to B´ etermin– ˇSamaj–Travˇ enec [17], B´ etermin–Petrache [15], and Sarnak–Str¨ ombergsson [57], together with the references therein. Our previous work [49] proves that the face-centred cubic lattice minimizes Epstein zeta, and that the face-centred cubic lattice minimizes theta for α > 1, while the body-centred cubic lattice minimizes theta for α < 1, as conjectured by Sarnak–Str¨ ombergsson [57]. In dimension four, relatively few results are available. Early claims of local minimality appear in ´Endibaev [32] and Shushbaev [60, 59]; rigorous local minimality of D4 for theta and Epstein zeta was established by Sarnak–Str¨ ombergsson [57]. Partial results and further references to earlier work can be found in Lim–Teo [40]. In dimensions eight and twenty-four, Sarnak–Str¨ ombergsson [57] proved local minimality of E8 and the Leech lattice for theta and Epstein zeta. Cohn–Kumar–Miller–Radchenko– Viazovska [27] proved the deep universal optimality theorem for their interaction energies, which in particular establishes global theta and zeta minimality. Fourier linear programming connects sphere packing (Cohn-Elkies [25]) with universal optimality for completely monotone functions of squared distance (Cohn-Kumar [26]). This approach proves universal optimality of E8 and the Leech lattice among fixed-density point configurations [27], and hence minimality of Coulomb and Riesz renormalized energies (Petrache-Serfaty [52]).
