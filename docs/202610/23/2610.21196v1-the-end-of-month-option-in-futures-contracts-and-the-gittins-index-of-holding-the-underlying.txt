THE END-OF-MONTH OPTION IN FUTURES CONTRACTS AND THE GITTINS INDEX OF HOLDING THE UNDERLYING 

ERHAN BAYRAKTAR 

Abstract. The end-of-month option allows the short in a futures contract to choose when to deliver during a window in which the delivery price is fixed. We characterize optimal delivery through a universal index obtained from the Bank–El Karoui representation theorem. In the semimartingale setting, this is the Gittins index of holding the underlying and collecting its convenience yield, and the futures price is the retirement reward for which the value of this project equals the spot price. This characterization yields price bounds and a criterion for immediate delivery. Dinkelbach’s iteration reduces the price computation to a sequence of standard optimal stopping problems, with an explicit contraction bound. We apply the theory to geometric Brownian motion and the Gibson–Schwartz model with stochastic convenience yield. For geometric Brownian motion, we characterize when the futures price equals spot and derive a square-root boundary asymptotic near expiry. For the Gibson–Schwartz model, we establish monotonicity in the yield and characterize the critical yield below which the price equals spot. Numerical results illustrate the price iteration and delivery regions.

Biagini and Björk [4, pp. 267–268] solved the timing option of the delivery month in this journal and distinguished it from the end-of-month option, under which delivery occurs after the futures price has been fixed. Tomas Björk told the author about it during a visit to Ann Arbor in 2007, in the form (1.3): the futures price is a ratio of two expectations, each over the same stopping time, and one wants to know the price and the delivery strategy. A ratio of expected accumulated reward to expected discounted time also appears in the forward-induction description of the Gittins index for a diffusion in [13]; this suggests looking for a bandit behind the contract. Lindensjö [15] gave the definition and the formula (1.3) rigorously in a general incomplete market with an adapted interest rate, obtained two explicit cases (if the discounted underlying is a submartingale, deliver at once; with discrete dividends, deliver at activation or at dividend dates), characterized the futures price process when the end-of-month option is combined with the timing option of [4], and wrote that “there is to our knowledge no general way of directly calculating” m∗.This paper characterizes the optimal delivery strategy through a universal index and reduces the endogenous price problem (1.2) to a provably convergent sequence of standard optimal stop-ping problems, which we implement in specific models. The index comes from the representation theorem of Bank and El Karoui [2], applied to the excess process Zt = e−rt Xt − E[e−rT XT | F t],which vanishes at T . For each fixed price m, the resulting signal K gives an optimal delivery rule: deliver at the first time Kt ≤ m before the fixed expiry T , or at T if no such time occurs (Theorem 2.1). Related work. The obligation to exercise by expiry also appears in the FX “open forward” contract. Hok and Tse [12] solve its exercise problem for a given fixed exchange rate under geometric Brownian motion, allowing negative interest rates. They characterize the exercise boundary and its near-expiry behaviour. A change of numeraire identifies their stopping problem with that of Subsection 3.2 at a given delivery price (Remark 3.1). Thus the boundary structure and square-root asymptotic have precedents in their work, the latter through the American-option analysis of Lamberton and Villeneuve [14]. Our derivation identifies the coefficient as the value of a Brownian ratio problem and yields the asymptotic behaviour of the critical delivery-window length as q ↑ r. The futures problem also requires the endogenous price m∗,selected by the zero-value condition (1.2). Concluding remarks 

Björk asked for the price of a contract that is a ratio of two expectations and for the delivery strategy behind it. The answer is that the contract is a bandit: the short holds a project whose reward is the convenience yield of the underlying—a proportional flow in the constant-yield model and a stochastic flow in the Gibson–Schwartz model—and retires by delivering. The futures price is the retirement reward at which the project is worth the spot, delivery is optimal the first time the project’s Gittins index falls to the price, and the index is the Bank–El Karoui signal of the excess of the discounted price over its terminal expectation; Dinkelbach’s iteration computes the price from standard stopping problems with a guaranteed rate. A remaining question is the robust version of the problem under drift uncertainty. Dinkel-bach’s reduction survives: the short’s price is the zero of m 7 → sup τ sup P∈P EP[Dτ (m − Xτ )] ,the long’s that of the inf P version, and the fair price becomes an interval, with Theorem 2.3 available for the short’s end. The representation theorem and hence the index do not survive as such: under a sublinear expectation the signal would have to be an essential supremum over stopping times and priors, and the level-crossing structure, which rests on the linearity of the representation, is not available. This calls for a different set of tools and is the subject of a separate paper.
