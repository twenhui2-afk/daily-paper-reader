# CLIS-MPPI: Closed-Loop Model Predictive Path Integral Control With Initial-State Sampling Under Execution Delay 

Zidong Liu 1 and Xu Chen †,1

Abstract — Sampling-based motion planning such as Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control has become attractive for robotics due to its ability to handle nonlinear dynamics and non-smooth objectives without gradient information, while leveraging parallel rollout computation for real-time perfor-mance. However, in real-world robotic systems, the optimized control input is often applied after a non-negligible planning delay, causing a mismatch between the state used in forward simulation and the actual state at control execution, which can significantly degrade planning accuracy and may even lead to unsafe behavior such as collisions. This paper proposes Closed-Loop MPPI with Initial-State Sampling (CLIS-MPPI), which explicitly addresses execution-time state mismatch by modeling planning delay as a distribution over plausible execution-time initial states, performing rollout optimization from sampled initial states, and applying a rollout-based local feedback correction during control execution. The proposed method is validated through real-world experiments on a 6-DoF UR5e manipulator in a narrow-passage task involving obstacle avoidance and end-effector target reaching. Across 5 real-world runs of the same task, standard MPPI collided in 2 runs, whereas CLIS-MPPI remained collision-free in all 5 runs. Averaged over all collision-free runs for each method (3 for MPPI and 5 for CLIS-MPPI), CLIS-MPPI reduced the average task completion time from 25.4 s to 24.4 s and the average end-effector path length from 0.456 m to 0.425 m. It also reduced the overall joint-position and joint-velocity root mean square errors (RMSEs) from 0.100 rad and 0.108 rad/s to 0.017 rad and 0.011 rad/s, respectively. These results demonstrate improved safety, planning-execution consistency, and motion smoothness. 

I. INTRODUCTION With the rapid growth of computing hardware, sampling-based motion planning methods [1], [2], especially Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control [3], have emerged as a powerful framework for real-time robotic motion planning and control. MPPI repeatedly samples stochastic control rollouts, performs forward simulation, evaluates trajectory costs, and refines the control sequence through a path-integral weighted update over a receding horizon. Compared with tra-ditional gradient-based model predictive control (MPC) [4], MPPI does not require differentiable cost functions, making it particularly suitable for robotic systems with nonlinear dynamics, obstacle-rich environments, and complex non-smooth objectives [5]. Furthermore, unlike learning-based methods such as reinforcement learning [6], which typically depend on large-scale offline training, MPPI is a fully online planning method and can leverage GPU-parallelized rollout computation [7] to enable real-time decision-making.

Despite these advantages, the performance of MPPI in real-world robotic systems can be significantly degraded by execution-time delay. In particular, the quality of MPPI critically depends on the accuracy of its forward simulation and cost evaluation, since both are used to determine the control update at each planning cycle. However, forward sim-ulation is typically the most computationally expensive step in MPPI, and the resulting planning latency can introduce a substantial mismatch between the state used for optimization and the state at which the optimized control is actually applied. This issue becomes particularly critical in high-frequency control settings or when the robot operates with large control actions, where even a small planning delay may produce a significant state deviation. In safety-critical tasks such as obstacle avoidance in narrow spaces, such mismatch can cause severe performance degradation and even collisions, despite the fact that the planned rollouts themselves are collision-free during forward simulation. To address the delay-induced state mismatch, we propose closed-loop MPPI with initial-state sampling (CLIS-MPPI), as illustrated in Fig. 2. The key idea is to explicitly convert execution delay into an initial-state uncertainty at each plan-ning cycle, and to compensate for the resulting mismatch arXiv:2610.21239v1 [cs.RO] 15 Oct 2026 through a closed-loop control update. Instead of assuming that the control optimization starts from a single measured state, CLIS-MPPI samples a distribution of perturbed initial states around the currently measured state to represent the possible system evolution during the planning interval. For each sampled initial state, perturbed control sequences are generated and parallel forward simulations are performed to obtain rollout trajectories and their associated costs. Based on the weighted rollout distribution, a nominal state-control trajectory is constructed, from which a local feedback gain is further extracted in a rollout-based manner. The final control input is then computed as a closed-loop combination of the MPPI feedforward update and a state-feedback correction term. In this way, the proposed method enables the applied control command to better match the actual robot state at execution time, thereby improving consistency between planning and execution, motion smoothness, and safety in delay-sensitive real-world tasks. In contrast to standard MPPI, which assumes immediate actuation after planning and samples only control perturba-tions from a single planning state, CLIS-MPPI replaces this assumption with a distribution over plausible execution-time initial states, jointly samples both initial-state and control perturbations, and augments the control update with rollout-based feedback correction. 

A. Related Work 

MPPI was originally introduced for aggressive au-tonomous driving tasks, where its sampling-based formu-lation demonstrated strong capability in handling high-speed, nonlinear systems under complex objectives [3]. Since then, MPPI has been extended to a wide variety of robotic platforms, including robotic manipulators [5], [8], [9], aerial robots [10], and legged systems [11]. In robotic manipulation, STORM [5] and RAMP [8] showed that GPU-accelerated MPPI can achieve real-time obstacle avoidance and goal-reaching on 6–7 Degrees of Freedom (DoF) manipulators in cluttered environments. For dynamic environments, Zhou et al. [9] proposed a parallel MPPI framework for manipulators that improves computational efficiency and incorporates a gradient-velocity modulated signed distance field cost for dynamic obstacle avoidance. Zhai et al. [10] developed a perception-aware MPPI method for quadrotor navigation in unknown environments, where perception-driven objectives are integrated into the planning process to promote exploration and path recovery under occlusion. Delay-induced state mismatch causes the optimized con-trol input to be applied to a state that is no longer consistent with the one used during planning, which may lead to degraded performance and even unsafe behavior. To improve the safety of MPPI, several works have combined MPPI with control barrier functions (CBFs) [12], [13], [14]. Tao 

et al. [12] proposed SCBF-MPPI, which constrains the sampling distribution using stochastic control barrier func-tion conditions to improve probabilistic safety and sample efficiency. Parwana et al. [13] combined an MPPI planner with a CBF-based safety filter so that MPPI handles task planning while the CBF layer enforces safe execution. Rabiee and Hoagg [14] proposed GS-MPPI, which incorporates a composite CBF into the system dynamics and enables MPPI to optimize only over guaranteed-safe trajectories. In a different direction, Belvedere et al. [15] proposed Feedback-MPPI, which augments MPPI with local feedback gains extracted from rollout sensitivity analysis to improve closed-loop responsiveness. However, these methods mainly focus on safety filtering or high-frequency feedback enhancement, and do not explicitly address the execution-time state mis-match caused by planning delay. 

B. Contributions 

The main contributions of this paper are as follows: 1) We propose CLIS-MPPI, a novel MPPI formulation that explicitly compensates for execution-time state mismatch. Unlike standard MPPI, which implicitly assumes immediate actuation after the planning stage, CLIS-MPPI models planning delay as a distribution over plausible execution-time initial states, jointly sam-ples both initial-state and control perturbations, and computes the applied control input using a closed-loop combination of the MPPI feedforward update and a rollout-based local feedback correction. 2) We validate CLIS-MPPI through real-world experi-ments on a UR5e manipulator in a narrow-passage task involving both obstacle avoidance and end-effector target reaching. Across 5 real-world runs of the same task, standard MPPI collides with the environment in 2 runs, whereas CLIS-MPPI remains collision-free in all trials. Averaged over all collision-free runs for each method (3 for MPPI and 5 for CLIS-MPPI), CLIS-MPPI reduced the average task completion time from 25.4 s to 24.4 s and shortened the average end-effector path length from 0.456 m to 0.425 m. In addition, the overall joint-position and joint-velocity root mean square errors (RMSEs) were reduced from 0.100 rad and 0.108 rad/s under MPPI to 0.017 rad and 0.011 rad/s under CLIS-MPPI, corresponding to reductions by factors of approximately 5.9 and 9.7, respectively. EXPERIMENTS In this section, we validate the proposed CLIS-MPPI through real-world experiments on a 6-DoF manipulator for obstacle-avoidance and goal-reaching tasks. As we shall observe, compared with standard MPPI, the experimental results verify the effectiveness of the proposed method in narrow-space obstacle avoidance, achieving collision-free execution, shorter total path length, and significantly lower error relative to the reference state.

As a consequence, the robot motion becomes smoother and more stable, with less noticeable hesitation or jerky behavior during obstacle avoidance and target reaching. IV. CONCLUSIONS This paper proposed CLIS-MPPI, a delay-aware closed-loop MPPI for real-world robotic systems. By explicitly modeling planning delay as initial-state uncertainty and com-bining MPPI with online state-feedback correction, the pro-posed method substantially mitigates the mismatch between the state used in forward simulation and the actual state at which the control command is applied. As a result, CLIS-MPPI effectively improves the safety of MPPI in delay-sensitive tasks, for example by preventing collisions that can arise from delay-induced state mismatch, while also producing smoother and more stable robot motion during execution. The effectiveness of the proposed method was validated through real-world experiments on a 6-DoF UR5e manipulator, where CLIS-MPPI demonstrated improved col-lision avoidance, state consistency, and motion smoothness compared with standard MPPI.
