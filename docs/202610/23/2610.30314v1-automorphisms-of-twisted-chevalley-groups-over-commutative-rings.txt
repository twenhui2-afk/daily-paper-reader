AUTOMORPHISMS OF TWISTED CHEVALLEY GROUPS OVER COMMUTATIVE RINGS 

ELENA I. BUNINA AND DEEP H. MAKADIYA 

Abstract. We classify all abstract automorphisms of the elementary and full twisted Cheval-ley groups of types 2Aℓ (ℓ ≥ 3), 2Dℓ (ℓ ≥ 4), 2E6, and 3D4 over arbitrary commutative rings, for every invariant isogeny type. The coefficient automorphism θ has order dividing that of the diagram automorphism. We assume that 2 is invertible and, for triality, that 3 is invertible as well. Three features distinguish the answer from the usual inner–diagonal and coefficient decom-position. The elementary groups of types 2A3 and 2A4 admit an additive family of exceptional automorphisms supported at θ-fixed maximal ideals with residue field F3. In type 2D4, dia-gram automorphisms may vary on idempotent components, subject to compatibility with the involution and the isogeny lattice. In triality, the coefficient automorphisms preserve the trace operator r 7 → r + θ(r) + θ2(r) but need not commute with θ. We determine which elemen-tary automorphisms extend to the full groups and describe the remaining central factors. Anonzero exceptional unitary factor extends precisely in the simply connected type 2A3, giving a classification for the hyperbolic group SU 4(R, θ ; Q4). The proof glues uniquely determined normalization data for a finite root frame and then recovers the coefficient action from root-subgroup relations. 

Introduction 

An abstract automorphism of a group defined over a ring need not preserve its root subgroups or act coefficientwise in a chosen representation. The automorphism problem asks how much of the defining algebraic structure is determined by the group law. For twisted Chevalley groups, this includes a coefficient automorphism as well as the ring. We classify automorphisms of elementary and full twisted Chevalley groups over arbitrary commutative rings. In most types, every elementary automorphism is induced by an adjoint group element and a ring automorphism commuting with the coefficient operator. Three cases modify this answer: nilpotent exceptional parameters in 2A3 and 2A4, componentwise diagram symmetries in 2D4, and trace-preserving coefficient actions in 3D4. For full groups, central automorphisms account for the ambiguity of extension, while the low-rank unitary cases also have an extension obstruction.

Relation to earlier work. The study of automorphisms of linear groups began with the field-level results of Schreier and van der Waerden and the subsequent work on classical groups by Rickart and Dieudonn´ e [32, 31, 18]. For Chevalley groups over fields, the standard descriptions of automorphisms and abstract isomorphisms were developed by Steinberg, Humphreys, and Borel–Tits [36, 37, 25, 6]. Over rings, early results include those of Hua and Reiner on the integral unimodular group and of O’Meara on linear groups over integral domains [24, 28]. Automorphisms over local and general commutative rings were studied by Pomfret and McDonald, Waterhouse, Petechuk, and others [30, 45, 29]. The work of Golubchik and Mikhalev and of Zelmanov established broad isomorphism theorems over associative rings containing 1 /2, under the appropriate rank hypotheses [22, 46]. For unitary groups, Golubchik and Mikhalev worked under hyperbolic-rank and central-unit assumptions [23]; Zelmanov’s method used graded Lie algebras and Jordan systems [46]. Ismagilova obtained an isomorphism theorem under suitable idempotent hypotheses [26]. Their hypotheses do not cover the arbitrary-ring hyperbolic-rank-two situation considered here. The exceptional family in 2A3 and 2A4 explains why the lower-rank answer differs from the stable one. For Chevalley groups over commutative rings, the structural work of Abe, Suzuki, Vaserstein, Vavilov, and others [1, 3, 4, 43, 44] provided the tools for local automorphism theorems and their extension to arbitrary commutative rings [8, 9]. Twisted groups and their central coverings were constructed over rings by Abe [2]; their normal subgroups, elementary subgroups, and centers were studied by Suzuki [40, 42, 41]. More recent normal-structure, normalizer, and factorization results are due to Garge and Makadiya [19, 20, 21]. The local inputs for this paper are provided by [11, 12, 13, 14, 10]. Passing to arbitrary rings requires more than applying a local automorphism theorem at each maximal ideal: alocalized global automorphism has a possibly nonlocal source and need not be surjective onto its local target. We use the available relative results and prove the additional normalization and recognition statements needed for these maps. We then glue the resulting data and handle central isogenies and passage to the full groups.
