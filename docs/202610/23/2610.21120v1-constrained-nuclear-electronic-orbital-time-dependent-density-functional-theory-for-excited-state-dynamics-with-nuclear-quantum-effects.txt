## Constrained Nuclear-Electronic Orbital Time-Dependent Density Functional Theory for Excited-State Dynamics with Nuclear Quantum Effects 

Yiwen Wang, 1 Zehua Chen, 1 Xi Xu, 2 and Yang Yang 1, ∗

Nuclear quantum effects can strongly influence excited-state processes involving light nuclei, yet their inclusion in practical molecular dynamics remains challenging. We develop constrained nuclear-electronic orbital time-dependent density functional theory (CNEO-TDDFT), which incorporates quantum-nuclear delocalization into full-dimensional effective excited-state potential energy surfaces while retaining the formal structure and computational scaling of conventional TDDFT. Analytic gradients are also developed, enabling efficient on-the-fly excited-state dynamics. CNEO-TDDFT substantially improves excited-state vibrational frequencies involving hydrogen motion and reduces the root-mean-square deviation from experiment from 113 cm −1 to 26 cm −1. For excited-state pro-ton transfer in 10-hydroxybenzo[ h]quinoline (HBQ), CNEO-TDDFT yields a characteristic transfer time of 14.0 fs, compared with 25.5 fs from conventional TDDFT, consistent with the experimentally inferred ultrafast timescale of 12 ± 6 fs. These results establish CNEO-TDDFT as an accurate and efficient framework for excited-state dynamics with nuclear quantum effects. 

INTRODUCTION 

Nuclear quantum effects (NQEs) can strongly influ-ence physical and chemical processes, particularly those involving light nuclei. These effects often include zero-point effects arising from quantum nuclear delocalization as well as tunneling, and their importance in ground-state properties and dynamics has been widely studied.[1, 2] NQEs can also play an important role following electronic excitation, where changes in electronic structure rapidly reshape the underlying potential energy surface (PES) and can strongly affect hydrogen-bonding and proton-transfer processes dominated by light-nuclear motion.[3, 4] Accurate descriptions of such excited-state properties and dynamics therefore require treatment of NQEs on excited-state PESs, which remains computationally chal-lenging. Widely used excited-state electronic-structure methods, such as time-dependent density functional the-ory (TDDFT), conventionally treat nuclei classically,[5] whereas combining ab initio electronic-structure calcula-tions with path-integral approaches is generally computa-tionally demanding for on-the-fly simulations of realistic molecular systems.[1] A practical framework that can ac-curately and efficiently describe NQEs in excited states is therefore highly desirable. Multicomponent quantum approaches provide a natu-ral starting point by treating some or all nuclei quantum mechanically together with the electrons,[6–8] and mul-ticomponent extensions to electronically excited states have also been developed.[9, 10] However, when a nucleus is represented by a quantum wave function, its conven-tional geometrical coordinate is no longer an independent variable, and the resulting PES is reduced-dimensional with respect to the quantum nuclei. On such a reduced-dimensional PES, adiabatic dynamics breaks down be-cause the entire quantum subsystem, including the elec-trons and quantum nuclei, is assumed to respond instan-taneously to the classical nuclear coordinates. While such an adiabatic separation is justified between elec-trons and nuclei by their large mass difference, it is not justified between quantum and classical nuclei, whose masses are of the same order of magnitude.[11] There-fore, accurate dynamical simulations with this treat-ment generally require computationally expensive cou-pled electron–nuclear quantum dynamics.[12–14] The constrained nuclear-electronic orbital (CNEO) frame-work, recently developed by our group, addresses this problem by constraining the position expectation value of each quantum nucleus to a designated molecular coordinate.[11, 15] The resulting energy is then a func-tion of both the classical nuclear positions and quantum-nuclear position expectation values, leading to a full-dimensional effective CNEO PES analogous to that of the Born–Oppenheimer framework while incorporating nuclear delocalization and nuclear kinetic-energy contri-butions. In contrast to the reduced-dimensional multi-component PES, the restored full-dimensional effective PES allows adiabatic dynamics to be performed with-out unphysically assuming the instantaneous response of quantum nuclei to classical nuclei. This construction has enabled accurate and efficient calculations of a range of ground-state properties and dynamics, including molec-ular structures, vibrational spectroscopy, proton-transfer dynamics, and reaction kinetics.[16–18] However, to date, the development and applications of the CNEO framework have been focused on ground-state properties and dynamics. To extend these capabili-ties to electronically excited states, here we develop con-strained nuclear-electronic orbital time-dependent den-sity functional theory (CNEO-TDDFT). In this formu-lation, linear response is applied to the CNEO ground-state reference under a frozen-nuclear-orbital approxima-arXiv:2610.21120v1 [physics.chem-ph] 15 Oct 2026 2tion for the electronic excitation response, preserving the formal structure and computational scaling of conven-tional linear-response TDDFT. Because the nuclear po-sition constraints are retained, the resulting excitation energies remain functions of the same molecular coordi-nates that define the CNEO ground-state PES, thereby generating full-dimensional effective excited-state PESs that incorporate nuclear quantum effects, particularly quantum-nuclear delocalization effects. We further de-velop analytic excitation-energy gradients with respect to both classical coordinates and quantum nuclear position expectation values, enabling efficient on-the-fly excited-state molecular dynamics on CNEO-TDDFT PESs. We assess the resulting excited-state PESs through vibrational analysis for a series of molecules with experimental excited-state vibrational results avail-able. CNEO-TDDFT substantially improves predicted excited-state vibrational frequencies associated with hy-drogen motion relative to conventional TDDFT. We then apply CNEO-TDDFT to excited-state intramolec-ular proton transfer in 10-hydroxybenzo[h]quinoline. In-cluding NQEs produces markedly faster proton-transfer dynamics than conventional TDDFT, and brings the sim-ulated ultrafast dynamics into closer agreement with ex-periment. These results establish CNEO-TDDFT as an efficient framework for incorporating NQEs into excited-state potential energy surfaces and molecular dynamics. 

> THEORY

In multicomponent quantum theory, some or all nuclei can be described quantum mechanically together with electrons.[6–8] A direct consequence is that the positions of these quantum nuclei no longer appear as independent geometrical variables, so that the resulting energy sur-face is a reduced-dimensional PES and adiabatic dynam-ics on this surface breaks down. In contrast to a conventional electronic PES with classical point-charge nuclei, the nuclear orbitals introduce quantum-nuclear delocaliza-tion and nuclear kinetic-energy contributions. There-fore, the CNEO PES naturally incorporates NQEs, particularly zero-point effects associated with nuclear delocalization.[11] Here, we extend this construction to electronically ex-cited states by formulating linear-response TDDFT on a CNEO ground-state reference. Following the Franck– Condon picture, we employ a frozen-nuclear-orbital ap-proximation for the electronic linear response. Specifi-cally, at a given molecular geometry, the electronic den-sity responds to the excitation while the quantum-nuclear orbitals remain at their CNEO ground-state stationary solutions. The nuclear position constraints therefore nat-urally remain unchanged during the electronic response, and the excitation energy remains a function of the same geometrical variables that define the CNEO ground-state PES. Note that the frozen-nuclear-orbital approximation freezes the nuclear orbitals only during the electronic ex-citation response at a fixed geometry, while their vari-ation with molecular geometry is retained through the underlying ground-state CNEO reference and is also re-flected in the excitation-energy analytic nuclear gradient to be discussed later. > RESULTS Excited-state vibrational analysis

We first assess the CNEO-TDDFT excited-state PESs through harmonic vibrational analysis. Vibrational fre-quencies directly probe the local curvature of a PES around its minimum and therefore provide a stringent test of how NQEs modify excited-state nuclear mo-tion. We consider molecules for which experimental excited-state vibrational frequencies are available, with particular emphasis on modes involving hydrogen mo-tion. The benchmark set includes HCN, CH 2O, C 2H2,C2H2O2, C 3H2O, C 6H6, and C 6H5OH, together with the deuterated isotopologues C 2D2, C 6H5D, C 6H4D2,C6H3D3, C 6H2D4, and C 6D6.[25–33] For each system, the first singlet excited-state geometry was optimized us-ing both conventional TDDFT and CNEO-TDDFT, and harmonic frequencies were calculated using the respective methods. Fig. 1 compares the calculated and experimental fre-quencies for modes dominated by hydrogen motion. Con-ventional TDDFT systematically overestimates most hy-drogen stretching frequencies, whereas CNEO-TDDFT produces a substantial downshift and brings them into much closer agreement with experiment. Improvements are also observed for bending modes involving hydrogen motion. Taken together, the root-mean-square deviation for the modes in Fig. 1 decreases from 112.7 cm −1 with conventional TDDFT to 26.1 cm −1 with CNEO-TDDFT. For vibrational modes with little contribution from hydrogen motion, CNEO-TDDFT and conventional TDDFT yield almost identical frequencies, as shown in Fig. S1. This similarity is expected in part because only H and D nuclei are treated quantum mechanically in the present CNEO-TDDFT calculations. Nevertheless, these modes can still be affected indirectly through changes in equilibrium geometry and coupling to coordinates involv-ing hydrogen motion, and no systematic change relative to conventional TDDFT is observed. Moreover, previous full-quantum ground-state CNEO calculations, in which all nuclei are treated quantum mechanically, show much smaller quantum-nuclear corrections for heavier nuclei 4(a) (b)

> CONCLUSIONS

We have developed CNEO-TDDFT as an efficient framework for incorporating NQEs into full-dimensional effective excited-state potential energy surfaces and molecular dynamics. By formulating linear-response TDDFT on a CNEO reference, the method retains the formal structure and computational scaling of conven-tional TDDFT while incorporating quantum-nuclear de-localization and zero-point contributions into the excited-state PES. Analytic excited-state energy gradients, which include the responses of the electronic orbitals, quantum-nuclear orbitals, and position constraints, further enable efficient on-the-fly dynamics on these quantum-nuclear-corrected surfaces. Excited-state vibrational benchmarks show that CNEO-TDDFT substantially improves the prediction of vibrational frequencies dominated by hydrogen mo-tion. In HBQ, CNEO-TDDFT predicts substantially faster excited-state proton-transfer dynamics than con-ventional TDDFT, with a characteristic timescale in ex-cellent agreement with experiment. Together, these re-sults demonstrate that quantum-nuclear delocalization can significantly reshape the excited-state PES, affecting both local vibrational properties and reaction dynamics. CNEO-TDDFT therefore provides a practical route for incorporating NQEs into excited-state simulations with-out the computational cost of a fully quantum treatment of nuclear dynamics. More broadly, the combination of full-dimensional quantum-nuclear effective excited-state PESs and analytic forces opens the way to routine sim-ulations of photoinduced processes in which electronic excitation drives substantial light-nuclear motion.

> I. STATIONARY LAGRANGIAN FOR THE CNEO-TDDFT EXCITATION ENERGY

In this section and the next, we provide the detailed derivation of the analytic CNEO-TDDFT excitation-energy gradient. The derivation follows the stationary-Lagrangian strategy established for conventional TDDFT deriva-tives [1, 2], extended here to account for the quantum-nuclear orbitals and CNEO position constraints [3, 4]. Because the electronic linear-response problem retains the formal structure of conventional TDDFT under the frozen-nuclear-orbital approximation, several purely electronic steps closely parallel those of conventional TDDFT gradient theory.

III. COMPUTATIONAL DETAILS 

CNEO-TDDFT excitation energies and analytic excited-state energy gradients have been implemented in both PySCF [6–9] and NWChem [10]. The analytic gradients were validated against numerical finite-difference gradients. Two representative results are shown in Table S1, covering cases with one and multiple quantum protons.

CNEO-TDDFT geometry optimizations were performed using the geomeTRIC optimizer [11] interfaced with PySCF, whereas conventional TDDFT excited-state geometry optimizations and normal-mode analyses were per-formed using Gaussian 16. [12] Ground-state sampling and excited-state molecular dynamics simulations were carried out using the Atomic Simulation Environment (ASE).[13] 27 Unless otherwise specified, calculations employed the PBE0 exchange–correlation functional, [14] the aug-cc-pVDZ electronic basis set, [15, 16] and a numerical integration grid containing 99 radial shells and 590 angular points per shell. The response of the atom-centered numerical integration grid to nuclear displacements was included in the ground-state CNEO-DFT gradients for molecular dynamics sampling, but neglected in the CNEO-TDDFT excited-state gradients. In the CNEO calculations, all hydrogen nuclei were treated quantum mechanically. The PB5F nuclear basis set [17] was employed for the excited-state vibrational calculations. For deuterium nuclei, the proton basis exponents were multiplied by the square root of the deuterium-to-proton nuclear mass ratio. No electron– nucleus correlation functional was included. Conventional TDDFT calculations used the same electronic basis set, exchange–correlation functional, and integration grid. For the excited-state vibrational calculations, all Coulomb and exact-exchange contributions were evaluated without density fitting. The ground-state CNEO-DFT calculations used an SCF energy convergence threshold of 10 −9 hartree and an orbital-gradient threshold of 10 −7. The CNEO-TDDFT response equations were converged to a residual threshold of 10 −5. Excited-state geometry optimizations used energy-change and maximum-gradient thresholds of 

10 −6. CNEO-TDDFT excited-state Hessians were evaluated by central finite differences of the analytic gradients with a displacement of 0.001 Å, and harmonic vibrational frequencies were obtained by diagonalizing the corresponding mass-weighted Hessians. Conventional TDDFT calculations employed the default Gaussian 16 SCF, TDDFT, and geometry-optimization convergence criteria, including a root-mean-square (RMS) density-matrix convergence threshold of 10 −8

for the SCF calculations and a response-vector convergence threshold of 10 −6 for the TDDFT response equations. For the excited-state intramolecular proton-transfer simulations, the PB4D nuclear basis set was used in the CNEO-TDDFT calculations. Initial configurations and velocities were sampled from ground-state molecular dynamics at 300 K using an Andersen thermostat and a time step of 0.5 fs. Following equilibration, 50 sets of nuclear positions and velocities were selected to initialize the excited-state trajectories. Each trajectory was propagated for 65 fs in the microcanonical (NVE) ensemble using a time step of 0.5 fs. Density fitting with the aug-cc-pVDZ-JKFIT auxiliary basis set [18] was used for the electron–electron Coulomb and exact-exchange contributions during both ground-state sampling and excited-state dynamics.
