ZERO FORCING ON MAXIMAL OUTERPLANAR GRAPHS: CAPACITIES AND PHASE REPAIR 

ZONGJIAN HAN AND YIRAN LIANG 

Abstract. We give a formula for the zero forcing number of a maximal biconnected outer-planar graph. If its weak dual is a path, then P (G) = Z(G) = 2. Otherwise the branching faces determine a canonical core C and a family of terminal strips. We prove that an optimal forcing process can be chosen to omit the terminal root edges and to satisfy explicit vertex and face capacities on C. Fix full forests on the terminal strips. For an ear allocation θ and an allowed core selection J, let τC (θ, J ) be the least number of root connections whose removal makes the resulting forest executable. Then 

Z(G) = |V (C)| − max 

> θ,J

(|J| − τC (θ, J )).

The path covering number is obtained by maximizing |J| alone. We characterize executable directions by equations associated with blocking circuits. The two-path equations define an obstruction in the first cohomology of a signed contact multigraph. Local replacements and a finite comparison of boundary recurrences give the required normal form. A 56-state recurrence constructs a minimum zero forcing set and a full chronology in linear time from indexed triangle adjacency. We also give exact replacements of rooted blocks and infinite families for which P (G) < Z (G).

1. Introduction 

A zero-forcing process starts from black vertices and uses the rule that a black vertex with exactly one white neighbour may color that neighbour black. Its minimum initial-set size is Z(G). Each completed process decomposes the vertices into directed induced paths, one starting at each initial vertex. If P (G) denotes the minimum number of vertex-disjoint induced paths covering G, then 

P (G) ≤ Z(G).

The difference Z(G) − P (G) measures the obstruction to executing a path cover. Even after its paths are oriented, the prescribed forces may require several paths to wait for one another. For a maximal biconnected outerplanar graph, we describe this obstruction in terms of its polygon triangulation. We first put an optimal forcing process into a normal form. Its core edges satisfy local degree and face conditions, and its terminal strips use fixed forests with selected root connections removed. The remaining direction problem is expressed by blocking circuits among the covering paths. These constructions give formulas for both P (G) and Z(G). 1.1. The core and its capacities. Such a graph is a triangulated polygon. Its weak dual is the tree with one vertex per bounded face and one edge for two faces sharing a diagonal. If this tree is a path, the two-root construction in Section 2 gives P (G) = Z(G) = 2. Otherwise let C be the union of the faces in the smallest subtree containing all degree-three vertices. Every remaining component is a chain of triangles attached to an outer side of C; we call it a terminal strip. A strip with at least two triangles is a fan if one root is adjacent to every interior vertex, and a serpent otherwise. In a fan, call that root its key and the other root its nonkey. A single triangle is an ear. Fix the full forests as in Definition 2.7 and the construction following it. A fan requires one root connection at its nonkey; a serpent requires one at each root. For every ear choose one demanded root, and write θ for these choices. If tv demanded connections meet a core vertex v, its remaining capacity is 

cv = 2 − tv ∈ { 0, 1, 2}.

Let R be the terminal root edges. The allowed core selections are 

J (C, θ ) = {J ⊆ E(C) \ R : deg J (v) ≤ cv, |J ∩ E(∆) | ≤ 1 for every core triangle ∆ } .

In particular, a shared key of capacity two may use two core edges. A bare outer side of the core remains eligible. Theorem 4.5 proves that an optimal forcing process can be chosen with J ∈ J (C, θ ). It omits every terminal root edge and uses the fixed terminal forests with some root-to-interior edges removed. 1.2. Exact path and forcing formulas. Put r = |V (C)|. The proof first replaces an arbitrary terminal protocol by one of finitely many modes without increasing its seed cost or its demands on the exterior. A rooted polygon then exposes only four incidence ports, the incoming and outgoing root occupations, and the causal reachability relation among those ports. There are 56 such states. Gluing two child polygons to a triangle is exact on these data: every compatible state pair lifts, and every parent protocol restricts to a compatible pair. To compare the unrestricted recurrence with the recurrence imposing core capacities, we retain their two values on the same partial graph. 2.2. Local tests in a polygon triangulation. We regard a maximal biconnected outerplanar graph with at least three vertices as a convex polygon triangulation. A fixed outerplane embedding justifies this model: biconnectivity makes the outer boundary a simple cycle through all vertices, and maximality makes every bounded face a triangle. The auxiliary single-edge graph will also be used as a rooted block. 

Lemma 2.3. For a polygon triangulation, L ⊆ E is an induced linear forest spanning all vertices if and only if 

(4) deg L(v) ≤ 2 (v ∈ V ), |L ∩ E(∆) | ≤ 1 (∆ a bounded triangular face ).

Consequently P (G) = |V | − max |L| subject to (4) .Proof. If L is a union of induced paths, its vertex degrees are at most two. Two selected sides of a triangle would lie consecutively in one component, and the third side would be a chord of that path. Thus the two local conditions are necessary. Assume now that they hold. Since the selected degrees are at most two, any component which is not a path must be a cycle. Suppose there is a selected cycle with k edges. In a convex polygon, no other polygon vertex lies strictly inside the convex hull of those cycle vertices. The region bounded by the cycle is therefore a triangulated k-gon and contains exactly k − 2triangular faces. Each of its k boundary edges belongs to one face on its inside. Two selected boundary edges cannot belong to the same face, by the face condition. This would inject k

edges into k − 2 faces, a contradiction. Hence every selected component is a path or a singleton. Suppose a selected path has a chord. Take the subpath between the chord’s ends; it has 

k ≥ 2 selected edges. Together with the unselected chord it bounds a ( k + 1)-gon, again with no extra interior vertices. Its triangulation has k − 1 faces. Assigning each of the k

selected boundary edges to its inward face gives the same forbidden injection. Thus the path is induced. The whole selected graph is a spanning forest, whose component number is |V | − | L|.Minimizing that number proves the final formula. □

2.4. Terminal strips. The terminal branches of the weak dual are paths of triangles. We describe their attachments from the root edge in order to compute their forcing modes.
