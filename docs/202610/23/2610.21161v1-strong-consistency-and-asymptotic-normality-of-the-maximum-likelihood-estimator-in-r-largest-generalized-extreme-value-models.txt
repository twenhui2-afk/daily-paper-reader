Abstract 

We study maximum likelihood estimation in the three-parameter r-largest generalized extreme-value ( rGEV) model with a fixed number r of upper order statistics per block. Because the support depends on all three parameters, the classical regular theory does not apply. We show that the maximum likelihood estimator is strongly consistent on compact parameter sets contained in k < 1, where k = −γ denotes the shape parameter. For k0 < 1/2, the range in which the model is differentiable in quadratic mean, the estimator is root-n consistent, asymptotically normal and asymptotically efficient. The proof verifies the conditions of the moving-support likelihood theorem of B¨ ucher and Segers for the ordered r-dimensional likelihood; the main new ingredients are a sequential modified likelihood criterion and a support-loss bound showing that the regularity threshold k0 < 1/2 does not depend on r. In simulated heavy-tailed cases with only 50 blocks, using five to seven order statistics instead of the block maximum reduces the relative bias of the 100-year return level from 6–13% to below 5%. 

Keywords : Asymptotic efficiency; asymptotic normality; maximum likelihood; moving support; strong consistency; r largest order statistics. 

# 1 Introduction 

Likelihood inference for the three-parameter generalized extreme-value (GEV) distribution is not a routine application of regular theory, because the support depends on the parameters. Smith (1985) showed for families with moving endpoints that the local behavior of the density at the boundary determines whether the classical root-n normal theory survives, and Cheng and Traylor (1995) surveyed non-regular maximum likelihood problems more broadly. A rigorous modern theory of maximum likelihood in extreme-value models has since been built in a series of works. In the peaks-over-threshold setting, Drees et al (2004) established asymptotic normality of the maximum likelihood estimator of the extreme value index, and Zhou (2009) settled its existence and consistency. 1arXiv:2610.21161v1 [stat.ME] 15 Oct 2026 For the GEV family, B¨ ucher and Segers (2017) proved strong consistency of the maximum likelihood estimator for γ0 > −1, and gave a general theorem for asymptotic linearity of maximum likelihood estimators in models with parameter-dependent support, which they applied for γ0 > −1/2. In the convention k = −γ used here, these ranges are k0 < 1 and k0 < 1/2. This theorem is the backbone of the present paper: it isolates precisely which properties of a moving-support model have to be checked. A complementary line of work treats the GEV distribution as an approximation to block maxima rather than as the exact sampling model: Dombry (2015) established existence and consistency of the maximum likelihood estimator in the block-maxima framework, Dombry and Ferreira (2019) established asymptotic normality of the maximum likelihood estimator under a second-order condition and quantified the bias of the finite-block approximation, and B¨ ucher and Segers (2018) treated block maxima extracted from stationary time series, including sliding blocks. The rGEV model, which uses the r largest observations of each block (Smith, 1986; Tawn, 1988), is widely used for environmental extremes (Soares and Scotto, 2004; An and Pandey, 2007; Wang and Zhang, 2008; Hamdi et al, 2014; Naseef and Kumar, 2017; e Silva et al, 2020). Dupuis (1997) proposed a robust estimation approach for the r largest annual events. The model has also been extended in several directions (Shin and Park, 2023, 2024, 2025; Busababodhin et al, 2025, 2026; Silva et al, 2022). It is implemented in the R packages ismev (Heffernan and Gilleland, 2025), which accompanies Coles (2001), eva (Bader and Yan, 2020) and evmr (Shin and Park, 2026). The choice of r is studied by Bader et al (2017) and Shin et al (2026). In all of these works the standard errors of the estimates and of derived quantities such as return levels are computed from the inverse observed information; that is, the usual normal approximation for the maximum likelihood estimator is taken for granted. The selection procedure of Shin et al (2026), for instance, imposes this approximation for each candidate r as a regularity assumption. For the exact three-parameter 

rGEV likelihood with moving support, however, it has not been justified. B¨ ucher and Haufs (2026) note that theory beyond the block maximum has remained limited; in a time-series setting they develop a careful theory for blockwise top-two order statistics and show that a naive independence likelihood can be inconsistent under serial dependence. The present paper is complementary: it treats any fixed r, but only for independent blocks with the exact rGEV law. In a companion paper (Park et al, 2026) we showed that the rGEV family with fixed r is differentiable in quadratic mean if and only if k0 < 1/2, that its Fisher information Ir(θ0) is positive definite in this range and equals the negative expected Hessian, and that the model is then locally asymptotically normal. The first is the companion result; the other three need new arguments for the ordered r-dimensional likelihood. The main difficulty lies in the rate. The modified-criterion method of B¨ ucher and Segers (2017) replaces the log-likelihood ratio by a bounded-below criterion built from a power of the density, but for the joint density of the r largest order statistics a single power works only for k0 < 1/3 (Appendix B): the largest coordinate of an observation vector can be close to the moving endpoint while its smallest lies far in the lower tail. For 0 < k 0 < 1/2 we therefore apply the criterion separately to the marginal factor of X(1) and to the conditional factors of X(s) given X(s−1) , and for k0 ≤ 0 we reduce the drift of a joint criterion to that of the first marginal by the data-processing inequality. For the support conditions, the arrival representation shows that only the leading maximum (for k0 > 0) or the smallest retained order statistic (for k0 < 0) can leave the common support, which gives a support-loss probability o(ε2)and an L2 envelope exactly when k0 < 1/2. The regularity threshold is therefore the same for every fixed r as for the block-maximum GEV model. We assume independent blocks without further serial dependence or clustering, which in applica-tions corresponds to filtering or declustering (Tawn, 1988). This assumption can be checked with goodness-of-fit tests based on the conditional distribution functions of successive order statistics, that is, on the Rosenblatt transform of the rGEV vector, which Shin et al (2026) proposed for selecting 

r; a similar construction is used by Heinonen and Neˇ slehov´ a (2026) to detect serial dependence. Inference based on the maximum likelihood estimator (observed information, likelihood ratio tests and the parametric bootstrap) is the subject of ongoing work; for bootstrap consistency in the block-maxima setting see B¨ ucher and Staud (2026). Section 2 recalls the model and the results of Park et al (2026) used here, Section 3 proves strong consistency, Section 4 establishes the rate, asymptotic normality and efficiency, Section 5 examines finite-sample behavior, and Section 6 discusses the results. The proofs are in the appendix.

# 3 Strong consistency 

Consistency does not require differentiability in quadratic mean, and it holds in a wider range of shape parameters than the asymptotic normality of Section 4. Let Pn denote the empirical measure of the independent blocks X1, . . . , Xn, so that Pnf = n−1 Pni=1 f (Xi) for a measurable function f ,and let Θ1 = {(μ, σ, k ) : σ > 0, k < 1}.

Theorem 3.1 (Strong consistency) . Let r ≥ 1 be fixed and let X1, X2, . . . be i.i.d. from the rGEV model with true parameter θ0 = ( μ0, σ 0, k 0), where σ0 > 0 and k0 < 1. Let K be a compact subset of Θ1 with θ0 in its interior. Then, for every n and every sample, the set arg max θ∈K Pnℓr, θ

By Remark 2.1, the condition k0 < 1 is exactly the condition γ0 > −1 of B¨ ucher and Segers (2017, Proposition 3.1). The proof, in Appendix A, is a Wald-type argument (cf. van der Vaart, 1998, Theorem 5.14), adapted as in Dombry (2015) to log-likelihoods that take the value −∞ . Two features of the fixed-r likelihood make it work. First, for k < 1 the log-likelihood is bounded above uniformly in θ ∈ K and in the observation, because (4) is at most −r log σ − Tr + r(1 − k) log Tr.Second, θ 7 → ℓr, θ(x) is upper semicontinuous for every x: as a parameter path approaches the boundary of the support, the log-density tends to −∞ . Identifiability is inherited from the first marginal, which is the ordinary GEV distribution. The restriction to k < 1 is natural: for k > 1 the density is unbounded near the upper endpoint, so that the likelihood is unbounded if such shapes are allowed, as for the GEV model (Smith, 1985, Sections 1 and 7). 64 Root-n rate and asymptotic normality

# 5 Finite-sample behavior 

To see how well the normal approximation of Theorem 4.4 and Corollary 4.6 describes the maximum likelihood estimator at realistic sample sizes, we simulated n ∈ { 50 , 200 } independent blocks from the rGEV model with θ0 = (0 , 1, k 0) and r ∈ { 1, 3, 5, 7}, with 2000 replications for each setting. Since heavy-tailed distributions are the most relevant ones for environmental and financial extremes, 9we put the emphasis on k0 ∈ {− 0.4, −0.3, −0.2}, and add k0 ∈ { 0, 0.2, 0.4} for comparison. Heavy tails. For k0 ∈ {− 0.4, −0.3, −0.2} the asymptotic approximation is accurate at n = 200 for every r: the standard deviation ratios lie between 0 .98 and 1 .09, and the relative bias of the return level is at most 3%. At n = 50 the block-maximum estimator ( r = 1) of the return level is noticeably biased upwards and more variable than the asymptotic theory predicts, with relative biases of 6–13% and ratios of 1 .26–1 .42. Additional order statistics remove most of this discrepancy: with r = 5 or 7 the relative bias falls to 1 .3–4 .4% and the ratio to 1 .04–1 .12. At k0 = −0.4, for example, the relative bias decreases from 12 .5% for r = 1 to 4 .4% for r = 7, and the ratio from 1.42 to 1 .07. Thus, in heavy-tailed cases, the lower order statistics improve not only the asymptotic efficiency quantified in Corollary 4.6, but also the accuracy of the normal approximation for small numbers of blocks. The shape estimator behaves well throughout, with ratios between 1 .02 and 1 .04 for r = 7 already at n = 50.

is an ordered dependent vector, and both the likelihood and its support involve several order statistics. The parameter-free arrival representation (3) reduces this structure to exponential spacings and shows that only the leading maximum or the smallest retained order statistic can reach the moving boundary. The root-n argument requires different devices in different shape regimes: for 0 < k 0 < 1/2 a sequential modified criterion built from the marginal and conditional likelihood factors, and for k0 ≤ 0 a joint criterion whose quadratic drift is reduced to that of the first marginal by the data-processing inequality. The sequential criterion is a proof device, not a new estimator: it is evaluated at the maximum likelihood estimator, which need not maximize it. The same strategy should apply to other fixed-dimensional order-statistic likelihoods whose supports are described by 11 finitely many smooth endpoint inequalities and whose local scores have an L2 envelope. We have aimed to keep the proofs checkable by hand. Through the arrival representation (3), every probabilistic bound reduces to moments of Gamma variables and exponential spacings, and apart from standard empirical-process results (van der Vaart, 1998) the analytic steps use only Taylor expansions of Tθ and elementary inequalities. Appendix A proves consistency, Appendix B the root-n rate, and Appendix C the support-loss and Lipschitz conditions, so that each lemma can be matched to the condition of B¨ ucher and Segers (2017, Proposition 2.1) that it verifies. 

6.2 Scope and extensions 

The efficiency result (Corollary 4.5) also gives a benchmark for other estimators of the rGEV parameters. Robust procedures such as that of Dupuis (1997) trade some efficiency at the model for protection against outliers, and Ir(θ0)−1 quantifies this price. Between the two thresholds, for 1 /2 ≤ k0 < 1, Theorem 3.1 still gives consistency, but the model is not differentiable in quadratic mean (Park et al, 2026). In the leading coordinate the rGEV density has the endpoint form c(x − ϑ)α−1 with α = 1 /k 0 studied by Smith (1985, Theorem 3 and Section 7), who showed that the classical √n normal theory applies when α > 2 but not when 

α ≤ 2. We expect the same non-regular behavior for every fixed r, since only X(1) approaches the endpoint, but do not pursue this here. Allowing r = rn → ∞ would create a growing-dimensional observation vector and a sequence of information matrices Irn (θ0). The appropriate normalization could then depend on the rate at which information grows with rn, and uniform versions of the support and empirical-process arguments would be required. If the rGEV model is viewed as an approximation arising from blocks of an underlying sample, the rate of growth of rn would additionally have to be related to the underlying block size, as in the block-maxima analysis of Dombry and Ferreira (2019). A second extension is data-adaptive selection of r. If 

P (brn = r0) −→ 1for a fixed finite r0, the present results supply the fixed-model component needed for an oracle-type post-selection argument. When the limiting selection probabilities do not collapse to a single value, however, the distribution of bθn, brn may depend on the selection event and need not reduce to a single normal law. This question is particularly relevant to procedures that choose how many largest order statistics to retain from the data (Bader et al, 2017; Shin et al, 2026). For the procedure of Shin et al (2026), the present results supply the consistency and asymptotic normality assumed there for every fixed candidate r whenever k0 < 1/2. Finally, the exact-model assumptions made here separate the behavior of the estimator from dependence and finite-block approximation. Smith (1986) already emphasized that r should remain small when the asymptotic largest-order-statistic approximation is used in practice, and B¨ ucher and Haufs (2026) show in a top-two time-series setting that naive independence-based likelihood procedures can fail under serial dependence and develop bias-corrected alternatives. Extending the present theory to dependent blocks remains open. 12 7 Conclusion 

For the exact three-parameter rGEV model with a fixed number r of order statistics, the maximum likelihood estimator is strongly consistent on compact parameter sets contained in k < 1. Whenever 

k0 < 1/2, the range in which the model is regular, it is root-n consistent, asymptotically linear with influence function Ir(θ0)−1 ˙ℓr, θ0 , asymptotically normal and asymptotically efficient. The regularity threshold is the same for every fixed r as for the block-maximum GEV model, because only the leading maximum or the smallest retained order statistic can leave the support of a shrinking parameter neighborhood. These results justify the large-sample normal approximation that is routinely used with r-largest models, and they are the basis for likelihood-based inference with a fixed number of upper order statistics.
