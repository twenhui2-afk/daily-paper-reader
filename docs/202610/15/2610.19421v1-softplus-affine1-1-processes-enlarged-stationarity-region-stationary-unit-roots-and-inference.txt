# Softplus Affine(1 , 1) Processes: Enlarged Stationarity Region, Stationary Unit Roots, and Inference 

Yang Lu ∗

, Fukang Zhu †

October 15, 2026 

Abstract 

Softplus affine(1 , 1) models keep the conditional mean positive while allowing coefficients of either sign. We study them under Poisson, negative binomial, gamma and related conditional distributions. A stationary solution with moments of all positive orders exists whenever the coefficient sum is less than one, strictly enlarging previously known sufficient regions. At the unit root, the coefficient sum equals one. Under an invertibility condition, a stationary solution exists in this case if and only if the intercept is negative. At a stationary unit root, the conditional mean has a power-law tail with an explicit index, and the observations can exhibit long memory. For a fixed softplus scale, the Poisson quasi-maximum likelihood estimator is consistent and asymptotically normal at the usual √n rate below and at the unit root, with the coefficient sum left unrestricted in estimation. At the unit root, these results require only a finite mean and yield standard Wald inference even when the observations have infinite variance or long memory. Simulations and two applications examine finite-sample inference and sensitivity to the softplus scale.

Mt = sc(ω + αX t−1 + βM t−1), (1) where ω, α, β are real parameters, c > 0 is fixed, and sc(x) = c log(1 + ex/c ), x ∈ R, denotes the softplus transformation. They also establish the following sufficient condition for stationarity: max(0 , α ) + max(0 , β ) < 1, |β| < 1. (2) This condition leaves open how far signed feedback can extend stationarity and whether the boundary α + β = 1 can itself support a stationary process with conventional inference. These questions also arise when the conditional law is not Poisson. In this paper, we first extend the softplus INGARCH model beyond conditional Poisson ob-servations. We retain the conditional-mean recursion (1), but allow infinitely divisible conditional families indexed by Mt, with mean Mt and variance proportional to Mt. This includes the Poisson, Gamma, negative-binomial, inverse-Gaussian, and Tweedie families. We call the resulting models softplus affine(1 , 1) processes. Letting ϕ = α + β, we establish the following common sufficient region for stationarity and moments of all positive orders: 

ϕ < 1,

which strictly enlarges the region covered by condition (2). In particular, this condition places no lower bound on ϕ, as the softplus transformation is naturally lower bounded by 0. We also show that under some additional assumptions, the process is also geometrically β-mixing. We then study the boundary case ϕ = 1, with |β| < 1, which we call the unit-root case. For large Mt, the state dynamics resemble a random walk with drift and state-dependent fluctuations. Michel (2020) shows that a unit-root INGARCH(1 , 1) process is nonstationary. In contrast, we show that the softplus affine(1 , 1) process is stationary if and only if the intercept ω is negative. Its stationary state has a power-law tail, and its dependence is more persistent than below the unit root and can even feature long memory. When ω < 0, this approximate random walk has negative drift, bringing Mt back towards lower levels, while softplus keeps Mt positive. Viewed as a one-state stochastic recurrent network, the softplus affine model shows that state–observation feedback can produce stationary long memory. Finally, we establish consistency and asymptotic normality of the Poisson quasi-maximum likelihood estimator (QMLE). The estimator is consistent both below the unit root and at the stationary unit root, with the same √n convergence rate (where n denotes the sample size) for all dynamic parameters, provided the process has a finite marginal mean. This differs from the conventional nonstationary unit-root affine setting, in which estimators of different parameters can have rates such as n, √n, or √log n. For conditionally Poisson observations, we further derive the 2local power of the unit-root Wald test against stationary alternatives when ϕ is close to 1. Section 2 establishes the stationarity and dependence results. Section 3 develops estimation and unit-root inference, followed by simulations in Section 4 and empirical applications in Section 5. Section 6 concludes; proofs and additional simulation results appear in the appendices.

2.5 Link with the neural network time series literature 

In recurrent-network terms, Mt is a scalar recurrent state, softplus is the activation function, and the stochastic output Xt feeds back into the state recursion (1). In the related literature, Leisch et al. (1998) establish stationarity and geometric ergodicity for stable autoregressive neural networks. Zhao et al. (2020) show short memory under contraction and motivate memory-augmented architectures. For a constant-gate memory-augmented gated recurrent unit, Yang et al. (2025) establish polynomially decaying input–output coefficients. Such coefficient decay does not by itself prove that the autocorrelations of a stationary endogenous output are nonsummable. In contrast, Theorem 4 establishes nonsummability directly. Filter contraction under |β| < 1 is compatible with long memory in the endogenous output.

# 6 Conclusion 

The central finding of this paper is that a unit root need not imply nonstationarity, a result that originates in the nonlinearity of the softplus link. In the linear model, positivity of the conditional mean requires a nonnegative intercept, and a unit-root process then has no restoring force. The softplus link removes this constraint. At high levels it is asymptotically linear, so the model retains the local dynamics of a linear unit-root process. At low levels its curvature ensures a positive conditional mean irrespective of the sign of the intercept. A negative intercept is therefore admissible, and it induces a negative drift that returns the process from high levels. The resulting unit-root process is stationary, yet it differs from a conventional short-memory process: its stationary distribution has a power-law tail, and the observations can exhibit long memory. The same nonlinearity explains why conventional inference remains valid. Because the process is stationary at the unit root, standard asymptotic theory applies in place of nonstandard unit-root limit theory. A finite mean suffices for the Poisson QMLE to be asymptotically normal at the usual rate and for the unit-root Wald test to have a standard normal limiting null distribution, even when the observations have infinite variance or long memory. Because these results depend on the nonlinearity, they are sensitive to the scale parameter that governs it. In the simulations and in both applications, estimated persistence increases with the fitted scale, so that an excessively large scale can generate a spurious unit root. We therefore recommend that unit-root tests be reported over a grid of scales. Joint estimation of the scale and the dynamic parameters is left for future research.
