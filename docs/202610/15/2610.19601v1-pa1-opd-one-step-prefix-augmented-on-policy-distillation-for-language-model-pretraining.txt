# PA1-OPD: One-Step Prefix-Augmented On-Policy Distillation for Language Model Pretraining 

Zhenhe Wu 1, Jian Yang 2, Yaping Jin 1, Xinlai Kang 1, Jie Hu 1, Hanting Chen 1*1Huawei Technologies, 2Beihang University

Abstract 

Knowledge distillation (KD) improves language-model pretraining by matching teacher distributions at corpus prefixes, but provides no direct supervision at contexts induced by student predictions. We introduce PA1-OPD, which augments corpus-path distillation with teacher feedback after one student-sampled transition per selected prefix. This expands the supervised states while retain-ing next-token prediction and corpus-path KD. Data-anchored roots support parallel sampling, and differentiable tree attention shares prefix computation without changing inference. In two continued-pretraining settings, full coverage improves held-out cross-entropy over KD across C4, PG-19, and Pile at every evaluated checkpoint. In Stage2, selecting the 10% of roots with highest student surprisal adds 48.85% to KD’s Macro cross-entropy gain over next-token training. Gains remain when branch supervision excludes successor states already on the corpus path, supporting the value of feedback beyond observed contexts. Additional depth or breadth improves aggregate results at unchanged per-term supervision weight, with benefits varying by corpus. These findings support local student-induced supervision as a complement to pretraining distillation. 

1 Introduction 

Knowledge distillation (KD) enriches pretraining with a teacher’s predictive distributions beyond observed next-token targets (Hinton et al., 2015). Conventional pretraining KD applies these targets at corpus prefixes. Student predictions can induce different contexts, leaving their successor states without direct supervision. Beyond the choice of distillation loss, this raises the question of which prediction states should receive teacher feedback. 

> ∗Corresponding author.

On-policy distillation learns from student-generated trajectories (Ross et al., 2011; Agarwal et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2024). For pretraining, cor-pus prefixes provide many roots from which to sample student transitions. Even one sampled to-ken creates a successor state with a new teacher target. This allows us to test whether local feed-back beyond the corpus trajectory improves KD without generating complete continuations. We propose PA1-OPD , one-step prefix-augmented on-policy distillation (Figure 1). At each selected corpus prefix, the current student samples one token. Both models consume it, and the teacher supervises the student’s next-token distribution at the resulting successor state. This branch objective complements next-token predic-tion and corpus-path KD. Its on-policy component is local: the transition follows the current student, while its root remains anchored to observed data. Data-anchored roots support parallel sampling across positions, and tree attention shares their pre-fixes while keeping branches independent. Differ-entiable student prefixes retain branch gradients; coincident-state reuse avoids reevaluating corpus states; and chunked KL reduces temporary proba-bility storage. Student-surprisal selection controls where feedback is applied. These operations leave the student architecture and inference unchanged. We evaluate two OLMo2 continued-pretraining settings. Full coverage improves on KD across all three evaluation corpora in both settings. In Stage2, selecting 10% of roots adds 61.60%, 22.24%, and 57.12% to KD’s CE gains over next-token training on C4, PG-19, and Pile (48.85% Macro). Gains persist without branch losses at coincident corpus states. Stage1 favors full coverage over Top10, while deeper or wider supervision improves Macro CE in both settings. 3.3 Objective-Preserving Execution 

When a sampled token matches the next corpus token, we reuse the available successor logits with student gradients intact and evaluate only un-matched branches. Chunked KL limits temporary probability storage with full-vocabulary normaliza-tion. Both preserve the sampled objective: reuse re-tains coincident-state losses, and chunking retains the complete teacher distribution. Appendix A de-tails the execution and reuse conditions.

4.2 Main Results Local supervision adds to corpus-path KD. 

Full PA1 improves CE and PPL over KD on all three datasets at every evaluated checkpoint in both settings (Table 1). After 10B tokens, its Macro CE is lower by 2.87 × 10 −4 in Stage1 and 2.29 × 10 −4

in Stage2, adding 4.65% and 27.69% to KD’s gain over NTP. The direction of improvement holds across the reported training trajectory and does not depend on a single evaluation corpus or checkpoint.

5 Analysis 

We examine three aspects of the added supervision: whether gains require coincident corpus states, how root selection distributes feedback, and whether deeper or wider signals add further benefit. Com-parisons follow the controlled protocols in Sec-tion 4.1 and Appendix B.1. 

5.1 Supervision Beyond Corpus States 

A branch can revisit a corpus-path state when its sampled token matches the observed continuation. To test whether these repeated targets are neces-sary, we remove only the corresponding branch KL terms while retaining the original normalization. The resulting variant still improves on KD on every dataset (Table 2). Its Macro CE is 2.531165, com-pared with 2.531173 when coincident terms are retained. The small difference does not establish an advantage for removal; the substantive result is that feedback at noncoincident student-induced states remains useful. Loss removal changes super-vision; state reuse instead preserves all loss terms while avoiding redundant state evaluations. 

5.2 Allocating Feedback Across Roots 

Sparse selection improves the controlled Stage2 results (Figure 2): Macro extra/KD increases from 26.63% with full coverage to 40.04% with Top10. Every tested sparse variant improves on Full PA1 for each dataset. C4 favors the smallest tested bud-get, whereas PG-19 and Pile favor broader cover-age. Equation (10) retains nominal branch weight while concentrating it on fewer roots, so coverage controls both the number and distribution of su-pervised states. 6 Related Work 

Distillation and student-induced supervision. 

KD transfers predictive distributions, sequences, and representations (Hinton et al., 2015; Kim and Rush, 2016; Sanh et al., 2019; Jiao et al., 2020); pretraining applications study compression, data refinement, and compute allocation (Gemma Team, 2024; Muralidharan et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2025; Busbridge et al., 2025). DAgger and sched-uled sampling motivate learning at learner-induced states (Ross et al., 2011; Bengio et al., 2015), and ImitKD, GKD, and MiniLLM apply related principles to distillation (Lin et al., 2020; Agar-wal et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2024). Subsequent work explores objectives and data reuse (Ko et al., 2024, 2025; Jin et al., 2026), interleaved sam-pling (Xu et al., 2025), and token or rollout-prefix selection (Huang et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026; Zhao et al., 2026). Broader efforts explore task-specific supervision and context construction (Wu et al., 2026c,b, 2025b), alongside cache-aware ex-pert management for MoE inference (Wu et al., 2026a). PA1-OPD focuses on a complementary design choice: applying forward-KL supervision after local student transitions from corpus prefixes during pretraining. 

Additional computation and execution. Re-peated tokens, pause tokens, and multi-token pre-diction add computation or prediction targets (Wu et al., 2025a; Goyal et al., 2024; Gloeckle et al., 2024). PA1-OPD adds teacher-supervised training states without changing inference, adapting shared-prefix trees from speculative verification (Miao et al., 2024; Cai et al., 2024) for differentiable student computation. Cut Cross-Entropy avoids global logit materialization (Wijmans et al., 2025); our chunked KL retains logits but reduces probabil-ity workspace, preserving the teacher distribution. 

7 Conclusion 

PA1-OPD augments pretraining KD with teacher supervision after local student-generated transi-tions. Full coverage improves on corpus-path KD in both evaluated continuation settings, and Stage2 Top10 adds 48.85% to KD’s Macro CE gain over NTP. Gains remain without coincident-state branch losses, supporting feedback beyond observed cor-pus states. Additional depth or breadth further im-proves aggregate results at fixed per-term weight, while coverage and corpus determine the returns. Teacher-supervised state allocation thus offers a practical design dimension for improving pretrain-ing distillation with local student feedback. 8Limitations 

Our evaluation covers one teacher–student fam-ily, two continuation settings, and three held-out corpora. Each condition uses one training run, so independent-seed replication is needed to assess the small absolute improvements. The results establish observed likelihood gains in these settings, not gen-erality across model families, scales, languages, or downstream tasks. The main KD and PA1 objectives also allo-cate their nominal distillation weights differently; additional controls could separate weighting ef-fects from supervision location. Our experiments cover PA1 and its extensions to two sequential steps (PA2) or two branches per root (dual PA1), with fixed per-term branch weights. Future work could extend PA1 to PA x with x > 2 and explore wider branching to examine how supervision depth and breadth affect pretraining gains. Comparisons match training tokens rather than compute, and state counts do not measure runtime or FLOP ef-ficiency. These boundaries leave open how the observed gains scale with longer pretraining and additional teacher feedback. 

Ethical Considerations 

This study uses publicly available language mod-els and existing pretraining and evaluation corpora, with no human participants or collection of new personal data. Distillation may inherit biases or harmful content from the teacher and training data. Our likelihood evaluations do not assess these ef-fects, and no method-specific ethical risks were identified in the reported experiments.

B Experimental Details 

Backbone models. The frozen OLMo 2teacher (Team OLMo et al., 2025) is 

OLMo-2-1124-7B (32 layers, hidden size 4096). Both students share the OLMo-2-0425-1B archi-tecture (16 layers, hidden size 2048) and Dolma2 tokenizer. Section 4.1 defines the initializations; Stage2 uses commit a1847dff . Continuations load student weights with a fresh optimizer. 

Datasets. For Stage1, we draw approximately 10B tokens according to the official token-weighted proportions of its seven-component mixture, includ-ing configured source repeats (chiefly DCLM, with code, scientific, mathematical, and Wikipedia text). For Stage2, we follow the six-component propor-tions of its 50B-token Dolmino-Mix-1124 recipe (DCLM, math, FLAN, Wikipedia, PeS2o, and StackExchange). In each setting, sampling selects nonoverlapping contiguous blocks aligned to 2048-token sequences; all methods share the data and order. Each run consumes exactly 10,001,317,888 training tokens. We evaluate on fixed tokenized files from C4 validation (web text) (Raffel et al., 2020), PG-19 test (long-form books) (Rae et al., 2019), and Pile validation (multi-domain text) (Gao et al., 2020). All complete 2048-token windows are scored, yielding 9.998M, 9.871M, and 9.998M evaluation tokens, respectively. 

Implementation details. The main experiments are conducted on a single training node equipped with eight accelerator cards. Training uses BF16 autocasting, 2048-token sequences, one sequence per GPU per microbatch, and a global batch of 2,097,152 tokens (1024 sequences). Each run takes 4,769 optimizer steps, with checkpoints at approxi-mately 2.5B, 5B, 7.5B, and 10B tokens. AdamW uses (β1, β 2) = (0 .9, 0.95) , gradient clipping at 1, and weight decay 0.033 in Stage1 or 0.1 in Stage2; embeddings are exempt from decay. Stage1 uses an initial learning rate of 10 −4 with its inher-ited cosine schedule (zero warmup, final ratio 0.1, 

Tmax = 1 ,907 ,359 steps); Stage2 uses a constant 

3 × 10 −6. Branch attention selects FlexAttention when available, with 256-query reference chunks otherwise; full-vocabulary KL uses chunks of 8192. Model and data seeds are 12536 and 34521, respec-tively. Evaluation uses batch size four.

Root coverage and scoring. Student Top10, Top25, and Top50 retain the indicated percent-age of eligible roots in each sequence using stu-dent surprisal and the normalization in Eq. (10). Coincident-state supervision. The removal ab-lation retains Student Top50 selection but zeros branch KL terms for sampled successors that co-incide with available corpus states. NTP, RealKD, and the original branch denominator remain un-changed. Both variants use coincident-state reuse.
