Abstract 

Generalized Pellet’s theorem is extended to analytic matrix-valued functions using Descartes’ rule of signs for infinite series. Cauchy’s estimate is incorporated to allow numerical computation of the bounds. To address the issue that Pellet’s theorem and its generalizations may not always guarantee the existence of bounds, for the case of matrix size one, we employ the Dandelin– Lobachevsky–Graeffe method, which ensures the existence of the bounds for the roots and further improves them as the order of the transform increases. Without the need for any a priori information on the function, these theorems allow us to compute mathematically verified bounds for the roots in a certain region using an interval arithmetic implementation of the function. Numerical examples and an implementation are provided to illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed method. 

Keywords: Pellet’s theorem, Graeffe iteration, Interval arithmetic, Verified computation, Nonlin-ear eigenvalue problems 

# 1 Introduction 

Nonlinear eigenvalue problems refer to the problem of finding z ∈ C such that det F (z) = 0, where 

F : C → Cl×l is a matrix-valued function. If l = 1, the problem reduces to finding the roots of a scalar function. In this work, F is assumed to be analytic. There are multiple methods to solve these problems, including iterative methods (e.g., Newton’s method) and contour integration-based methods [1, 2]. However, to locate all roots in a certain region, in most cases it is necessary to have a priori information about the number and approximate location of the roots in the region. In particular, for contour integration-based methods, the contour must not be too close to the roots to reduce the numerical error of the integration. Thus, the localization of roots remains an important problem. Bindel and Hood [3] proposed several methods to localize nonlinear eigenvalues, including ex-tensions of Gershgorin disks and pseudospectral regions. Michailidou and Psarrakos [4] developed further Gershgorin-type sets for the case where F is a matrix polynomial. However, the compu-tation of these sets may require checking if a certain condition holds for all points in the region, which may be computationally expensive.

In this work, an annulus x1 < |z| < x 2 where no roots exist, obtained by Pellet’s theorem or its extensions, is called a “Pellet bound.” A Pellet bound is said to be “improved” if the annulus widens (i.e., x1 becomes smaller, x2 becomes larger, or both) because the possible region of the roots becomes smaller. Niu, Sakurai and Sugiura [7] proposed a method to compute verified Pellet bounds for the roots of analytic functions using Pellet’s theorem, Cauchy’s estimate and interval arithmetic. However, the two methods are used separately and a certain condition [7 (2.5)] must be met for the Pellet bound obtained by Pellet’s theorem to be valid, which may not always be the case. In this work, both methods are deeply integrated using Descartes’ rule of signs for infinite series [8 §1] to eliminate the need to check such additional conditions. Theorem 1.1 has been recently extended by Bini, Noferini and Sharify [9 Theorem 2.1] and Melman [10 Theorem 3.3] to matrix-valued polynomials as follows. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In section 2, the direct extension of Pellet’s theorem for analytic matrix-valued functions is proved. In section 3, the extension is further improved by incorporating Cauchy’s estimate to allow numerical computation of Pellet bounds. In section 4, the known results on verified computation of Fourier coefficients are summarized, which 2allow verified computation of the bounds. In section 5, the convergence of the bounds obtained by applying the Dandelin–Lobachevsky–Graeffe method is proved. Finally, in section 6, numerical examples using interval arithmetic are provided to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method.
