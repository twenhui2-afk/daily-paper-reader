## ABSTRACT 

Long-horizon agentic RL is constrained by the futures visited by the current pol-icy. When rollouts fail to reach useful behaviors, improved credit assignment can-not provide supervision for states and actions that were never encountered. On-policy distillation (OPD) introduces teacher guidance on policy-induced states, and recent multi-turn variants improve how this guidance is scheduled, replayed, refined, or validated. However, they do not directly use the oracle-controlled future itself as the training trajectory. We introduce State-Rooted Executable Rollout Optimization (SERO) , which augments on-policy agentic RL with exe-cutable oracle continuations. SERO restores a state actually reached by the current policy and lets a training-only oracle continue interacting with the real environ-ment, making the downstream observations and oracle actions themselves avail-able for learning. It then applies policy-reachability weighting , which weights each oracle step by the frozen behavior policy’s compatibility with the preceding oracle actions. This allows deeper supervision to become effective as the policy becomes more compatible with the oracle branch. The oracle loss is optimized separately from the base RL estimator and no oracle is used at inference time. We evaluate SERO on two challenging interactive-agent benchmarks, ALFWorld and WebShop, using Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. On ALF-World, learning the full executable continuation is substantially more effective than supervising only the root oracle action, while policy-reachability weighting further improves out-of-distribution performance. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Large language models and vision-language models are increasingly used as the decision-making backbones of interactive agents for embodied control, web navigation, tool use, and software tasks (Yao et al., 2022b; Shinn et al., 2023; Schick et al., 2023; Brohan et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023; Xie et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2026; Hong et al., 2024; Gur et al., 2024; Xia et al., 2026; Gou et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2026; Rong et al., 2026). Unlike single-turn reasoning, an agent must repeat-edly observe the environment, choose an action, receive a new observation, and continue until the task is finished (Ma et al., 2026). This sequential decision-making setting provides a natural oppor-tunity for reinforcement learning (RL), where policies can be improved directly from environment feedback (Sutton & Barto, 2018; Schulman et al., 2017; Ouyang et al., 2022; Guo et al., 2025; Tan et al., 2024; Zhai et al., 2024; Bai et al., 2024). Outcome-based group RL assigns trajectory-level credit, state-level group RL compares sampled actions at shared states, and OPD distills a single corrective action. In contrast, SERO restores a state reached by the current policy, executes a multi-step oracle continuation, and applies policy-reachability weighting to downstream action losses. However, these methods typically assign a single outcome-based advantage to all actions in a tra-jectory, which makes credit assignment a central difficulty as interaction horizons grow. An episode may contain many decisions, while reward is often sparse or delayed (Peng et al., 2019; Wen et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025; Song et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025b; Jin et al., 2025). Recent work there-fore seeks more precise feedback for the trajectories collected by the current policy (Feng et al., 2026; He et al., 2026; Cheng et al., 2026). These methods substantially improve how sampled ex-perience is credited. Nevertheless, the learning signal is still bounded by what appears in the rollout group. If every rollout misses a useful action sequence, finer attribution can rank the sampled behav-iors more accurately, but it cannot provide the missing behavior as a positive target. This limitation is particularly important early in training and on difficult tasks, where successful branches may be absent from an entire group. In such cases, the missing targets must be supplied from outside the rollout group, which moti-vates teacher guidance. Interactive imitation and off-policy guidance learn from expert behavior on learner-induced inputs (Ross et al., 2011; Sun et al., 2017; Yan et al., 2026). OraRL shows that priv-ileged supervision can be optimized directly, while on-policy distillation (OPD) queries a teacher on states reached by the current policy (Li et al., 2026b; Agarwal et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2026). Recent multi-turn OPD methods extend teacher guidance over multiple steps or use subsequent policy roll-outs to validate an intervention (Wang et al., 2026f; Liao et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026). However, their supervision is still centered on a corrective action, a teacher prefix, or the outcome of the in-tervention, rather than the full missing branch. In interactive environments, executing a correction changes the next observation and therefore the decisions that follow (Ross et al., 2011; Ma et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2022). When these downstream transitions are not directly learned, the training signal captures only part of the benefit of the correction and leaves its long-horizon consequences under-supervised. We propose State-Rooted Executable Rollout Optimization (SERO) to learn from executable multi-step oracle continuations rooted at policy-visited states. As shown in Figure 1, SERO first collects the ordinary on-policy trajectories required by the base RL algorithm. It then selects a state actually reached by the current policy, restores the environment at that state, and lets a training-only oracle continue interacting for multiple steps. Every oracle action is executed in the environment, so each later target is conditioned on the real observation produced by the preceding oracle actions. The resulting continuation therefore shows not only what action should be taken at the root state, but also the sequence of decisions that follows once that correction is actually carried out. Deeper states in an oracle continuation are increasingly off-policy, since reaching them requires the current policy to follow the preceding oracle actions. We therefore introduce policy-reachability weighting . A frozen behavior policy scores each oracle action, and the supervision weight at step k

2is determined by the compatibility of the preceding oracle prefix under the behavior policy. Within a branch admitted by the improvement gate, the root target receives unit prefix weight, while deeper targets receive larger weights as the policy becomes more compatible with the oracle prefix. This down-weights supervision on poorly supported oracle prefixes and allows the effective supervision horizon to extend as compatibility improves. The oracle objective is added as an auxiliary loss, leaving the rewards, advantages, and policy ratios of the base RL algorithm unchanged, and the oracle is used only during training. We evaluate SERO on ALFWorld (Shridhar et al., 2020b) and WebShop (Yao et al., 2022a) with Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. Adding SERO to GraphGPO or HGPO improves ALFWorld success and WebShop task success at both scales. We also compare against prompting and strong RL baselines, and ablate root-only oracle targets, uniformly weighted full oracle continu-ations, and policy-reachability weighting. On ALFWorld, full executable continuations outperform root corrections, with reachability weighting adding further gains, especially on unseen tasks. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose State-Rooted Executable Rollout Optimization (SERO) , which turns an oracle correction into a multi-step source of executable training experience. Starting from a state reached by the current policy, SERO executes the oracle in the environment and learns the downstream oracle actions under the observations created by that intervention. • We introduce policy-reachability weighting as a reachability-aware way to learn from oracle-induced states. By tying each downstream target to the behavior-policy compati-bility of the preceding oracle prefix, SERO suppresses supervision on poorly supported oracle-induced states and lets deeper guidance become effective as the policy improves. • We design SERO as an auxiliary objective that complements existing on-policy credit as-signment while leaving the base RL credit estimator unchanged. Experiments at both model scales show improvements in ALFWorld success and WebShop strict success for both GraphGPO and HGPO, while controlled ablations isolate the effects of executable continuation learning and reachability weighting. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Group-based RL for long-horizon agents. Group-based RL has become a practical alternative to actor–critic training for large language models by replacing a learned value function with relative comparisons among sampled rollouts (Ahmadian et al., 2024; Shao et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2025). In interactive tasks, however, sparse and delayed re-wards make it difficult to determine which decisions actually helped. Recent work has also extended online RL to self-evolving web curricula and end-to-end multi-turn interaction, while newer meth-ods study hindsight credit assignment and uncertainty-guided exploration in long-horizon agents (Qi et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2025; Tan et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026d). Recent agentic RL therefore moves from one advantage per trajectory toward finer signals recovered from the collected experi-ence, using repeated states, interaction history, or cross-trajectory transition structure to refine credit assignment (Zhou et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2025; Feng et al., 2026; He et al., 2026; Cheng et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026a). These methods improve how the policy learns from its own rollouts, but they share a structural limit: supervision is still extracted from behavior already present in the sampled group. If every rollout misses a useful branch, a better estimator can re-rank the observed decisions but cannot provide that missing branch as a positive target. 

Teacher guidance on learner-induced states. Imitation and distillation address the same diffi-culty from another direction by querying an expert on states induced by the learner rather than only replaying fixed demonstrations (Ross et al., 2011; Sun et al., 2017; Agarwal et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2024; Yan et al., 2026). Related approaches also construct corrective supervision from failed trajectories or expert interaction experience (Yuan et al., 2025; Lu et al., 2026). OPD and recent multi-turn extensions bring this principle to language-model post-training, aligning teacher supervi-sion with the current policy and extending it through prefixes, refined trajectories, or short teacher interventions (Li et al., 2026a; Sun et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026f; Liao et al., 2026; Jiang et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026). Their common goal is to align teacher guidance with the learner’s current 3behavior. SERO instead learns the executable future induced by the correction: after restoring a policy-visited state, the oracle continues the branch, and the resulting observations and downstream actions are used directly for training. 

Oracle-guided optimization. Privileged information can also enter RL directly as an optimiza-tion signal. OraRL shows that an annotation can serve as a positive target rather than only a reward reference, while CAST uses solver information to refine turn-level credit (Li et al., 2026b; Wang et al., 2026g). SERO shares the motivation that finite on-policy samples can benefit from reliable privileged supervision, but interactive dynamics make the target fundamentally sequential: execut-ing an oracle action changes the environment and creates new states. SERO therefore keeps the original on-policy estimator unchanged and learns a separate executable oracle continuation rooted at on-policy experience. Policy-reachability weighting then controls the influence of deeper oracle-induced states according to the behavior-policy compatibility of the preceding oracle prefix.

## 4 PROPOSED METHOD 

4.1 OVERVIEW 

SERO is a modular, oracle-augmentation framework that can be integrated with different on-policy RL objectives without modifying their underlying credit-assignment mechanisms. As is shown in Figure 2, each training iteration has three stages. First, the base RL method collects ordinary on-policy rollouts and computes its original RL objectives. Second, SERO selects a state from an on-policy trajectory, restores the environment to that decision point, and lets an oracle execute a fresh multi-step continuation. Third, the oracle actions are optimized with weights that reflect policy support for the oracle prefix leading to each downstream state. Oracle data are used only by the auxiliary branch and never enter the reward statistics, advantages, or importance ratios of the base method. 4.2 EXECUTABLE ORACLE CONTINUATION

## 5 EXPERIMENTS 

We evaluate SERO on long-horizon interactive decision making with two complementary goals. First, we ask whether learning an executable oracle continuation improves the policy beyond outcome-based RL and single-step oracle supervision. Second, we isolate whether the gain comes from the executable oracle continuation or from policy-reachability weighting. 5.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Benchmarks and baselines. ALFWorld evaluates multi-step household instruction following in an interactive text environment (Shridhar et al., 2020b). We report success on the in-distribution and out-of-distribution splits ( In-Success and Out-Success ). WebShop requires an agent to search, navigate product pages, select attributes, and purchase an item (Yao et al., 2022a); following prior work, we report both task score and strict task success rate. We compare against prompting baselines (Qwen2.5, ReAct (Yao et al., 2022b), and Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2023)), critic-based PPO (Schul-man et al., 2017), group-based RLOO (Ahmadian et al., 2024) and GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), and recent long-horizon agentic RL methods GiGPO (Feng et al., 2026), GraphGPO (Cheng et al., 2026) and HGPO (He et al., 2026). 

Models and training. Our main experiments use Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. We use the ALFWorld expert planner and a goal-metadata oracle for WebShop as training-time oracles. An eligible group contains at least one failed rollout, from which one pre-action state is sampled as the oracle root. Unless otherwise noted, SERO uses one root per eligible group and

Figure 4 shows why local com-patibility alone is insufficient for weighting a long oracle contin-uation. In Figure 4(a), zk fluc-tuates with depth and often re-mains relatively high even for later oracle actions. This is be-cause zk only measures whether the action at the current oracle state is compatible with the be-havior policy; it does not ac-count for whether the policy could follow the preceding or-acle actions to reach that state. Figure 4(b) captures this miss-ing information through the pre-fix weight wk = Q

j<k zj . Once an earlier oracle action has low compatibility, the weights of all subsequent actions are reduced accordingly. As a result, wk drops quickly at the early checkpoint, while the later checkpoint maintains larger weights over a longer portion of the continuation. As training progresses, SERO therefore makes use of increasingly deeper oracle supervision, while avoiding assigning the same weight to every downstream oracle action. 96 CONCLUSION 

We presented SERO, a framework that incorporates executable oracle continuations into multi-turn agentic RL. Starting from states visited by the current policy, SERO restores the environment, ex-ecutes oracle continuations, and uses the resulting action sequences as auxiliary supervision. To better utilize long-horizon guidance, we introduce policy-reachability weighting, which adjusts or-acle supervision based on the compatibility between oracle prefixes and the current policy. With Qwen2.5-1.5B and 7B models, adding SERO improves ALFWorld success and WebShop strict suc-cess for both GraphGPO and HGPO. Ablations show that learning from full executable continuations provides substantially stronger gains than isolated corrective actions, while reachability weighting further improves generalization. These results highlight the value of executable multi-step oracle experience and the importance of accounting for policy reachability when extending supervision beyond immediate actions.

## D TASK PROTOCOLS AND ORACLE BACKENDS 

D.1 ALFW ORLD 

We use the text interface of ALFWorld (Shridhar et al., 2020b). For an oracle branch, the adapter re-constructs the selected on-policy prefix and verifies the reconstructed prompt and admissible actions. It then obtains commands from the environment’s expert-plan interface ( extra.expert_plan ), checks each command against the current admissible actions, and executes it through the ordinary environment interface. For each task group containing at least one failure, one failed rollout is selected and one of its pre-action turns is sampled uniformly as the root; all-success groups are skipped. D.2 WEB SHOP 

WebShop (Yao et al., 2022a) requires search, navigation, option selection, and purchase actions. Root selection follows the ALFWorld rule: from each group with at least one failure, we sample one failed rollout and then one of its pre-action turns uniformly; all-success groups are skipped, and eligibility uses strict success rather than the dense task score. The training-time oracle is a deterministic rule-based policy using the target product identifier and required options from goal metadata. When search is needed, it tries the sanitized retrieval query, full product name, and its first eight words in order; it navigates to the target, selects the required options, and purchases it through the same legal search /click interface as the policy. Restoration reproduces the page/session state, navigation history, selected options, task identity, and observation renderer. We report both task score and strict success, while the oracle gate uses strict task success.

## G LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK 

SERO assumes that the training environment can be restored to a policy-visited state and that an executable oracle is available during training, and our experiments are limited to ALFWorld and WebShop. When exact restoration is unavailable, learned world models and diffusion-based gen-erators could synthesize the observations along an oracle continuation (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018; Hafner et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2024a; Bruce et al., 2024; Ho et al., 2020; Rombach et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2026c;b). Extending SERO to visually grounded embodied settings (Shridhar et al., 2020a) would require grounding the objects named in oracle commands across successive observa-tions. Vision-language pretraining and grounding (Radford et al., 2021; Li et al., 2022b; Kirillov et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2025), multimodally guided segmentation and localization in images and videos (Li et al., 2022a; Yang et al., 2022; 2024b;c; Zhou et al., 2022; Gao et al., 2017; Ma et al., 2022; 2023b; Zhang et al., 2023; Mao et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2023; 2024; Ju et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2025a), and diffusion-based dense prediction (Xu et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2023a; 2025; Yang et al., 2026) are natural tools for this, together with 3D scene representations (Mildenhall et al., 2020; Kerbl et al., 2023; Shi et al., 2025). Finally, oracle roots are sampled uniformly from failed rollouts, so rare failure types may be under-supervised; prioritized sampling (Schaul et al., 2016) and techniques for domain shift and long-tailed data (Ganin et al., 2016; Cui et al., 2019; Ye et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2024) may help prioritize such roots, and scaling beyond 7B models and two benchmarks is left for future work. 24
