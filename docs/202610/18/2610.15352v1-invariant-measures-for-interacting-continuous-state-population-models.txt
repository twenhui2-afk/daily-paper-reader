INVARIANT MEASURES FOR INTERACTING CONTINUOUS-STATE POPULATION MODELS 

LINA JI, JIE XIONG, WEN XU, AND XU YANG 

Abstract. We study invariant probabilities for nonlinear two-population branching systems with competition, cooperation, or predator–prey interaction, driven by Brownian noise and spectrally positive stable jumps. Without immigration, every invariant probability of a con-servative competitive or predator–prey system is concentrated on the boundary under either boundary convention. With nontrivial axis dynamics, coordinatewise continuation admits only the point mass at the origin. For cooperative systems, we give criteria for boundary concentration and interior stationarity, including a sharp criterion for bilinear cooperation with a common relative noise exponent. With immigration, explicit parameter conditions yield existence and stationary moment bounds. For square-root diffusions with positive immigration, these conditions ensure interior concentration and full support on the closed quadrant. Further lower bounds on immigration rates give uniqueness and convergence of time-averaged transition laws. 

1. Introduction and main results 

Continuous-state branching processes, introduced by Jiřina [18], describe the evolution of population mass under independent reproduction and arise as scaling limits of discrete branching processes; see Lamperti [23]. In the subcritical regime, a continuous-state branching process converges to zero almost surely. Continuous-state branching processes with immigration were first studied by Kawazu and Watanabe [19]. Dawson and Li [8] provided constructions of continuous-state branching processes with immigration via stochastic equations as pathwise unique strong solutions, thereby establishing a framework for incorporating nonlinear rates and interactions. The stationary behavior of continuous-state branching processes with immigration was investigated by Pinsky [30] and further developed by Keller-Ressel and Mijatović [20]. In the strictly subcritical case, the existence of a finite logarithmic moment of the immigration Lévy measure ensures convergence in distribution to a unique invariant probability; see Keller-Ressel and Mijatović [20]. Stationary distributions have also been studied for one-dimensional nonlinear branching models, which generally lack the classical branching property. Under suitable dissipativity and noise assumptions, Li and Wang [26] established existence and uniqueness of an invariant probability and exponential convergence in the L1-Wasserstein distance and the total variation norm. For continuous-state branching processes with immigration and competition, Li et al. [24] proved exponential ergodicity in a weighted total variation distance under appropriate conditions. Chen et al. [5] established exponential ergodicity in a weighted total variation distance for models with immigration in Lévy random environments, competition, and catastrophes, under conditions balancing the branching, environmental, and regulatory terms. Related results are available for stochastic Lotka–Volterra systems driven by environmental noise. Without immigration, we distinguish between freezing the system at its first boundary hit and allowing the surviving population to continue along its axis (see Definitions 1.1 and 1.2). For conservative competitive and predator–prey systems, every invariant probability is concentrated on ∂E (see Theorems 1.1 and 1.2). Under coordinatewise continuation and nontrivial axis dynamics, δ(0 ,0) is the unique invariant probability. For cooperative systems, we give sufficient conditions for boundary concentration and for the existence of a nondegenerate invariant probability (see Theorems 1.3 and 1.4). A highlight of the paper is Theorem 1.5, which gives a necessary and sufficient criterion for interior stationarity under bilinear cooperation with a common relative noise exponent. This criterion includes the critical case, where the interaction strengths and branching noise parameters determine whether stationary coexistence is possible. For models with immigration, we give explicit parameter conditions ensuring conservativeness, existence of invariant probabilities, and stationary moment bounds in all three interaction regimes (see Theorem 1.6). Under additional restrictions on the noise exponents, we obtain exact stationary balance identities (see Theorem 1.7). For square-root diffusions satisfying these conditions, positive immigration ensures that every invariant probability is concentrated on E◦

and has full support on E. Further lower bounds on the immigration rates yield uniqueness and weak convergence of the time-averaged transition laws (see Theorem 1.9). The proofs combine test functions, martingale arguments, and boundary-hitting estimates. Without immigration, occupation estimates follow from powers chosen to cancel the interaction terms in predator–prey systems and from the supermartingale property of the total population in competitive systems. For cooperative systems, we construct Lyapunov functions combining negative powers near the boundary with a weighted linear term at infinity. With immigration, weighted linear Lyapunov functions provide occupation estimates and stationary moment bounds. The existence proofs combine the resulting tightness estimates with the weak Feller property, established through localization. The critical criterion for bilinear cooperation further uses stationary identities justified by concave truncation. For square-root diffusions, the analysis also uses localized comparison with Cox–Ingersoll–Ross diffusions, interior ellipticity, and the support theorem to establish boundary behavior, full support, and uniqueness.
