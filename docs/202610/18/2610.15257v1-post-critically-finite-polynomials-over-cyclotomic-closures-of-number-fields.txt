1. Introduction 

A polynomial f (z) ∈ C[z] is post-critically finite (PCF) if and only if the orbit under f of each critical point of f is finite. It follows from the compactness of the connectedness locus in the moduli space of polynomials that every PCF polynomial is conjugate, by a linear change of variables, to a monic polynomial with coefficients in Q. It turns out that the algebraic degree of the coefficients offers a stratification of conjugacy classes of PCF polynomials of a given degree. Specifically, the main result of [8] shows that the coefficients of PCF polynomials written in monic centred form lie in a set of bounded height, and so in particular one has that there are only finitely many PCF polynomials defined over and given finite extension K/ Q, up to change of variables. An analogous situation occurs with torsion points on an abelian variety. If A is an abelian variety defined over a number field K, then A(K) contains only finitely many torsion points, by a standard argument involving heights. Ribet [9] showed something much stronger, namely that A(Kcyc ) contains only finitely many torsion points, where Kcyc is the cyclotomic closure of K, the infinite algebraic extension obtained by adjoining all roots of unity to K. Our main result shows something similar for PCF polynomials. 

Theorem 1. Let K be a number field and d ≥ 2. There are only finitely many monic centred PCF polynomials of degree d with coefficients in Kcyc .

We recall that a polynomial is centred if and only if its roots sum to zero. Since every polynomial f (z) ∈ K[z] is conjugate over K to a centred polynomial, and two monic centred polynomials are conjugate only by z 7 → ζz with ζd−1 = 1, Theorem 1 is equivalent to the statement that there are only finitely many conjugacy classes of monic PCF polynomials with coefficients in Kcyc .Note it follows from Theorem 1 that if B is an affine curve, and f (z) ∈ K[B, z ] is a non-isotrivial family of monic polynomials whose coefficients are regular functions on B, then only finitely many specializations ft for t ∈ B(Kcyc ) are PCF. There are several problems with the proof sketch above. First, it might hap-pen that some of our Laurent polynomials Wn,m vanish identically, for instance if 

any critical point of the generic fibre is preperiodic. One can replace (1) with a product over non-preperiodic critical points on the generic fibre of a given family, but restriction to the torsion coset discussed above may introduce more critical orbit collisions. At least as problematic is that the torsion coset might only be de-fined only over a proper extension of the field K, which changes the output of the result of Dvornicich and Zannier, and consequently the order-of-vanishing bound derived from Haj´ os Lemma. Both of these problems are solved by proving the non-existence of families of polynomials with certain properties over non-trivial tori over any number field, by induction on the dimension of the torus.
