# On the Structural Optimization of Industrial Control Systems Using Metaheuristic Algorithms 

Ruslan Zakirzyanov 

NEXT engineering, LLC 

Kazan, Russia zr@nexteng.ru 

Abstract —The paper addresses the synthesis of a cost-optimal hardware architecture of the automated process control system (APCS) for continuous processes. The problem is formalized as the search for a minimum-cost rooted tree of devices subject to constraints on the number of I/O channels, the types and number of communication interfaces, the information capacity of processors, the regulation time and the reliability. The signals are grouped by control rooms. The problem is NP-hard, and ahybrid method is proposed that combines a deterministic hierarchy construction with a modified ant colony optimization (ACO) for device-type selection, enhanced by an adaptive heuristic. On ten instances the method is compared with a genetic algorithm (GA), particle swarm optimization (PSO), simulated annealing (SA) and CP-SAT over 30 runs each. By the best found cost the hybrid ACO outperforms GA, PSO and SA on all instances with more than 100 IOs (by 14-38 %) and runs 9-14 times faster. By the median cost, however, the deterministic-greedy baselines are more reliable – GA attains the best Friedman rank – because the cost of the ACO runs is highly variable. All differences are confirmed by the Friedman and Wilcoxon tests. The method targets the early-design search for the cheapest feasible architecture and synthesizes one within minutes at industrial scale (A up to 1086). 

Keywords—optimization, process control system, distributed control system, hierarchical architecture, metaheuristic algorithm, ant colony optimization 

I. INTRODUCTION 

Modern distributed automated process control systems of large continuous production facilities in the chemical, petrochemical and oil-and-gas industries are built on control systems integrating hundreds and thousands of technical devices: programmable logic controllers (PLCs), input/output modules, network switches and gateways [1, 2]. The cost, the response time and the reliability of the system are largely predetermined by its hardware architecture, chosen at the early design stages. 

In practice the hardware architecture of the control system is formed empirically, relying on the designer's experience and on manufacturer recommendations, which does not guarantee optimality and leads to excessive costs [3]. It also makes an objective comparison of alternative architectures difficult, as cost, reliability and response time are interdependent. 

The synthesis of optimal hierarchical architectures has been studied in the theory of organizational and technical systems [4-6], where fundamental results for tree hierarchies were obtained. Applied methods of structural optimization of distributed control systems and production complexes were considered in [7-10], including approaches based on simulation modeling [9] and machine learning [10]. Since the problem is NP-hard combinatorial optimization with multiple constraints, metaheuristics [11-13] are a natural choice. They do not guarantee a global optimum but provide high-quality solutions in acceptable time. For problems naturally represented as a graph, ant colony optimization (ACO) [14] is especially convenient and combines well with local search [15]. This algorithm has been applied to the knapsack problem [16], topology optimization [17], chemical-process optimization [18], parametric [19] and routing [20] problems. 

Metaheuristics form a broad family of evolutionary and swarm algorithms, local-search methods and their hybrids [11-13]. Their advantage is finding high-quality solutions in polynomial time and their drawback is sensitivity to the solution representation and to parameter tuning. For problems whose structure is naturally represented as a graph, genetic algorithms are less convenient, as they encode solutions of fixed dimension, whereas ant algorithms construct a solution incrementally, matching the synthesis of a tree hierarchy. 

In [21] criteria for selecting the structure of a distributed APCS were proposed and the applicability of metaheuristics was outlined. The present work goes beyond by contributing a refined mathematical model that jointly accounts for channel capacity, interface types, processor information capacity and reliability, a hybrid deterministic-plus-ACO synthesis method with an adaptive heuristic, and a systematic comparison with GA, PSO, SA and the exact CP-SAT method. Most prior approaches either rely on simplified models that do not jointly capture these constraints or are limited to small dimensions.

III. SOLUTION METHOD 

The hybrid method separates synthesis into a deterministic and a stochastic part. The deterministic procedure builds the tree trunk bottom-up and greedily connects the signals grouped by rooms and types, adding new leaves, intermediate nodes and processors as needed while keeping exactly one processor per path. If the set of admissible types is empty or the reliability constraint is violated, the attempt is infeasible and the construction restarts. The stochastic part – a modified ACO – selects only the type of each added device.

In more detail, the deterministic procedure first builds the trunk: a device admitting signal connection is placed on the bottom level 𝑆 , after which on each level a device with a suitable free port is selected up to the root. The signals are then connected greedily by rooms and types; a new leaf is added when the channels are exhausted, and a new processor (with intermediate nodes built up to the connection point) when the capacity or regulation time is exceeded. The algorithm is compared with genetic algorithm (GA), particle swarm optimization (PSO) and simulated annealing (SA). In GA an individual encodes a sequence of device-type selections with tournament selection, crossover and mutation; in PSO a particle position is a vector of device-type preferences; in SA a neighbor is obtained by replacing a device type with a decreasing acceptance temperature. As an exact method, the CP-SAT model (OR-Tools) is used with constraints (4)-(9) in linear form and a 600 s time limit. 

For reproducibility, every stochastic algorithm is run 30 times per instance with seeds 1...30. The hybrid ACO uses 𝛼 = 2, 𝛽 = 1 , evaporation 𝜌 = 0.25 , 𝑞 = 1 and 𝜏 0 = 1 , with the number of iterations and ants set per instance. The baselines use their default settings: GA – a population of 30 over 30 generations, 3 elites, tournament size 3 and mutation probability 0.3; PSO – a swarm of 30 over 30 iterations with inertia 𝑤 = 0.5 and 𝑐 1 = 𝑐 2 =1.5; SA – 200 cooling steps with factor 0.95, five neighbors per temperature and an auto-calibrated initial temperature; each makes up to 40 attempts to build a feasible initial tree. All algorithms share the same constraint checker, cost function and equipment catalog, so that only the type-selection strategy differs; the full configuration is reproducible from the software [24]. The hyperparameters 𝛼 , 𝛽 

and 𝜌 can be tuned further by Bayesian optimization and the multi-objective NSGA-II, which is treated as a separate problem .

IV. SCIENTIFIC NOVELTY 

The novelty is threefold. First, a model of a distributed APCS that jointly accounts for constraints on the structure, information capacity, reliability, number of I/O channels and types of communication interfaces, with signals grouped by control rooms. Second, a hybrid method combining deterministic hierarchy construction with metaheuristic device-type selection by ACO. Third, an adaptive heuristic dynamically accounting for the hierarchy level, the number of available channels and the branching possibilities. Together they yield, for industrial-scale objects, the cheapest feasible architecture by the best found cost. 

V. COMPUTATIONAL EXPERIMENTS 

The experiments use ten instances: seven synthetic ( A = 9...800 ) and three real ones from a sulfuric-acid production APCS project ( A = 1063, 1067, 1086 ). Synthetic instances are generated by sampling, for each control room, the number, types and directions of the field signals; all algorithms draw device types from the same catalog. Each stochastic algorithm is run 30 times; the best, mean and median cost, the runtime and the feasibility are recorded.

On A = 9 it proves the optimum (cost 790) and slightly beats ACO (805); as the size grows it hits the 600 s limit and returns a non-optimized feasible solution, and for 𝐴 ≥ 400 the linearized model alone needs over 10 GB (MODEL_TOO_LARGE), as it produces millions of Boolean variables. Thus this particular CP-SAT formulation is unsuitable for industrial-scale synthesis; tighter encodings or decomposition might raise the threshold, but exactness at A of several hundred signals remains out of reach for off-the-shelf solvers.

In runtime (Fig. 3) the hybrid ACO is markedly faster: the speed-up over GA is 9.9x at A = 400 and 13.5x at A = 800 , due to the repeated-initialization overhead of GA/PSO/SA, which grows super-linearly with A. A fair engineering criterion is the quality attainable for an equal time budget rather than for an equal number of runs: within the wall-clock time of one GA experiment (30 runs) the hybrid ACO performs from 43 ( A = 200 ) to 426 ( A = 800 ) runs, so its advantage by the best cost only grows. 

A combined cost-times-time indicator (the product of best cost and runtime, smaller is better) summarizes quality and speed jointly: the hybrid ACO outperforms GA by a factor of 3.4 at A = 100 , 10.8 at A = 400 and 18.1 at A = 800 . This indicator, not the runtime alone, is the source of the 18x figure; the pure runtime speed-up stays in the 9-14x range. The efficiency thus grows with the problem dimension, which is valuable in the main working range of industrial APCS (several hundred to over a thousand signals).

The practical significance of the method lies in automating the labor-intensive selection of the hardware structure and in enabling a comparison of architecture variants at early design stages under incomplete information about the controlled object. The comparative study covers A up to 800. On the sulfuric-acid project (1063 signals) it produced, within minutes, a feasible architecture (best cost 4769 c. u.) meeting the reliability and regulation-time constraints, based on REGUL R500 controllers. 

VI. CONCLUSION 

A hybrid method for synthesizing a cost-optimal hardware architecture of a distributed APCS  has  been proposed, 

combining determinist ic hierarchy construction with a

modified ACO and an adaptive heuristic. Experiments on ten 

instances showed that the present CP -SAT formulation does 

not scale ( 𝐴  ≥ 400 ), and that by the best found cost the hybrid 

ACO outperforms GA, PSO and SA on all insta nces with  𝐴  ≥

100  (by 14 -38 %), running 9 -14 times faster (up to  18x  on the 

cost -time indicator). By the median cost, however, the 

deterministic -greedy baselines remain more reliable, because 

the cost of the ACO runs is highly variable; this trade -off, 

conf irmed by the Friedman and Wilcoxon tests, is the main 

limitation to be addressed. The method is implemented as 

registered software [2 4].
