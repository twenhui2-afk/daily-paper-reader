Abstract —Oscillation monitors have root-mean-square (RMS) energy detectors at their core. These detect oscillation in apredefined frequency band by comparing the signal power in that band with a threshold (usually set empirically). A key design decision is the choice of frequency bands. Traditionally, for monitoring the stability of wide-area electromechanical modes, finer bands were set below 1 Hz, while higher frequencies were covered by wider bands. However, this approach can yield poor detection in modern power systems with fast and largely unknown local dynamics. This letter proposes an algorithm to set frequency bands from the ambient noise spectrum to maximize the average detection performance. 

Index Terms —Oscillation detection, power system monitoring, RMS energy, synchrophasors 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

ynchrophasors are critical for tracking power grid dynamics, particularly in the modern grid with a growing number of high-frequency oscillations due to power electronics and a lack of accurate models to analyze them. The real-time detection of electromechanical and forced oscillations is a key function of wide-area monitoring systems. Among automated monitoring tools, the root-mean-square (RMS) energy detector has become an industry standard due to its model-free architecture and low computational cost. The work in [1] championed the use of signal processing techniques on continuous grid data to characterize system dynamics, laying the foundation for modern RMS energy filters. The work in [2] proposed the standard framework for RMS energy detectors, which isolates the band of interest by filtering, followed by squaring and smoothing, yielding a computationally lightweight index reflecting the localized energy of an oscillation. However, early field deployments required intensive tuning, particularly of the alarm threshold. Recent work [3], [4] developed multiple statistical thresholding approaches relying on characterization of ambient noise to minimize false alarms, the more recent of which also adds amplitude-based thresholds to filter out low-impact events. 

Traditionally, RMS energy detectors were mainly used to track the stability of known dynamics, with an emphasis on slow inter-area modes. This is reflected in the bands typically set for frequency signals: a narrow 0.15 –1 Hz band followed by wide 1 –5 Hz (for local modes) and 5 –30 Hz for inverter-based resource (IBR) and other fast dynamics [2]. With the growth of IBRs [5], high-frequency dynamics have become common, altering the ambient noise characteristics. Therefore, the choice
