## Abstract
The paper addresses the synthesis of a cost-optimal hardware architecture of the automated process control system (APCS) for continuous processes. The problem is formalized as the search for a minimum-cost rooted tree of devices subject to constraints on the number of I/O channels, the types and number of communication interfaces, the information capacity of processors, the regulation time and the reliability. The signals are grouped by control rooms. The problem is NP-hard, and a hybrid method is proposed that combines a deterministic hierarchy construction with a modified ant colony optimization (ACO) for device-type selection, enhanced by an adaptive heuristic. On ten instances the method is compared with a genetic algorithm (GA), particle swarm optimization (PSO), simulated annealing (SA) and CP-SAT over 30 runs each. By the best found cost the hybrid ACO outperforms GA, PSO and SA on all instances with more than 100 IOs (by 14-38 %) and runs 9-14 times faster. By the median cost, however, the deterministic-greedy baselines are more reliable (GA attains the best Friedman rank) because the cost of the ACO runs is highly variable. All differences are confirmed by the Friedman and Wilcoxon tests. The method targets the early-design search for the cheapest feasible architecture and synthesizes one within minutes at industrial scale (A up to 1086).
