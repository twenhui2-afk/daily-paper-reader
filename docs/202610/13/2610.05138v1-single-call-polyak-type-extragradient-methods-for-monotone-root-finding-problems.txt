# Single-call Polyak-Type Extragradient Methods for Monotone Root-Finding Problems 

Keke Li 

College of General Education, Chongqing Polytechnic University of Electronic Technology, Chongqing, China 

Abstract 

We propose a single-call variant of Polyak extragradient, termed SPolyakEG, by recy-cling the previous extrapolation operator evaluation. Under a delayed critical condition, we establish that SPolyakEG achieves an O(1 /K ) residual convergence rate for monotone operators and linear convergence under strong monotonicity. Compared with PolyakEG, SPolyakEG reduces the number of operator evaluations from two to one per iteration. 

Keywords: Extragradient methods; Polyak step-sizes; monotone root-finding; single-call meth-ods; adaptive step-sizes 

Mathematics Subject Classification 65K15, 47H05, 90C33, 62L20 

## 1 Introduction 

Finding a zero of a nonlinear operator is a fundamental problem in optimization, machine learning, and equilibrium computation [1]. Given an operator F : Rd → Rd, the root-finding problem aims to find F (x⋆) = 0. A broad class of iterative methods has been developed for solving such problems, including gradient-based methods [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6], extragradient methods [1, 2, 3, 4], and optimistic schemes [1, 5, 6, 7]. The extragradient (EG) method [1, 4, 8] is one of the most representative approaches for solving monotone root-finding problems. At each iteration, EG performs an extrapolation step followed by a correction step, which requires two evaluations of the operator. Specifically, ˆxk = xk − γF (xk), and xk+1 = xk − ηF (ˆ xk). Although EG enjoys favorable convergence guarantees, the requirement of two operator evaluations per iteration becomes computationally expensive when evaluating F is costly. To reduce the oracle complexity, several single-call variants have been proposed by reusing previous operator evaluations. Representative examples include Popov’s method [9], optimistic gradient descent [5, 10, 11, 12], and reflected gradient methods [13, 14, 15]. The main idea behind these methods is to replace the current operator evaluation F (xk) in the extrapolation step by previously computed information. Consequently, only one new operator evaluation is required per iteration. Recently, Polyak-type adaptive stepsizes have been investigated for extragradient methods [2, 3]. The Polyak extragradient method dynamically adjusts the stepsize by using the inner product between the extrapolation direction and the operator value. However, existing Polyak extragradient schemes still require two operator evaluations per iteration, since the extrapolation step is constructed by the current operator value F (xk). This motivates the following question: Can Polyak-type adaptive stepsizes be incorporated into single-call extragradient schemes while preserving convergence guarantees? 1arXiv:2610.05138v1 [math.OC] 4 Oct 2026 In this paper, we answer this question affirmatively by proposing a single-call Polyak extra-gradient method (SPolyakEG). Instead of using the current operator value, SPolyakEG reuses the previously computed value:ˆ xk = xk − γkF (ˆ xk−1). After obtaining the new operator eval-uation F (ˆ xk), the Polyak stepsize is computed as αk = ⟨F (ˆ xk ),x k −ˆxk ⟩∥F (ˆ xk )∥2 . Therefore, SPolyakEG requires only one new operator evaluation per iteration. The main challenge introduced by the single-call strategy is the delayed operator information in the extrapolation step. To address this issue, we introduce a delayed critical condition 

∥F (ˆ xk) − F (ˆ xk−1)∥ ≤ A∥F (ˆ xk−1)∥. Based on this condition, we establish a convergence theory for SPolyakEG. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: 

• We propose SPolyakEG, a single-call variant of Polyak extragradient methods, which reduces the number of operator evaluations from two to one per iteration. 

• We introduce the delayed critical condition and establish several fundamental properties, including bounds on consecutive operator values and adaptive stepsizes. 

• For monotone operators, we prove a Fej´ er-type descent inequality and establish the sub-linear residual convergence rate min 0≤k≤K ∥F (ˆ xk)∥2 = O(1 /K ). 

• For strongly monotone operators, we prove that SPolyakEG achieves linear convergence under a suitable stepsize condition. 

• We compare the oracle complexity of SPolyakEG with the classical Polyak extragradient method and demonstrate that SPolyakEG achieves the same order of convergence rate with reduced operator evaluations. 

## 2 Preliminaries 

We consider the monotone root-finding problem find x⋆ ∈ Rd such that F (x⋆) = 0 , (1) where F : Rd → Rd. Throughout the paper, Rd denotes the d-dimensional Euclidean space, 

⟨· , ·⟩ denotes the standard inner product, and ∥ · ∥ denotes the associated Euclidean norm. We assume that the solution set X ∗ := {x ∈ Rd : F (x) = 0 } is nonempty. When the solution is unique, we denote it by x⋆.We first recall the Lipschitz continuity of the operator. 

Definition 2.1 (L-Lipschitz continuity) . The operator F : Rd → Rd is L-Lipschitz continuous if there exists L > 0 such that 

∥F (x) − F (y)∥ ≤ L∥x − y∥, ∀x, y ∈ Rd.

We also recall the monotonicity condition. 

Definition 2.2 (Monotonicity) . The operator F : Rd → Rd is monotone if 

⟨F (x) − F (y), x − y⟩ ≥ 0, ∀x, y ∈ Rd.

Unless stated otherwise, F is assumed to be monotone. For linear convergence, we addi-tionally consider strong monotonicity. 2Definition 2.3 (μ-strong monotonicity) . The operator F : Rd → Rd is μ-strongly monotone if there exists μ > 0 such that 

⟨F (x) − F (y), x − y⟩ ≥ μ∥x − y∥2, ∀x, y ∈ Rd.

## 3 Single-call Polyak-type extragradient algorithm (SPolyakEG) 

The proposed SPolyakEG method reduces the number of new operator evaluations by reusing the previously computed value F (ˆ xk−1) in the construction of the extrapolation point. Specif-ically, the standard evaluation F (xk) used in PolyakEG is replaced by the recycled operator evaluation F (ˆ xk−1). This modification leads to a single new operator evaluation per iteration after initialization. 

3.1 From extragradient to single-call Polyak-type extragradient

## 4 Convergence analysis 

We establish the convergence properties of SPolyakEG under the delayed critical condition introduced in the previous section. We first derive several consequences of this condition, including bounds on consecutive operator evaluations and the Polyak stepsize. We then establish a sufficient condition under which the delayed critical condition is guaranteed for Lipschitz continuous operators. Based on these properties, we derive a Fej´ er-type descent inequality and an O(1 /K ) rate for the squared operator residual. Finally, under μ-strong monotonicity, we establish linear convergence of the iterates.

## 6 Conclusion 

We proposed SPolyakEG, a single-call variant of the Polyak extragradient method for solving the root-finding problem F (x⋆) = 0. The method recycles the previously computed extrapolation operator evaluation, reducing the number of new operator evaluations from two to one per iteration after initialization. To analyze the effect of the delayed operator information, we introduced a delayed critical condition and established the resulting bounds on consecutive operator values and the adaptive Polyak stepsize. These properties ensure the positivity of the adaptive stepsize and lead to a Fej´ er-type descent inequality. Under monotonicity, this yields an O(1 /K ) convergence rate for the squared residual evaluated at the extrapolation points. Under strong monotonicity and a suitable stepsize condition, we further establish linear convergence of the iterates. For Lipschitz continuous operators, we also provide sufficient conditions on the extrapolation stepsize under which the delayed critical condition is satisfied. From the computational perspective, the main advantage of SPolyakEG is its reduced ora-cle complexity. PolyakEG requires two fresh evaluations of the operator per iteration, whereas SPolyakEG requires only one after the initialization step. Hence, for problems where operator evaluations dominate the computational cost, the proposed scheme provides a single-call alter-native while maintaining the same order of residual convergence and linear convergence under strong monotonicity within the established theoretical conditions.
