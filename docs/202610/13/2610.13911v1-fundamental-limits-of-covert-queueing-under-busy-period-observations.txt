Abstract —Consider a queueing system operated by Bob, which is intended to serve only jobs generated by a legitimate user, Willie. Willie’s jobs arrive according to a Poisson process with rate λw , and the service times are exponentially distributed with rate μ. Bob seeks to covertly serve additional jobs arriving according to an independent Poisson process with rate λb,without Willie detecting their presence. As the legitimate user, Willie is provided with certain statistics of each busy period by Bob. In particular, we consider two statistics: (i) the number of jobs served during each busy period and (ii) the duration of each busy period. We establish that, under either statistic, the maximum expected number of additional jobs that Bob can covertly serve obeys a square-root law. Specifically, over n busy periods, Bob can covertly serve additional jobs in the order of √n whereas any scaling which is above √n enables Willie to asymptotically detect the presence of these additional jobs with vanishing error probability. Furthermore, we characterize the scaling constant associated with the square-root law, which we refer to as the covert queueing constant, for each of the two statistics. We compare the scaling constants corresponding to the two statistics and argue that the scaling constant under statistic (i) is no greater than that under statistic (ii). 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Consider a scenario in which two parties, Alice and Bob, collaborate to accomplish a common objective, while some of their actions or observable statistics are monitored by a warden, Willie. Alice and Bob wish to achieve their objective without Willie detecting their collaboration. Such problems broadly fall under the framework of stealth . When the ob-jective is specifically to communicate information without revealing the very presence of communication, the problem is commonly referred to as covert communication .In a standard covert communication setting, a transmit-ter Alice communicates with a receiver Bob over a noisy channel, while a warden Willie observes the transmission through another channel and attempts to determine whether Alice is transmitting. The fundamental question is how much information Alice can reliably communicate to Bob while keeping the transmission undetectable to Willie. A seminal result of Bash et al. [1] established the square-root law :over n channel uses, Alice can covertly transmit on the order of √n bits, whereas transmitting asymptotically more than √n bits makes reliable detection by Willie possible. Following this seminal work, covert communication has been extensively studied under a variety of communication models [2]–[13]. In this paper, we investigate an analogous stealth problem in a queueing system. Consider a queue operated by Bob that is intended to serve only jobs generated by Willie. Bob, however, colludes with Alice and wishes to additionally serve Alice’s jobs, which we refer to as non-Willie jobs ,without Willie being able to detect the presence of extra jobs being served. Specifically, we consider an M/M/ 1 queue in which Willie generates jobs according to a Poisson process with arrival rate λw, and the server operates at service rate 

μ. To monitor the usage of the queue, Willie is provided with certain statistics of the resulting busy periods. The fundamental question is then: how many non-Willie jobs can Bob serve while keeping their presence undetectable from the statistics observed by Willie? We refer to this problem as 

covert queueing .Several works have studied covert insertion in packet and queueing systems [14]–[16]. Soltani et al. [17] studied a Poisson packet channel in which Bob inserts packets while Willie observes packet timings and showed that the number of covert packets follows square-root scaling. This was fur-ther extended by Soltani et al. [18], where general renewal inter arrival times and established the same scaling. Covert insertion has also been studied in queueing systems. Jiang 

et al. [19] considered a single FIFO server and showed that the number of covert jobs depends on the service rates of Bob’s and Willie’s jobs, with square-root scaling under certain conditions. Most closely related to our work, Yardi 

et al. [20] considered a queue in which Willie observes either the number of jobs served or the busy period duration. They showed O(√n) scaling for the former, but only O(1) 

expected covert service time for the latter. Yardi et al. [21] also considered a Markovian statistic and obtained square-root scaling. Motivated by these works, we consider a queueing system in which covert or illegitimate traffic is deliberately intro-duced alongside the legitimate traffic generated by Willie. Bob provides Willie with one of the following two statistics for each busy period: 1) Statistic-1 : the number of jobs served during the i-th busy period, denoted by Xi;2) Statistic-2 : the duration of the i-th busy period, denoted by Yi.Based on the statistics observed over n busy periods, Willie arXiv:2610.13911v1 [cs.IT] 9 Oct 2026 seeks to determine whether Bob is serving non-Willie jobs. This problem was first introduced in [20], where the analysis was limited to achievability results for the scaling of the number of additional jobs under the two statistics. Specifi-cally, an achievability result of order √n was established for Statistic-1, whereas only a constant-order achievability result was obtained for Statistic-2. In this work, we establish the fundamental scaling law for both statistics by proving that the square-root law holds in both cases. In this work, we characterize the fundamental scaling law for both statistics. We show that the square-root law holds in both cases: Bob can covertly serve on the order of √n non-Willie jobs, whereas serving asymptotically more than √n non-Willie jobs enables Willie to reliably detect their presence. Having established the square-root scaling, we next inves-tigate the corresponding scaling constants. In conventional covert communication, the optimal constant associated with the square-root law is commonly referred to as the covert capacity . Such constants were characterized for classes of dis-crete memoryless channels and the Gaussian channel in [13]. Motivated by this notion, we characterize the corresponding square-root scaling constants for the two queueing statistics considered here. Specifically, Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 characterize the scaling constants associated with Statistic-1 and Statistic-2, respectively. The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section II presents the preliminaries and problem formulation. Sec-tion III establishes the main results for the two statistics, including their square-root scaling laws and covert queueing constants. Section IV compares the two statistics and discuss about covert capacity. Section V concludes the paper and discusses future directions. Detailed proofs are provided in the appendices. II. The scaling constants KX and KY , characterized in The-orems 1 and 2, respectively, can be interpreted as the covert queueing capacities from Bob’s perspective when Willie observes statistic X or Y . Intuitively, a statistic that provides Willie with more information about Bob’s activity enables more effective detection, thereby restricting the number of jobs Bob can covertly insert while satisfying the covertness constraint. In this work, we investigated covert queuing in which Bob inserts an independent Poisson stream of jobs into a queue serving Willie’s legitimate traffic. We considered two statis-tics available to Willie for detecting the presence of covert traffic: (i) the number of jobs served during a busy period 

X and (ii) the busy-period length Y . For both statistics, we established the square-root law, showing that the maximum expected number of covert jobs satisfies E∗ 

> b,U

(n) = Θ( √n),for U ∈ { X, Y }. Moreover, inserting ω(√n) jobs on average enables Willie to reliably detect the covert traffic. We further characterized the corresponding covert queuing scaling con-stants, KX and KY , and argued that KX ≤ KY , indicating that the number of jobs served is at least as informative as the busy-period length for detecting covert traffic. Several directions remain open for future investigation. One direction is to analytically characterize the asymptotic behavior of KX and KY as λw → 0 and λw → μ−,along with the corresponding asymptotics of J(λw, μ ) and
