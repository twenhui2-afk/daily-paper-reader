CLASSIFICATION OF REDUCTIVE MONOIDS     

> JINGREN CHI
> Abstract. We classify reductive monoids over arbitrary base schemes by descent from the split classification in [CJ26]. For a reductive group scheme G, we construct its sheaf of based root data, abstract Cartan torus, abstract Weyl group, and a scheme of Weyl cones. The last of these represents the functor of isomorphism classes of rigidified reductive monoids with unit group G.As applications, we deduce a criterion for the existence of monoids that are not groups, prove an extension theorem over normal integral bases, and construct the Vinberg monoids without a quasi-split hypothesis.

1. Introduction 

The goal of this article is to classify reductive monoids over arbitrary base schemes in complete generality. In [CJ26] we have solved this problem when the unit group is split and the base scheme is connected. We build on this result and perform the reduction step here. In particular, this generalizes the works of Vinberg [Vin95], Rittatore [Rit98] and Renner [Ren05] over algebraically closed fields. We refer the reader to the introduction in [CJ26] for further discussion on the historical backgrounds. 1.1. Main results. In [CJ26] split reductive monoids over connected schemes are classified in terms of weight monoids (see [ loc. cit. , Definition 2.11]) in the dominant weight cone. In this paper we will use certain combinatorial object called the Weyl cones , which carry essentially the same information as the weight monoids. These are by definition the Weyl group stable rational convex polyhedral cones of full dimension in the Q-vector space generated by the weight lattice. The weight monoids are used in loc. cit. mainly because they are more relevant to the explicit construction of the split reductive monoids. To perform the descent step and obtain a general classification theorem, we find it better to use the Weyl cones because they do not depend on choices of global pinnings (which are not available for non-quasi-split groups). The rough idea for the descent step is as follows. Given a reductive group scheme G over a base scheme S. If we view it informally as a family of split reductive groups on S, we should use a family of Weyl cones over S to classify the reductive monoids over S with unit group G. This is formalized by introducing a sheaf on S whose fiber at a geometric point ¯ s ∈ S is the set of Weyl cones for the group G¯s. We show that this sheaf is represented by a scheme that is ´ etale locally constant over S. To achieve this, we sheafify the usual notion of based root data, and define the abstract Cartan and abstract Weyl group for G in a canonical way, without any choices. Our main result is Theorem 5.3, which identifies rigidified reductive G-monoids with global sections of the scheme of Weyl cones. Forgetting the rigidification corresponds to taking orbits under the global section of the automorphism group Aut G(S).We will derive the following applications of our main result. In Corollary 5.5 we show that there exists a reductive monoid over S with unit group G but not itself equal to G if and only if the center admits a nonzero S-group homomorphism Gm → ZG. In Corollary 5.6 we show that over a normal integral base scheme, every rigidified reductive monoid over the generic point extends uniquely (up to rigidified isomorphisms) once its unit group extends to a reductive group scheme. In §6.2, we construct the Vinberg monoid for any reductive group scheme, quasi-split or not, generalizing the case of split groups in [CJ26, §5.2, §5.3].

1.2. Plan of the article. Section §2 recalls the needed facts about reductive group schemes. Section §3 reformulates the split classification over connected bases [CJ26] in terms of Weyl cones. In Section §4 we construct the canonical sheaves associated with a reductive group scheme, extend the split classification to disconnected bases, and prove independence of the pinning. Section §5first treats the case of a field and then proves the general classification theorem and its applications. Section §6 discusses basic examples of the multiplicative monoids of central simple algebras and the Vinberg monoids. 1.3. General notations and conventions. All categories are assumed to be small. A thin cat-egory is the same as a preordered set , and is canonically equivalent to a poset (partially ordered set). For an abelian group X we put XQ ∶= X ⊗Z Q. For any set E and any scheme S, we let ES

T -action factors through the character α.1.4. Related work. The statement of our main result Theorem 5.3 can be viewed as a direct generalization of Renner’s classification of reductive monoids over algebraically closed fields (see [Ren05, Theorem 5.4]). Our previous work [CJ26] that establish the special case of split monoids over connected base generalizes Vinberg’s approach [Vin95] to the classification problem over alge-braically closed field of characteristic 0. As a comparison, the “dual” approach of Rittatore [Rit98], classifies reductive monoids over any algebraically closed fields in terms of cones in the coweight space (as opposed to the weight space in our approach), builds on the theory of spherical embed-dings of Luna-Vust [LV83] and Knop [Kno91]. This latter theory has been generalized by Huruguen [Hur11] and Wedhorn [Wed18] to arbitrary fields. It would be an interesting question whether one can have a classification result for spherical embeddings over general base schemes.
