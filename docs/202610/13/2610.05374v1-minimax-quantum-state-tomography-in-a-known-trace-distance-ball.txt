# Minimax Quantum State Tomography in a Known Trace-Distance Ball 

Yonglong Li School of Mathematics and Statistics Xi’an Jiaotong University liyonglong@xjtu.edu.cn 

Abstract 

We study quantum state tomography when the unknown state lies in a trace-distance ball of radius R around a known reference state ρ. For arbitrary collective measurements, we determine the minimax expected risk and confidence radius up to universal constants, uniformly over the dimension, center spectrum, radius, and sample size. Write Γ = d(Tr √ρ)2 + d2R. For d ≥ 2, 0 < R ≤ 1, and n ≥ 1 copies, the expected risk is Θ(min {R, √Γ/n }). At failure probability 0 < δ ≤ 1/3, the minimax confidence radius is Θ

For the lower bound we use a weighted trace-norm witness to retain all spectral scales in a square-root perturbation family. For the upper bound, we give a direct spectral and concentration analysis of a modified existing purification-based procedure.

# 1 Introduction 

Suppose that a preparation procedure is designed to produce a known quantum state ρ, and that calibration or a model gives the deterministic promise 

T (σ, ρ ) ≤ R (1) for the state σ actually produced. Here T (σ, ρ ) = 12 ∥σ − ρ∥1 is the trace distance, which controls the difference between the probabilities of every measurement event [1]. The task is to reconstruct 

σ from independent copies. The promise is assumed as part of the model; verifying it is a separate statistical problem. Returning ρ gives error at most R without using any copies. To improve on this estimate, one must learn the variations allowed by the ball. These variations depend on more than the rank of the center. Even a small ball around a pure state contains both coherent changes of direction and mixed states with mass on previously unoccupied directions. We ask for the minimax risk uniformly over the dimension, the entire spectrum of the center, the radius, and the sample size. 1arXiv:2610.05374v1 [quant-ph] 4 Oct 2026 Tomography in a known trace-distance ball 

For unrestricted collective measurements, global trace-distance tomography has copy complexity Θ( d2/ε 2) at constant confidence, and the corresponding rank-at-most-r rate is Θ( rd/ε 2) [2, 3, 4]. These results do not by themselves give a matching lower bound in an arbitrary prescribed neighborhood. Our main result identifies its coefficient as Γ( ρ, R ) = d(Tr √ρ)2 + d2R. (2) The first term retains all spectral scales of the center, while the second accounts for additional states admitted by the radius. Theorem 2.1 gives the expected risk and the confidence radius for every sample size; Corollary 2.2 gives the resulting high-accuracy copy complexity. The main contribution is the matching local converse for arbitrary spectra, including zero and arbitrarily small eigenvalues. We regularize the center within the ball and perturb its square root by a full family of sign matrices. Neighboring-state tests force many weighted sign errors. A trace-norm dual witness built from the true sign matrix then converts these errors into the full sum Tr √ρ,without selecting a single spectral scale. This step applies the dual witness of Acharya et al. [5, Lemma 4.7], the true sign pattern, to a spectrally weighted square-root family. The argument yields a constant-probability error event uniformly in all parameters. A local two-point construction supplies the additional confidence term at every center. For achievability, we analyze the existing purification/Hayashi procedure [6, 7, 8]. Its measure-ment uses neither the center nor its spectrum. An exact Gaussian representation gives a direct expected-loss bound and a tail bound in which log(1 /δ ) enters additively with Γ. Combined with the option of returning ρ, this one measurement is rate-optimal over every prescribed ball. The theorem thus identifies the difficulty of the promise class; it does not require a new center-dependent measurement. The upper rates themselves are implied by earlier results. The expected spectral rate follows from the second moments of this procedure [7, Theorem 1.7], and the confidence rate follows from its observable concentration [9, Proposition 4.7] by generic chaining [10]. These trace-distance bounds are not stated in the papers of Pelecanos et al. , so Section 5 gives a short direct proof with explicit constants; Section 2.3 and Remark 5.5 separate the inherited ingredients from the local converse. In Section 2 we state the results, their consequences, and the comparison with earlier guarantees. Section 3 collects the required standard facts. In Sections 4–6 we prove the lower bound, upper bound, and confidence refinement. In the appendices we state two analytic tools and the fixed-rank rate at a normalized-projector center, which is implied by existing results.

# 7 Conclusion and future work 

The local minimax risk is governed by d(Tr √ρ)2 + d2R, and the confidence radius adds log(1 /δ ) to this coefficient. The bounds hold uniformly over finite dimensions, arbitrary center spectra, and all sample sizes. The matching converse combines square-root perturbations with a weighted trace-norm witness; the upper bound uses an existing universal measurement and a confidence-dependent choice between that estimator and the known center. Equation (7) identifies the resulting sample savings, including the regime in which confidence cost dominates dimension. 23 Tomography in a known trace-distance ball 

Several questions remain. First, what is the corresponding center-dependent rate under single-copy or bounded-batch measurements? Existing rank-dependent results [11] show that restricting joint measurements changes global complexity, but do not give the full local spectral dependence. Second, can the same local and confidence guarantees be attained with controlled running time and quantum memory? The streaming methods of Hu et al. [26] provide relevant implementation tools. Two statistical questions also remain open. A procedure without a rank bound may improve the finite-sample remainder while retaining the leading spectral term √d/n S (σ); the ancilla-dependent bias of the present estimator does not exclude this possibility. Finally, the fixed-rank rate at normalized-projector centers with r ≤ d/ 2 is implied by existing results (Corollary C.2), but the corresponding uniform characterization at arbitrary nonuniform fixed-rank centers remains unresolved.
