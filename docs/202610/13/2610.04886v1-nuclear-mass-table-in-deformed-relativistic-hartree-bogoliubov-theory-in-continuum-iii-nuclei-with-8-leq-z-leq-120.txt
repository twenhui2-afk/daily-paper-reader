# Abstract 

The mass table in the deformed relativistic Hartree–Bogoliubov theory in continuum (DRHBc) with the PC-PK1 density functional has been established for nuclei with 8 ≤ Z ≤ 120, extended from the previous works for even–even nuclei (Zhang et al. (DRHBc mass table collaboration), At. Data Nucl. Data Tables 144, 101488 (2022)) and for even-Z nuclei (Guo et al. (DRHBc mass table collaboration), At. Data Nucl. Data Tables 158, 101661 (2024)). The calculated binding energies, two- and one-nucleon separation energies, root-mean-square (rms) radii of neutron, proton, matter, and charge distributions, quadrupole deformations, neutron and proton Fermi surfaces, and the blocked neutron (proton) orbitals of odd-N (Z) nuclei are tabulated and compared with the avail-able experimental data. A total of 9495 nuclei are predicted to be bound, with an rms deviation of 1.444 MeV from the 2380 mass data. Good agreement with the available experimental pairing gaps, α decay energies, and charge radii is also achieved. The accuracies of the calculated nuclear masses and nucleon separation energies as well as the prediction for drip lines are compared with those obtained by other relativistic and nonrelativistic density functional calculations. It turns out that the DRHBc theory with PC-PK1 provides one of the best microscopic descriptions for nuclear masses. The systematics of nucleon separation energies, pairing gaps, pairing energies, two-nucleon gaps, α decay energies, rms radii, quadrupole deformations, potential energy curves, neutron density distributions, and neutron mean-field potentials are discussed.

Explanation of Tables 47 5I. INTRODUCTION 

In the previous works [1, 2], following the strategies and techniques presented in Refs. [3, 4], the nuclear mass table calculated by the deformed relativistic Hartree-Bogoliubov theory in continuum (DRHBc) [5–7] with the density functional PC-PK1 [8] was constructed for even-even nuclei [1] and even-Z nuclei [2]. A total of 2583 even-even nuclei [1] and 2246 even-Z odd-N [2] with 8 ≤ Z ≤ 120 have been predicted to be bound. The root-mean-square (rms) deviation for 1244 nuclei with mass measured is 1.433 MeV, providing one of the best density functional descriptions for nuclear masses [2]. With the completion of DRHBc calculations for odd-Z nuclei, this paper reports the DRHBc mass table for all the nuclei with 8 ≤ Z ≤ 120. The DRHBc theory, based on meson-exchange [5–7, 9] or point-coupling [3, 4] density functionals, solves the deformed relativistic Hartree-Bogoliubov (RHB) equations in a Dirac Woods-Saxon (DWS) basis [10]. In this way, the deformation, pairing, and continuum effects are self-consistently incorporated, and thus a microscopic description for both exotic and stable nuclei is provided. The DRHBc theory has described successfully the halo structures in 17 B [11], 19 B [12], 15 ,19 ,22 C [13–15], 12 N [16], 29 F [17], 31 Ne [18, 19], and 37 Mg [20–22], predicted the possible halo in 31 F [17], 39 ,41 Na [23], 42 ,44 Mg [5, 6], and 43 ,45 Si [24], and explored the possibility of a one-proton halo in 22 Al [25, 26]. In addition to the halo phenomena, it has also succeeded in describing many other nuclear phenomena and properties, such as the prolate-shape dominance in Te, Xe, and Ba isotopes [27], the shape evolution of Ho isotopes [28], the α decay half-lives for even-even nuclei from W to U [29], the shape evolution of neutron-deficient Au and kink structure of charge radii in Pb isotopes [30], the odd-even shape staggering and the charge-radius kink structure in Hg isotopes [31], the neutron skin thickness in Pb isotopes [32], the inner fission barrier of U isotopes [33], the charge radii and their correlations with quadrupole deformation [34], and newly measured mass data [35]. To probe novel structures or mechanisms in nuclei, the DRHBc theory has been used to investigate the nucleon in the classically forbidden region in Mg isotopes [36], the deforma-tion effects on the drip-line locations [37], the possible peninsula of stability [38–41], the possible shape coexistence and bubble structure [42–45], the prolate-oblate shape competi-tion and the volume/surface contribution to neutron skin thickness of Bk isotopes [46–48], the evolution of N = 20 , 28, and 50 shell closures in the 20 ≤ Z ≤ 30 region [49], the 6double magicity of 164 Pb [50], possible magic numbers in the superheavy mass region [51– 55] and the influence of bubble structure [51] and high-angular-momentum single-particle states [56], the symmetry energy in relation with two-nucleon separation energies of Ca and Pb isotopes [57], the interdependence of pairing energy and mean field energy in reaching the energy minimum [58], and the sensitivity of r-process abundances to nuclear deforma-tion [59, 60]. Based on the DRHBc theory, significant extensions and optimizations have been done and reviewed in Ref. [61]. The angular momentum projection has been implemented to study the rotational excitations of exotic nuclei [62–64]. The finite amplitude method has been implemented to investigate the monopole and dipole resonances in exotic nuclei [65, 66]. Two-dimensional collective Hamiltonian (2DCH) method has been implemented to study the beyond mean-field dynamical correlations and improve the descriptions of ground-state properties [67, 68]. The interaction cross section, longitudinal momentum distribution, and the charged-particle evaporation process contribution to the charge-changing cross section have been investigated based on the Glauber model combined with the structure inputs from DRHBc calculations [15, 18, 21, 69, 70]. The one-proton emission in 149 −151 Lu has been studied based on the WKB model combined with the structure properties from DRHBc calculations [71–73]. The DRHBc theory has been improved by optimizing the Dirac Woods-Saxon basis [74], implementing the Lipkin-Nogami method [75, 76], and including the time-odd component [19]. The DRHBc theory has been extended to include the triaxial degree of freedom, i.e. , the triaxial relativistic Hartree-Bogoliubov theory in continuum (TRHBc) [77– 79]. In Refs. [80, 81], the correction of the mass table in relativistic continuum Hartree-Bogoliubov (RCHB) theory [82] has been investigated by machine learning and compared with the DRHBc mass table. In this paper, we present the DRHBc mass table for nuclei with 8 ≤ Z ≤ 120. The theoretical framework of DRHBc is briefly introduced in Sec. II. Numerical details are listed in Sec. III. The results and discussions are compiled in Sec. IV, including nuclear masses, nucleon separation energies, pairing gaps, pairing energies, two-nucleon gaps, alpha-decay energies, rms radii, quadrupole deformations, potential energy curves, neutron density dis-tributions, and neutron potentials. Finally, a summary is given in Sec. V. 7II. THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

The radial wave functions of the DWS basis have a proper asymptotic behavior at large r, enabling the DRHBc theory to properly account for the contributions of the continuum and describe the exotic nuclei with large spa-tial extensions. Due to the spatial reflection symmetry and axial symmetry, the parity π and the third component of the angular momentum m are good quantum numbers. The RHB matrix can then be decomposed into separate mπ blocks, greatly simplifying the diagonal-ization. The resulting quasiparticle wave functions are used to construct new densities and then new potentials, which are iterated in the RHB equations until convergence is achieved. For an odd-A or odd-odd nucleus, the blocking effect of the unpaired nucleon(s) must be taken into account [86]. The blocked orbital(s) will break the time reversal symmetry, leading to the emergence of nucleon currents. This will increase the computational cost, especially for large-scale calculations. Therefore, the equal filling approximation (EFA) [87] is adopted to save computational resources [4, 7], which is expected to reproduce the energies of the ground state and low-lying excited states around 50 keV on average in comparison with the exact blocking procedure [88]. Due to the mean-field approximation, the translational and rotational invariances are violated in the DRHBc theory. The broken symmetry can be restored by beyond-mean-field approaches such as the angular momentum projection [89]. In the present DRHBc calcu-lations, beyond-mean-field correlations are included through the microscopic calculation of center-of-mass and rotational correction energies, and the details can be found in Refs. [2, 3]. 10 III. NUMERICAL DETAILS 

The numerical details for the DRHBc mass table calculations have been investigated in Refs. [3, 4]. For completeness, in the following we summarize the numerical details. 

• The density functional PC-PK1 [8], which provides one of the best density-functional descriptions for nuclear masses [35, 38, 90–92], is employed. 

• In the pairing channel, the density-dependent zero-range pairing force in Eq. (10) is adopted, where the pairing strength V0 = −325 MeV fm 3 and the saturation density 

ρsat = 0 .152 fm −3 together with a pairing window of 100 MeV. With these, the odd-even mass differences for calcium and lead isotopes are well reproduced [3]. 

• The energy cutoff for the DWS basis in the Fermi sea is set to E+cut = 300 MeV and the number of states in the Dirac sea is the same as that in the Fermi sea, which guarantees the convergence accuracy of 0.01 MeV for the total energies of doubly-magic nuclei 

> 40

Ca, 100 Sn, and 208 Pb [3], and the deformed heavy odd-A nucleus 301 Th [4]. 

• The angular momentum cutoff for the DWS basis Jmax = 23 /2 ℏ, which guarantees the convergence accuracy of 0 .01% for the total energies of the deformed heavy nuclei 

> 300 ,301

Th [3, 4]. 

• In Refs. [3, 4], the Legendre expansion truncations in Eq. (12) are chosen as λmax = 6 and 8 for nuclei with 8 ≤ Z ≤ 70 and Z ≥ 71, respectively. This guarantees the convergence accuracy for the total energies of 0 .03% of 20 Ne and 112 Mo, and of 0 .01% of 300 ,301 Th, when their deformations are constrained as β2 = 0 .6. In the calculations for the mass table [1, 2, 4], λmax = 10 is adopted to guarantee the same accuracy for superheavy nuclei with 101 ≤ Z ≤ 120. 

• For odd-A and odd-odd nuclei, the automatic blocking procedure is implemented [4], in which the orbital with the lowest quasiparticle energy is blocked during the iteration. If the automatic blocking procedure doesn’t work, the orbitals near the Fermi surface will be blocked independently, and the result with the lowest total energy is identified as the ground state. 11 IV. V. SUMMARY 

In summary, we have constructed the nuclear mass table with the effects of pairing, deformation, and continuum incorporated in a self-consistent and microscopic way for all nuclei with 8 ≤ Z ≤ 120 by using the DRHBc theory with the density functional PC-PK1, extending and completing the previous works on even-even [1] and even-Z [2] nuclei. The calculated binding energies, two-nucleon and one-nucleon separation energies, rms radii 36 of neutron, proton, matter, and charge distributions, quadrupole deformations, neutron and proton Fermi surfaces, and neutron and proton blocked orbitals when applicable, are tabulated. In total, 9495 nuclei with 8 ≤ Z ≤ 120 are predicted to be bound in the DRHBc mass table. The rms deviation for binding energies from the 2380 available experimental data is 1.444 MeV, placing this work among the most accurate density functional descriptions of nuclear masses to date. The extracted nucleon separation energies and their evolution across the nuclear landscape are presented and discussed. The drastic changes at magic numbers in nucleon separation energies are discussed as well, revealing traditional shell closures and potential new ones. The weakly bound nuclei with nucleon separation energies less than 1 MeV are discussed. The accuracy of the nuclear masses and nucleon separation energies and the predicted limits of the nuclear landscape are compared with the results from other mass models. The predicted proton drip line is generally consistent with experimental observations, and close to those predicted by other models. The predicted neutron drip line is generally the most extended in comparison with other models, mainly because of the proper treatment of the continuum and the adopted density functional. Several peninsulas of stability are obtained near the neutron drip line at Z ≈ 60 , 88, and 110, which are mainly attributed to the effects of deformation. The pairing gaps, the pairing energies, and the two-nucleon gaps are extracted for both neutron and proton. Based on the vanishing pairing energies and pronounced two-nucleon gaps, the traditional magic numbers are well reproduced, and new neutron magic numbers 

N = 184 and 258 are predicted. Similarly, the pronounced two-neutron gaps at N = 40 and 162 for several isotones indicate possible shell closures. The pronounced two-proton gaps also appear in the nuclei around Z = 14 and along the N ∼ Z band. The pronounced pairing energies and vanishing two-neutron gaps indicate the quenching of traditional neutron shell closures at N = 28 , 50, and 82 near the neutron drip lines. The α decay energies are extracted and compared. With the inclusion of deformation effects, the rms deviation from 1090 available experimental data is σ(Qα) = 0 .841 MeV, which is significantly improved from σ(Qα) ≈ 2 MeV in the RCHB results [116]. Future work is expected to improve the description of Qα and to estimate systematically the α

decay half-lives. 37 For the 1014 nuclei with charge radius measured, the rms deviation between the calculated results and the data is σ(Rch ) = 0 .035 fm. The systematic trend of the neutron rms radii for nuclei with 8 ≤ Z ≤ 120 generally follows the empirical formula Rn ∝ N 1/3 with a few exceptions. The pronounced deviations for some extremely neutron-rich nuclei are possible signals for the halo or giant halo phenomena. The drastic increases after the predicted shell closures at N = 184 and 258 can be attributed to the deformation effects. The quadrupole deformations for nuclei with 8 ≤ Z ≤ 120 and the PECs for selected isotopes are presented and discussed. The weakening or even collapse of the traditional shell closures is further demonstrated by the deformation deviating from the spherical shape. The drastic shape changes, e.g. , those around N = 115 with 60 ≤ Z ≤ 71, come from the competition between different minima with comparable energies. The possible triaxial deformations and shape coexistence in those regions are discussed with reference to the results in calculations including triaxial deformations. The shape evolution in Al, Eu, and Nh isotopic chains is further understood from PECs in constrained DRHBc calculations. Finally, the angle averaged neutron density distributions and neutron mean-field poten-tials as well as those along and perpendicular to the symmetry axis for selected odd-odd Al, Eu, and Nh isotopes and their corresponding neighbors are presented. The diffuseness of the neutron density distributions and mean-field potentials generally increases with the increase of neutron number. The deformation effects are also manifested in the evolutions of the density distributions and mean-field potentials. The successful construction of the DRHBc mass table with relativistic density functional PC-PK1 provides a comprehensive and microscopic description for nuclei with 8 ≤ Z ≤ 120. Based on the DRHBc results, future work will be devoted to predict nuclear level densities, neutron capture rates, and β-decay lifetimes to establish a microscopic and self-consistent database for nuclear astrophysics.
