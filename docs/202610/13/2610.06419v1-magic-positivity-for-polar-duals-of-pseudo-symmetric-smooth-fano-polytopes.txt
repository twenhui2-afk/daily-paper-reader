MAGIC POSITIVITY FOR POLAR DUALS OF PSEUDO-SYMMETRIC SMOOTH FANO POLYTOPES 

HIDEFUMI OHSUGI AND AKIYOSHI TSUCHIYA 

Abstract. Motivated by Gal’s conjecture, Ferroni and Higashitani conjectured that the 

h∗-polynomial of any Gorenstein lattice polytope admitting a quadratic triangulation is 

γ-positive. Together with conjectures predicting quadratic properties of toric ideals of smooth lattice polytopes, this suggests that smooth Gorenstein lattice polytopes should have γ-positive h∗-polynomials. In this paper, we prove that the Ehrhart polynomial of the polar dual of every pseudo-symmetric simplicial reflexive polytope is magic positive. For pseudo-symmetric smooth Fano polytopes, we prove the stronger statement that all magic coefficients are strictly positive. Consequently, for every pseudo-symmetric simplicial reflexive polytope, its polar dual is Ehrhart positive and has a real-rooted and γ-positive 

h∗-polynomial. We also prove that the Ehrhart polynomial of the polar dual of the sym-metric edge polytope of every cycle is magic positive. This gives an affirmative answer to a question of Konoike, who had previously proved partial positivity results for this family.

Another long-standing problem concerns the toric ideals of smooth lattice polytopes. The Bøgvad conjecture predicts that the toric ideal of every smooth lattice polytope is gener-ated by quadratic binomials. A stronger version asks for a quadratic Gr¨ obner basis, while a squarefree quadratic initial ideal corresponds to a quadratic triangulation via the stan-dard correspondence between initial ideals of toric ideals and regular triangulations; see [25, Chapter 8]. Although these statements are logically distinct, together with the conjecture of Ferroni and Higashitani they suggest the following positivity problem. 

Conjecture 1.1. Every smooth Gorenstein lattice polytope has a γ-positive h∗-polynomial. 

For background and classification results on smooth Gorenstein polytopes, see [19]. The purpose of this paper is to establish a stronger positivity property for the polar duals of pseudo-symmetric simplicial reflexive polytopes. A reflexive polytope is called pseudo-symmetric if it has a pair of opposite facets. Nill classified pseudo-symmetric simplicial reflexive polytopes in arbitrary dimension [21]. His classification decomposes every such polytope into a free sum of a centrally symmetric reflexive crosspolytope, even-dimensional del Pezzo polytopes, and pseudo-del Pezzo poly-topes. The smooth Fano members form an important subclass, classified earlier by Ewald [9, 10]. The smooth Fano subclass has been particularly tractable in several aspects of toric geometry. For instance, Haase used these polytopes and their polar duals to compute stringy Hodge numbers of generic hypersurfaces in the associated toric varieties and to study related questions concerning mirror symmetry and crepant resolutions [15]. The toric Fano varieties associated with the centrally symmetric members of this family have also been studied in connection with Tian’s α-invariant and K¨ ahler–Einstein metrics [3, 23]. On the Ehrhart side, in our previous work [22, Theorem 5.8], we stated that the h∗-polynomial of every pseudo-symmetric simplicial reflexive polytope is γ-positive. The proof there invokes Nill’s decomposition, but the centrally symmetric reflexive crosspolytope factor was inadvertently treated as a standard crosspolytope. We supply the missing argument in Appendix A. The present paper instead studies the polar duals and proves the stronger property of magic positivity. These structural, geometric, and enumerative properties make the polar duals of pseudo-symmetric smooth Fano polytopes a natural testing ground for Conjecture 1.1. We recall the relevant geometric terminology and the correspondence between smooth Fano polytopes and smooth reflexive polytopes in Section 2. Our first main result is the following.

For a reflexive polytope, real-rootedness in turn implies γ-positivity. Recent work has established magic positivity for other families, including Pitman–Stanley polytopes and broad classes of partial permutohedra [2, 18]. We therefore obtain the following consequences. 

Corollary 1.3. Let P be a pseudo-symmetric simplicial reflexive polytope. Then P ∨ is Ehrhart positive, and h∗(P ∨; t) is real-rooted and γ-positive. 

In particular, if P is smooth Fano, then P ∨ is a smooth reflexive, and hence smooth Gorenstein, lattice polytope. Thus Theorem 1.2 verifies Conjecture 1.1 for the polar duals of pseudo-symmetric smooth Fano polytopes. Haase proved that the polar dual of every pseudo-symmetric smooth Fano polytope admits a quadratic triangulation [15, Corollaries IV.2.6 and IV.2.10]. Thus these polytopes satisfy the triangulation hypothesis in the conjecture of Ferroni and Higashitani. On the other hand, Ferroni constructed Gorenstein lattice polytopes admitting quadratic triangulations whose 

h∗-polynomials are not log-concave [12, Theorem 1.4 and Section 6]. Since magic positivity implies real-rootedness of the corresponding h∗-polynomial, whose nonnegative coefficients are then log-concave by Newton’s inequalities, these examples show that the quadratic-triangulation hypothesis alone does not imply magic positivity, even in the Gorenstein case. A second application concerns symmetric edge polytopes, introduced and studied in [20]. For a graph G on [ n] := {1, 2, . . . , n }, its symmetric edge polytope is 

PG = conv {± (ei − ej ) : {i, j } ∈ E(G)},

where ei is the i-th standard unit vector of Rn. For a connected graph G, we regard PG as a full-dimensional lattice polytope in H = {x ∈ Rn : x1 + · · · + xn = 0 }, equipped with the lattice H ∩ Zn, and take its polar dual with respect to the dual lattice. Konoike studied magic positivity of the polar duals associated with cycles. For the cycle Cn, he obtained an explicit Ehrhart formula [17, Proposition 4.6], proved positivity of the first three and the last three magic coefficients [17, Theorem 1.4], and asked whether all magic coefficients are nonnegative [17, Question 4.5]. The strict positivity results for the del Pezzo and cycle cases are based on a positivity result for divided differences, while the pseudo-del Pezzo case follows from an averaging identity. The general pseudo-symmetric simplicial reflexive case is then obtained from Nill’s classification and the binary-code description of centrally symmetric reflexive crosspolytopes, while Ewald’s classification yields strict positivity in the smooth Fano case. We refer to [6] for background on lattice polytopes and toric varieties. We first recall the terminology used throughout the paper. Let M ≃ Zd be a lattice, let N = Hom( M, Z) be its dual lattice, and write MR = M ⊗Z R and NR = N ⊗Z R. For a full-dimensional polytope 

P ⊂ MR containing the origin in its interior, its polar dual is 

P ∨ = {y ∈ NR : ⟨y, x ⟩ ≤ 1 for every x ∈ P }.

A lattice polytope P ⊂ MR is called reflexive if the origin lies in its interior and P ∨ is again a lattice polytope. A full-dimensional lattice polytope containing the origin in its interior is called pseudo-symmetric if it has a pair of opposite facets. A lattice polytope P is called 

Gorenstein if rP − m is reflexive for some positive integer r and some lattice point m ∈ M .The integer r is called the index of P . A lattice polytope is Gorenstein if and only if its 

h∗-polynomial is palindromic [16, 13]. A d-dimensional lattice polytope P ⊂ MR is called smooth if, at every vertex v of P , the primitive lattice vectors along the d edges emanating from v form a basis of M . A full-dimensional lattice polytope R ⊂ NR containing the origin in its interior is called a Fano polytope if all its vertices are primitive lattice points. A Fano polytope is called smooth Fano 

if the vertices of every facet form a basis of N . We use the conventions of [9, 10, 15]. A lattice polytope is smooth if and only if the projective toric variety defined by its normal fan is smooth [6, Theorem 2.4.3].
