# Channel Knowledge Maps for FR3 UAV Base Stations: Dataset and ML-based Modeling 

Guillem Moreno Garcia, Sergi Abadal, Evgenii Vinogradov 

Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya (UPC), Barcelona, Spain guillem.moreno.garcia@estudiantat.upc.edu 

Abstract —This paper introduces a dense upper mid-band Frequency Range (FR3) Channel Knowledge Map (CKM) dataset for Uncrewed Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) with an on-board base station (UxNB). It contains 16 180 CKM samples from 3 236 urban locations in 66 cities at 7.125 GHz and UxNB heights of up to 500 meters. Every 513 ×513 sample stores the building geometry, the line-of-sight (LOS) state, attenuation, delay spread, angular spread, and variable transmitter height. As a focused first use-case, we propose an interpretable height-aware attenuation model. Its LOS branch combines free-space loss, two-ray term, and compact data-based height/range calibration. Its non-LOS (NLOS) branch combines a COST 231-Hata basis with local environment-specific linear calibration. The proposed model reaches 1.93 dB overall root mean square error, with 1.74 dB in LOS and 3.54 dB in NLOS on over 500 unique locations in 14 unseen cities (2 590 samples in total). The result provides attenuation data for FR3 UxNB deployments and a physical anchor for future FR3 modeling efforts. 

Index Terms —air-to-ground channel, FR3, channel knowledge map, 6G, radio map, unmanned aerial vehicle. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Uncrewed Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) with an on-board base station (UxNB) are often seen as one of the key enablers of future 6G networks due to their mobility and better Line-of-Sight (LOS) channels [1], [2]. However, UxNB deploy-ment faces challenges such as location optimization and real-time channel state estimation on resource-constrained UxNBs. Environment-aware communication enabled by Chan-nel Knowledge Maps (CKMs) aims to resolve these chal-lenges [3]. A CKM is a site-specific database containing the locations of transmitters and/or receivers, providing location-specific channel knowledge useful to enhance environment-awareness and facilitate channel acquisition or obviate the need for it entirely. Any relevant information can be stored in a CKM, including but not limited to channel gains, multipath delays/angles, or even the full channel impulse response [4]. CKMs can be obtained via measurements or channel mod-eling. Measurements provide the most accurate results, but may be cumbersome. The models used for simulating wireless channels range from a fully stochastic to fine-grained deter-ministic Ray Tracing (RT) model depending on the require-ments for precision and the geographical scale. Most available CKM datasets [5], [6] rely on RT simulated data. Since

RT simulators are known to be resource-demanding, faster learning-based simulation approaches have become popular [7]–[10]. Prediction of dense channel gain maps is commonly approached as an image-to-image regression problem: Ra-dioUNet [8] and PMNet [9], [10] systems demonstrate the strength of convolutional encoder–decoder models when city topology and sparse or simulated channels are available. Recently, the upper mid-band Frequency Range (FR3, be-tween 7 and 24 GHz) has started to gain attention as apromising spectrum candidate to enhance both coverage and capacity of 6G networks [11]. FR3 offers a balance between the reliable propagation of sub-6 GHz bands (FR1) and the large bandwidth of millimeter-wave bands (FR2). FR3 is very attractive for UxNB deployments that have better LOS conditions but still must reliably function in Non-LOS (NLOS) scenarios. To assess and harness the potential of UxNB-enabled networks in FR3, we need accurate channel data in diverse urban environments. Unfortunately, to our knowledge, there are no publicly available UxNB CKM datasets in FR3.

Since UxNBs can use heights up to 500 m, the resulting channel is very different from terrestrial channels [1], [14]. The only existing dataset containing some sort of 3D channel is UrbanRadio3D [12] using receiver heights of up to 20 m but it is not sufficient. Moreover, Air-to-Ground (A2G) channel modeling requires accurate information about building height [15]. The existing

b) Limitations of the existing CKM modeling approaches: 

Existing learning-based channel map estimation frameworks suffer from several architectural and practical limitations. First, dense attenuation-map prediction is commonly approached via pure image-to-image regression, employing heavy, over-parameterized convolutional networks to rediscover propaga-tion physics (e.g., free-space path loss) independently for every environment. Second, these architectures typically assume fixed terrestrial geometries and fail to reproduce the effect of varying transmitter altitudes typical for UxNB deployments. Finally, pure deep learning approaches struggle with spatial generalization; they often require local fine-tuning when de-ployed in unseen cities and urban topologies. 

c) Beyond state-of-the-art contributions: In this paper, we address these gaps. The main contribution thus is two-fold: 1) We introduce an FR3 (7.125 GHz) Channel Knowledge Map dataset 1 generated via ray tracing simulations in 3 236 city layouts across 66 diverse cities. This dataset relies on complete, global-scale 3D building data [17], [18]. UxNB applications are enabled by using Tx heights of up to 500 meters (5 random heights per layout resulting in a total of 16 180 CKM samples). 2) We propose a height-aware channel prediction frame-work 2 that explicitly separates LOS and NLOS prop-agation using local city geometry. By calibrating de-terministic baseline models (e.g., free-space, two-ray, COST 231-Hata) via lightweight regression, the pro-posed approach achieves highly accurate, zero-shot spa-tial generalization to completely unseen cities while offering computational speedups. The paper is organized into dataset description, modeling, evaluation, and conclusions (Sections II–V).

A. CKM Definition and Mapping Formulation 

We define a CKM sample as a spatial representation com-bining environmental features, channel parameters, and opera-tional metadata. For any given CKM sample n ∈ { 1, . . . , N },the propagation environment is represented as a tensor En ∈

E ⊂ RH×W ×L, where H and W denote the spatial grid dimensions (spanning element indices 1 to 513 ), and L is the number of environmental layers (e.g., building heights, terrain information). The radio propagation characteristics are represented as a channel state tensor Cn ∈ C ⊂ RH×W ×F ,storing F channel parameters at the receiver grid (e.g., attenua-tion, multipath delay spread, angular parameters). Operational metadata (e.g., carrier frequencies, UxNB location informa-tion) are defined via a metadata vector mn ∈ M ⊂ RK .Hence, CKM estimation can be framed as finding a para-metric mapping function Φθ :

Φθ : E × M → C, (1) where parameters θ are optimized such that the estimated channel tensor bCn = Φθ (En, mn) approximates the ground-truth target Cn.

B. The FR3 UxNB CKM Dataset 

We build a comprehensive benchmark dataset using high-precision deterministic RT simulations. 1) 3D urban environment modeling ( E): The environmental tensor En in our dataset relies on global-scale 3D building datasets [17], [18]. For UxNB deployments, this offers a clear advantage over relying on OSM data popular in literature. OSM is highly valuable for footprint extraction, but height-related tags such as "height" and "building:levels" are optional and their availability is less than 10% [16]. By contrast, combining 3D-GloBFP [17] and GlobalBuildingAtlas [18] provides consistent building height information 3. This is cru-cial for UxNBs that depend heavily on deployment height [1]. We extract city layouts of 3 236 urban locations in 66 diverse cities worldwide to cover a range of propagation topologies (from open low-rise suburbs to dense high-rise downtown areas). Each rasterized environmental map En

spans a spatial grid of H = W = 513 at a horizontal resolution of 1 m. The L = 2 environmental channels encode: 1) Building height profile maps H indicating building height in meters ( 0 m over ground level). 2) Binary building occupancy masks B indicating presence of structural obstacles. 

2) Ray Tracing channel simulation ( C): High-precision ray tracing simulations are conducted to generate the spatially con-sistent channel tensors Cn. To avoid using separate software for 3D model construction (e.g., Blender) and for RT (e.g., Wireless InSite or Sionna), SiteViewer and RT capabilities of MATLAB are used in this work. RT simulation parameters are summarized in Table II. The 

7.125 GHz frequency was selected since the recent World Radiocommunication Conference (WRC-23) indicated it as the only FR3 test band available in all ITU regions. The 

1-m resolution corresponds to 24 wavelengths, ensuring spatial coherence [13]. The other parameters are selected to be transferable to typical UxNB deployments. The total size of the simulated area is larger than in other datasets [5], [6] and corresponds to an increased UxNB coverage footprint. Moreover, the UxNB is always located in the middle of the layout at elevated heights (10 m to 500 m, sampling 5 heights per location) to reflect the focus on UxNB-centric scenarios. This results in a total dataset size of N = 16 180 samples (3 236 layouts × 5 Tx heights). The receivers are placed at ground level in all non-building coordinates. We use the number of ray interactions from CKM literature [5]. The resulting channel state tensors Cn store radio parameters across F = 4 channels: 1) Binary visibility mask V: LOS ( 1) vs. NLOS ( 0) states. 2) Channel attenuation in dB. 3) Multipath Root Mean Square (RMS) delay spread in ns. 4) Multipath RMS angular spread in degrees. In-building elements are assigned a value of 0 in Cn and should be masked out during training and evaluation.

Channel attenuation modeling requires resolving two prop-agation regimes: (i) LOS channels experience free-space path loss and two-ray coherent ground reflections, and (ii) NLOS channels are dominated by multipath propagation affected by local buildings (especially near the receiver). Furthermore, as the UxNB altitude htx increases, the probability of LOS and the dominant propagation mechanisms change. Standard 2D image-to-image networks struggle to natively capture these altitude-dependent 3D spatial variations. To address this, we propose a propagation-informed CKM attenuation simulator that explicitly separates the environment into LOS and NLOS areas (see Fig. 2). The model implements the attenuation-focused mapping function Φθ (En, mn) by combining distinct LOS and NLOS models:

Using CKM training data, we employ geometry-dependent calibration for LOS and NLOS branches. The proposed LOS attenuation model relies on the free-space and two-ray models corrected with data-learned terms. The proposed NLOS model takes into account the ITU topology characteristics and uses a CKM data-corrected version of the COST 231-Hata model. Given the deployment geometry ( htx , hrx , and the fixed horizontal UxNB position), we pre-compute spatial geometry matrices over the 513 × 513 grid: horizontal distance D2D ,direct 3D distance D3D , ground-reflected path length DGR ,and elevation angle Θ2D . For any receiver location p = ( i, j ),the scalar geometric parameters ( d2D , d 3D , d GR , θ ) are indexed directly from these matrices. For notational brevity, we omit the spatial index p in subsequent expressions.

A. Experimental Setup and Evaluation Protocol 

The dataset split contains 10 840 samples from 37 training cities, 2 750 samples from 15 validation cities, and 2 590 samples from 14 final test cities. No city is moved between these partitions to ensure strict spatial generalization. Training procedures described in Section III use only training cities, where all nine topology and height regimes are fitted on the 10 840 CKM training samples. The validation subset is reserved for development checks and component ablation. Final performance results use the unseen 14-city test subset. After excluding building interiors and outdoor channels lacking valid ray tracing outputs, the test dataset evaluates over 

4.23 ×10 8 ground receivers. Of these, only 7.4% are in NLOS states due to the elevated UxNB geometry. Although the element-weighted overall score is therefore LOS-dominated, 424 of the 2 590 test maps contain more than 25% NLOS receivers, motivating our separate regime-specific evaluations. C. Ablation and Computational Complexity Studies 

Component ablation on the 2 750-sample validation set confirms the necessity of each calibrated term. Incorporating the coherent ground-reflection term reduces LOS RMSE from 

3.79 dB to 1.7412 dB . For NLOS propagation, using the raw uncalibrated COST 231-Hata model yields an unacceptable RMSE of 25 dB at 7.125 GHz, whereas COST 231-Hata augmented with the fitted regression coefficients and topology-and height-specific calibration reaches 3.20 dB on validation. Recalibrated trial-and-error tests revealed that a compact model retaining eight of the 14 features (only l =41 and no interaction cross-terms) preserves over 90% of the complete model’s improvement over COST 231-Hata model. C ONCLUSIONS 

This paper introduced a dense FR3 (7.125 GHz) UAV CKM dataset providing path loss, delay spread, angular spread, and geometric visibility across 16 180 simulated samples from 66 global cities. Using this dataset, we proposed a computa-tionally efficient, height-aware channel attenuation prediction framework. By relying on conventional models calibrated on the dataset, the model generates accurate attenuation maps and can generalize to unseen city layouts. The proposed simulator achieves an overall test RMSE of 1.93 dB with supplied visibility masks and a median CPU runtime of 0.06 s per map. REFERENCES [1] E. Vinogradov, H. Sallouha, S. De Bast, M. M.
