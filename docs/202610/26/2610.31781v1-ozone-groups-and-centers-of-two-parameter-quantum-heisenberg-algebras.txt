# Ozone groups and centers of two-parameter quantum Heisenberg algebras 

James Gómez, Héctor Suárez and Helbert Venegas 

Abstract 

Let p, q be roots of unity with pq ̸ = 1, and put ℓ = |⟨ p, q⟩| and t = ord (pq ). Up to isomor-phism, the two-parameter quantum Heisenberg algebras Hp,q are exactly the noetherian PI graded down-up algebras, so everything here holds for that whole class. The ozone group is μℓ × μℓ/t, generated by conjugation by three normal elements, and the center is recov-ered from the fixed ring of the ozone group as the invariants of a cyclic group of order t. We give a presentation of the center as a hypersurface over a cyclic quotient surface singularity. It is regular exactly when 1 is a characteristic root, which disproves a conjecture of Chan, Gaddis, Won and Zhang, and Gorenstein exactly when pm/h = qn/h, where m = ord (p),

n = ord (q) and h = gcd (m, n); for p = ζ5, q = ζ25 it is not. We also decide when the fixed ring of the ozone group is Gorenstein and when Hp,q is Calabi–Yau, and show that the al-gebras with regular center have non-affine automorphisms and nonzero locally nilpotent derivations. 

Keywords: two-parameter quantum Heisenberg algebra; graded down-up algebra; PI algebras; cen-ter; ozone groups; Gorenstein rings; complete intersections; locally nilpotent derivations.

# 1 Introduction 

Kirkman and Small [21] introduced the quantum Heisenberg algebra 

Hq = k⟨x, y, z⟩/(zx − qxz , yz − qzy , xy − qyx − z),a deformation of the enveloping algebra of the three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra. It is a noetherian AS regular domain of global dimension three, and a central quotient of it is Hayashi’s q-analogue of the Weyl algebra [14]. Gaddis [10] added a second parameter, 

Hp,q = k⟨x, y, z⟩/(zx − p−1 xz , zy − pyz , yx − qxy − z), p, q ∈ k×, (1.1) an iterated Ore extension which, for pq ̸ = 1, is PI exactly when p and q are roots of unity [10, Propositions 3.4 and 3.11]. The condition pq ̸ = 1 is missing there, and it cannot be dropped (Section 5). Since z = yx − qxy , the algebra is generated by x and y, and eliminating z [10, (3.7)] identi-fies it with a graded down-up algebra: 

Hp,q ∼= A(α, β) = k⟨x, y⟩/(y2 x − αyxy − βxy 2, yx 2 − αxyx − βx2y), α = p−1 + q, β = −p−1q.1arXiv:2610.31781v1 [math.QA] 22 Oct 2026 The characteristic roots, the roots of X2 − αX − β, are ω1 = q and ω2 = p−1, and the two normal elements Ωi = yx − ωi xy are z and θ := yx − p−1 xy . Up to isomorphism, the Hp,q

with p, q roots of unity and pq ̸ = 1 are exactly the noetherian PI graded down-up algebras (Proposition 2.7). So this paper is about that whole class, and we switch between the two presentations as convenient. The ozone group of an algebra A with center Z is Oz (A) = Aut Z-alg (A), the automorphisms fixing Z pointwise. Chan, Gaddis, Won and Zhang introduced it to study centers of PI skew polynomial rings [5]. In [6] they showed that for a noetherian PI AS regular algebra Oz (A) is fi-nite of order dividing rk Z A, that a trivial ozone group forces the Calabi–Yau property, and that among algebras generated in degree one the skew polynomial rings are the ones with abelian ozone group of order rk Z A. Down-up algebras were proposed as a test case [5, Section 6], and part of the work is already there. Ozone automorphisms of noetherian PI down-up algebras are diagonal on x and y, so the group is abelian [6, Corollary 4.7]; Liu, Wu and Zhu later proved that the ozone group of every noetherian PI AS regular algebra is abelian [27, Theorem 3.4]. For A(0, 1 ) and A(0, −1) the ozone group, the center and the rank are computed in [6, Exam-ple 4.8]. For these two algebras AOz (A) is commutative and the center is its fixed ring under a Galois group of order two [6, Examples 5.4 and 5.5]; this is how the center of A(0, −1) is seen to be Gorenstein. In general, the authors note, no presentation of the center was known, nor whether it is always Gorenstein [6, Section 5.1]. Generators had been found by Zhao [35]. (The quantum Heisenberg algebras of [6, Section 1.1] have z2 instead of z in the last relation. They form a different family.) Here we carry out the computation for the whole class. # 6 Applications to questions of Chan, Gaddis, Won and Zhang 

Section 6 of [5] ends with a list of questions on noetherian PI Artin–Schelter regular algebras, and down-up algebras are proposed there as a test case. Question 6.4 was answered in Sec-tion 3. Here we look at three more. Two of them turn out to be the same question for down-up algebras, and an example of Liu, Wu and Zhu [27] already settles it; what the family Hp,q adds is an exact criterion in terms of p and q. The third, on affine automorphisms and locally nilpo-tent derivations, has a negative answer for every noetherian PI graded down-up algebra that satisfies its hypotheses.
