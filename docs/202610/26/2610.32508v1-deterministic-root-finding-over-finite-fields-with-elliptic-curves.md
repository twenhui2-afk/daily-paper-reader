## Abstract
Finding the roots of a polynomial over a finite field $\mathbb F_q$ in deterministic polynomial time is a long-standing open problem. Classical deterministic methods work in the multiplicative group $\mathbb F_q^\times$ and are efficient only when $q-1$ is smooth, which is rarely the case.   We propose a method that replaces $\mathbb F_q^\times$ by the groups of rational points of an elliptic curve $E$ and of its quadratic twist $\widetilde E$. The computation splits into an offline phase, which depends only on $\mathbb F_q$ and outputs a short \emph{elliptic preprocessing datum} $\mathcal D$, and an online phase, which processes the input polynomial. Writing $Ξ(\mathcal D)$ for the largest prime factor of $\lvert E(\mathbb F_q)\rvert\cdot\lvert\widetilde E(\mathbb F_q)\rvert$, the online phase is deterministic and finds the roots of any $f\in\mathbb F_q[X]$ of degree $n$ within $\widetilde O(Ξ(\mathcal D)^2\,n\log^2 q+n^ω\log q+n\log^3 q)$ operations in $\mathbb F_q$. A precursor of our method is an algorithm by Rónyai, which recovers the roots through Galois computations over number fields, relies on GRH, and comes with a polynomial bound of unspecified degree; in contrast, our online phase works entirely over $\mathbb F_q$ and is unconditional. Moreover, $\mathcal D$ can be certified deterministically and unconditionally in time polynomial in $\log q$ and $Ξ(\mathcal D)$.   We conjecture that every $\mathbb F_q$ of large enough characteristic admits a datum with $Ξ(\mathcal D)\leq(\log q)^{O(1)}$, and support this with a heuristic and with experiments. Constructing such a datum in polynomial time remains open.
