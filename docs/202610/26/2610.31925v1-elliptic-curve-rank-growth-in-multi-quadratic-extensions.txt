1. Introduction 

Let K be a number field and let E be an elliptic curve defined over K. The set of K-rational points on E forms a finitely generated abelian group by the Mordell–Weil theorem. Thus, the structure theorem of finitely generated abelian groups provides a group isomorphism 

E(K) ∼= E(K)tors × ZRE/K 

where E(K)tors is the (finite) subgroup formed by all the elements of finite order and the non-negative integer RE/K = rank E(K) ≥ 0 is the rank of E(K)/E (K)tors as a Z-module. By Merel’s proof of the uniform boundedness conjecture (see [39]), if we fix d ≥ 1, and we let E/K be any elliptic curve over a number field K of degree [ K : Q] = d, then E(K)tors is, up to isomorphism, one of finitely many possibilities. The concrete lists of torsion subgroups that can occur for d =[K : Q] = 1, 2, 3, and 4 are known by work of Mazur (for d = 1; see [37]), Kamienny, Kenku, and Momose (for d = 2; see [30, 31]), Derickx, Etropolski, van Hoeij, Morrow, and Zureick-Brown (for 

d = 3; see [6], and [29] for rational elliptic curves), and Derickx and Najman (for d = 4; see [7]). In addition, Najman has claimed the classification of torsion subgroups for d = 5 in a recent preprint (see [46]). The complete classification of possible torsion subgroups in degree d > 5 is not known, though much work has been done in that direction (see [19], for instance). One can also ask what are the possible torsion subgroups for a specific number field K, and this is known in several cases, such as K = Q (due to Mazur), or Q(i), Q(√−3) (due to Najman, [43]). For a classification result of torsion subgroups in the function field case, see McDonald’s [38]. In contrast to the progress in the classification of torsion subgroups of elliptic curves, the clas-sification of ranks over number fields continues to be wide open, even for K = Q (more is known over function fields, see for instance [57], [21]). For a fixed d ≥ 1 and a number field K, we define two sets: 

RK = {RE/K : E is an elliptic curve defined over K}, and 

Rd = {RE/L : E is an elliptic curve defined over some L with [ L : Q] = d}.

A number field is multiquadratic if it is a compositum of quadratic fields. Let G be a finite subgroup of Q×/Q×2, and put K = Q(√G) = Q({√g : g ∈ G}), so that Gal( K/ Q) ∼= G∨ is 2-elementary abelian of order |G|. Thus rank growth in multiquadratic extensions is the same problem as finding twists of positive rank indexed by a subgroup (or a coset of a subgroup) of Q×/Q×2. In this section we summarize known results in this direction. 

• Mestre (see [40]) constructed a parametric quadratic twist of rank ≥ 2 for certain elliptic curves over Q. We will come back to this construction in Section 7 to explain some potential uses and limitations of Mestre’s construction.

In this section we explain how, in some cases, one can obtain twists of rank ≥ 2 unconditionally (with two independent rational points that are explicitly given), and conjecturally of rank ≥ 3, using a classical construction of Mestre. However, we also explain that this method cannot be extended to all elliptic curves over Q, while our methods above apply to all of them. Ideally, we would like to use Corollary 7.2 to construct a sequence of square-free integers dk to be used as in Lemma 5.2, but several difficulties arise. For instance, Corollary 7.2 does not work for every elliptic curve over Q.

Example 7.3 (j = 0 and j = 1728) . If a = 0 (that is, j = 0) or b = 0 ( j = 1728), then g(t) ≡ 0and Theorem 7.1 does not apply. This is the case for the curve E : y2 = x3 − 2 of Examples 2.10 and 6.1, which has j = 0; similarly for any curve y2 = x3 + Ax , which has j = 1728. For these curves, Theorem 1.2 still applies and produces explicit twists of positive rank and prescribed root number. Another possible situation is when the Mestre twist always gives W (Eg(t)/Q) = 1 for every 

t ∈ Q with g(t)̸ = 0. For instance, one can show this to be the case for the curve 162c1 . Then we cannot guarantee even under Conjecture 4.3 that any of the quadratic twists produced by the Mestre construction have rank ≥ 3. As a future direction, the authors are currently looking into sufficient conditions on an elliptic curve E/ Q to apply Mestre’s construction and conclude certain conditional rank increases in multi-quadratic extensions by multiples of, conjecturally, at least 3.
