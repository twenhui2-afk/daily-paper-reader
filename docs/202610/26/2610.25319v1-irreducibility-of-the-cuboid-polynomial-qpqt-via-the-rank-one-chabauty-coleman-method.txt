# Irreducibility of the Cuboid Polynomial Qp,q (t) via the Rank-One Chabauty–Coleman Method 

## Valery Asiryan 

asiryanvalery@gmail.com 

October 18, 2026 

Abstract 

Let Qp,q (t) ∈ Z[t] be Sharipov’s even monic degree-10 second cuboid polynomial, with coprime positive integers p̸ = q. We prove that Qp,q (t) is irreducible over Q,and hence over Z. More generally, the normalized associated quintic Ps(x) and its composition Ps(t2) are irreducible over Q for every s ∈ Q \ { 0, ±1}.The proof reduces linear and quadratic factors to rational points and horizontal con-jugate pairs on one fixed genus-2 curve. A certified rank upper bound for its Jacobian, together with an exact divisor calculation, gives a rank-one annihilating differential at the prime 3. For the quadratic-factor case, the symmetric coordinates of a horizontal pair lie on an explicit double cover of an elliptic quartic. All rational affine points on this model are 3-adically integral and reduce to six smooth residue classes. The pulled-back differential is a unit in each class, proving that six displayed points form the complete list. Their coordinates exclude every admissible parameter. A negative real root of the irreducible quintic then supplies the additional degree argument re-quired for irreducibility of the degree-10 polynomial. The rank computation and all exact arithmetic certificates are specified explicitly for reproduction. 

Keywords: irreducibility; perfect cuboid; cuboid polynomials; hyperelliptic curves; Jacobians; Chabauty–Coleman method; rational divisors; computer-assisted proof. 

Mathematics Subject Classification: 11D41, 11G30, 14H25, 12E05, 11Y16. 

# 1 Introduction 

The perfect cuboid problem asks for a rectangular box with positive integer edges whose three face diagonals and space diagonal are integers. In Sharipov’s polynomial formulation [1, 2], one encounters explicit even polynomials depending on integer parameters. The second cuboid polynomial Qp,q (t) has degree 10 and is conjectured to be irreducible for coprime positive integers p̸ = q.

Goal. We prove this irreducibility statement. The intermediate result is stronger than re-quired by the original parametrization: the associated normalized quintic Ps(x) is irreducible for every s ∈ Q>0 \ { 1}, without assuming that s is a rational square. 1arXiv:2610.25319v1 [math.GM] 18 Oct 2026 Method. A reducible quintic has an irreducible factor of degree at most two. An explicit transformation sends a root of Ps to a fixed genus-2 curve C, with second coordinate (s −

1) /(s + 1) . A rational root gives a rational point of C. An irreducible quadratic factor gives a conjugate pair on C whose two points have the same second coordinate. Thus both cases are controlled by the same Jacobian. We use the rank-one form of the Chabauty–Coleman method [3, 4]. The treatment of conjugate pairs is related to symmetric-power methods [5], but only a one-dimensional locus of horizontal pairs is needed here. We construct its model directly and prove the required local uniqueness rather than invoke a rational-point theorem for the entire symmetric square. A rank bound for the genus-2 Jacobian is obtained computationally, in the setting of explicit descent [6], using Magma [7]. All subsequent steps use a small exact divisor calculation, power series over Z3, and enumeration over F3. In particular, the proof does not assume completeness of a bounded search for rational points. The article is organized as follows. Section 2 states the normalization and the main theorem. Sections 3–4 establish the rank-one differential. Section 5 excludes linear factors. Sections 6–7 determine the horizontal-pair obstruction and exclude quadratic factors. Sec-tion 8 completes the degree-10 argument. The appendix records the computational inputs and reproducible verification code.

# 8 Completion of the irreducibility proof 

Proof of Theorem 1. A reducible polynomial of degree five over a field has an irreducible factor of degree at most two. Indeed, if every irreducible factor had degree at least three, two nonconstant factors would already have total degree at least six. Corollaries 1 and 2 therefore prove irreducibility of Ps(x) over Q.We next establish irreducibility after composition with a square. By (27), 

Ps(−1) = 4( s − 1) 4(s + 1) > 0, Ps(0) = −s5 < 0.

Thus Ps has a real root α ∈ (−1, 0) . Its irreducibility gives [Q(α) : Q] = 5 . View Q(α) as the subfield of R defined by this negative real root. A square in this real field is nonnegative, so α is not a square in Q(α). The polynomial u2 − α is consequently irreducible over Q(α).If β2 = α, then Q(α) ⊂ Q(β) and 

[Q(β) : Q] = [ Q(β) : Q(α)] [ Q(α) : Q] = 2 · 5 = 10 .

Since β is a root of the monic degree-10 polynomial Ps(u2), that polynomial is irreducible over Q. For s = ( p/q )2, the invertible rational change of variable u = t/q 2 and the nonzero scalar factor in (3) preserve irreducibility. Hence Qp,q (t) is irreducible over Q. # A Magma scripts and transcripts 

The only rank computation used in the proof is the upper bound in Lemma 2. Except for the auxiliary genus-two rational-point verification, the remaining computational checks use exact rational polynomial arithmetic or finite enumeration over F3. The code below is arranged to keep these two roles separate. Its rank target is 1; the remaining assertions check the isomorphism of curve models, the root-map and reciprocal identities, the divisor certificate, its trace and norm, and the residue-class calculations. The analytic uniqueness arguments are proved in the main text and do not depend on a rational-point search.
