FOURIER QUASICRYSTALS WITH POSITIVE INTEGER MASSES ARE SECTIONS OF LEE–YANG CYCLES 

DONGSHENG WEI 

Abstract. We characterize Fourier quasicrystal measures with positive integer masses as real sections of effective Lee–Yang cycles, with weighted local intersection degrees. The section matrix has positive maximal minors, and the mass at each root is its weighted local intersection degree. For Delone support the reduced support of the cycle can be chosen strict. The support of every integer-mass Fourier quasicrystal measure is the common complex zero set of finitely many real-valued trigonometric polynomials. For unit masses the representing cycle can be chosen reduced, with local degree one at every physical root. These results answer Questions (1), (2), and (4) of Alon, Kummer, Kurasov, and Vinzant. The proof recovers a finite-dimensional torus from the spectrum, proves that the resulting analytic set is algebraic, and reconstructs its complex branches and their integer coefficients from their moments. A support-height theorem for holonomic sequences gives the required linear spectral bound. Singular Lee–Yang sections are Fourier quasicrystal measures with their natural local intersection degrees; discarding those degrees can destroy the Fourier quasicrystal property.

Crystalline measures are tempered measures whose Fourier transforms are measures, with both supports locally finite and without the weighted growth condition above [25]. Meyer constructed crystalline measures beyond finite combinations of lattice combs [25]. For general coefficients the weighted growth condition is not automatic: there are crystalline measures on R that are not Fourier quasicrystal measures [13, 24]. The distinction between local finiteness and uniform discreteness of the spectrum is essential to the inverse problem. Lev and Olevskii proved that a measure with uniformly discrete support and spectrum is a finite sum of translated lattice combs multiplied by trigonometric polynomials, for complex measures in one dimension and for positive measures in every dimension [22, Theorems 1–3]. In the present setting the spectrum is only locally finite. Kurasov and Sarnak [18] constructed explicit positive crystalline measures and Fourier quasicrystals from pairs of stable polynomials. The one-dimensional description by real-rooted exponential polynomials is due to Olevskii–Ulanovskii [28]; Alon–Cohen–Vinzant [1] then obtained a Lee–Yang polynomial representation. In higher dimensions, Lawton and Tsikh [20] study Fourier quasicrystals in Rn defined by systems of real-rooted trigonometric polynomials. Earlier, Lawton and Tsikh [21, Abstract] gave sufficient conditions for measures with positive integer weights to be Fourier quasicrystals and suggested that these conditions might also be necessary. The inverse theorems here address the necessity direction by representing every such Fourier quasicrystal as a section of an effective Lee–Yang cycle. INTEGER-MASS FOURIER QUASICRYSTALS 3

1.1. Lee–Yang sections and the inverse problem. For a variety X ⊂ (P1)n and a real n × d

matrix L, set Λ( X, L ) = {x ∈ Cd : exp(2 πiLx ) ∈ X}.

The Lee–Yang condition combines reciprocal conjugation invariance with an exclusion on the logarithms of the coordinate moduli. On the complex torus, it says that the amoeba of X meets no positive d-plane away from the origin. A positive plane is one represented by a matrix whose maximal minors are all positive. In dimension one these are lines spanned by vectors with all coordinates positive. The full definition, including the projective boundary and strictness, is recalled in Section 2. All varieties in this paper are reduced. If L has positive maximal minors and X is Lee–Yang, then every point of Λ( X, L ) is real. Indeed, Log exp(2 πiLx ) = −2πL Im x ∈ LRd,

and amoeba avoidance forces this vector, hence Im x, to vanish. Under the strict Lee–Yang condition the construction gives Delone FQ-sets [3, Theorem 2.3].

12. Further questions 

The classification leaves open questions about equation multiplicities, effective bounds, and the choice of a Lee–Yang representative. The distinction between local intersection degree and colength is relevant to the first of these. In the Cohen–Macaulay case, for example when c = 1, can the masses be realized as the local colengths of a finite system of trigonometric equations? Question (5) of [3, §2.4] asks for a Lee–Yang representation of the zero set of an arbitrary real-rooted system of trigonometric equations. Our inverse statement assumes the existence of an FQ measure with positive integer masses on the zero set, so it does not address that question. Separately, can the number of real equations in Theorem B always be taken equal to d?Can the rank of the spectral group be bounded effectively in terms of the data in (1.1)? Can one choose a basis of the spectral group for which the constant CL in Theorem D is also bounded effectively in terms of these data? Some choice of basis is needed: for the Dirac comb of Z2, which is its own Fourier transform, the bases with rows (1 , 0) and ( q, 1), q ∈ N, give the spectral point (0 , 1) the coordinates ( −q, 1). Finally, Proposition 11.1 makes Y intrinsic in Hom(Γ , C∗); its coordinates in ( C∗)n depend on the chosen basis L. Its Lee–Yang representative also depends on the compression matrix S. Can a Lee–Yang representative be chosen intrinsically, independently of these choices?
