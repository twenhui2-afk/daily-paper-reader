# A Hawkes Microfoundation for Multitype Inverse Gaussian Subordinators 

Yingli Wang ∗ Wei Xu † Lingjiong Zhu ‡

October 8, 2026 

Abstract 

We provide an event-level Hawkes microfoundation for a multitype inverse-Gaussian stochas-tic clock. We show that the event counts and integrated intensities of nearly critical multivariate linear Hawkes processes converge jointly to a multivariate pure-jump subordinator when repro-duction delays have finite mean and immigration is balanced so that rare large families remain visible. The dependence among the limiting coordinates is inherited from microscopic cross-excitation, and the limit admits a Brownian additive-field first-passage representation. We call this process the multitype inverse-Gaussian subordinator. Its diagonal case consists of indepen-dent classical inverse-Gaussian subordinators, with the univariate model as a further special case. The square-root specialization recovers the inverse-Gaussian clock obtained at the boundary of hyper-rough square-root models by Abi Jaber–Attal–Rosenbaum ( Ann. Appl. Probab. 36 (4): 3635–3660, 2026). Our cluster proof exposes the underlying mechanism: finite-variance near-critical branching creates rare but macroscopic families, while their internal timing disappears on the observation scale, so each family becomes a jump. We also clarify the relation with the multivariate Hawkes scaling theory of Xu (arXiv:2412.14459): its diagonal-atom condition yields the diagonal specialization, whereas the genuinely coupled limit requires a separate uniqueness argument. In addition, under a common tilted-stability condition, we replace the high-intensity assumption in that theory by a weaker accumulated-activity condition and provide the Riccati identification needed when the potential has an atom. We strengthen convergence of the count and compensator and extend the scalar conclusion to finite-variance age-dependent branching clusters.

Our main contributions are as follows. (i) We introduce the multitype inverse Gaussian (IG) subordinator through the coupled quadratic system (10) and establish its representation as the first-passage time of a Brownian additive field. This construction recovers the univariate, diagonal multivariate, and square-root IG limits as successive special cases (Corollaries 2.3–2.5). (ii) We prove the multivariate Hawkes-to-IG limit throughout the finite-mean calendar-collapse region. Coupled Poisson family transforms identify the limiting quadratic exponent, collapsed multitype families converge as compound-Poisson subordinators, and a matrix renewal es-timate restores calendar time and yields joint product-M1 convergence of the counts and compensators (Theorem 2.2 and Lemmas 4.1–4.3). M1-topology (Theorems 3.2 and 3.7). We also show that the diagonal-atom condition in [47] recovers precisely the diagonal specialization of our limit. (iv) For a fixed scalar reproduction-time density, we prove that calendar-collapse at any fixed positive time occurs exactly when T r T → ∞ under the rare-family balance (Lemma 5.1). # 6 Branching structure and universality 

This section complements the Volterra first-passage interpretation in [3, Lemmas 2.3–2.4 and Theo-rem 3.1] with a branching construction. # 7 Conclusion 

We have shown that a nearly critical multivariate Hawkes system can converge directly to a mul-titype inverse-Gaussian clock. The first-order matrix defect B records cross-type interaction, and the coupled quadratic system (10) determines the joint Laplace exponent. The same process is the first-passage subordinator of a Brownian additive L´ evy field. Thus the multivariate limit is not introduced by an arbitrary dependence construction: its dependence is inherited from microscopic cross-excitation. The cluster proof isolates the mechanism. Multitype finite-variance branching produces rare families with masses of order r−2 

> T

; the balance T r T μT → η keeps a non-degenerate collection of such families visible; and the matrix age estimate (65), together with T r T → ∞ , collapses their internal calendar time. Product M1 merges the resulting microscopic staircases into macroscopic jumps. When B is diagonal, the coupled exponent separates into independent classical IG clocks; d = 1 gives the scalar result, and the square-root normalization reproduces the hyper-rough-boundary law of [3, Theorem 3.1]. Our comparison with [47, equations (2.8)–(2.9), Theorem 2.9, and Remark 2.21] draws a sharp boundary. Its scaling theory depends on accumulated activity nθ n, so the assumption θn → ∞ 

can be weakened to nθ n → ∞ under common tilted stability and the diagonal-atom condition. The positive-resolvent and local Riccati arguments above establish this limit, and monotonicity upgrades the count and compensator to product M1. For our array the formal potential is B−1δ0.The diagonal-atom condition [47, Assumption 2.8 and Remark 2.21] therefore proves the diagonal multitype and scalar cases, while the non-diagonal theorem requires the direct cluster identification and M -matrix uniqueness argument given here. 32 In terms of financial interpretation, a jump of Zi represents a rapidly completed endogenous avalanche of type-i activity, while dependence among coordinates records how one event class propagates into others. Corollary 2.7 shows that a Brownian motion run on the scalar square-root clock is symmetric NIG, but a process-level return model would additionally need signs, imbalance, leverage, and a rule for composition at clock jumps [15, 6]. Heavy-tailed delays may preserve family timing and produce rough memory, whereas infinite-variance offspring or marks should lead to stable rather than IG clocks. Extending the genuinely coupled result beyond the identity critical eigenspace, and treating those tail regimes jointly with multitype interaction, remain natural directions.
