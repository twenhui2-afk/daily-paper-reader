# Accelerating Finite-Sum Bilevel Optimization with Fully First-Order Oracles 

Qihao Zhou ∗ Shanshui Niu †

Abstract 

We study nonconvex–strongly-convex bilevel optimization with finite-sum objectives and fully first-order oracles. We propose Sampled Implicit Corrections for Bilevel Optimization (SICBO), which combines a penalty approximation with variance-reduced corrections for the lower-level variables. Under componentwise lower strong convexity, SICBO finds a point x

satisfying E∥∇ Φ( x)∥2 ≤ ε2 within ˜O(N + √N κ 7/2ε−2) component-gradient calls, where N is the total number of components and κ is the normalized lower-level condition number. The bound achieves square-root finite-sum dependence using only gradients and preserves the best known deterministic fully first-order condition-number exponent for smooth problems. Our improvement follows from a curvature-dependent bound for nonlinear correction errors, which permits epochs of length ˜Θ( √N/κ ) with batches of size ˜Θ( √N κ ). A single-level reduction shows that the dependence on N and ε is optimal up to logarithmic factors for fixed κ.

23 Related Work 

Applications. Bilevel problems arise in hyperparameter optimization [9, 21], training-example reweighting [23], and meta-learning [9]. In the validation-based setting of Section 6.2, the upper and lower sums are validation and training losses, respectively. 

Hypergradient methods. Implicit hypergradient methods use Hessian- or Jacobian-vector products of the lower objective. Stochastic approximation methods were analyzed by Ghadimi and Wang [10], Hong et al. [11] and Ji et al. [12]; momentum and recursive variance reduction improve their sample complexity [15, 27]. These methods track both the lower solution and a linear-system solution. 

Fully first-order methods. Fully first-order methods replace second-order products by differences of gradients. Liu et al. [19] proposed a value-function method, and Kwon et al. [16] developed a penalty-based stochastic approximation method. Kwon et al. [17] studied the penalty landscape, and Shen and Chen [24] analyzed penalty gradient descent without lower-level strong convexity. Under normalized smoothness assumptions, Chen et al. Lower bounds. Ji and Liang [13] established lower bounds for bilevel problems with strongly convex or convex upper objectives. For our nonconvex finite-sum class, the following reduction gives the optimal dependence on N and ε.

Proposition 3.2 (Optimal dependence for fixed condition number) . For every fixed κ ≥ 1,sufficiently large N , and sufficiently small ε > 0, the worst-case component-gradient complexity of 

(3) under Assumptions 1–3, with ∆ = 1 , is at least Ω( N + √N ε −2).

Appendix F proves the proposition by reduction from the individual-smoothness lower bound of Peng et al. [22], which permits unrestricted randomized component queries. Thus (2) is optimal up to logarithms in N and ε for fixed κ. Dagréou et al. [7, Theorem 2] previously proved an Ω( √nε −2)bound for a restricted class of linear bilevel algorithms, where n is our upper-level sum size. Their regime is ε2 ≲ 1/n with other constants fixed, and their result does not characterize κ dependence. For condition-number dependence, Chen et al. [4, Theorem 4.1] prove an Ω( κ5/2ε−2) bound for deterministic first-order algorithms with quadratic lower objectives. Their class does not impose our global bound on ∇yf , so the result does not directly apply here. Appendix F gives further lower bounds under our assumptions. The sharp joint dependence on N and κ remains open; separate lower bounds do not establish a product bound.

4.2 Sampled implicit corrections 

SICBO maintains zt, v t and the field estimates ̂ At,̂ Bt,̂ Gt. Write wt = ( xt, z t, v t). Each epoch begins with population inner updates and full field evaluations. Between refreshes, SICBO updates x, then 

z, then v, using the residual maps 

rzt (u) := ASzt (xt+1 , u ) − ASzt (xt, z t) + ̂ At,rvt (u) := BSvt (xt+1 , z t+1 , u ) − BSvt (wt) + ̂ Bt. (10) We use capped Katyusha iterations to obtain ∥rzt (zt+1 )∥ ≤ τ and then, with zt+1 fixed, ∥rvt (vt+1 )∥ ≤ 

τ . Each residual is the gradient of a strongly convex objective. Every batch contains b fresh, independent uniform index pairs. The evaluation batch Rt is drawn after both corrections, making its increments conditionally centered at the updated points. The first inner solves start at zinit , v init and later solves at the preceding iterates. We cap every solve and check its residual. Sampled updates must also satisfy ∥zt+1 − zt∥ ≤ 1/(512 κ) and 

∥vt+1 ∥ ≤ 2κ. A failed test returns x0. The output set U uses the estimates that generated the outer steps. Clipping and the inner checks bound all accepted trajectories, allowing the high-probability tracking estimates to imply (3). A failed check terminates the run without an additional full pass. # 7 Conclusion 

We proposed SICBO for finite-sum nonconvex–strongly-convex bilevel optimization. Its component-gradient complexity ˜O(N + √N κ 7/2ε−2) is optimal up to logarithms in N and ε for fixed κ and preserves the best known deterministic fully first-order condition-number exponent for smooth problems. The analysis combines curvature-dependent correction bounds with pathwise control of evaluation errors. Experiments on synthetic and LIBSVM data show the fewest component-gradient calls among the compared methods, with an advantage over full refresh that decreases as 

κ−1/2. Extending the result to average lower strong convexity, obtaining a single-loop method, and establishing the sharp joint dependence on N and κ remain open. 9Acknowledgements
