LATTICE MODULI AND RESIDUAL ATOMS 

MAURICIO CORRÊA 

Abstract. We study prescribed holomorphic lattices for regular-singular connections and residual factors of quantum F -bundles. For an arbitrary ordered monodromy spectrum, we classify the fully framed local groupoid: each residue fibre is a discrete affine space of dimension r(r − 1), with a unique normal form and trivial stabilisers. We then analyse elementary changes of lattice. A strictly positive integral residue difference obstructs the oriented wall; otherwise, in arbitrary rank, the extending normal-form arcs form an affine torsor of relative dimension r(r − 1), and specialisation to the shifted residue slice is polynomial and triangular by root height. Adjacent walls exhibit the composite-root correction dictated by the type-A root-subgroup relations, while an example over the dual numbers detects a nilpotent saturation defect. For one-branch F -bundles, the scalar branch is recovered from the residual trace, and scalar residualisation yields a symmetric monoidal equivalence with zero-branch 

F -bundles. This construction yields residual atoms, functorial Jordan strata, and a residual chemical formula compatible with blow-ups and projective bundles. In the A-model, residualisation removes the unit direction; the rationally marked large-radius residual germ recovers the divisor cubic and distinguishes the marked residual germs of the Pfaffian and Grassmannian Calabi–Yau threefolds. 

1. Introduction 

Let ∆ ⊂ C be a disc centred at the origin and containing the point 1. Kontsevich and Szabó consider regular-singular connections on the punctured disc with a prescribed holomorphic lattice across the singular point, an order-two pole bound in that lattice, and an ordered basis at 1 whose full flag is monodromy-invariant [19, §§1–3]. Their principal theorem identifies each connected component of the resulting coarse moduli space with an affine space when the prescribed diagonal monodromy eigenvalues are pairwise distinct and different from 1 [19, Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 4.1]. The underlying regular-singular connection belongs to the classical Levelt–Deligne theory [20, 9, 8, 26]; the prescribed lattice, however, is additional structure and need not be a Deligne or Levelt lattice. Closely related enriched Riemann–Hilbert problems retain parabolic or parahoric data [5, 13, 14], while quotient-stack descriptions of logarithmic connections arise in the Lie-groupoid formalism of Bischoff [2, 3]. The moduli problem considered here is more rigid: the full ordered frame is retained, and morphisms are required to act on the connection and on that frame simultaneously. Consequences and the global gluing problem. The preceding results determine the local geometry of a fixed residue stratum, a permitted simple wall in arbitrary rank, and the first compatibility between adjacent walls. Each residue stratum has an affine normal-form slice. A simple wall is either excluded by positive integral resonance or represented by a smooth affine torsor whose specialisation to the shifted residue slice is polynomial and triangular by root height. These results provide the local data expected in a global wall-crossing space, but they do not construct such a space. Three issues remain. An affine flag position requires a comparison lattice, which is not part of the original prescribed-lattice functor. The wall torsors and their transition data must be descended from the fully framed slices rather than from their individual coordinates. Finally, the rank-three calculation controls the first adjacent-wall compatibility, whereas higher intersections and non-reduced bases require further descent and saturation arguments.

The descent results of Section 7 give the transformation laws under re-framing, identify the flagged quotient, and determine the further fibre over the unflagged constituent.
