Abstract. We establish a strong Hodge decomposition theorem for Novikov type Landau–Ginzburg models, that is, triples ( M, g, θ ) con-sisting of a non-compact complete K¨ ahler manifold ( M, g ) and a non-trivial closed holomorphic one-form θ on M . Under the well-tameness condition and under the assumption that M is Stein, we prove that the space of L2-forms on M admits an orthogonal decomposition into the space of harmonic forms of the twisted Laplacian ∆ θ and the images of the twisted Dolbeault operators ¯∂ + θ∧ and ¯∂† + ι¯θ♯ ; moreover, the harmonic space is concentrated in degree n = dim C M and is isomorphic to the Milnor rings of θ.As applications, we endow the complement of a hyperplane arrange-ment with a complete cylindrical K¨ ahler metric, and we show that the differential of a master function with nonresonant weights is well-tamed with respect to this metric. After passing to a log resolution in the non-normal-crossing case; we then apply the results to the Yang–Yang functional of the Gaudin model, whose critical points are the Bethe roots: the top-degree harmonic space is described by the corresponding Milnor rings, and the number of Bethe roots is given by the Varchenko– Orlik–Terao lattice formula. 

1. Introduction 

Landau–Ginzburg (LG) models have been studied for a long time by physicists as fundamental objects of topological field theories and mirror symmetry [CV91]. They are regarded as the mirror dual of Fano manifolds: the A-model of a Fano manifold is mirror to the B-model of the correspond-ing LG superpotential [BK98; HV00]. From the perspective of singularity theory, LG models appear naturally as the targets of FJRW theory for non-degenerate quasi-homogeneous polynomials [FJR13] and as the natural geo-metric settings for primitive forms, Frobenius manifolds and tt ∗-geometry [DFN90; Dub96]. A systematic differential-geometric study of LG models was initiated by the first author of [Fan11]. Several notions of tameness have been proposed for this purpose. The fundamental tameness (only finitely many critical points and full range of f ) and strong tameness were discussed in [Fan11]; the strong tameness is also called the elliptic condition in the early paper [KL91]. 3. Main Theorem and Proof 

3.1. Main Theorem. This section is devoted to the statement and the proof of the main theorem. The proof proceeds in three steps. First, a homotopy argument identifies the L2-cohomology of the ¯∂θ-complex with its ordinary and compactly supported cohomologies. Second, a ¯∂θ-Poincar´ elemma on Stein domains reduces these cohomologies to the holomorphic Koszul cohomology of θ. Third, a sheaf-theoretic computation localizes the Koszul cohomology at the critical points of θ and shows that it is concen-trated in degree n and finite-dimensional.
