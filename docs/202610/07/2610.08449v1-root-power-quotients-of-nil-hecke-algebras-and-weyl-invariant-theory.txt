ROOT-POWER QUOTIENTS OF NIL-HECKE ALGEBRAS AND WEYL INVARIANT THEORY 

ZHI-WEI LI 

Abstract. We study nil-Hecke quotients defined by powers of root coordinates. We identify their central ideals through the determinant components of root-orbit power ideals. Apolarity and discriminant removal reduce the graded dual centers to directional bounds on invariant polynomials. We determine their Hilbert series for every exponent and root orbit in all irreducible crystallographic types except E6, E 7, E 8.Relative discriminants compare centers for reflection subgroups and transfer equivariant resolutions. Coordinate-root centers in types B and 

C are Grassmannian cohomology rings. We classify self-injectivity in type 

A, in rank two, and in type F4, and determine the first nonsemisimple centers in the remaining classical families. For an explicit open set of reflection parameters, the polynomial image of the rational Cherednik algebra at t = 0 is the full nil-Hecke algebra. This realizes our algebras as Cherednik quotients and their finite centers as closed subschemes on a section of the generalized Calogero–Moser space. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 20C08, 13A50, 13D02, 14M15, 16E30. 

Key words and phrases. nil-Hecke algebra, root-power quotient, rational Cherednik algebra, Weyl invariant, apolarity, discriminant, Calogero–Moser space.

1. Introduction 

We study the quotient of a polynomial nil-Hecke algebra obtained by imposing a power of one root coordinate. Its center is a quotient of the Weyl invariant ring. The problem is to identify the defining ideal and determine how the root system and the root orbit control the resulting finite-dimensional algebra. The central ideal has two complementary descriptions: by polynomial content and the determinant component of a Weyl-orbit power ideal, and by apolar conditions on the projective root orbit. Removing the Weyl discriminant converts the graded dual of the center into a directional-degree bound on invariant polynomials. We solve this bound for every exponent and root orbit in all irreducible crystallographic types except E6, E 7, E 8.Relative discriminants compare the centers for reflection subgroups, and the differential action recovers multiplication and socles from their inverse systems. We also identify these algebras as quotients of rational Cherednik algebras at t = 0, through their polynomial representation. Their centers define closed subschemes on a specified section of the generalized Calogero–Moser space. A formal exponential comparison gives their completed zero-residue quiver Demazure interpretation. The polynomial proofs are independent of the general QDA construction and its mixed-residue PBW theory.

Relation to existing work and scope. The nil-Hecke endomorphism theo-rem and its relation to flag varieties are classical [2, 4, 10, 11]. The harmonic description of coinvariants is due to Steinberg [14]. The correspondence between powers of linear forms and fat-point inverse systems is also classi-cal; see Emsalem–Iarrobino and Ardila–Postnikov [5, 1]. Crowley–Larson interpret arrangement power-ideal inverse systems as spaces of sections of line bundles [3]. We use these established inputs to identify the determinant component selected by a root-power content ideal and then remove the dis-criminant. The resulting invariant directional filtration is the computational object of this paper. Ordinary strand-power cyclotomic nil-Hecke algebras have matrix descrip-tions over Grassmannian cohomology rings [12, 6]. Root coordinates give different quotients. In type An, n ≥ 2, the centers are non-Gorenstein be-yond the first nonzero exponent pair. The coordinate-root families in Bn, C n

recover Grassmannian rings, while other root orbits have different Hilbert functions and socles. Our main results identify these ideals, compute the Hilbert series outside the three exceptional E types, transfer centers and resolutions through reflection subgroups, and determine the stated algebra structures and self-injectivity ranges. Geometric descriptions of centers of cyclotomic quiver Hecke algebras occur in [16, 13, 7]. No identification of our root-power centers with those geometric centers is assumed. The non-Gorenstein examples in particular require care with any proposed cohomological interpretation. Section 10 gives the zero-residue interpretation needed here and states the precise comparison hypothesis for transfer to a cyclotomic Hecke orbit summand. The Dunkl representation is classical [19]. Our Cherednik comparison is a consequence of that representation and the nil-Hecke endomorphism theorem, with a direct proof of the parameter condition. Central supports of t = 0 Verma modules have been studied by Bellamy [20]; we identify the specific content ideals on the polynomial section and do not require a description of the full space. Liu’s comparison [18] concerns a broader residue-dependent setting and is not used in the proofs of the center formulas in this paper. The scope of the classification is precise. Hilbert series are determined in every exponent for A, B, C, D, F 4, G 2. Complete algebra presentations in every exponent are obtained for the coordinate-root Bn, C n families and in rank two. We give the first nonsemisimple centers in the remaining classical families and all F4 centers in the self-injective range. The finite differential ROOT-POWER QUOTIENTS AND WEYL INVARIANTS 11

Remark 9.9 (Comparison with ordinary cyclotomic nil-Hecke algebras) . Or-dinary cyclotomic nil-Hecke algebras are matrix algebras over Grassmannian cohomology rings and admit symmetrizing forms [12, 6]. The quotient here imposes a power of a root coordinate. Even in type A, this is a different condition from imposing a power of an individual strand variable. In par-ticular, in type An with n ≥ 2, all root-power quotients with m ≥ 2n + 2, and the type-G2 quotients for m ≥ 12, cannot be Morita equivalent to a nonzero ordinary cyclotomic nil-Hecke algebra. The same holds for the first nonsemisimple type-Dn quotients, at m = 4 n − 4, 4n − 3, by Theorem 7.7. Self-injectivity is Morita invariant, so the computed socles give the obstruc-tion. For coordinate roots in Bn, C n, the Grassmannian identification follows instead from the explicit content calculation of Theorem 7.2. The root-lattice setting of [9] likewise distinguishes its Hecke algebras from the usual type-A

Brundan–Kleshchev setting.
