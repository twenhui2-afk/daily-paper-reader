# A Finite Certificate for the Positive n = 11 Vasc Inequality 

Tiancheng Xu *, Wensheng Yu *

Abstract 

We establish the Vasc cyclic inequality for all strictly positive real eleven-tuples by a finite exact certificate. Cyclic rotation places a minimum at the first position, and rank words together with cumulative gaps reduce the problem to 10! = 3,628,800 homogeneous integer polynomials of degree eleven. Independent coefficient reconstruction proves 3,358,617 roots directly. The remaining 270,183 roots are covered by 267,952 ordinary midpoint certificates, 2,195 complete binary subdivision certificates, and 36 complete certificates containing partial sorting nodes. Each nontrivial leaf is checked by subtracting weighted AM–GM midpoint circuits from a positive multiple of the transformed polynomial and verifying every residual coefficient. Explicit word-set comparisons certify disjointness and exact coverage. We prove the soundness of the leaves and both subdivision rules, then recover the rational inequality by positive denominator clearing and continuity. The resulting proof combines explicit mathematical reductions with independently checked exact integer certificates. 

Keywords: cyclic inequality; polynomial nonnegativity; finite certificate; difference substitution; exact arithmetic; computer-assisted proof.

Xia and Yang [1] describe algorithms for polynomial inequality proving and discovery, including successive difference substitution (SDS). Yao [2] studies struc-tured difference substitutions based on column stochastic matrices. These methods motivate the cumulative-gap coordinates used below: order constraints become nonnegative parameters, and polynomial coefficients become directly checkable proof data. 3.2 Finite covers of gap cones

# 4 Discussion 

The reduction combines three elementary constructions: cumulative sums encode ordered values, midpoint AM–GM circuits supply nonnegative polynomial summands, and comparison or sort-ing substitutions cover the gap orthant. Exact expansion then reduces the certificate conditions 8Vasc Cyclic Inequality Tiancheng Xu and Wensheng Yu 

to integer coefficient inequalities. The disjoint partition in Proposition 10 connects these local decompositions to all normalized rank words. Binary comparison uses two children to resolve a single ordering relation. Partial sorting resolves all ordering relations in a selected group at once, with s! children. Combining these rules allows the certificate to concentrate the larger sorting steps on 36 roots, while direct coefficients and ordinary midpoint leaves settle more than 99.9% of the root family.
