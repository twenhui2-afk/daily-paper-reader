QUIVER DEMAZURE ALGEBRAS I: RESIDUE DESCENT, FINITE QUOTIENTS, AND PBW BASES 

ZHI-WEI LI 

Abstract. We study quiver Demazure algebras through residue covers and root-power quotients. The cover determined by the zero-root subsys-tem identifies the additive algebra with a finite fixed algebra of Sauter’s generalized quiver Hecke construction. Her basis theorem then gives additive PBW in every finite Weyl type, for arbitrary admissible residue data and uniform quivers. A sequence-cover argument gives global mul-tiplicative PBW in simply-laced type. Under a directed-path condition, root-power quotients are finite dimensional and the formal exponential identifies their additive and multiplicative forms. For arrow-free quiv-ers, we reduce mixed-residue quotients to matrix algebras over finite skew group rings, obtain sharp nonvanishing criteria from coinvariant geometry, and determine their simple modules. In a mixed type-D4 fam-ily we compute an explicit coefficient presentation, dimensions, Loewy lengths, and Cartan matrices. We also specify the direct and finite-cover comparisons with Liu’s algebras. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 20C08, 16G20, 16S32. 

Key words and phrases. quiver Demazure algebra, residue descent, PBW basis, root-power quotient, nil-Hecke algebra, coinvariant algebra, Cartan matrix.

1. Introduction 

Quiver Demazure algebras combine divided differences at zero root residue with weighted Weyl reflections at nonzero residue. A finite Weyl group, a uniform residue quiver, and a coordinate law are specified independently. This flexibility raises two questions: which residue data have an integral reduced-product basis, and when do their root-power quotients admit a usable description of their representations? We answer the basis question in all finite Weyl types for additive co-ordinates, and in simply-laced finite types for uncompleted multiplicative coordinates. The additive result is obtained by identifying a canonical finite cover with a generalized quiver Hecke algebra of Sauter and descending her PBW basis. This places the additive basis theorem within an existing geometric construction. We then use the residue data to develop explicit applications to mixed-residue root-power quotients. For arrow-free quivers we give a matrix description, a sharp nonvanishing criterion, and the simple modules. A type-D4 family has explicit dimensions, radical lengths, and Cartan matrices.

Relation to earlier work. The divided-difference and nil-Hecke results are classical [2, 8, 19]; the sequence PBW input is the quiver Hecke theorem of Khovanov–Lauda and Rouquier [14, 26]. Rostam’s fixed-point PBW and cyclotomic descent results [25] precede the method used here. Our construc-tion specifies the cover attached to an arbitrary root-residue datum and its coefficient ideals. Theorem 5.4 matches that cover explicitly with Sauter’s centralizer, index set, and root weights [27], and uses her PBW theorem. The mixed quotient calculation combines classical nil-Hecke endomorphisms and coinvariant geometry with this residue data. When there is a compatible weight-torus realization, the additive algebra is Liu’s finite-root algebra BΩ [21]. We retain the attribution of its PBW, Frobenius, and affine-corner results. Our mixed D4 orbit has no direct weight-torus realization, but it is a fourfold quotient of a twelve-point weight-torus orbit. Thus even this example is covered by finite descent from Liu’s theory; the direct realization obstruction is not a separation from that theory. The Kong–Li root-coordinate and Brundan–Kleshchev–Rouquier strand comparisons are recovered results [4, 16, 17, 18]. We place the explicit maps and their hypotheses in an appendix. They require the specified residue quiver and spectral parameters. Neither our general PBW theorem nor the same-data exponential comparison implies an unrestricted isomorphism with a fixed affine Hecke target. The zero-residue restriction theorem uses Huang’s 6 ZHI-WEI LI 

regular coinvariant representation [13]. Questions concerning centers, matrix orders, fibers, and root-power invariant theory lie beyond the scope of the present paper.

Remark 2.2 (Uniformity and Dynkin diagrams) . Uniformity is a condition on the arrow multiplicities on the whole residue group. In particular, all vertices have the same incoming and outgoing arrow multiplicities. An oriented finite path with at least three vertices, or a finite Dynkin diagram of type D or E,has vertices of different degrees and cannot be the whole underlying graph of a uniform residue quiver. Finite paths do occur as full subquivers of the integer-line example. The term “uniform” does not describe the Dynkin diagram of the finite Weyl group: any permitted Weyl type can be chosen independently of the examples above, subject to the coefficient and residue hypotheses.
