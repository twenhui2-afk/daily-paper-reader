ON THE K-TYPES OF CERTAIN FAMILIES OF UNITARY HIGHEST WEIGHT REPRESENTATIONS OF HERMITIAN LIE GROUPS 

PAVLE PAND ˇ ZI ´ C AND GENKAI ZHANG ABSTRACT . Let (g, k) be the complexication of a Hermitian symmetric pair and let 

γ1 < · · · < γ r be the Harish-Chandra strongly orthogonal roots, with γ1 the low-est non-compact root, and let ζ be the normalized central character of k. We consider the unitary highest weight representations L(λ(x)) of g of highest weights of the form 

λ(x) = −kγ 1 − xζ , x ∈ R. In particular, we are interested in the case x = a, the last reduction point in the analytic continuation in x. We prove that L(−kγ 1 − aζ ), k ≥ 1, is 

K-multiplicity free and we describe all its K-types. 

1. I NTRODUCTION 

Let (G, K ) be an irreducible Hermitian symmetric pair and let (g, k) be the complexifi-cation of the symmetric pair (g0, k0) of the Lie algebras of G and K. In the present paper we shall find the K-types for a family of unitary highest weight representations of g.To describe our result let γ1 < · · · < γ r be the Harish-Chandra strongly orthogonal roots with γ1 the lowest non-compact root. 3.3. Littlewood-Richardson rule. We shall need the Littlewood-Richardson rule for the decomposition of the tensor product of polynomial representations of gl (n, C); see e.g. [9, Chapter 9]. Denote by F (α) the representation of gl (n, C) with highest weight α =(α1, · · · , α n), α1 ≥ · · · ≥ αn ≥ 0, corresponding to a Young diagram with α1 boxes in the first row, α2 boxes in the second row, etc. The multiplicity m(F (γ), F (α) ⊗ F (β)) ,with α ⪯ γ, i.e., αj ≤ γj ∀j, is given by the Littlewood-Richardson coefficient LR γα,β 

defined as the number of semistandard skew tableaux of shape γ − α whose weights are given by the fixed β = ( β1, β 2, · · · ). More precisely, the skew tableau γ − α is obtained from the Young diagram of γ with the diagram of α being removed, and a semistandard skew tableau of weight β = ( β1, β 2, · · · ) is a filling of β1 boxes of 1’s β2 boxes of 2’s etc, inserted in the tableau γ − α, such that 

• each row is weakly increasing from left to the right; 

• each column is strictly increasing from top to the bottom; 

• the word obtained from top to bottom, right to left, is a Yamanouchi word, namely at each fixed position j in the word, the number of any i appearing before the position j is not smaller than the number of i + 1 appearing before the position j.
