BRANCHING GAPS, COEXISTENCE, AND BOUNDARY INFLUENCE FOR THE ISING MODEL ON SPHERICALLY SYMMETRIC TREES 

FARRUKH MUKHAMEDOV AND OTABEK KHAKIMOV 

Abstract. In this paper, we study the ferromagnetic nearest-neighbor Ising model on rooted spherically symmetric trees with one or q children per vertex, according to a prescribed sequence of branching levels. Unbounded runs of one-child levels force uniqueness at every nonzero homo-geneous field and every finite inverse temperature. We quantify this effect through the locations of sufficiently long nonbranching stretches. Conversely, uniqueness need not entail any uniform rate: for each prescribed vanishing sequence, we construct a density-one tree whose finite-volume boundary influence decays more slowly along a subsequence. The tree retains branching number q,although its nonzero-field coexistence region disappears. For eventually bounded gaps, we prove an optimal uniform comparison with the periodic tree having the largest allowed gap and obtain an explicit interval of coexistence fields. Thus bounded gaps are equivalent, within this family, to coex-istence at some finite temperature and nonzero field. For eventually periodic gaps, Gibbs uniqueness is equivalent to fixed-point uniqueness of the full-period return map. A negative Schwarzian de-rivative yields a complete classification: in the supercritical regime the map has three fixed points inside a closed coexistence interval, two at its endpoints, and one outside. The nonzero endpoints are automatically nondegenerate folds, with square-root splitting of the merging branches. 

1. Introduction 

In the ferromagnetic Ising model, branching growth is closely linked to the zero-field transition. A homogeneous nonzero field raises the question of whether border influence may persist without branching, even while the exponential growth rate remains constant. We answer this question for leafless, spherically symmetric trees with offspring numbers in {1, q }, where q ≥ 2. In this study, uniqueness refers to the uniqueness of all Dobrushin-Lanford-Ruelle (DLR) Gibbs measures. Let α = {n1 < n 2 < · · · } ⊂ Z≥0 be the branching levels, let ℓj = nj+1 − nj − 1 count the intervening one-child levels, and put θ = tanh( βJ ) and H = βB . The results develop in three directions. (i) Geometric uniqueness and a matching coexistence comparison. Theorem 3.1 proves unique-ness at every H̸ = 0 when sup j ℓj = ∞. For H > 0, a long gap first makes the limiting minus field positive; a logarithmic contraction identifies the extremal Gibbs measures. For eventually L-bounded gaps, Theorem 6.1 proves coexistence wherever the constant-gap-L

tree has coexistence. This is the optimal guarantee uniform over that class. Corollary 6.2 gives an explicit positive field interval when qθ L+1 > 1, and Corollary 6.3 completes the geometric dichotomy for existence of nonzero-field coexistence at some temperature. (ii) Quantitative boundary influence and its limitations. Theorem 4.1 bounds finite-volume boundary influence using the locations of sufficiently long gaps. Theorem 3.3 constructs two trees of branching density one and branching number q with different nonzero-field coexistence sets. Theorem 4.2 strengthens this comparison: for each prescribed vanishing rate and fixed parameters in the regular tree’s nonzero-field coexistence region, a density-one tree can have uniqueness and slower boundary-influence decay along a subsequence.

Periodic compositions and distinctions between uniqueness and correlation decay also occur for general ferromagnetic two-spin systems. Guo and Lu [6, Section 5] analyze an alternating-offspring tree by composing its level recursion maps. Here the applied Ising field is homogeneous, and the periodic classification is complemented by a density-one construction with uniqueness and an arbitrarily prescribed slow rate along a subsequence. Nonzero-field coexistence is controlled by the gap sequence, which cannot be detected by branch-ing density or number. Unbounded gaps provide uniqueness in nonzero homogenous fields. Bounded gaps allow for coexistence at limited temperatures and nonzero fields. The periodic tree with the biggest allowable gap provides the ideal coexistence interval, which is guarantyd uniformly through-out all ultimately bounded gap sequences. The full-period map provides both an accurate Gibbs uniqueness criteria and a comprehensive fixed-point categorization for gaps that inevitably occur. The nonzero-field coexistence endpoints are nondegenerate folds due to the negative Schwarzian derivative, which implies only three fixed points. The square-root law illustrates how two branches of boundary law merge. Finite-volume estimations indicate how lengthy nonbranching sections reduce border influence. For given nonzero-field parameters in the regular tree’s coexistence area, the density-one construc-tion eliminates uniform rates across all density-one trees with unbounded gaps. The precise coex-istence interval for a limited aperiodic gap sequence, including endpoint behavior, is still unknown beyond the uniform comparison and adequate conditions shown here.
