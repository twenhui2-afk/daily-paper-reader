# SFlexRCA: Lightweight, Scalable, and Flexible Root Cause Analysis for IIoT Edge Systems 

Amr M. Zaki, Farhoud Jafari, Honggeun Ji, Komal Sarda, Marin Litoiu 

Abstract —Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) systems gen-erate high-dimensional sensor telemetry from interconnected components, where faults can propagate across the system. Existing root cause analysis (RCA) approaches often depend on incomplete dependency or observability information, while non-causal methods may require abundant normal-operation data and causal approaches can incur substantial computational and pa-rameter costs. To address these challenges, we propose SFlexRCA (Scalable and Flexible Root Cause Analysis), a topology-free RCA framework designed for resource-constrained IIoT environments. SFlexRCA transforms multivariate telemetry into compact or-thogonal representations and applies shared lightweight linear modeling, avoiding explicit graph construction, message passing, and per-variable or lag-specific parameter growth. We evaluate SFlexRCA on three publicly available IIoT datasets, BATADAL, SWaT, and WADI, spanning different numbers of monitored variables, temporal characteristics, and training-data regimes. SFlexRCA is compared with 10 statistical, causal, and non-causal baselines in terms of RCA accuracy, training efficiency, inference latency, and memory consumption. In addition, inference effi-ciency and memory consumption are evaluated on Raspberry Pi 3 and Raspberry Pi 5, while energy consumption is additionally measured on Raspberry Pi 5. We further investigate temporal-context sensitivity, architectural and loss components, and al-ternative representations. SFlexRCA achieves the best AC@1 on BATADAL (0.476) and the best AC@5 on SWaT (0.367), while using only 279.4K and 108.1K parameters, respectively. Notably, SFlexRCA maintains strong localization performance on BATADAL despite its limited normal-operation training data, while its compact shared architecture avoids the parameter growth associated with causal and graph-based approaches. On Raspberry Pi 5, SFlexRCA achieves 20.3, 8.2, and 9.3 ms/sample on BATADAL, SWaT, and WADI, respectively. These results demonstrate that SFlexRCA provides a practical alternative to computationally intensive causal and graph-based RCA ap-proaches, achieving strong localization with substantially smaller models while remaining effective under limited training data. Its lightweight shared architecture further enables efficient deploy-ment on resource-constrained IIoT edge devices. The SFlexRCA code is available at https://github.com/theamrzaki/RootCause-Analysis-Correlation-Attentive-Modeling. 

Index Terms —Industrial Internet of Things, Root Cause Anal-ysis, Multivariate Time Series, Linear Models, Edge Computing, Resource-Constrained Devices.

Anomaly detection can identify deviations from normal behavior [6], but does not determine which component or process variable initiated the failure [7]. Root cause analy-sis (RCA) addresses this limitation by identifying the most likely sources of abnormal behavior and supporting corrective actions [8]. This task is particularly challenging in industrial environments because sensors, actuators, control loops, and physical subsystems are strongly coupled. Existing RCA approaches face several practical limitations in IIoT settings. Recent IoT and industrial approaches have increasingly explored causal structure discovery, causal interpretability, and dynamic causal graphs to capture dependencies among system variables [14], [15], [16]. Other approaches com-bine reinforcement learning and graph-based causal diagno-sis for root cause localization [17]. Some methods further incorporate large language models (LLMs) to assist causal reasoning and root cause localization [16]. While these ap-proaches can exploit rich dependency information, explicit causal structures, graph construction, reinforcement-learning-based discovery, and LLM-based components can introduce additional computational and memory requirements, making deployment challenging on resource-constrained edge devices. Moreover, graph message passing [8], independent models for individual variables [18], and lag- or context-dependent archi-tectures [19], [20] can further increase parameter and compu-tational costs. These constraints are particularly important for distributed IoT systems, where efficient computation, energy use, and resource allocation remain central challenges for edge intelligence [21], [22], [23]. At the same time, non-causal forecasting approaches can provide compact representations but may require substantial normal-operation data for reliable localization, which can be difficult to obtain in industrial settings. We address this gap with SFlexRCA (Scalable and Flex-ible Root Cause Analysis) , a lightweight and topology-free framework for industrial root cause localization. SFlexRCA represents multivariate telemetry in a compact orthogonal space and uses shared linear modeling to capture temporal and cross-variable patterns. Industrial measurements often contain redundancy, correlation, and shared temporal structure, making compact representations a promising alternative to explicitly modeling every variable and dependency [24], [25], [26], [27]. By operating in a shared representation space, SFlexRCA avoids explicit dependency graphs, message passing, and independent per-variable models, thereby limiting parameter growth as telemetry dimensionality and temporal context in-crease. Its modular architecture also allows the model to adapt to different industrial systems. We evaluate SFlexRCA on SWaT, WADI, and BATADAL [28], [29], [30], covering different telemetry dimensionalities and training-data regimes. We assess RCA accuracy, parameter complexity, training and inference efficiency, and memory consumption on both Raspberry Pi 3 and Raspberry Pi 5 , while evaluating energy consumption on Raspberry Pi 5. SFlexRCA achieves the best AC@1 on BATADAL and the best AC@5 on SWaT while using substantially fewer trainable parameters than competing approaches, demonstrating its practical scalability without the parameter growth associated with dependency-aware causal and graph-based models. It also remains effective under limited normal-operation training data, as observed on BATADAL. We further examine temporal-context sensitivity, architectural and loss components, alternative representation methods, and the learned representations underlying localization. RCA in multivariate systems lies at the intersection of performance engineering and system diagnosis, where the goal extends beyond anomaly detection toward scalable and operationally practical fault localization. 

A. Root Cause Analysis 

Existing RCA approaches can be broadly categorized into statistical, multimodal, non-causal forecasting, and neural dependency-based methods, each making different assump-tions about the available observability, training data, and system structure. Statistical RCA methods primarily identify abnormal de-pendencies or distributional shifts among monitoring signals. 

ϵ-Diagnosis [31] uses two-sample statistical testing, while RCD [32] employs conditional independence tests to infer causal relationships among system metrics. BARO [33] further improves robustness through median- and IQR-based scoring. These methods are computationally efficient and interpretable, but are primarily designed for microservice and software-system telemetry and can struggle to capture the complex tem-poral dependencies and nonlinear interactions characteristic of industrial processes. A second line of work exploits multimodal observability by jointly analyzing metrics, logs, and traces. Approaches such as ART, Eadro, MSTGAD, and LLM4MST [9], [10], [11], [12], [13] leverage complementary information across modalities to improve fault localization. However, these ap-proaches assume the availability of sufficiently complete and aligned multimodal telemetry, which may be impractical in industrial environments dominated by sensor and control mea-surements. Industrial systems may lack distributed traces or consistent logs across heterogeneous controllers and devices, making single-modality approaches more suitable for partially observable and resource-constrained deployments. Non-causal forecasting approaches provide an alternative by learning temporal patterns directly from multivariate observa-tions without explicitly modeling causal or dependency struc-tures. Recent models such as TimeMixer++ [36] and iTrans-former [35] can capture complex temporal and cross-variable 3

This limitation is particularly relevant to IIoT environments, where collecting representative normal-operation data can be difficult due to operational costs, system changes, or limited periods of stable operation. Moreover, forecasting accuracy does not necessarily translate directly into reliable root cause localization, since accurate prediction of system behavior does not explicitly identify the variable responsible for an observed failure. Thus, while non-causal models can provide attractive computational efficiency, their data requirements and indirect localization mechanism can limit their applicability to data-limited diagnostic scenarios and resource-constrained deployments. Neural dependency-based methods instead attempt to learn temporal or causal relationships directly from multivariate observations. Methods such as cMLP and cLSTM [18] use neural networks associated with individual variables to re-cover dependency structures. CUTS [34] addresses irregular and incomplete time-series data through a neural Granger-causality framework that jointly imputes missing observations and learns causal graphs using graph neural networks. Recent work has further investigated causal structure discovery for industrial processes, including nonlinear systems with complex noise [14], while IoT-oriented RCA approaches have employed dynamic causal graphs to model evolving dependencies and have incorporated LLMs to assist causal reasoning and root cause localization [16]. GVAR [19] and AERCA [20] use deep temporal architectures that process historical monitoring win-dows, requiring deep models to be repeatedly applied across temporal windows during training and inference. CUTS+ [8] further employs message passing over learned representations to model interactions among variables. These approaches provide more direct dependency-aware mechanisms for lo-calization and can remain effective when training data are limited. However, learning explicit causal structures, using per-variable neural networks, repeatedly processing temporal win-dows, or performing graph-based message passing can intro-duce substantial computational and memory overhead. LLM-assisted causal approaches further increase these resource requirements, making deployment challenging on resource-constrained IIoT edge devices, particularly as the number of monitored variables and temporal context increase. Overall, existing RCA approaches exhibit a fundamental trade-off between data requirements, dependency modeling, and computational cost. Non-causal forecasting methods can provide efficient temporal modeling but may require sub-stantial normal-operation data and do not directly model root-cause dependencies. Causal and neural dependency-based methods provide more explicit dependency-aware localization but often rely on computationally intensive per-variable, tem-poral, graph-based, or LLM-assisted architectures. In contrast, SFlexRCA targets topology-free RCA from a single modal-ity of industrial telemetry by combining orthogonal repre-sentations with shared lightweight linear components. This design captures temporal and cross-variable information with-out explicitly constructing dependency graphs, using message passing or LLMs, or relying on per-variable and lag-specific architectures, enabling efficient root cause localization across different training-data regimes and supporting deployment on resource-constrained IIoT edge devices. 

B. Orthogonal and Linear Temporal Modeling 

Recent advances in high-dimensional time-series modeling suggest that structured transformations can simplify tempo-ral learning while reducing computational cost. Frequency-domain methods such as FITS [26], FEDformer [39], Fil-terNet [37], and FourierGNN [40] demonstrate the effec-tiveness of alternative representations for modeling long-range temporal dependencies. More recently, OLinear [38] and VLinear [25] show that dataset-specific linear projec-tions can achieve competitive performance with substantially lower computational cost, suggesting that complex temporal structure can be captured in transformed spaces without deep nonlinear architectures. Recent efforts have also investigated efficient anomaly detection directly on resource-constrained 4

devices. For example, [2], [3] evaluate lightweight time-series anomaly detection models on edge platforms, including Raspberry Pi, STM32, and NVIDIA Jetson Xavier. While these studies demonstrate the feasibility of deploying anomaly detection at the edge, they focus on identifying anomalous behavior rather than determining its underlying root cause. In contrast, SFlexRCA applies lightweight orthogonal pro-jections and shared linear components to root cause localiza-tion. By avoiding explicit dependency graphs, message pass-ing, and per-variable or per-window architectures, SFlexRCA enables topology-free RCA from single-modality industrial telemetry while targeting efficient deployment on resource-constrained IIoT edge devices. A. Problem Formulation 

Let X ∈ RT ×P denote a multivariate IIoT telemetry window containing P sensor variables observed over T time steps. Given normal operating data, SFlexRCA learns the temporal and cross-variable behavior of the system and uses deviations from this learned behavior to localize potential root causes. Given an input window X, SFlexRCA produces a future-state prediction ˆY and an implicit variable-interaction repre-sentation C:

ˆY , C = SFlexRCA( X), (1) where ˆY represents the predicted future system state and 

C ∈ RT ×P ×P captures pairwise interactions inferred from the learned latent representations. Unlike topology-aware RCA ap-proaches, SFlexRCA does not require a predefined dependency graph, process topology, or explicit communication structure among monitored variables. 

B. SFlexRCA Architecture 

SFlexRCA is designed for single-modality IIoT telemetry, where large numbers of heterogeneous sensor measurements must be modeled efficiently under the computational con-straints of edge devices. As illustrated in Fig. 1, SFlexRCA consists of five main stages: (i) orthogonal representation, (ii) shared variable encoding, (iii) temporal context modeling, (iv) context refinement and cross-variable interaction modeling, and (v) temporal aggregation and prediction. The architecture uses lightweight shared components rather than per-variable neural networks or graph-based message-passing architectures. 

(1) Orthogonal Representation. 

IIoT monitoring data typically contain highly correlated measurements and substantial temporal redundancy across sensor windows. Directly learning from the raw temporal coordinates can therefore require the downstream RCA model to repeatedly capture dependencies that are already present in the normal operating data. To provide a compact temporal rep-resentation, SFlexRCA constructs a dataset-specific orthogonal basis from normal IIoT telemetry following [38]. For each monitored variable p, we compute its temporal cor-relation matrix over the normal training windows and obtain a shared correlation structure by averaging across variables:

D. Complexity Analysis 

The computational complexity of SFlexRCA can be sum-marized according to its five architectural stages. Steps (1) and (2) perform the orthogonal representation and shared variable encoding, with the latter mapping each variable into the shared hidden space, giving a dominant complexity of O(P H ). Steps (3) and (4) perform temporal context modeling and context refinement using shared latent representations, resulting in a complexity of O(H2) without introducing separate neural networks for individual variables. Step (5) performs temporal aggregation and prediction in the hidden space, contributing 

O(HK ). Consequently, the overall complexity is summarized as 

O(P H + H2 + HK ). (14) This formulation highlights that SFlexRCA performs its main neural computation in a shared hidden space rather than replicating the computation across monitored variables. In contrast, cMLP and cLSTM introduce complexity that grows with the number of variables, GVAR and AERCA introduce multiplicative dependence on the temporal order through ex-plicit coefficient modeling, and CUTS+ incurs additional com-plexity from graph-based message passing and its associated layers. SFlexRCA therefore shifts the dominant computation toward the compact hidden dimension H, while requiring only a shared linear dependence on the number of monitored variables. IV. E XPERIMENTS 

We evaluate SFlexRCA on three IIoT datasets to assess its effectiveness for RCA under the computational constraints of edge deployment. We compare SFlexRCA with statistical and causal deep learning baselines and investigate: (1) RCA accuracy across multiple datasets, (2) training and inference efficiency, particularly on resource-constrained Raspberry Pi devices, (3) the contribution of architectural and loss compo-nents, and (4) sensitivity to the temporal window size. 

A. Datasets 

We evaluate SFlexRCA on three industrial control sys-tem datasets with different numbers of monitored variables and temporal characteristics, as summarized in Table III. BATADAL [30] is a water distribution benchmark containing 43 monitored variables and captures temporal dynamics in a water distribution system. SWaT [28] is a water treat-ment benchmark with 51 monitored variables and longer temporal dependencies arising from the interactions among multiple physical processes. WADI [29] is a water distribution benchmark containing 127 monitored variables and provides a higher-dimensional setting for evaluating the scalability of RCA methods. Together, these datasets provide progressively different dimensional and temporal settings for evaluating root cause localization.

Non-causal baselines include TimeMixer++ [36] and iTrans-former [35], which model multivariate temporal dependencies without explicitly learning causal relationships. For causal deep learning, we consider per-variable architectures (cLSTM and cMLP [18]), lag-specific architectures (GVAR [19] and AERCA [20]), and shared message-passing architectures (CUTS+ [8]). To assess the contribution of the proposed representation, we compare orthogonal projection with seven alternatives: no projection, learned projection, and static Leg-endre, Laguerre, Chebyshev, Hermite, and Fourier projections, following the experimental methodology of [44], [38]. All methods operate on the same metric-only telemetry inputs, and RCA scores are ranked using the same evaluation procedure. In particular, no additional anomaly thresholding or EVT-based post-processing is applied to SFlexRCA, ensuring a consistent comparison with the statistical and causal baselines. 

Evaluation Metrics. 

We evaluate the methods from two complementary per-spectives: RCA accuracy and computational efficiency. For RCA accuracy, we report Recall at Top-k (AC@k) for k ∈

1, 5, 10 and AUC@10, following established RCA evaluation protocols [32], [42]. AC@ k measures whether the ground-truth root-cause variables are included among the top-k vari-ables ranked by each method, while AUC@10 evaluates the ranking quality across the top-10 predictions. For training efficiency, we report the number of trainable parameters, peak memory consumption, and average training time per epoch. For inference efficiency, we report average inference latency per sample on resource-constrained edge devices. Specifically, experiments are conducted on a Raspberry Pi 5 equipped with an ARM Cortex-A76 processor and 16 GB RAM, and a Raspberry Pi 3 equipped with an ARM Cortex-A53 processor and 1 GB RAM. For edge energy evaluation, inference was performed on the Raspberry Pi 5 while monitoring PMIC power using the vcgencmd pmic_read_adc interface. Total energy was obtained by trapezoidal integration of the measured power trace over the inference workload, and energy per sample was calculated by dividing the measured energy by 7

the total number of evaluated samples. 

Implementation Details. 

All experiments are conducted using multiple random seeds on a Linux workstation equipped with an Intel(R) Core(TM) i9-10900K CPU @ 3.70GHz (20 cores), 32 GB RAM, and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3070 GPU with 8 GB memory, running Ubuntu 22.04.2 LTS. The implementation uses Python 3.10.12, PyTorch 2.7.1+cu126 with CUDA 12.6, and PyTorch Geometric 2.6.1. Hyperparameters are tuned independently for each dataset, and the best configuration is selected. The latent dimension D is set to 128 for SWaT and WADI and 256 for BATADAL. The number of training epochs is set to 200 for SWaT and BATADAL and 50 for WADI, with early stopping applied. The learning rate is set to 1 × 10 −4 across all experiments. For SFlexRCA, the orthogonal transformation is implemented using a dataset-specific linear projection.

RCA Accuracy Across Statistical and Learned Methods. 

Table IV compares SFlexRCA with statistical, non-causal forecasting, and causal RCA methods across BATADAL, SWAT, and WADI. Statistical methods require no trainable pa-rameters but provide inconsistent localization accuracy across datasets, reflecting their limited ability to capture complex temporal and cross-variable dependencies. 

Effect of Training Data and Causal Modeling Non-causal forecasting models, including TimeMixer++ and iTransformer, perform substantially better on SWAT and WADI than on BATADAL. This difference is particularly pronounced for BATADAL, where only approximately 8K normal training samples are available: iTransformer achieves an AC@1 of only 0.048, while TimeMixer++ achieves 0.095. With sub-stantially larger training sets of approximately 400K and 1.2M samples on SWAT and WADI, respectively, both models become considerably more competitive, with iTransformer reaching AC@1 values of 0.167 and 0.378. These results suggest that forecasting-based approaches benefit substantially from abundant normal-operation data, but their localization performance can deteriorate under data-limited conditions. For IIoT environments where collecting large volumes of representative normal-operation data may be impractical, this dependence can limit their applicability. In contrast, causal methods explicitly model temporal and cross-variable depen-dencies associated with system behavior, providing a mecha-nism more directly aligned with root-cause localization than forecasting alone. This provides an advantage when training data is limited. For example, cLSTM achieves an AC@1 of 0.381 on BATADAL, substantially exceeding the 0.048 and 0.095 obtained by iTransformer and TimeMixer++, respec-tively, despite all methods being trained using the same limited dataset. However, this dependency modeling generally comes at a substantial architectural cost, with parameter requirements increasing considerably as the number of monitored variables and temporal dependencies grow. On WADI, for example, cMLP and cLSTM require 14.5M and 16.7M parameters, respectively, while GVAR and AERCA require 14.8M and 44.4M parameters. 

RCA Accuracy with Compact Architecture SFlexRCA achieves competitive or superior RCA accuracy while main-taining a substantially smaller architecture than most causal deep learning baselines. On BATADAL, SFlexRCA achieves the highest AC@1 of 0.476 with only 279.4K parameters, compared with 13.3M for cLSTM and 7.1M for cMLP. The advantage is also seen on WADI, where SFlexRCA achieves AC@1, AC@5, and AC@10 of 0.333, 0.289, and 0.378, respectively, while requiring only 35.3K parameters. Notably, this lower parameter count on WADI does not result from hav-ing fewer monitored variables: WADI contains substantially more variables than both BATADAL and SWAT. Rather, as discussed in RQ3, the WADI configuration does not require the linear-attention component used in the other configurations, further reducing the model size. Thus, the increase in the num-ber of monitored variables does not necessarily translate into increased model complexity in SFlexRCA. This scalability advantage stems from SFlexRCA’s shared variable encoding, which avoids the per-variable neural architectures used by cMLP and cLSTM and therefore prevents the substantial pa-rameter growth observed as the number of monitored variables increases. Similarly, GVAR and AERCA incur substantially larger models due to their dependency- and lag-specific archi-tectures. On SWAT, SFlexRCA remains competitive, achieving an AC@5 of 0.367, higher than the strongest baseline value of 0.350, while using only 108.1K parameters. Compared with the recent CUTS+ framework, SFlexRCA further achieves higher AC@1, AC@5, and AC@10 on SWAT, with values of 0.233, 0.367, and 0.417 compared with 0.217, 0.300, and 0.342, respectively. Although CUTS+ uses fewer parameters than SFlexRCA on BATADAL and SWAT, its message-passing architecture introduces additional computational overhead that is not reflected by parameter count alone, as examined in RQ2. 

Summary. Overall, these results highlight the central trade-off addressed by SFlexRCA: non-causal forecasting methods can become competitive when large volumes of normal-operation data are available, while causal methods can pro-vide stronger dependency-aware localization but typically re-quire substantially larger architectures. SFlexRCA combines dependency-aware modeling with a compact shared repre-sentation, achieving competitive or superior RCA accuracy with substantially fewer parameters, without requiring the per-variable, lag-specific, or explicit message-passing mechanisms of competing approaches, and while remaining effective in data-limited settings. The resulting training and inference efficiency are examined in RQ2.

E. RQ-3: Ablation Study 

We evaluate the contribution of the major loss and archi-tectural components of SFlexRCA through ablation studies on BATADAL, SWaT, and WADI. To isolate the effect of the loss formulation, all loss ablations use a common reference architecture with the linear prediction head and linear attention enabled. Starting from this fixed architecture, we remove one loss component at a time while keeping all other architec-tural and training settings unchanged. Architectural ablations are conducted separately to examine the contribution of the orthogonal representation, temporal mixer, linear attention, and prediction head and to determine the dataset-specific configurations reported in Table IV. Thus, the parameter counts in the loss-ablation results correspond to the fixed reference architecture rather than necessarily to the final selected con-figuration for each dataset. 

Contribution of Loss Components. The loss ablations show that the sparse and smoothness losses are particularly important for the more complex SWaT and WADI datasets. Removing the sparse loss reduces AC@1 from 0.233 to 0.217 on SWaT and from 0.333 to 0.133 on WADI, indicating that sparsity helps isolate the variables most strongly associated with failures. Similarly, removing the smoothness loss reduces AC@1 to 0.167 on SWaT and 0.133 on WADI, suggesting that temporally stable interaction estimates become more important as temporal dependencies become more pronounced. The KL loss has its strongest effect on WADI, where removing it reduces AC@10 from 0.378 to 0.293. Its effect on BATADAL is limited: removing the KL term preserves AC@1 at 0.476 and produces comparable overall performance, consistent with the relatively simpler dependency structure of BATADAL, therefore in our experiments we maintain the KL loss for WADI and SWaT but remove it for BATADAL. Overall, the results indicate that the loss components are complementary, with their importance depending on the temporal and structural complexity of the dataset. 

Contribution of Architectural Components. The archi-tectural ablations highlight the complementary roles of or-thogonal representations, temporal mixing, linear attention, and the prediction head. Removing the orthogonal coefficients consistently degrades performance across the three datasets, particularly on BATADAL and WADI, supporting their im-portance for constructing informative variable interactions. The temporal mixer primarily benefits top-ranked localiza-tion: removing it reduces AC@1 from 0.233 to 0.133 on SWAT and from 0.333 to 0.133 on WADI, indicating that temporal mixing provides useful information for distinguish-ing variables associated with failures. The contribution of linear attention is dataset-dependent. While BATADAL and SWAT retain linear attention in their selected configurations, WADI achieves its best overall RCA performance without it, demonstrating that SFlexRCA can achieve effective localiza-tion using its core orthogonal representation and lightweight prediction components alone. This architectural flexibility also supports scalability across systems with different monitoring dimensionalities: although WADI has the largest number of monitored sensors ( P = 127 ), its selected configuration re-quires only 35.3K parameters. Rather than imposing increasing architectural complexity as the number of sensors grows, SFlexRCA can adapt its components to the characteristics of each system, highlighting its flexibility and scalability across heterogeneous IIoT environments. The prediction head also exhibits dataset-dependent behavior. The linear prediction head is used as the default configuration because it provides a more compact model, while the MLP prediction head is considered as an alternative in the ablation study. Accordingly, Pred (Linear) denotes the default linear prediction head with linear attention retained, whereas Pred (MLP) replaces the prediction head with an MLP while keeping linear attention enabled. The w/o L-Attn configuration, in contrast, removes the linear attention module while retaining the default linear prediction head. Based on the results, the linear prediction head is used for BATADAL and WADI, whereas the MLP prediction head provides the selected configuration for SWAT. Overall, these results highlight the flexibility of SFlexRCA to adapt its architecture to dataset-specific characteristics while balancing RCA accuracy and model complexity. 

Summary. Overall, the ablation results demonstrate that SFlexRCA provides both **architectural flexibility and scala-bility**. Its components can be selectively enabled or sim-plified according to dataset characteristics, allowing RCA accuracy to be balanced against model complexity. This adapt-ability enables SFlexRCA to remain compact across systems with different monitoring dimensionalities, making it well suited to resource-constrained edge environments.

G. RQ-5: Effect of Dataset-Specific Orthogonal Projections 

We evaluate the effect of the proposed dataset-specific orthogonal projection by replacing it with alternative repre-sentations while keeping the remaining SFlexRCA architecture unchanged. Specifically, we compare the proposed projection against no projection (i.e., none), a learned projection, and sev-eral fixed basis transformations, including Legendre, Laguerre, Chebyshev, Hermite, and Fourier representations following the experimental methodology of [44], [38]. 

Comparison of Projection Methods: Table VI shows that the dataset-specific orthogonal projection provides the strongest overall RCA performance. On BATADAL, the pro-posed projection achieves the highest AC@1, AC@10, and AUC@10, reaching 0.476 , 0.213 , and 0.234 , respectively. On SWaT, it ties for the highest AC@1 and AC@5. The gains are more modest on WADI, where the different projections produce similar results, but the proposed projection remains competitive across all metrics. The observed differences are consistent with how the projection is constructed. Unlike fixed bases, the proposed transformation is derived from the tem-poral covariance structure of normal operating data for each dataset. Consequently, the resulting orthogonal coordinates are adapted to the characteristic dynamics of the monitored IIoT system rather than imposing a universal temporal basis. This distinction is particularly relevant because the datasets differ substantially in sampling frequency and temporal characteris-tics (Table III). V. T HREATS TO VALIDITY 

Internal Validity: Potential threats arise from implemen-tation choices, hyperparameter tuning, and differences in the optimization requirements of competing RCA methods. We mitigate these effects through a consistent evaluation pipeline, multiple random seeds, and ablation and sensitivity analyses. 

External Validity: We evaluate SFlexRCA on BATADAL, SWAT, and WADI, which cover different telemetry dimen-sionalities, temporal characteristics, and training-data regimes. However, these benchmarks may not capture the full diversity of real IIoT deployments, including noisier telemetry, incom-plete fault labels, and evolving system behavior. Results on Raspberry Pi hardware may also not generalize to other edge platforms or deployment configurations. 

Construct Validity: RCA effectiveness is measured pri-marily using ranking-based metrics (AC@1, AC@5, AC@10, and AUC@10), which do not directly capture diagnostic interpretability or remediation effectiveness. Training time, memory, latency, and energy consumption provide practical proxies for edge efficiency but do not capture all deployment costs, such as communication, storage, and orchestration over-head. Comparisons between causal and non-causal approaches may also depend on dataset characteristics and training-data availability. VI. C ONCLUSION 

We presented SFlexRCA, a lightweight, scalable, and flex-ible root cause analysis framework for IIoT telemetry that avoids explicit dependency graph construction and message passing. Across BATADAL, SWAT, and WADI, SFlexRCA achieved competitive or superior RCA accuracy while main-taining a compact architecture. Its parameter requirements decreased on WADI despite having the largest number of mon-itored variables, demonstrating that SFlexRCA can adapt to different telemetry dimensionalities without parameter growth proportional to the number of variables. The efficiency eval-uation further demonstrated low training memory and com-putational overhead and efficient inference on Raspberry Pi 3 and Raspberry Pi 5. The results highlight an important trade-off in IIoT RCA. Non-causal forecasting-based ap-proaches can provide efficient modeling but may require substantial normal-operation data, limiting their effectiveness under data-constrained regimes. Causal and dependency-aware approaches can capture relationships underlying fault propa-gation but may introduce substantial parameter and compu-tational overhead through explicit dependency modeling, per-variable networks, or message passing. SFlexRCA addresses this trade-off by using orthogonal representations and shared lightweight linear modeling to capture cross-variable and tem-poral structure without explicitly constructing a dependency graph. This enables effective RCA under limited training data while avoiding parameter growth with increasing telemetry dimensionality. The ablation and sensitivity analyses further demonstrate that the architecture, loss formulation, and tempo-ral context can be adapted to different dataset characteristics. Representation comparisons and t-SNE visualization provide additional evidence that the learned orthogonal representations capture informative and separable structure for root cause localization. Overall, SFlexRCA provides a practical bal-ance among RCA accuracy, training-data requirements, model complexity, and edge computational cost, enabling topology-free and resource-efficient root cause localization for IIoT systems.
