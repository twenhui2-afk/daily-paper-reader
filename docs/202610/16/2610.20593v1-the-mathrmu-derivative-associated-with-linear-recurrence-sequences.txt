# The U-Derivative Associated with Linear Recurrence Sequences

Abstract 

We associate a canonical operator to every linear recurrence sequence admitting a distinct-root Binet representation U n “ řkj“1 Aj λnj . The construction is based on the spectral Binet operator BU “ řkj“1 Aj Tλj , where Tλj f pxq “ f pλj xq is the dilation operator associated with the characteristic root λj . The corresponding recurrence derivative is defined by D U “ x´1BU. Its fundamental property is the monomial identity D Upxnq “ Unxn´1, which establishes a direct correspondence between recurrence sequences and degree-lowering operators. We develop the operator theory of D U, including iteration, a spectral product rule, the associated Leibniz defect, and a quotient rule. The construction reveals a natural bifurcation determined by the value U 0, producing the Ward and non-Ward regimes as intrinsic operatorial phenomena. The product structure is shown to be governed by k ´ 2 residual spectral channels, leading to notions of spectral cancellation and spectral balance. As an application, we obtain a complete qualitative classification of second-order recurrence calculi. Using Horadam sequences as a universal second-order model, we prove that every such calculus is determined by the invariant triple pW0, ∆, Z Wq,

where W 0 identifies the Ward/non-Ward type, ∆ determines the spectral geometry, and ZW determines regularity or singularity. 

Keywords. Linear recurrence sequences; Binet representation; Binet operator; recurrence derivative; spectral product rule; Leibniz defect; Ward calculus; operator classification.

# 1 Introduction 

Generalized calculi associated with numerical sequences have played an important role in finite operator theory, q-calculus, and related areas of discrete analysis. Classical examples include Jackson’s q-derivative [1, 2, 3], Ward’s calculus of sequences [6], and more recent calculi based on Fibonacci and Lucas sequences [9, 23, 24]. A common feature of these theories is that ordinary integers are replaced by an alternative sequence governing the action of a derivative-like operator. Throughout the paper we restrict attention to recurrence sequences with distinct char-acteristic roots. Singular and confluent regimes are considered only insofar as they are necessary for the completeness of the second-order classification and will be developed systematically in subsequent work.

# 10 Future Directions 

The results obtained in this paper suggest that recurrence-induced calculus should be viewed not merely as a collection of isolated examples, but as the beginning of a broader operator-theoretic classification program. The construction

associates an operator calculus to spectral recurrence data and reveals several independent structures that remain only partially understood. A first direction concerns the classification of recurrence operators. For second-order calculi, Theorem 22 shows that the qualitative regime is determined by the invariants

Whether analogous finite systems of invariants exist for higher-order recurrences remains an open problem. A second direction concerns operator equivalence. Given two recurrence sequences
