# The best Ulam constant of the n-th order linear differential operator with constant coefficients 

## Jawad Boutarfass, Iz-iddine EL-Fassi ⋆ and Lahcen Oukhtite Department of Mathematics, Faculty of Sciences and Techniques, S. M. Ben Abdellah University, Fez, Morocco 

Abstract 

Let X be a Banach space over the field K ∈ { R, C}, and let L : Cn(R, X ) →C(R, X ) be the n-th order linear differential operator defined by Ly := y(n) +

a1y(n−1) + · · · + any, where a1, . . . , a n ∈ K are fixed scalars and n is a fixed positive integer. It is well known that L is Ulam stable if and only if its characteristic equation has no roots on the imaginary axis. In this paper, we determine the best Ulam constant of L under this condition, for an arbitrary order n and without any restriction on the multiplicities of the roots of the characteristic equation. This improves and completes several known Ulam stability results for this operator and provides the best estimate possible for the error between approximate and exact solutions of the equation Ly = f , where f ∈ C (R, X ). Moreover, as an auxiliary result, we give an explicit formula for the general solution of this equation. 

Keywords : Ulam stability, Best Ulam constant, Linear differential operator. 

MSC (2020) : 34D20, 34A30, 39B82, 41A65, 47E05. 

# 1 Introduction 

Differential equations are among the most powerful tools for describing natural and social phenomena, arising in physics, engineering, biology, and economics. This modeling paradigm has given rise to a rich and ever-evolving theory devoted to the study of such equations and their solutions. For general background, we refer to [13, 14, 19, 21, 22, 26, 44, 56]. In many situations (e.g., those arising from the use of numerical methods) only ap-proximate solutions of a differential equation are available, and it is natural to ask how far such approximate solutions may deviate from exact ones. This question lies at the heart of Ulam stability theory, a subject closely connected with several areas of math-ematics, including the theories of shadowing, approximation, fixed point, perturbation, and optimization; see, e.g., [4, 17, 20, 52, 58]. Motivated by this question, in this paper we focus on the Ulam stability of linear differential operators with constant coefficients. To be more precise, throughout the paper, let K denote the field of real or complex numbers, let X be a Banach space over

Our main objective is to completely solve the problem of determining the best Ulam constant of this operator, and thereby complete the results obtained in [5, 7, 8, 36, 42, 54]. Let us first recall some basic notions from Ulam stability theory that will be used throughout the paper. We begin with the notion of a gauge on a vector space E over the field K (see also [16, 35]). 

Definition 1.1. A mapping ρE : E → [0 , ∞] is said to be a gauge on E if: 

(i) ρE (x) = 0 if and only if x = 0 ;

(ii) ρE (λx ) = |λ| ρE (x) for all x ∈ E and λ ∈ K \ { 0}.

The notion of a gauge extends that of a norm and provides a general approach for studying the Ulam stability of operators; see [15, 16] for further details. Let E and F be vector spaces over K equipped with gauges ρE and ρF , respectively, and let T : E → F be a linear operator. 

Definition 1.2 ([35]) . We say that T : E → F is Ulam stable (or Hyers–Ulam stable )if there exists a constant K ≥ 0 with the following property: for every ϵ > 0, every 

b ∈ T (E), and every y ∈ E satisfying ρF (T y − b) ≤ ϵ, there exists y0 ∈ E such that 

T y 0 = b and ρE (y − y0) ≤ Kϵ .

An element y ∈ E satisfying the inequality ρF (T y − b) ≤ ϵ for some ϵ > 0 is called an 

approximate solution of the equation T x = b. Therefore, Definition 1.2 can be roughly reformulated as follows: the operator T is Ulam stable if every approximate solution of the equation T x = b is close to an exact solution. This kind of stability dates back to a 1940 talk by S. M. Ulam at the University of Wisconsin, in which he raised this question with regard to group homomorphisms: must an approximate homomorphism lie near an exact one? (see [57]). A year later, D. H. Hyers gave a partial answer to Ulam’s question by establishing the stability of Cauchy’s additive equation in Banach spaces [28]. Since then, the notion of Ulam stability has been extensively investigated and extended to numerous classes of equations (e.g., functional, differential, difference, and integral) in a wide range of structures and contexts (see, e.g., [3, 10, 11, 12, 20, 32, 25, 29, 50, 58]). The study of the Ulam stability of linear operators was initiated in [27, 35, 53] and has since been developed considerably, yielding results for broad classes of linear operators arising in various areas of mathematics; see [16, 15, 49, 47]. Obłoza appears to have been the first author to investigate the Ulam stability of differential equations [40, 41]. A few years later, Alsina and Ger [1] showed that the real-valued differential equation y′ = y is Ulam stable with Ulam constant K = 3, a result that was soon refined and extended in several works (see [36, 42, 54, 55]). The most significant advance, however, came with Miura, Miyajima, and Takahasi [35], who characterized the Ulam stability of n-th order linear differential operators with constant 2coefficients. Generalizations of Theorem 1.1 can be found in [18, 45, 46, 30]; for related Ulam stability results on other classes of differential and partial differential equations and operators, we refer to [16, 31, 34, 39]. The constant K appearing in Definition 1.2 is called an Ulam constant of T . In general, the infimum of the set of all Ulam constants of T need not itself be an Ulam constant (see [16, 49]). When this infimum is itself an Ulam constant, it is called the 

best Ulam constant or simply the Ulam constant of T , denoted KT . To the best of our knowledge, Rassias and Tabor [51] were the first to raise the question of the best Ulam constant for certain functional equations. This question was subsequently taken up by numerous specialists for other classes of equations, as well as for operators (see, e.g., [5, 6, 16, 32, 36, 42, 43, 48, 49, 54, 58]). It is worth noting that determining the best Ulam constant of an operator or an equation is an interesting problem, as it provides the sharpest possible measure for the distance between an approximate solution and the nearest exact solution. Such informa-tion is important in practice, since many real-world problems cannot be solved exactly and are instead treated using approximate methods, such as numerical methods. There-fore, knowing how far the resulting approximate solutions may differ from exact ones is essential. For the operator L, the best Ulam constant has been determined only in a few special cases. The simplest such case is n = 1, where L takes the form Ly = y′ + λy for some λ ∈ K; here L is Ulam stable if and only if Re( λ)̸ = 0, with best Ulam constant 

KL = | Re( λ)|−1 [36, 42, 54]. This constant was determined for n = 2 in [5] and for n = 3 in [8]. Related results for first- and second-order linear differential equations with certain classes of variable coefficients can be found in [2, 9, 23, 24]. For general n > 1, a formula for it has been obtained in [7], but only under the restrictive assumption that all roots of the characteristic equation are simple (i.e., have multiplicity one). The results in [5, 8] show that the constant in (1.1) is not optimal in general (see also Corollary 3.1 at the end of this paper). 3The main objective of this paper is to complete and unify the aforementioned results from [5, 7, 8, 36, 42, 54]. Namely, we determine the best Ulam constant of L for an arbitrary order n, without any restriction on the multiplicities of the roots of the char-acteristic equation. Moreover, we derive several results on the best Ulam constant for important special cases of L. As an auxiliary result, we also provide, in the next section, an explicit formula for the general solution of the nonhomogeneous equation Ly = f

(f ∈ C (R, X )).

# 4 Conclusion 

In this paper, we have completely determined the best Ulam constant of the n-th order linear differential operator L with constant coefficients. This extends and completes sev-eral known results in the literature, where the best Ulam constant of L had previously been obtained only for the particular cases n = 1 , 2, 3 and, for general n, only under the restrictive assumption that all the roots of the characteristic equation of L are simple. Our result provides the optimal estimate of the difference between an approximate so-lution of the differential equation associated with the operator L and an exact solution. Consequently, it can be applied to the study of perturbations of dynamical systems gov-erned by such an equation, with potential applications in various branches of science, including physics, engineering, biology, and economics. 17 Contributions
