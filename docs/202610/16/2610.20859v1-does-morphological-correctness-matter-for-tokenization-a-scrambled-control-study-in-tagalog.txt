Abstract 

Typology-aware tokenizers are motivated by the claim that segmentation which respects mor-pheme boundaries preserves information that frequency-driven segmentation destroys. Stud-ies routinely establish that it helps while as-suming the boundaries must be linguistically correct. We separate them. Tagalog marks voice with infixes ( s-um-ulat ‘wrote’, s-in-ulat 

‘was written’), so the root is not contiguous and no sequence of BPE merges can recover it. We build MorphAustro-Tok, a tokenizer that emits the root as a contiguous unit followed by a positional infix token, and compare it against a corpus-matched BPE baseline and, at two of three fertility levels, against a scrambled-morpheme control that preserves morph count, invertibility and per-type consistency while ran-domising the pseudo-infix. Across 33 runs, MorphAustro-Tok improves Tagalog machine translation over matched BPE by 0.84, 1.49 and 3.40 mean sentence-level chrF++ points. Token count does not explain the gain, since it grows as segmentation coarsens, and neither does capacity: at the coarsest level the scram-bled arm carries more parameters and scores lower. Scrambling the analysis moves the gain by less than between-seed dispersion at both controlled levels, so we detect no contribution from morphological correctness at this scale. 

1 Introduction 

The case for typology-aware tokenization rests on an irreversibility argument. A tokenizer fixes the alphabet in which a model perceives its input, and a contrast destroyed by segmentation cannot be re-covered by any amount of downstream capacity. Where a language marks grammatical function by means that frequency-driven merges systematically fragment, the argument predicts a cost that no en-coder can repay (Bostrom and Durrett, 2020; Park et al., 2021). Tagalog is close to the strongest available test of that argument in a Latin-script language. Voice is marked by the infixes -um- (actor voice) and -in-

(patient voice), inserted after the root-initial on-set, so that sulat ‘write’ becomes s-um-ulat ‘wrote’ and s-in-ulat ‘was written’ (Schachter and Otanes, 1972; Kroeger, 1993). The root is discontinuous in the surface string. A BPE merge joins adja-cent symbols (Sennrich et al., 2016), so no merge sequence can represent sulat as a unit inside 

sumulat , and the paradigm fragments into unre-lated whole-word types. This is the same class of problem that root-and-pattern morphology poses for Semitic, arriving inside an otherwise ordinary Latin-script word. If the irreversibility argument is right, a tokenizer that recovers the root should help. Our results say that it does, and they also say that the recovery does not have to be correct. That distinction is the contribution of this paper. A tokenizer intervention has at least two separable properties: it changes where the cuts fall, and it changes how consistently a given word type is cut. A design that varies both at once, as ours does and as the interventions in this literature generally do, cannot attribute an observed gain to either property. A control that holds granularity and consistency fixed while randomising linguistic content can at least remove one of them from the explanation, and running that control is what separates a positive result from an attributable one. 1

2 Related work 

The tokenizer stage. Subword segmentation de-termines what a model can represent, and tok-enizer quality tracks downstream quality (Rust et al., 2021; Gowda and May, 2020). Work on morphologically rich languages has reported that morphology-aware segmentation improves down-stream results and has attributed the improvement to morphological validity (Bostrom and Durrett, 2020; Hofmann et al., 2022; Park et al., 2021), rarely testing that attribution against a granularity-matched control. A tokenizer that changes segmen-tation consistency alongside correctness leaves the two confounded, and Schmidt et al. (2024) make the related point that compression alone does not explain what tokenizers contribute. We take that argument further by removing correctness while holding consistency and granularity fixed. 

Infixation and symmetrical voice in Philippine languages. Tagalog exhibits a symmetrical voice system in which the semantic role of the pivot argument is determined by verbal morphology (Schachter and Otanes, 1972; Kroeger, 1993; Him-melmann, 2005). Two of the productive markers are infixes. Because an infix is inserted inside the root rather than concatenated to it, the root is not a contiguous substring, and the standard subword machinery, which operates by merging adjacent units, is structurally unable to isolate it. Unsuper-vised segmenters such as Morfessor (Smit et al., 2014) inherit the restriction, since they too model words as concatenations of contiguous morphs. 

Numeral classifiers in Malayic. Indonesian re-quires a classifier between a numeral and a counted noun ( tiga ekor kucing ‘three cats’), and its most frequent realisation fuses the numeral ‘one’ with the classifier into one orthographic word ( seekor kucing ‘one cat’) (Sneddon et al., 2010). 

Evaluation resources. FLORES+ provides paral-lel evaluation across Austronesian varieties (Goyal et al., 2022; NLLB Team, 2024), and WikiANN provides named-entity annotation (Pan et al., 2017; Rahimi et al., 2019). Training bitext for Tagalog is scarce (Baliber et al., 2020; Lovenia et al., 2024): OPUS-100 (Zhang et al., 2020) contains no Taga-log configuration, and the mined NLLB corpus is distributed in a form no longer loadable by cur-rent tooling. We therefore fetch ParaCrawl v9 (Bañón et al., 2020) directly from OPUS (Tiede-mann, 2012), which keeps the two arms on a com-mon source. 

3 Methodology 

3.1 Hypotheses and the decisive criterion 

We test one hypothesis and one anti-hypothesis, both fixed before the runs were executed. H1 states that a morpheme-boundary constraint on BPE im-proves downstream quality for Tagalog relative to a corpus-matched BPE baseline. The anti-hypothesis states that any observed gain reduces to compres-sion and disappears once tokens per character are held equal. A decisive criterion was fixed alongside them: if performance survives a scramble of the morphological analysis, correctness of the analysis is not what produced the gain. The Indonesian clas-sifier arm is an exploratory feasibility check rather than a hypothesis test (§4.4). 3.2 MorphAustro-Tok 

The tokenizer has two stages, of which the first is the intervention. 

Stage 1: morphological pre-segmentation. Asurface word maps to a sequence of morphs by Al-gorithm 1. For an infixed form the root is emitted as a contiguous unit followed by a positional in-fix token, so that sumulat becomes ["sulat", "<INF:um@1>"] . The root is now contiguous in the token stream although discontinuous in the string, which is precisely what no sequence of BPE merges achieves. Root-then-infix ordering keeps the word-boundary marker on the root and makes voice a discrete decision the decoder takes after the root. For Indonesian, a NUM CLF N or fused 

seCLF N sequence binds classifier and noun into a single span morph. 

Stage 2: constrained BPE over morphs. 

Merges are learned by frequency within morphs and can never cross a morph boundary, because the trainer sees morphs as pre-tokenized units. Granu-larity therefore remains learned and corpus-driven; only the positions at which cuts may fall are con-strained. 

Invertibility. Exact reassembly is a hard require-ment, since decoding is meaningless without it, and the positional offset in the infix token is what makes it exact. Section 4.3 reports a defect this requirement caught.

3.4 The scrambled-morpheme control 

The control fires on exactly the word types the real analyzer fires on and always emits exactly two morphs, but it excises a pseudo-infix at an (offset, length) pair guaranteed to differ from the true analysis (Algorithm 2). The assignment is cached per type, so it is deterministic given the seed and stable across the corpus. Both arms use the same positional marker schema, 

<INF: string @offset >, so the control intro-duces no marker category of its own, although the string a given marker carries differs between the arms. 

Worked example. For sumulat , A contains 

(1 , 1) , (1 , 3) , (2 , 1) , (2 , 2) and (3 , 1) , giving the residues smulat , slat , suulat , sulat and 

sumlat . For sinulat the same offsets give 

snulat , slat , siulat , silat and sinlat .The two residue sets meet only at slat , so of the 25 joint assignments exactly one lets the paradigm share a unit, and that unit is not the root. For 

kumain /kinain the residues are disjoint, so no assignment collapses the paradigm. The residue is therefore not a real root, and paradigm members stop collapsing onto a shared unit. What the control preserves is granularity (morph counts are identical line by line, verified in Appendix D), invertibility, and per-type consis-tency of the decomposition. What it destroys is the intended morphological alignment: the substring 3excised, its length and offset, and the residue all change, so the control is not a correctness-only manipulation. 

3.5 What is tokenized, and where vocabulary parameters enter 

A single tokenizer is trained over both sides of the bitext and shared by the encoder and the decoder; the embedding matrix is shared and the output pro-jection tied to it, so vocabulary parameters enter the count exactly once. Stage-1 segmentation therefore applies to the English source as well as the target, but the root evidence that licenses an infix analysis is induced from target-side text alone, so an En-glish word is decomposed only if its residue is an attested Tagalog type. Vocabulary matching refers to this single joint vocabulary. 

3.6 Fertility matching, and what it costs 

A typology-aware tokenizer changes how fine it cuts and where it cuts at the same time, and only a fertility-matched comparison separates the two. We match on tokens per character rather than tokens per word, because classifier-span binding merges two whitespace words into one unit and would drive tokens per word below 1.0 as a pure artifact. Target levels are derived from the measured in-tersection of each condition’s achievable envelope rather than fixed a priori, since a target reachable for one arm can be unreachable for another. The derived levels are 0.319, 0.379 and 0.438 tokens per character, and no cell deviates from its target by more than 0.005, the tolerance the matching gate asserts. Appendix C gives the achieved value and vocabulary size for every cell. Matching fertility forces different vocabulary sizes. MorphAustro-Tok requires 33.2%, 19.8% and 16.3% more vocabulary entries than BPE to reach the same granularity at the three levels, since morph-constrained merges buy less compression per entry. We report this as a result in its own right (§5.5) and treat the consequent parameter differ-ence as a confound to quantify rather than a defect to forbid. 

3.7 Two sources of uncertainty, and the correction families 

Model-level uncertainty is variation across seeds, reported as per-condition means, standard devia-tions and t-intervals. At n = 3 (tcrit = 4 .30 ) these intervals are wide, and we treat them as descriptive. 

Test-example-level uncertainty is variation at-tributable to the evaluation sample with trained models held fixed, captured by a two-sided paired bootstrap over matched test examples with 10,000 resamples (Koehn, 2004; Efron and Tibshirani, 1993). Per-example scores are averaged across seeds before resampling, so the bootstrap measures example-level variance and does not re-count seed variance. Holm correction (Holm, 1979) is applied within two families fixed in advance: the 22 contrasts of each non-baseline condition against matched BPE, pooled across both languages, all tasks and all fer-tility levels; and, separately, the nine MorphAustro-Tok versus scrambled contrasts. Pooling the first family across tasks is conservative, since it corrects the MT contrasts for the NER and probe compar-isons as well. A bootstrap p-value here is a tail proportion over the 10,000 resamples, so the procedure resolves nothing below 10 −4 before correction. Contrasts whose uncorrected tail proportion is zero are re-ported as reaching that floor rather than with a more precise figure. Neither source of uncertainty subsumes the other and we never combine them. A bootstrap inter-val excluding zero is not evidence that an effect would survive retraining. We therefore apply a pre-specified stability criterion, under which a claim about mechanism requires both example-level sig-nificance and a seed-level difference exceeding between-seed dispersion. One standard deviation over three seeds is a conservative heuristic rather than an inferential test. Section 5.2 reports a case of divergence between the two, and the divergence is itself the finding. 

4 Implementation 

4.1 Corpora and provenance 

Training bitext is OPUS ParaCrawl v9 ( en-tl ,

en-id ), fetched from pinned versioned URLs with SHA-256 recorded over the downloaded bytes; a failed fetch raises rather than substitut-ing. Filtering (deduplication, length ratio, lan-guage identification) retained 90.0% of Tagalog and 70.8% of Indonesian pairs and is applied iden-tically to every condition. Evaluation uses FLO-RES+ (fil_Latn , ind_Latn , eng_Latn )and WikiANN. Translation quality is measured with chrF++ (Popovi´ c, 2015, 2017). Appendix A gives the training configuration and Appendix B 4the corpus sizes and the sources we rejected. 

Contamination. FLORES+ test text was checked against training bitext by normalised exact match, character 5-gram Jaccard, and containment. Containment matters independently: a training sentence that is a prefix of a longer test sentence has low Jaccard while leaking entirely. Measured contamination was 0.0% on both languages by all three criteria. 

A register gap we could not close. FLORES+ labels Tagalog as Filipino ( fil_Latn ), the stan-dardised national register, while ParaCrawl is la-belled Tagalog. Infixation is identical across both, so the mechanism is unaffected, but the train/test gap is large: type-Jaccard is 0.179, with 60.9% of test types unseen in training. It applies iden-tically to all conditions and cannot manufacture a between-condition difference, although it holds absolute scores at floor level. 

4.2 The mechanism is verifiably active 

On held-out infixed forms whose roots the corpus attests, segmentation recovers root and infix in 11 of 11 cases, and on a negative fixture of words that merely look infixed ( bumbilya ‘light bulb’, lumpia 

‘spring roll’) precision is 1.000, so the analyzer does not fire on everything. The mechanism fires on 33.75% of Tagalog lines and 1.63% of whites-pace words, and MorphAustro-Tok separates from matched BPE by 6.94% in tokens per character. Section 4.4 gives the much lower Indonesian fig-ures and explains why that arm is exploratory. 

4.3 The harness, and the defects it caught 

The credibility of a control-based null depends on the reader believing that the null was not produced by a defect. Every validation criterion is runnable code that halts on failure, and Appendix D lists the gates and the defects they caught. One defect shaped the design. Stage 1 originally computed the infix offset on the raw surface string while the tokenizer normalised to NFC afterwards, so where a code point before the offset was decomposed, reassembly inserted the infix at the wrong posi-tion and produced plausible-looking wrong words rather than errors. The roundtrip gate caught it at a rate of 1 line in 2,000, and a fixture-based check would not have. Normalisation now precedes any offset computation, which is why it is the first line of Algorithm 1. 

4.4 Capability gating, and one arm we treat as exploratory 

A component whose requirement is unmet is recorded as SKIPPED with a reason rather than run against a substitute. Malay and Indonesian cannot be pooled, so Indonesian runs alone (Ap-pendix E). Tagalog UD tagging is unusable, since UD_Tagalog-TRG contains 55 sentences and Ug-nayan 94 (Nivre et al., 2020); WikiANN tl is substituted, and the substitution is logged rather than silent. The top rung of the supervision lad-der does not exist either, because no maintained Philippine morphological analyzer is available off the shelf. Rungs 1 (corpus-derived root evidence) and 2 (lexicon) are implemented, and rung 1 alone attested 9 of 10 held-out fixture roots. 

Indonesian is a feasibility arm. The classifier mechanism reaches 3.95% of lines and 0.27% of words, giving a fertility separation of 0.167% against 6.94% for Tagalog, which is too rare to move corpus-level scores at any seed count this budget affords. We therefore cut Indonesian MT and NER on measured evidence and kept only the classifier-agreement probe, which is scored on classifier-bearing sentences and so is unaffected by corpus-level rarity. Appendix E gives the detail. 

4.5 A circular probe, measured rather than assumed 

The Tagalog voice probe is solvable by reading the 

<INF:...> token alone: a trivial baseline that in-spects only which infix token appears scores 1.000 under MorphAustro-Tok and 0.000 under matched BPE. Attempts to break the circularity all failed for one structural reason, since any corruption of the infix also changes whether the analyzer fires, which is itself visible in the token stream. We therefore report the probe as diagnostic rather than eviden-tial; it carries no evidential weight. The Indonesian probe is not circular in the same way (Appendix E).

5.3 No detectable contribution from correct boundaries 

Table 2 reports the study’s principal finding. At the central fertility level the scrambled control matches or exceeds the real analysis on both evaluation sets, and at the coarsest level the real analysis leads by 0.661 chrF++, which clears the example-level floor but amounts to one third of between-seed dispersion. In the table, “at floor” marks a bootstrap tail proportion at the resolution limit of §3.7. Two levels, opposite signs, both far below the retraining floor. By the stability criterion of §3.7, a contribution from linguistic correctness is not established at either level. That is an absence of detection rather than equivalence: we specified no margin within which the arms would count as equal, and a difference below our floor is not the same as no difference.

6 Discussion 

The natural reading of our results is that amorpheme-boundary constraint on BPE improves machine translation for Tagalog without requiring the boundaries to be correct .Both MorphAustro-Tok and its scramble impose the same structural change on the same set of word types. A word that would otherwise be segmented by whatever the merge table favours is instead al-ways decomposed the same way, everywhere it oc-curs. Frequency-driven BPE has no such guarantee, since a rare inflected form may be cut differently from a frequent one. Both conditions remove that inconsistency. Consistency is the property the two arms share that the baseline lacks, and it remains the leading candidate, but the design does not isolate it from what the arms also share: the same triggering types, morph count and root-plus-token shape. If the reading is right, it has a practical implica-tion that runs against the motivation for this class of intervention: the expensive part may not be the part that pays. Morphological analyzers are costly and scarce for exactly the languages that most need them, and a linguistically arbitrary decomposition captured the same MT gain here. 

Threats to validity. The scrambled control is not linguistically random in every respect: it fires on the same word types the real analyzer selects, so it inherits the decision about which words to de-compose while randomising how . The selection of infixed types is itself linguistic information and may carry the effect, in which case the conclu-sion narrows further to “correct placement does not matter given correct selection”. That is the most important open question our design leaves. Scale is a second threat: an effect visible only once a model can exploit paradigm structure would be invisible at the size we trained. 

The sequence-labelling asymmetry. The NER result sits awkwardly with the MT result and we do not have a confident account of it. Sequence labelling appears sensitive to wrong boundaries in a way that generation is not: on NER, scram-bling costs 0.023 F1 relative to real morphology, while on MT it costs nothing we can detect. One possibility is that word-level label pooling makes token-internal structure matter more than it does for generation, whose decoder can compensate. The result replicates at only one of two levels, and we flag it as a lead rather than a finding. 

7 Conclusion 

Across 33 runs, a morpheme-boundary constraint on BPE improved Tagalog machine translation over a matched baseline at all three fertility levels, by 0.84, 1.49 and 3.40 mean sentence-level chrF++ points. Token count does not explain the gain, since it grows as segmentation coarsens, and neither does the capacity that fertility matching buys: at the coarsest level the arm with more parameters scores lower. A scrambled control preserving granularity, in-vertibility and per-type consistency but not mor-phological validity matched or exceeded the real analysis at the central fertility level and trailed it by one third of a standard deviation at the coarsest. Sequence labelling differs: scrambling costs 0.023 F1 at one level, although MorphAustro-Tok still does not beat the baseline. In conclusion, the claim we defend is narrow: for this language, at this scale, we detect no contri-bution from segmenting correctly, and the result is consistent with a role for segmenting consistently that a further control would have to confirm. Three follow-ups are implied: a stronger scramble that randomises type selection, a consistency-only base-line, and more seeds. 

Limitations 

First, scale. Small models are a design choice rather than a constraint we worked around (§5.1), but the choice has a cost. The fertility-matched models carry between 1.12M and 1.92M inference-path parameters and are trained from scratch for 1,500 steps, roughly 1.6 epochs, so every model is under-trained. The constraint applies identically across conditions and the contrasts therefore hold, but absolute scores are at floor level (chrF++ 6–11) and we cannot claim that these effects persist at contemporary scale or under transfer from a pre-trained checkpoint. Second, three seeds. With tcrit = 4 .30 , seed-level intervals are wide and strictly descriptive. 8Any effect smaller than between-seed dispersion is undetectable here, and two of our three MT effects fall in that range. Third, there is no scrambled control at the finest fertility level. F0 was run with two conditions only, for budget reasons, so the F0 MT effect is uncontrolled and we make no attribution for it. Fourth, the scrambled control inherits type selec-tion. It randomises how a word is decomposed, not whether it is decomposed at all, so the design nar-rows the explanation without isolating consistency. Fifth, the Indonesian arm is underpowered by construction rather than by accident of sample size, since the mechanism reached 0.27% of words. It is reported as an exploratory feasibility check and supports no inference either way. Sixth, the voice probe is circular and is reported as diagnostic only (§4.5). The evidential weight rests on MT and NER. Seventh, the register gap. ParaCrawl Tagalog against FLORES+ Filipino leaves 60.9% of test types unseen in training. The gap is symmetric across conditions, but it holds absolute quality at floor level and may interact with the effects we measure in ways we cannot separate. Eighth, decoding is greedy and evaluation is capped at 500 FLORES+ and 300 in-domain sen-tences, both for budget. These settings are applied identically to every condition; the bootstrap runs on a smaller matched set and its intervals are corre-spondingly wider. Ninth, language identification in bitext filtering is weak. It is a function-word heuristic rather than a trained classifier, and it is unreliable on short sentences. Tenth, one line in 2,000 failed exact roundtrip under the morphological conditions after the nor-malisation fix. The line is a training sentence rather than an evaluation example, so no paired compari-son is affected, and the exclusion is applied to the union over conditions, so no condition is trained on material the others lack. Eleventh, the study covers two languages from one family and one tokenizer comparison. Aunigram-LM tokenizer (Kudo, 2018) at matched vocabulary is the obvious missing baseline. 

Ethical Considerations 

This work uses publicly released corpora under their stated licences. FLORES+ is distributed un-der CC-BY-SA-4.0 and is gated, so access requires accepting its conditions; ParaCrawl is released un-der CC0; and WikiANN is released under ODC-BY-1.0. We ask that work building on this paper cite the originating datasets and their contributors. ParaCrawl is web-mined and is known to contain noise and material its authors did not intend for redistribution, and our filtering removes neither personal information nor offensive content in any targeted way. We did not audit the Tagalog or Indonesian portions for either, and we release no model weights trained on them. Two of the languages studied here are under-served by language technology, and work of this kind carries a risk of overclaiming on their behalf. Our central result is a negative one, and we have tried to report it in terms that do not invite the op-posite reading: a scrambled control performing as well as a real morphological analysis is evidence about our experimental scale, not evidence that Tagalog morphology is unimportant or that linguis-tic expertise is dispensable in building resources for Philippine languages. The compute cost of the reported experiments is 0.76 GPU-hours on a single T4. A further 6.3 GPU-hours were spent on runs discarded after a throughput rescale and on tokenizer construction. We report the two figures separately rather than as an aggregate, since folding discarded compute into the reported cost misrepresents what reproducing this study requires.

D Gates and further defects 

Beyond the normalisation defect described in §4.3, the gates cover granularity parity between the real and scrambled segmenters (identical morph counts on 2,000 of 2,000 lines), derangement rate of the scramble (1.000 over 84 infixed types), marker-codepoint disjointness from corpus text, achieved-fertility tolerance, vocabulary-count parity, decoder causality (maximum logit difference 0.00 at po-sitions preceding the cut, asserted on every run), exact roundtrip, and a pooling audit asserting that each row covers exactly its word’s pieces. Three further defects are worth recording. The BPE trainer was seeing the literal characters of tokens already registered as atomic and learning merges from inside them, so that fragments oc-cupied real vocabulary slots and were emittable during decoding. The Indonesian classifier binder handled NUM CLF N but not the fused seCLF N 

form that carries most Indonesian classifier con-structions, which left activity at 0.02% of words 12 and a fertility separation of 0.07%, amounting to three labels on one tokenizer. Finally, a single wall-clock ceiling per run allowed machine translation to consume the entire allowance, leaving NER and the probe at step 1 of their schedules, and the first live run took 182 minutes against a 12.6-minute estimate, of which roughly 160 minutes was unbud-geted batch-of-one beam search. Ceilings are now per task, decoding is batched and greedy, and the affected runs were excluded after the matrix was rescaled. 

E The Indonesian feasibility arm 

Malay and Indonesian cannot be pooled, which FLORES+ makes directly measurable rather than assumed: over the 997 sentence pairs present in both zsm_Latn and ind_Latn , 0.0% are iden-tical, mean normalised edit distance is 0.425 and type-Jaccard is 0.391. Indonesian therefore runs alone. The classifier binder originally handled NUM CLF N but not the fused seCLF N form that car-ries most Indonesian classifier constructions, which left activity at 0.02% of words and a fertility sepa-ration of 0.07%, amounting to three labels on one tokenizer. After the fix the mechanism reaches 3.95% of lines and 0.274% of words, with a fer-tility separation of 0.167%. That is two orders of magnitude below the Tagalog separation of 6.94%, so Indonesian MT and NER were cut on measured evidence rather than attempted and reported as null. What remains is the classifier-agreement probe, a corruption-judgment task whose label is whether a classifier and its noun were swapped. No tok-enizer exposes that label, so the probe is not circu-lar in the way the Tagalog voice probe is, and the trivial token-inspection baseline scores 0.000 on it. Against matched BPE MorphAustro-Tok gains +0.003 ( pHolm = 0 .919 ), and against the scrambled control +0.007 ( pHolm = 0 .168 ). We read the arm as evidence that the mechanism can be built and exercised in a second Austronesian language, not as evidence about classifier-aware segmentation. 

F Reproducibility 

Corpora are fetched at run time from pinned versioned URLs and hub identifiers, with SHA-256 recorded over materialised content, and with licences and citations recorded in the run manifest; a failed fetch raises rather than substi-tuting. We release at https://github.com/ AbdulRehmanAntall/morphaustro-tok 

the notebook that implements the pipeline end to end, per-run JSON records with metrics and per-example scores, the full gate log, the dispersion and comparison tables in CSV, and the limitations log. Seeds are fixed and recorded, and string hash-ing uses SHA-256 rather than Python’s salted hash. Runs are keyed by architecture and configuration fingerprints, so a stale artefact is invalidated rather than silently reused. Dependency constraints are satisfiable version floors verified after import, with resolved versions recorded; exact pins were unsat-isfiable on the runtime available and would have been recorded as applied while silently failing. 

Determinism. Deterministic algorithms are en-abled in PyTorch in warn-only mode. On the avail-able hardware, fp16 reductions under automatic mixed precision have no deterministic implemen-tation. We therefore claim seed-controlled repro-ducibility of the experimental protocol, not bitwise reproducibility of a forward pass. 13
