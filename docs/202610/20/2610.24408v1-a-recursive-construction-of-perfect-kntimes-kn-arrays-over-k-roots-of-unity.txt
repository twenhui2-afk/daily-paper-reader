# A Recursive Construction of Perfect Kn × Kn Arrays over K Roots of Unity 

## Sam Blake 7 October 2026 

Abstract 

We give a uniform, explicit and elementary recursive construction of two-dimensional arrays with perfect (ideal) periodic autocorrelation over a fixed alphabet of K roots of unity and unbounded size: for every integer K ≥ 2 and every n ≥ 1 a perfect Kn × Kn array whose entries are K-th roots of unity, given by a nested sum of floor terms. The construction works with twisted (quasi-perfect) arrays indexed by a type v ∈ Z2 

> K

, of which ordinary perfect arrays are the type v = 0 members. For K = 2 the types are Jedwab’s generalised perfect binary arrays and the recursion specialises to the doubling constructions of Jedwab–Mitchell and Wild; for prime K the arrays are the relative difference sets whose existence in abelian p-groups was settled by Ma and Schmidt; and the bare existence of perfect Kn × Kn arrays over K roots of unity follows, for every K, from recent constructions of group-invariant Butson Hadamard matrices. The contribution of this note is therefore not existence but the calculus: types with arbitrary entries in Z2 

> K

; three spectral lemmas (a twisted periodic extension of a type-v array has a flat spectrum supported on one coset of KZ2; interleaving arrays whose spectra are flat on disjoint cosets gives a perfect array; and a shear of the index plane with a floor-function carry permutes types through the action of SL 2(ZK )on Z2 

> K

, whose orbits are the classes gcd( v1, v 2, K ) = g); a one-directional lift that produces every type at side KM from every type at side M for arbitrary K; and the resulting closed index function, which is the recursive form of the floor-function arrays of [6].

The present note shows that if one allows nested floor terms the side is unbounded at every phase: for every integer K ≥ 2 and every n ≥ 1 we give an explicit perfect Kn × Kn array over the K-th roots of unity (Theorem 4.4 in Section 4). The smallest instances beyond the single-floor family are a perfect 16 × 16 binary array, a perfect 27 × 27 array over the cube roots of unity, and a perfect 125 × 125 array over the fifth roots of unity; for odd prime K the side K3 already exceeds the single-floor ceiling K2 by a factor 

K.We stress at the outset that this is not new as an existence statement. For K = 2 it is the square case of the Turyn–Davis–Kraemer theorem on Menon difference sets in abelian 2-groups [41, 8, 27] (the square case itself is [8, Thm. 4.2]), realised explicitly by the recursive array constructions of Jedwab and Mitchell and of Wild [24, 42, 25, 23] and by Dillon’s closed formula [13]. For K = p prime it is contained in the theorem of Ma and Schmidt [33] that a (p2c, p, p 2c, p 2c−1)-relative difference set exists in an abelian group of order p2c+1 if and only if the exponent is at most pc+1 , and more recently in Do Duc’s local-ring construction of group-invariant Butson Hadamard matrices [15]. For prime powers K = pe a p-ary perfect array is a fortiori an array over K-th roots, and for composite K the prime-power cases combine by the Chinese remainder theorem (L¨ uke’s product, [30]). Section 7 gives the details. What we believe to be new is the uniform explicit formula and the machinery in which it is expressed: the full set of K2 types, the SL 2(ZK ) action on them, and a lift theorem valid for every 

K. The closed formula is a K-ary analogue of Dillon’s explicit rank-two formula for Menon difference sets in Z22a+1 [13], quoted in [10, Thm. 4.1]; it does not bear on the open problem posed there, which asks for explicit constructions in abelian 2-groups of higher rank. The idea behind the recursion is as follows. A perfect array of side KN decomposes into 

K2 polyphase components of side N , and it is perfect precisely when their spectra are flat on disjoint sets that cover the frequency plane. One would like to build such an array from perfect components, but an elementary counting argument (Section 3) shows that two perfect arrays can never have disjoint spectra, so the naive recursion “perfect → perfect” through components is impossible; this is precisely why the Frank–Heimiller array ω⌊x1x2/K ⌋

and its relatives, whose components are constants times a Frank–Heimiller core, stop at side 

K2. The components must instead be twisted tilings of smaller arrays which are perfect only after multiplication by a fractional linear phase. These twisted arrays, which we call arrays of type v ∈ Z2 

> K

, are closed under the recursion provided one tracks all K2 types simultaneously. For K = 2 they are exactly the generalised perfect binary arrays of Jedwab [23], and for general K with v ∈ { 0, 1}2 they are the generalised perfect K-phase arrays of Armario, Egan and Flannery [4]; the point of this note is that with all of Z2 

> K

as the type 3space the same machinery, made explicit, works verbatim for every K.

Before using these lemmas we record why the obvious recursion fails. If A is a perfect array on a group G of order |G|, its |G| cyclic shifts are an orthogonal basis of CG. Consequently no nonzero array is orthogonal to all shifts of A: two perfect arrays can never have disjoint spectral supports. Components with disjoint supports, as Lemma 3.3 requires, therefore cannot be perfect; they can only be complementary. ## Why the tower is two-dimensional 

Nothing in Lemmas 3.1 and 3.3 needs two coordinates, so it is worth seeing exactly where the same construction fails for sequences, since perfect sequences of unbounded length at a fixed alphabet do not exist (Section 7). A type is now a single residue b ∈ ZK : a sequence Q of length M over μK has type b if Q(x)e−2π√−1 bx/ (KM ) is perfect, i.e. if its autocorrelation with the wrapped terms weighted by ω−b vanishes off-peak. The one-dimensional lift is forced to interleave and twist the same coordinate: from sequences R(b) of length M and type b,

b ∈ ZK , form the twisted extensions T (b)(¯ x + M y ) = ωby R(b)(¯ x) of length KM and interleave them, S(Ki ′ + a) = T (a)(i′). Exactly as in Theorem 4.1, the T (b) have flat spectra on the K

cosets of KZKM , so S is a perfect sequence of length K2M . Starting from M = 1, where the one-term sequence [1] has every type, this gives S(Ky + a) = ωay : the Frank sequence of length K2. (The two-dimensional analogue from side 1 is Construction 4.6 with M = 1, which gives ωay 1+by 2 , the Frank–Heimiller array of side K2.) The recursion stops there, for a structural reason. The lift zeroes the type in the twisted coordinate, and in one dimension that is the only coordinate: the one-dimensional lift pro-duces perfect sequences and nothing else, so its output can never be fed back in. In two dimensions the lift keeps the type of the interleaved coordinate (Theorem 4.1 outputs ( v1, 0) 12 with v1 arbitrary), and the shears of Lemma 3.4, which need a second coordinate to mix with, regenerate every nonzero type from the axis types; there is no SL 1. So the sequence tower would need the nonzero types to be supplied from outside at each level, and they are not available: a type-b sequence of length M over μK is, for K = p prime and b̸ = 0, a ( M, p, M, M/p )-relative difference set in the cyclic group ZpM relative to its subgroup of order p (the group-theoretic reading of Section 2 with the carry cocycle in one coordinate). At M = p2, the length of the Frank sequence, such a relative difference set would live in 

Zp3 , whose exponent p3 exceeds the bound pc+1 = p2 of [33, Thm. 2.4] ( c = 1); so no perfect sequence of length p2 over μp has a nonzero type, and not even one further lift is possible. (At length p the exponent bound does not decide, but for p = 3 one checks directly that no sequence of length 3 over the cube roots of unity has a nonzero type.) The two-dimensional tower exists because the twisted directions can be rotated into one another; a sequence has nowhere to put the twist.

Binary arrays ( K = 2 ). A perfect binary array of size s × t is equivalent to a Menon– Hadamard difference set in Zs × Zt, and Jedwab’s generalised perfect binary arrays of type

Sequences and the fixed-phase question. In one dimension the analogous tower does not exist: for p an odd prime there are no p-ary perfect sequences of length ps, s ≥ 3, because there is no ( ps, p, p s, p s−1)-relative difference set in Zps × Zp [31, Thm. 4.6], due to Ma and Pott [32] (the length-p2 sequence of [31, Ex. 4.5] is the Frank sequence, the row reading of our side-p base), and for cyclic groups of prime-power order BH( Zpβ , p α) exists iff β ≤ 2α and ( pβ , p α)̸ = (2 , 2) ([16, Thm. 3.6], [39, Thm. 4.8]; cf. [22]); the discussion at the end of Section 4 locates the failure of our construction in one dimension at exactly this bound. Mow’s unified construction [35, 36] and his conjecture bound the length of a perfect K-phase sequence by K2 up to the known families. Theorem 4.4 shows that the two-dimensional fixed-phase problem is entirely different: the apparent ceiling 2 K2 for single floored products (attained by Construction V of [6]) is a property of single floored products, not of perfect arrays. # 8 Open questions 

1. Non-square shapes. Theorem 4.1 is stated for rectangular tiles, so from a full type set at shape m1 × m2 one obtains axis types at shape Km 1 × Km 2; the shears, however, need m1 = m2 (or m2 | m1 with the appropriate carry). Lifting by Kr in one direction at a time should give shapes Ka × Kb and meet the exponent bounds of [33, 34], which for prime K predict |a − b| ≤ 2; in difference-set language [33, Thm. 2.3] is such a one-direction lift with r = 2 (see Section 7), so the question is whether its outside-supplied components can be replaced by typed arrays. For K = 2 the shape 2d+1 × 2d is settled by [33, Thm. 4.4]: a (2 2d+1 , 2, 22d+1 , 22d)-relative difference set exists in an abelian group of order 2 2d+2 iff the exponent is at most 2 d+2 and the forbidden subgroup lies in a cyclic subgroup of order 4, so every nonzero type exists at that shape and type 0, whose forbidden subgroup is the direct factor Z2, does not ([9, Lemma 3.1]). The rectangular case runs into genuinely open territory: for p odd, a type-(0 , 1) array of shape pd+1 × pd over p-th roots is a ( p2d+1 , p, p 2d+1 , p 2d)-relative difference set in 

Zpd+1 × Zpd+1 , and a perfect array of that shape is one in Zpd+1 × Zpd × Zp; by [34, Result 1.1] (Davis–Jedwab) these are exactly the two groups left open by the exponent bound, and [34, Thm. 5.14] shows that for p = 3, d = 1 the first does not exist. Arectangular lift would need a full type set at shape pd+1 × pd and therefore cannot exist in general. 2. Equivalences. Are the arrays of Construction 4.5 equivalent (under the automorphisms of Z2 

> Kn

, shifts and multiplication by characters) to Dillon’s binary arrays [13], to Do Duc’s local-ring arrays [15], or to the Jedwab–Mitchell families? 3. Which perfect arrays arise? Is every perfect Kn×Kn array over μK whose K-polyphase components have disjoint spectral supports an interleaving of twisted extensions (Con-struction 4.6)? Disjoint supports together with perfection already force each support to be a set on which the component’s transform is flat; the question is whether the supports must be cosets of KZ2 and the components twisted tilings. 4. Higher dimensions. Section 6 reaches the cubes of odd side-exponent for odd prime 

K. Open are: the even cubes Kn × Kn × Kn with n ≥ 4 (the K2-cube exists as the Kronecker product of the K2 × K2 array with a Frank sequence, but at side K4 no perfect sequence over μK is available and the parity of the lift blocks the tower); the base for even K; composite odd K, where the rectangular intermediate shapes may not be filled by shears; dimensions r ≥ 4, where the same parity argument reaches the Kn-cube only when r(n − 1) is even; and which rectangular shapes are reached in general. For K = 2 the lift and shear in any dimension are [23, Thms. 7.5, 7.6], with the shape bounds of [23, Thm. 9.2], and the Kronecker-product recursion of [4, Cor. 5.5] raises the dimension of type-(1 , . . . , 1) arrays. 23
