THE CORNERS OF RAMANUJAN: PARTIAL- AND FALSE-THETA COMPANIONS TO THIRD-ORDER MOCK THETA FUNCTIONS 

GEORGE E. ANDREWS AND MOHAMED EL BACHRAOUI 

Abstract. Most of the traditional third-order mock theta functions occur naturally as specialized values of partition-rank generating func-tions. The same finite rank kernels also occur in the ordinary and odd unimodal-rank series studied previously in the literature. We organize a parallel collection of corner series by retaining these kernels and replacing their quadratic core weights by linear peak weights. Their root-of-unity specializations are governed by partial and false theta identities recorded in Ramanujan’s Lost Notebook; in one exceptional case the corner com-panion is itself a mock theta function. The classical rank series and Ramanujan identities are used as inputs. Our new results include exact two- and three-color partition identities with sparse theta discrepancies and an oriented three-dimensional corner with a distinct tower. In both color cases, positive decompositions separate the colored companion into copies of the relevant odd-corner series and a corresponding deco-rated even-corner series. Thus both sparse discrepancies become signed differences between natural corner families. Burson’s boxed modular involution provides a common rank-refined model for the two corrections, whose surviving fixed points produce the exceptional quadratic sequences. For the side rank of the three-dimensional corners we obtain an exact formula at z = −1, a strict parity bias, quartic and cubic root-of-unity rank-difference identities, and exact equidistribution modulo 3 on the progression 6 n + 5. The cubic identity also gives exact congruence for-mulas on the progressions 6 n + 2 and 6 n + 3. We further classify the associated binary triangular support modulo every integer and derive general congruence families. 

1. Introduction 

The partition rank reveals a remarkable coherence among several of Ra-manujan’s third-order mock theta functions [27, 5]. Replacing the underlying partitions by corners—plane partitions having one row and one column—leads to a closely parallel collection of generating functions.

Analytically, the passage from a mock-theta rank series to the corresponding corner series keeps the finite rank kernel and replaces the quadratic power of q by the linear power that records the peak of the corner. We use the descriptive phrase kernel-preserving linearization for this replace-ment. It is an organizing principle for the families considered here, not a claim that arbitrary analytic or modular properties are preserved. We have chosen the title The Corners of Ramanujan because the ana-lytic identities needed for the companion formulas are dispersed throughout Ramanujan’s Lost Notebook and acquire a unified corner interpretation here. The identities themselves are classical; their organization, the colored interpretations, and the new rank and arithmetic consequences are the focus of the present work. Corners have appeared in a variety of mathematical contexts. The un-derlying stack models occur in work of Auluck [11], Andrews [3], Temperley [24], and Wright [28]; corners also arise as wedge characters in the work of Datta, Gaberdiel, Li, and Peng [18], and as V-partitions in Stanley’s work [23]. The word “corner” appears to have been first used by Kreweras [21]; his paper is in French, where the term is encoignure , and the OEIS uses the English translation “corner” [22]. The ordinary corner numbers and their interpretation as unimodal se-quences are classical. More importantly for the present paper, the two-variable series Rc(z; q) in (3.1) is, up to reversal of the rank sign, exactly the unimodal-rank generating function studied by Kim and Lovejoy [19]. They used a partial theta identity of Ramanujan to obtain rank differences and a congruence for a rank moment, and subsequently developed analogous identities for several special unimodal families [20]. Bridges and Bringmann studied Taylor coefficients of the same rank function through false Jacobi forms [13]; Allen and Osburn obtained two-parameter Hecke–Appell expan-sions and mixed false-theta representations for generalized unimodal series [6]; and Bringmann, Pandey, and van Ittersum developed related partial-and false-Eisenstein-type series and differential structures [17]. Burson gave a boxed 2-modular sign-reversing involution for one of Ramanujan’s false theta identities and a multivariate boxed m-modular extension [14]. We show that the instances ( m, r ) = (3 , 1) , (3 , 2) and (2 , 1) give a common in-volutive realization of the rank-refined sparse corrections in the odd and three-dimensional corner identities. Root-of-unity specializations are a stan-dard source of rank differences and cyclotomic equidistribution in partition theory; see, for example, Garvan [26] and Folsom, Males, and Rolen [25]. For the odd family, Andrews’s convex compositions provide the earlier one-variable function [4], while Bringmann and Lovejoy later introduced and analyzed the two-variable odd-unimodal rank series used below [16, Thm. 1.1]. Thus neither the definitions of the ordinary and odd unimodal-rank series nor the previously known rank identities are claimed as new here. The paper has three layers. First, the known rank kernels and classical Lost Notebook identities are assembled into a single corner/mock-theta dictionary; this is primarily a synthesis and a change of viewpoint. Second, we give new colored-partition interpretations and exact two- and three-color relations whose errors are sparse theta series. Parallel positive decompositions separate the two-color companion into odd corners and staircase-decorated even corners, and the three-color companion into two copies of the restricted odd three-dimensional corners and a corresponding decorated even-corner family. Both errors are therefore differences of natural corner families. The two rank-refined correction terms are then placed in one boxed modular framework: Burson’s involutions cancel all nonfixed overpartition pairs, leaving exactly the exceptional quadratic families. Third, we develop new rank and arithmetic consequences: an oriented three-dimensional corner with a distinct tower, an exceptional parity specialization, quartic and cubic root-of-unity rank-difference formulas, exact rank equidistribution modulo 3 on 6 n + 5, and an all-modulus classification of the resulting binary triangular support. 12. Concluding remarks 

The corner viewpoint gives a parallel world for a substantial part of the third-order mock-theta family. The parallel is exact at the level of the finite rank kernels: the quadratic core of the mock-theta series is replaced by a linear corner peak, and Ramanujan’s Lost Notebook supplies the resulting partial- and false-theta evaluations. The exceptional identity Cψ(q) = qω 3(q)shows that the passage does not invariably leave the mock-theta world. The positive decompositions in Theorems 5.3 and 6.9 rewrite the two colored companions as 

Cω(q) + E△(q) and 2C3,o (q) + E3,△(q),

respectively. Consequently, both sparse corrections are signed differences be-tween natural corner families. The correction terms in Theorems 5.4 and 6.10 are also governed by one boxed modular mechanism. Burson’s involutions cancel all nonfixed overpartition pairs, and the fixed pairs (2.23) account for the two quadratic families in the two-color identity and the single quadratic family in the three-color identity. Corollaries 5.5 and 6.11 therefore give aux-iliary involutive models for the sparse errors. The remaining direct problems are now parallel: one seeks an involution between staircase-decorated even corners and odd corners in the two-color case, and an involution between staircase-and-tower-decorated even corners and two labeled copies of the restricted odd three-dimensional corners in the three-color case. Such con-structions would lift the boxed fixed-point models back to the original corner objects and explain the factors 2 and 4 without passing through generating functions. The exact specialization at z = −1 and the quartic and cubic identities in Theorems 6.4, 6.5, and 6.6 show that the two-variable three-dimensional series contains exact information not visible from its specialization at z = 1. In particular, the cubic specialization produces both exact rank equidistri-bution and a sparse rank difference. Several questions remain. Can the two corner-to-corner differences in Corollaries 5.5 and 6.11 be proved by direct sign-reversing involutions, and can the resulting positive decomposi-tions be realized by explicit bijections from the original colored companions? Can kernel-preserving linearization be realized by a common Bailey-pair, Hecke–Appell, or Appell–Lerch mechanism for the complete identities rather than only their correction terms? What further rank dissections follow from higher roots of unity, and which congruences arise from cancellation inside 

RM rather than merely from support exclusion? Finally, the corresponding one-arm linearization leads naturally to fifth-order mock theta functions. That direction appears sufficiently rich to merit a separate treatment.
