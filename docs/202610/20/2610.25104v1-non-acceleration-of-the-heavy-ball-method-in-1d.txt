Abstract. Despite having an asymptotically optimal rate of convergence on convex quadrat-ics, the acceleration of Polyak’s heavy-ball method and a characterization of its dynamics within more general settings have remained long standing open problems. Recently, the work of Goujaud et al. [Math. Program. (2025)] made progress regarding acceleration in the con-text of smooth, strongly convex minimization: for a small enough inverse condition number, either the method does not accelerate on quadratics, or there exists a smooth strongly convex function such that heavy-ball cycles over the roots of unity. The constructed functions have two variables, thus leaving the question of acceleration over univariate functions open. In this work, we use a Fourier-based analysis to i) provide a proof for the non-acceleration of the heavy-ball method over the class of univariate smooth strongly convex functions. We also ii) provide a strict enlargement of the previous root-of-unity cycling region, with insights regarding the “fractional” sets of Goujaud et al. [arXiv:2502.19916], and iii) give a higher dimensional cycling instance which is not covered by root-of-unity cycling. 

1. Introduction 

The efficiency of first-order methods in smooth, strongly convex optimization is governed by the condition number of the function to be minimized. Accelerated, momentum-type methods seek to mitigate the impact of the condition number on the rate of convergence compared to that of vanilla gradient descent. Among them, Polyak’s heavy-ball method (Polyak, 1964) is a canonical example, being asymptotically optimal for convex quadratic objectives. For a differentiable function f : Rd → R, initial points ( x0, x 1), a step size γ, and a momentum parameter β, the heavy-ball update is given by (HB) xt+1 = xt − γ∇f (xt) + β(xt − xt−1).

The difficulty in dimension 1 stems from the fact that one-dimensional cycling does not allow for the symmetry existent in root-of-unity cycling when one applies smooth strongly convex interpolation conditions (Taylor et al., 2017). Whether the method accelerates on the class 

Fμ,L (R) is stated as an open problem by Goujaud et al. (2025b,a). Goujaud et al. (2025b) also provide an equivalent semi-definite program characterizing general cycling. With the dimension free, Section 6 therein also provides a cycling condition via an averaging argument. However, they do not give a closed-form characterization of general cycling. Numerically, the parameter regions leading to one-dimensional and general cycling are indistinguishable from the region of root-of-unity cycling, suggesting equality of the three regions. Another observation includes the so-called “fractional sets” of Goujaud et al. (2025a), which are obtained by plugging in fractional values of the cycle length into the quadratic equation characterizing root-of-unity cycling obtained in (Goujaud et al., 2025b). Numerically, these form the complement of the convergence region of Taylor et al. (2018). Hence, non-acceleration in dimension 1, a closed-form characterization of general cycling, and the connection of cycling to the convergence set of Taylor et al. (2018) all remain open. To that end, we contribute as follows: (i) Augmented characterization and binding constraints. We consider the DFT decompo-sition of a general cycle to obtain an initial equivalent condition for cycling in two dimensions. For cycles over the roots of unity with m-jumps, the condition reduces equivalently to the non-negativity of a system of residuals. For m = 1, this recovers the region Ω ◦-cycle obtained by (Goujaud et al., 2025b), while the union over all m gives the strictly larger region Ω aug-◦. We argue that the “fractional sets” of Goujaud et al. (2025a) correspond to non-binding constraints of the above system. (ii) Projected cycles and 1D non-acceleration. For every parameter pair ( γ, β ) that admits a root-of-unity cycle, we construct a one-dimensional cycle by rotating the root-of-unity cycle by a carefully chosen phase and projecting it onto the first coordinate. Then, we prove existence of a function f ∈ F μ,L (R) on which (HB) follows exactly this one-dimensional cycle. This proves that the existence of a root-of-unity cycle implies that of a one-dimensional cycle. Combined with the results of Goujaud et al. (2025b), this shows that the method does not accelerate on Fμ,L (R). (iii) General cycling. By letting the dimension be free, we provide an example for which we have cycling that is covered by neither the Ω ◦-cycle region nor the Ω aug-◦ region. Instead, with the dimension being free, cycling is characterized by a mixed condition. We leave open the conjecture that Ω (2) Cycle = Ω aug-◦, i.e., that with the dimension fixed, root-of-unity cycling is indeed characteristic of two-dimensional cycling. Another closely related problem is the Kalman conjecture in absolute stability (Kalman, 1957). There, one is interested in analyzing stability of a closed loop Lur’e system with slope restricted nonlinearities. The original conjecture is false, and Seiler and Carrasco (2021) give a sufficient condition for cycling of a SISO Lur’e system. Despite the similarity of the two problems, we are not aware of any existing works that apply the result of Seiler and Carrasco (2021) to one-dimensional heavy-ball cycling. 6. Connection to absolute stability 

One of the earliest works highlighting the cycling behavior of heavy-ball also happens to be one of the first connecting it with control systems: Lessard et al. (2016) cast the update rules of several first-order methods in the form of dynamical systems with nonlinearities, and use techniques from control theory, such as integral quadratic constraints (Megretski and Rantzer, 1997), to obtain convergence guarantees for these algorithms. Despite this connection, in the realm of characterizing instability, there exists to the best of our knowledge little connection between the works of control theory and optimization. Here, we briefly make explicit the connection between heavy-ball cycling and the periodic Lur’e-system construction of Seiler and Carrasco (2021). To connect (HB) with Lur’e systems, consider the (HB) recursion on a function f . Let the cyclic signal {zt}t∈Z ⊆ C be the periodic extension of a cyclic heavy-ball trajectory given by the (HB) recursion for some parameter pair ( γ, β ) ∈ Ωcv (Qμ,L ). We studied the cycling behavior of the heavy-ball method in smooth, strongly convex minimization. We proved the non-acceleration of heavy-ball for smooth strongly convex univariate functions. More generally, we showed that if the method cycles over the roots-of-unity, then it also admits a one-dimensional cycle after a suitable rotation and projection. Regarding general cycling, we provided a higher dimensional cycling instance which is not captured by root-of-unity cycling, and we connected our analysis with the fractional sets observed by Goujaud et al. (2025a). Finally, we placed our work among existing literature of the absolute stability of Lur’e systems. We set a new conjecture regarding cycling restricted in two dimensions, namely that it is fully characterized by root-of-unity cycling, which is a possible direction for future research.
