QUANTUM SYMMETRIC SPACES AT ROOTS OF 1 

JINFENG SONG AND WEINAN ZHANG 

Abstract. We study the integral form of quantized coordinate algebras of symmetric spaces and its specializations at roots of unity. Our main tool is an integral localization, whose classical spe-cialization recovers the open Borel orbit in the symmetric space. At a suitable odd root of unity, we prove that the quantum symmetric space is a finite projective module over its Frobenius center, which is canonically isomorphic to the classical coordinate algebra of the symmetric space. We show that the fibre algebras are isomorphic along each double Bruhat cell of the symmetric space. We determine the PI degree and propose a conjectural dimension formula for irreducible modules. In the simply connected case, we introduce a framed Kac–Moody data, which leads to a description of fibre algebras of quantum symmetric spaces via those of quantum Schubert cells.

1.1. Background. The quantized coordinate algebra of a reductive group G arises from the theory of Drinfeld-Jimbo quantum groups [Dri87,Jim85]. The matrix coefficients of finite-dimensional type 1

modules over quantum groups form a Hopf algebra Oq[G], which quantizes the classical coordinate algebra O[G]. The theory of canonical bases provides an integral form OA[G] over A = Z[q, q −1];see [Kas93, Lus94]. Lusztig [Lus09] used this construction to establish the Chevalley group scheme over Z.De Concini–Lyubashenko [DCL94] initiated the study of the quantized coordinate algebra 

Oϵ[G] at an root of unity ϵ of suitable odd order; see also Brown–Gordon [BG02] and the references therein. A central feature of this theory is its connection with Poisson geometry, in the spirit of the orbit method. The dual quantum Frobenius map [Lus90] identifies O[G] with a central subalgebra over which Oϵ[G] is finite projective, reducing the study of irreducible modules to that of finite-dimensional fibre algebras. These fibre algebras are isomorphic along each double Bruhat cell [DCL94, Section 9]; the double Bruhat cells are precisely the T -leaves of the standard Poisson structure on G.It is natural to extend the above root-of-unity picture from reductive groups to symmetric spaces. The construction of quantum symmetric spaces by Letzter was one of the motivations for the theory of quantum symmetric pairs; cf. [Let03]. To pursue the root-of-unity extension, one needs an integral form of the quantum symmetric space. Using the icanonical bases developed by
