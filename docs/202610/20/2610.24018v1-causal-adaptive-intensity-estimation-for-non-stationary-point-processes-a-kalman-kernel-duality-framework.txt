## ABSTRACT 

Estimating the instantaneous conditional intensity of a non-stationary point process from a single realization of discrete event timestamps is a fundamental problem in computational neuroscience, quantitative finance, and network monitoring. Traditional adaptive kernel methods achieve variable bandwidths through Abramson’s square-root law, yet existing implementations require non-causal access to the complete event record and incur O(N 2) or at best O(N log N ) computational cost. Causal alternatives based on reciprocal inter-arrival times, meanwhile, suffer from systematic diver-gence under Poisson and bursty arrival dynamics due to Jensen’s inequality. This paper addresses both limitations by establishing a formal duality between steady-state Kalman filtering and adap-tive exponential kernel smoothing. An endogenous state-space observer is constructed in which Poisson counting statistics dictate the measurement noise ( R ∝ λ) and geometric diffusion governs the process volatility ( Q ∝ λ2). Under these physically motivated axioms, the algebraic Riccati equation autonomously yields an effective smoothing bandwidth scaling as τ ∝ λ−1/2, giving a causal, recursive, O(1) per-step realization of square-root adaptive bandwidth scaling. A second-order constant-velocity extension produces a λ−1/4 scaling that reduces approximation bias, and a probabilistic dormant-state gating mechanism suppresses phantom intensity leakage during structural zeros. Synthetic benchmarks together with neural and financial data show that the observer adapts across orders of magnitude in intensity with an empirical per-step cost of approximately 30 μs. 

Keywords Point processes, Kalman filtering, adaptive kernel smoothing, intensity estimation, Riccati equation, Abramson’s law, real-time signal processing. 

## 1 Introduction 

Temporal point processes provide the canonical mathematical framework for modeling discrete events occurring irregularly in continuous time [1]. The conditional intensity function λ(t | H t), which encodes the instantaneous expected event rate given the complete history of past arrivals, serves as the primary inferential target in domains ranging from neural spike train analysis [2–4] to high-frequency financial econometrics [5, 6] and communication network monitoring. In each of these settings, estimating λ(t) accurately and in real time from a single observed event stream is a prerequisite for downstream tasks including closed-loop brain-computer interface control, algorithmic trading, and anomaly detection.

A persistent tension confronts practitioners who must perform this estimation under operational constraints. Fixed-bandwidth kernel smoothing methods [7, 8] offer a principled nonparametric approach, yet a single global bandwidth cannot simultaneously suppress Poisson noise during quiescent periods and resolve rapid transients during intensity bursts. The resulting bias-variance dilemma is well characterized in the density estimation literature [9, 10]: narrow bandwidths amplify sampling variability while wide bandwidths attenuate genuine features. An attractive alternative is the reciprocal of successive inter-arrival intervals, which provides an instantaneous, zero-lag rate estimate with no smoothing at all. This heuristic is widely deployed in applied settings, yet Jensen’s inequality renders it systematically biased upward for any non-degenerate inter-arrival distribution. Under Poisson arrivals, the bias diverges entirely because the first inverse moment of the exponential distribution does not exist, a fact that disqualifies the reciprocal estimator from the most practically relevant arrival models. Abramson [11] resolved the bias-variance dilemma in the context of probability density estimation by proving that the asymptotically optimal variable bandwidth scales as hopt (x) ∝ f (x)−1/2, allocating narrow bandwidths to high-density regions and wide bandwidths to low-density regions. This square-root law has become a foundational result in nonparametric statistics. However, translating it into a practical algorithm for streaming point process data has remained an open problem for over four decades. All existing implementations of Abramson’s rule require a two-pass procedure: a pilot estimate computed from the entire dataset is used to set local bandwidths in a second pass [9, 12]. This offline structure is inherently non-causal and computationally expensive, scaling as O(N 2) in the number of events, or O(N log N ) with fast approximations [13]. Neither variant is compatible with the microsecond-latency, bounded-memory requirements of real-time inference on edge hardware. Bayesian alternatives such as online changepoint detection [14], point process generalized linear models [4], Gaussian process modulated Poisson processes [15, 16], sequential Markov chain Monte Carlo inference under state-space priors [17], and approximate Poisson-Kalman filtering [18] offer varying degrees of adaptivity but generally sacrifice either computational tractability or the ability to handle prolonged structural silences in the event stream. The central observation of this paper is that the gap between optimal offline bandwidth scheduling and causal constant-complexity filtering can be closed by embedding the statistical structure of the point process directly into the noise parametrization of a linear state-space model. Poisson counting statistics dictate that the measurement noise variance scales linearly with the local intensity ( R ∝ λ), while the requirement of non-negative, scale-invariant volatility uniquely determines a geometric diffusion in which the process noise covariance scales quadratically ( Q ∝ λ2). When these two physically motivated noise axioms are substituted into the discrete algebraic Riccati equation, the resulting steady-state Kalman gain scales as Kss ∝ √λ, and the effective continuous-time smoothing bandwidth of the equivalent exponential kernel scales as τeff ∝ λ−1/2. This provides an autonomous, first-principles derivation of Abramson’s square-root law from control-theoretic principles, without any reference to pilot estimation or global optimization. This work makes four contributions to the intersection of control theory, nonparametric statistics, and point process inference. First, it quantifies the reciprocal inter-arrival estimator’s systematic divergence through a closed-form bias ratio derived under the generalized Gamma renewal model, showing that the estimator is undefined or infinite for Poisson arrivals and for over-dispersed renewal processes within this family. Second, it proves the Kalman-Kernel duality theorem: a scalar steady-state Kalman filter is algebraically equivalent to a causal exponential kernel smoother, and, under the endogenous noise axioms, the Riccati equation recovers square-root adaptive bandwidth scaling in a recursive 

O(1) implementation. Third, extending the analysis to a second-order constant-velocity kinematic model yields a distinct λ−1/4 bandwidth scaling together with a probabilistic dormant-state gating mechanism that suppresses phantom intensity leakage during structural-zero intervals. Fourth, synthetic and real-data evaluations indicate competitive goodness-of-fit relative to domain-tuned baselines while preserving an empirical per-step cost of approximately 30 μs. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews point process fundamentals and exposes the reciprocal fallacy. Section 3 develops the endogenous state-space framework. Section 4 presents the Kalman-Kernel duality theorem and derives both scaling laws. Section 5 details the algorithmic implementation including the dormant-state mechanism. Sections 6 and 7 provide synthetic and real-world experimental validation, respectively. Section 9 discusses implications, limitations, and future directions, and Section 10 concludes.

5.2 The Dormant-State Problem 

Infinite impulse response filters, including the Kalman filter and all EWMA variants, produce non-zero output for all time once a non-zero input has been received. In physical systems where the true intensity can drop to exactly zero for extended intervals, such as a market trading halt, an extended neuronal quiescent period, or a sensor outage, the IIR tail of the filter estimate decays geometrically but never reaches zero. This leakage produces a small but persistent phantom intensity during genuinely dormant states, which corrupts downstream count predictions and triggers spurious event probability forecasts. A naive reset strategy that sets the state to zero and re-initializes the covariance upon detecting a long silence introduces a subtler failure mode. After the reset, the estimated intensity is zero (or ϵ), causing the adaptive gap threshold 

Tgap = − ln αg / max( ˆλ, ϵ ) to become astronomically large. Subsequent time steps therefore bypass the dormant-state guard and execute normal predict-update cycles with zero-valued observations. Because the process noise Qk ∝ ˆλ2 ≈ 0

is negligible in this regime, the covariance P contracts monotonically under repeated updates, driving the Kalman gain toward zero. When events eventually resume, the filter is unable to reacquire the signal. This covariance decay trap is avoided by introducing an explicit dormant-state flag, as described next.

5.5 Computational Complexity 

The complete per-step update, encompassing the prediction, noise parameter evaluation, Kalman gain computation, state update, projection, and gap check, involves a fixed number of arithmetic operations independent of the event history length or the total number of observed events. 

Proposition 3 (Constant complexity) . The proposed Adaptive Kernel-Intensity Observer requires O(1) time and O(1) 

space per update step. Proof. The state vector xk ∈ R2 and covariance Pk ∈ R2×2 have fixed dimension. The transition (8), noise evaluation (11) and (15), prediction, and update involve only 2 × 2 matrix multiplications and inversions, each requiring a bounded number of floating-point operations. The gap check (34) and projection (31) are scalar comparisons. The dormant-state flag is a single Boolean. No summation over past events, matrix factorization of growing dimension, or iterative optimization is performed. Storage consists of the state vector, covariance matrix, previous event time, dormant flag, and a small number of scalar parameters, totaling O(1) memory. This stands in contrast to batch kernel density estimation methods, which require O(N 2) operations (or O(N log N )

with fast multipole or dual-tree approximations) per evaluation, where N is the total event count, and to Gaussian process methods, which scale as O(N 3) in their exact formulation. The complete algorithm is summarized as follows. At each time step k, the observer: (i) checks for dormant-state entry via the adaptive gap threshold (34); (ii) evaluates the endogenous noise parameters Rk and Qk from the posterior intensity; (iii) executes the standard Kalman predict-update cycle; and (iv) applies the non-negativity projection (31) and velocity constraint (32). Upon awakening from a dormant state, the covariance is re-inflated to Pinit and the measurement noise is initialized from the current observation zk rather than the stale posterior. The complete pseudocode is provided in Section 5.4.

The gated observer reacquires the signal within a single bin (0.1 s), confirming that the covariance re-inflation mechanism effectively prevents the covariance decay trap. The one-bin latency, rather than zero, reflects the awakening initialization that uses the current measurement zk to set the measurement noise variance, producing a moderately conservative Kalman gain on the first active step. This single-bin cost is a deliberate trade-off that prevents the transient overshoot that would otherwise occur if the filter assigned K ≈ 1 to the first returning observation.

7.3.1 Dataset and Predictive Evaluation

## 9 Discussion 

The empirical evidence assembled in Sections 6 and 7 admits a single structural interpretation of the observer’s behavior across heterogeneous regimes. On Profile A the fast EWMA amplifies Poisson noise during quasi-stationary segments while the slow EWMA incurs a 1.9 s phase lag at step transitions, and on the BTCUSDT data the two time constants exchange rank between the event window and the post-event period (Table 3 and Section 7.3). No fixed-bandwidth estimator can resolve this conflict across regimes, whereas the proposed observer adapts autonomously because the Riccati equation couples Poisson measurement noise with geometric process diffusion to yield the gain scaling 

Kss ∝ √λ and the bandwidth law τeff ∝ λ−1/2 (Theorem 1 and Corollary 1). The bandwidth is thus endogenous to the noise axioms rather than supplied by external tuning, and the same mechanism predicts a shallower exponent 

τ CV eff ∝ λ−1/4 for the constant-velocity extension (Proposition 2), because the velocity state absorbs the first-order intensity gradient and shifts the dominant residual from bias to curvature. Numerical fits of −0.505 and −0.251 in Fig. 3 match both predictions to sub-percent accuracy, and the 11.3% RMSE reduction from the scalar to the CV observer on Profile A (Table 3) localizes the gain to the sinusoidal segments where the velocity state tracks a smooth gradient, recovering the classical α-β trade-off [24]. Three limitations circumscribe the present formulation and define concrete directions for future work. The linear Kalman update relies on a Gaussian approximation to the binned Poisson count, which becomes progressively less accurate in very sparse regimes where λk∆t approaches or falls below one event per bin and the distribution is dominated by zeros and ones; the neural results in Section 7.2 indicate that the observer remains useful at moderate count levels, but substantially sparser settings will likely require nonlinear extensions such as an extended or unscented Kalman filter, or sequential Monte Carlo updates with a Poisson observation likelihood, while offline analyses can invoke a Rauch-Tung-Striebel backward pass [31] for non-causal smoothing. A separate obstacle to scale is the univariate scope, which excludes the structured cross-channel dependencies present in high-density neural recordings and correlated order flows across venues, where a naive extension to dimension d would incur O(d3) cost per step and forfeit the constant-time advantage; block-diagonal covariance models and the data-driven Kalman variants of [32, 33] offer a principled route that can reuse the endogenous noise parametrization as physically grounded inductive bias. Exogenous modulation is likewise absent from the current formulation: the observer is driven entirely by the endogenous event history, so stimulus-response paradigms, news-driven trading surges, and seasonally modulated seismic activity require an exogenous control term Bu k in the state-transition equation, an extension that is algebraically straightforward and leaves the Riccati-based bandwidth mechanism intact. 

## 10 Conclusion 

This paper establishes a formal duality between Kalman filtering and adaptive kernel smoothing for point process intensity estimation. Under two physically motivated noise axioms, Poisson counting statistics dictating measurement uncertainty ( R ∝ λ) and geometric diffusion governing process volatility ( Q ∝ λ2), the steady-state algebraic Riccati equation yields an effective smoothing bandwidth that scales as τeff ∝ λ−1/2. This result gives a causal, recursive, 

O(1) per-step realization of square-root adaptive bandwidth scaling and explains how that scaling emerges from the interaction between Poisson measurement noise and geometric process diffusion. Empirical results support the theoretical predictions. Controlled synthetic experiments confirm the predicted scaling exponents for both scalar and constant-velocity model orders and the geometric noise model’s capacity to track self-exciting avalanches, while additional experiments verify the analytical divergence of reciprocal estimators and the dormant-state gating mechanism’s suppression of phantom intensity leakage. Two cross-domain applications, to motor-cortex spike trains and tick-level cryptocurrency trade data, indicate competitive goodness-of-fit relative to domain-tuned baselines under model-free diagnostics that require no access to ground-truth intensity. Computational profiling yields an empirical per-step cost of approximately 30 μs. The framework opens several avenues for future investigation, including nonlinear filtering extensions for low-count regimes, multi-channel generalizations for high-dimensional neural and financial data, and the incorporation of exogenous covariates for stimulus-response modeling. More broadly, the results demonstrate that embedding the statistical structure of the observed phenomenon directly into the noise parametrization of a state-space model can 25 Causal Adaptive Intensity Estimation A P REPRINT 

cause classical optimality results to emerge autonomously from the filtering dynamics, suggesting that similar duality principles may govern adaptive estimation in other counting and renewal processes.
