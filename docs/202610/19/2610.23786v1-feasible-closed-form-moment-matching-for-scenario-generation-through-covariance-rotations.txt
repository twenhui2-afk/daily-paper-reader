# Feasible closed form moment matching for scenario generation through covariance rotations 

Juan Pablo Contreras a, ∗

> aEscuela de Ingeniería Industrial, Universidad Diego Portales, Ejército 441, Santiago, Chile

Abstract 

Closed form moment matching constructs small scenario sets without nu-merical optimisation. We study two generators that match the mean, the covariance matrix, and the marginal third and fourth moments. Both place their scenarios along the columns of a covariance factor, usually the sym-metric or Cholesky root. Although every valid factor reproduces the same covariance matrix, its choice can determine whether the resulting weights are nonnegative. By viewing each column as a univariate mixture component, Pearson’s inequality gives the minimum probability needed to reproduce its assigned variance, skewness, and kurtosis, which leads to exact feasibility conditions whenever the relevant entrywise powers are nonsingular. The effect of the factor is governed by its entrywise fourth powers, whose sum can vary by a factor equal to the dimension. This explains why standard roots fail in high dimension. We combine these results into a rotated mo-ment matching procedure that searches for feasible factors without altering the target moments. Applied to stock returns and synthetic data, the proce-dure produces valid probabilities for both generators in nearly every instance considered, and it outperforms the original constructions, whose standard factors give invalid weights in most of them. Finally, the two asset case explains when such a rotation exists. With symmetric marginals, a suitable rotation reaches Pearson’s bound except in one degenerate case, whereas with skewness the factor must balance the allocation of third and fourth moments, and some attainable targets admit no feasible rotation. We also establish a sufficient condition for arbitrary correlation and equal marginal kurtosis.

1. Introduction 

Stochastic programs replace an uncertain distribution with a finite set of scenarios whose statistical quality can materially affect decisions in portfolio selection, asset liability management, capacity planning, and many other ap-plications. Their computational cost is equally important because a rolling study requires a new scenario set at every decision date, while a multistage model may require one at every node. Moment matching constructs scenarios that reproduce a small collection of statistics regarded as reliable, usually the mean, covariance, marginal skewness, and marginal kurtosis (Høyland and Wallace, 2001). Most meth-ods obtain such scenarios by solving an optimisation problem, whereas closed form generators adapt sigma point constructions from nonlinear filtering and express every point and weight directly in terms of the targets. Among the latter, Ponomareva et al. (2015), hereafter PRD, match the mean, covari-ance, and the averages of the marginal third and fourth moments, while the generalised unscented transform of Ebeigbe et al. (2025), hereafter GenUT, matches every marginal third and fourth moment with 2 n + 1 points for n

assets, so both methods are exact and grow only linearly with the dimension. The main practical difficulty is that some resulting weights can be neg-ative. Contreras et al. (2018) address this for PRD by deriving parameter choices that prevent the problem whenever such choices exist. Higher or-der filtering methods sometimes raise a kurtosis target until their formulas become real (Stojanovski and Savransky, 2021; Yang, 2024), and GenUT implements the same adjustment in its code, although changing the target also changes the distribution being represented and does not control the cen-tre weight. Negative weights may remain usable for numerical integration or filtering, but they cannot define scenarios for a stochastic program. This paper identifies the covariance factor as a central part of the fea-sibility question because both generators place points along the columns of a matrix L satisfying LL ⊤ = Σ. Since every such factor can be written as 

L = Σ 1/2Q for an orthogonal matrix Q, a rotation preserves the mean and covariance while changing how the third and fourth moments are allocated among columns. Only the choice of factor requires a numerical optimisation over the orthogonal group, and once the factor is fixed the scenarios and their probabilities remain explicit functions of the targets. 2−2 −1 0 1 2

As an example, consider two assets with correlation 0 .4, marginal skew-ness 0 .3, and raw marginal kurtosis 1 .6. These marginal moments satisfy Pearson’s inequality, but this alone does not ensure that GenUT produces nonnegative weights. Figure 1 compares two covariance factors for the same target moments. Each colour identifies the pair of points generated by one column of the factor, marker area represents the corresponding weight, and the ellipse shows the two standard deviation contour. With the symmet-ric factor in panel (a), the noncentral points require a total weight of 1 .22, which forces the remaining weight at the mean to be −0.22. The resulting scenario set therefore matches the target moments but does not define a probability distribution. Rotating the factor toward the principal directions changes how the third and fourth moments are allocated between the two column components. In panel (b), the noncentral points require only 0 .68 of the total probability, leaving a positive centre weight of 0 .32. The rotation thus preserves the covariance, the marginal moments, and the number of scenarios while restoring probabilistic feasibility. The analysis rests on the observation that each column is a one di-mensional mixture component, and Pearson’s inequality, applied to such a component together with its probability, gives the minimum mass required for prescribed second, third, and fourth moment contributions. We use this observation to obtain four main results. First, we estab-lish a general feasibility principle for scenario generators assembled from univariate mixture components, which determines the minimum probability required by each component from its assigned second, third, and fourth mo-ments. This principle yields exact conditions for column based generators 3with nonsingular entrywise powers, recovers the optimal PRD probabilities, and identifies the probability constraint missing from the published GenUT check. Second, we show that factor concentration is governed by the sum of the fourth powers of its entries, which can vary by a factor equal to the dimension, so that diffuse factors reduce the resulting dimension penalty, and we combine these results into a rotated moment matching method that searches over factors before any target is modified. Third, numerical exper-iments using equity returns and synthetic data confirm the effect of factor concentration and show that the method makes both generators valid on every dataset tested except one on which no factor of the PRD form can succeed. Fourth, for two assets with symmetric marginals we show that rotation reaches Pearson’s bound except in one limiting case, whereas with skewness rotation creates a tradeoff between third and fourth moments, for which we give a sufficient condition covering arbitrary correlation and equal marginal kurtosis, and we illustrate these thresholds numerically. The rest of the paper is organised as follows. Section 2 reviews the related literature. Section 3 presents the two constructions and their exact feasi-bility conditions, studies in Section 3.4 how the covariance factor changes those conditions, and closes with the rotated moment matching method. Section 4 evaluates the method on real and synthetic data, while Section 5 establishes the two asset existence results that explain when rotation suc-ceeds. Section 6 concludes, and the proofs not given in the main text are collected in Appendix A. 

2. Related literature 

Scenario generation methods for stochastic programming range from sampling and quasi Monte Carlo schemes to moment matching, approxima-tions based on probability metrics, and problem driven constructions, and broad treatments are given by Kaut and Wallace (2007), King and Wallace (2024), and Chou and Messina (2023). The empirical comparison of Löhn-dorf (2016) shows that the relative performance of these families depends on the problem and on the number of scenarios, while importance sampling can reduce the number of scenarios required in intertemporal portfolio choice by a factor of several hundred (Ekblom and Blomvall, 2020). Generators are usually assessed through the in sample and out of sample stability of the op-timal value (Kaut and Wallace, 2007) or through the quality of the resulting decisions (Keutchayan et al., 2017). These evaluation frameworks apply to rotated scenario sets as to any other, and because different rotations preserve 4the matched moments while changing the unmatched comoments and there-fore possibly the decisions, we leave decision quality aside and concentrate on the prior question of whether a valid set exists. Optimisation based moment matching was introduced by Høyland and Wallace (2001), who minimise a weighted discrepancy between scenario and target moments, and was made scalable by the heuristic of Høyland et al. (2003), which alternates marginal transformations with corrections of the correlation structure. Mehrotra and Papp (2013) match moments exactly through column generation, Staino and Russo (2015) solve the moment equa-tions of arbitrage free scenario trees with the monomial method, and Tanaka and Toda (2015) discretise distributions with exact moments by minimis-ing relative entropy, which yields positive weights by construction. More recent work selects scenarios from historical data through moment based optimisation (Kaut, 2021), formulates distribution and moment matching as a mixed integer linear program (Bounitsis et al., 2022), and reduces sce-nario sets while preserving higher order moments in portfolio applications (Zhang and He, 2022), and moment matching remains a standard choice in energy system models (Backe et al., 2021). These methods obtain valid probabilities through numerical optimisation whose cost recurs at every de-cision date or tree node, while quadrature based alternatives such as sparse grids (Chen et al., 2015) can themselves produce negative weights. Closed form constructions originate in the unscented transform and its higher order extensions (Julier, 1998; Tenne and Singh, 2003; Julier and Uhlmann, 2004; Liu et al., 2014; Menegaz et al., 2015; Adurthi et al., 2018; Easley and Berry, 2021), which place a small number of weighted points so as to reproduce prescribed moments. Date et al. (2008) used such constructions to sample from partially specified symmetric distributions, Ponomareva and Date (2013) matched averaged marginal skewness and kurtosis in a higher order sigma point filter, and PRD carried this design to scenario generation for portfolio optimisation, after which Contreras et al. (2018) derived the probability allocation that avoids negative weights in PRD whenever such an allocation exists. GenUT matches every marginal third and fourth moment and has so far been used in filtering and uncertainty quantification rather than in scenario generation. Negative weights recur throughout this litera-ture. The higher order unscented estimator of Stojanovski and Savransky (2021), as described by Yang (2024), keeps its last weight nonnegative by raising each normalised kurtosis Kj to at least n/ (1 − δH ) + S2 

> j

for a control parameter δH , so that its requirement grows with the dimension, while the square root variant of Yang (2024) accepts a negative weight and absorbs it through a downdate of the covariance factor, which is admissible in filtering 5but not in a stochastic program. Closed form feasibility conditions for small scenario sets also appear in operational research, where Geyer et al. (2014a) derive bounds under which scenario sets that match a covariance matrix with the least possible number of scenarios are free of arbitrage. The freedom to rotate a sigma point set is well known in nonlinear filter-ing, where the matrix square root and its rotation are chosen to improve inte-gration or filtering accuracy (Julier, 2002; Straka et al., 2013; Duník et al., 2014, 2016). Random orthogonal matrix simulation rotates samples with equal probabilities so that they reproduce a mean and covariance exactly (Ledermann et al., 2011), and the choice of rotation changes the marginal higher moments of the simulated returns (Ledermann and Alexander, 2012). Within this framework, rotations have been chosen to target multivariate skewness (Hanke et al., 2017; Alexander et al., 2022) and to produce arbi-trage free samples by rotating a simplex (Geyer et al., 2014b), while Alexan-der et al. (2024) give necessary and sufficient conditions for matching the multivariate skewness and kurtosis measures of Kollo and show that a suit-able orthonormal basis simplifies them. Foldnes and Olsson (2016) solve the same linear systems in entrywise powers for continuous simulation and choose between the symmetric and Cholesky roots without optimising the factor, and minimising the sum of fourth powers of a factor is the opposite of the quartimax criterion of factor analysis (Neuhaus and Wrigley, 1954). To our knowledge, no previous work chooses the covariance factor so that the probabilities of a moment matching scenario set are nonnegative, which is the role the rotation plays in this paper. Approaches based on probability metrics or on the decision problem itself address a different setting. Scenario reduction with respect to proba-bility metrics (Pflug, 2001; Dupačová et al., 2003) has known fundamental limits and approximation guarantees (Rujeerapaiboon et al., 2022), prob-lem based generation approximates the expected recourse function directly (Henrion and Römisch, 2022), and optimisation based reduction exploits the structure of two stage problems (Bertsimas and Mundru, 2023). Prob-lem driven methods place scenarios where tail risk measures are determined (Fairbrother et al., 2022) or decompose the output distributions of candidate decisions (Narum et al., 2024), while learning based methods train genera-tive models on empirical scenarios (Pisinger and Hemmati Golsefidi, 2026) or search for diverse scenario sets with genetic algorithms (Oliveira et al., 2022). These methods require a richer description of the distribution or access to the decision model, whereas closed form moment matching serves the common situation in which only a few moments are trusted and scenario sets must be regenerated frequently, and a valid moment matched set can 6also serve as their input.

6. Conclusion 

This paper shows that the covariance factor is a design choice rather than a fixed input in closed form moment matching. By rotating this factor, a generator can preserve the target moments and the number of scenarios while turning otherwise invalid weights into a probability distribution. Pearson’s inequality with component mass provides exact feasibility con-ditions for GenUT and PRD, while the entrywise fourth powers of the factor explain the effect of dimension and the advantage of diffuse rotations. The two asset analysis also clarifies how rotation balances skewness and kurto-sis. The numerical illustrations confirm these mechanisms and produce a feasible rotation on every dataset considered, including problems with up to 160 assets, except one on which no factor can make PRD feasible. 21 These results support a simple practical approach in which feasibility is checked for the selected factor and rotation is considered before the target moments are modified.
