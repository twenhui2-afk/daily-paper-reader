# 1 Introduction 

Absorption can alter a fixed-time logarithmic tail asymptotic even when the absorbing boundary is disjoint from the terminal target set. We establish this phenomenon for the SABR diffusion absorbed at zero: the volatility excursion that produces a large terminal value can simultaneously drive the forward toward the boundary, so paths contributing to the right tail incur an additional Brownian boundary-crossing cost. The main theorem concerns the terminal right tail of the absorbed diffusion; the implied-volatility wing then follows from a model-free transfer principle.

1.4 Relation to Prior Work 

The problem lies at the intersection of several established theories, but not inside any one of their proved regimes. Among the results we located, summarized in Table 1, none treats the fixed-maturity extreme right wing of correlated SABR with 𝛽 ∈ (0 , 1). The closest results concern the uncorrelated 𝛽 < 1 wing, the moment-explosion regime at 𝛽 = 1, asymptotics of survival probabilities, small-time geodesic expansions, and the left wing generated by the atom at zero. In particular, Paulot [Pau15] cautions that small-time geodesic expansions are valid only as long as the geodesic does not reach the boundary; the present result is a fixed-maturity, extreme-strike manifestation of the same boundary obstruction. 

1.5 Scope and Organization 

Before turning to the proofs, we record precisely what is proved. All limits are taken for fixed 𝑇 > 0, 𝛽 ∈ (0 , 1) and 𝜌 ∈ (−1, 1). We do not prove uniformity in these parameters, a 6quantitative remainder, results at 𝜌 = ±1 or 𝛽 ∈ { 0, 1}, or a left-wing theorem. No Dirichlet heat-kernel estimate is used or proved; Remark 6.3 describes what such an estimate would have to provide. For 𝜌 < 0 we state a conjectural 𝑘 3/2 correction (Conjecture 8.1), supported by formal calculations only; no proved result depends on it. The numerical results of Section 7 illustrate the theorems and play no role in the proofs. Selected deterministic steps have also been formalized in Lean; see the Supplementary Material section. Section 2 collects the reductions: the Lamperti decomposition, conditioning on volatility, and the transfer to implied volatility. Sections 3 and 4 prove the upper and lower bounds for 𝜌 < 0; Section 5 treats 𝜌 ≥ 0 and completes the proof of Theorem 1.1. Section 6 proves the intrinsic-distance identification, Section 7 presents numerical illustrations, and Section 8 discusses the subleading correction and neighbouring parameter regimes. Appendix A records measurability facts and Appendix B the numerical methods.

The geometry above identifies absorption, rather than correlation alone, as the source of the negative-correlation correction. The neighbouring regimes are therefore informative: the correlation structure persists, but the state space, and with it the tail mechanism, changes. At 𝛽 = 1 there is no absorption. Forde and Zhang [FZ15] show that the right tail for 𝜌 < 0is governed by the critical moment 𝑚 * = (1 − 𝜌 2)−1 [LM07], with a one-sided discontinuity at 

𝜌 = 0 −; the implied-volatility smile grows rather than approaching a finite right-wing limit, consistently with Lee’s moment formula [Lee04]. For 0 < 𝛽 < 1, by contrast, moments of every positive order are finite as a consequence of the quadratic log-moneyness tail proved here, and the right-wing limit is finite; its value is 𝐶 1 but not 𝐶 2 at 𝜌 = 0 (Corollary 1.3). The same factor (1 − 𝜌 2)−1 thus appears in the two regimes through distinct mechanisms: moment explosion at 𝛽 = 1, absorption at 0 < 𝛽 < 1. The normal SABR model 𝛽 = 0 supplies the complementary boundary case. Its state space is all of R and it has no absorbing boundary; its right-wing limit is 𝜈 for every 𝜌 [Hen08, Example 10.4]. The limit 𝛽 ↓ 0 in Corollary 1.2, however, retains the factor √︀ 1 − 𝜌 2, because it approaches 𝛽 = 0 through models that still have an absorbing boundary at 𝐹 = 0. The difference is caused by the change of state space, not by the CEV exponent alone. Finally, the mass-at-zero literature [GHJ18; DHJ17; Gul15] describes how the atom at the origin shapes the left wing. Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2 show that for negative correlation the same boundary also changes the right-wing limit, acting through the survival constraint rather than through the size of the atom. Taken together, these regimes show that the diffusion metric alone does not determine the extreme wing: its interaction with the survival domain is decisive.
