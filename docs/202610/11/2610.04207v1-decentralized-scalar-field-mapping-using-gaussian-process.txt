# Decentralized Scalar Field Mapping using Gaussian Process 

Hossein Papi 1, Muzaffar Qureshi 2, Kyle Volle 3, Omkar Sudhir Patil 1, Rushikesh Kamalapurkar 2

Abstract — Decentralized Gaussian process (GP) methods provide a scalable framework for multi-agent scalar-field es-timation by allowing each agent to maintain a local GP over its assigned subdomain. Because neighboring agents condition their GP models on different measurements, their GP poste-riors generally disagree over overlapping regions. This paper introduces the inducing points decentralized Gaussian process (IPD-GP) framework, which exploits these discrepancies to improve posterior consistency over shared regions. Each sender constructs receiver-specific data packets by selecting inducing locations targeted at the pairwise overlap and transmitting the corresponding posterior means and variances. Each receiver then selectively assimilates informative packets as persistent fictitious measurements in its local GP. The developed archi-tecture preserves decentralized computation and communica-tion without requiring full dataset exchange or centralized inference. Performance is evaluated using normalized root-mean-square error, negative log predictive density, and their overlap-restricted counterparts, with comparisons against non-communicating local GPs, decentralized aggregation methods, and a centralized GP. Simulation results show that IPD-GP achieves competitive predictive accuracy while reducing inter-agent communication. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Mapping unknown scalar fields is fundamental to many autonomy applications, including surveillance, wildfire mon-itoring, and plume monitoring [1]–[3]. Gaussian processes (GPs) are well suited to such applications because they provide flexible nonparametric regression together with prin-cipled uncertainty quantification [4]–[6]. They have conse-quently been used extensively for multi-robot environmental sampling, adaptive coverage, and active sensing [7]–[11]. However, GP inference becomes increasingly expensive as the number of measurements grows. For a dataset of size N,exact GP training requires O(N 3) computation and O(N 2)

storage, which can become prohibitive for mobile sensing teams operating with limited onboard computation and com-munication resources [12]–[14]. Decentralized scalar-field estimation addresses these limi-tations by distributing sensing and inference among multiple

When neighboring subdomains overlap, the correspond-ing agents generally obtain different posterior means and variances over the shared region because their GPs are conditioned on different datasets. Existing distributed estima-tion methods reduce such discrepancies through consensus, distributed fusion, or continuity constraints [16], [17]. In contrast, this paper treats posterior disagreement over an overlap as an indication that neighboring agents possess com-plementary information. This observation motivates selective exchange of posterior information specifically over shared regions, rather than reconstruction of a centralized posterior or global consensus among all local models. Several classes of scalable GP methods are related to this objective. Aggregation-of-experts methods partition data among local GP models and combine their predictive distri-butions at query locations. Representative approaches include products of experts (PoE) [18], [19], Bayesian committee machines (BCM) [20]–[22], and nested Kriging [23]. Decen-tralized implementations replace a central fusion mechanism with consensus or distributed computation [16], [24]–[27], and some approaches further restrict participating agents using covariance-based criteria [28]. These methods are pri-marily query-centric: they determine how local predictions or participating agents are combined at a requested test location. In contrast, the problem considered here is communication-centric and geometry-aware: a sender determines which portion of its posterior information should be communicated to a particular receiver based on their pairwise overlap. Other approaches address complementary aspects of scal-ability. Federated GP methods distribute hyperparameter learning or model training across agents [29]–[31], whereas the present work focuses on prediction-time information exchange between already established local GP models. Sparse GP methods reduce computational cost using pseudo-inputs or inducing points [32]–[39]. These methods generally select compact representations to approximate a GP model or improve prediction over prescribed targets. Here, induc-ing points instead serve as communication objects whose locations are selected specifically for each sender-receiver overlap. Patchwork kriging [17] is closely related in its use of pseudo-observations to improve consistency between neighboring local models. However, it imposes continuity through a predefined boundary construction, whereas the pro-posed method generates posterior-derived information online and selectively assimilates it according to the current local models. Motivated by this gap, this paper develops the inducing-arXiv:2610.04207v1 [eess.SY] 3 Oct 2026 points decentralized Gaussian process (IPD-GP) framework for communication-efficient scalar-field mapping with over-lapping local GP models. The computational calculation in decentralized GP in-ference has two components: the local cost incurred by each agent for training, storage, and prediction as its local augmented dataset evolves, and the communication cost re-quired to transmit candidate packets between agents. Because received information increases the computational load of the receiving agent and incurs a high communication cost, direct exchange of exact GP representations is not scalable. In this paper, we adopt the idea of inducing points to facili-tate the design of the transmission rule Φj→it . Commensurate with the central hypothesis of the paper, the transmission rules are designed to reduce the mismatch between the local GPs on the overlapping subdomains. To that end, each sender selects a small set of inducing points for a specific receiver so that the transmitted packets are the most informative over their overlap region Oji . Performance is evaluated using four metrics, defined as in [28]. The normalized root-mean-square error (NRMSE) is the root-mean-square prediction error divided by the range of f over X , and the negative log predictive density (NLPD) is the mean negative log Gaussian predictive density at the evaluation points, taken with the observation noise variance included, so that it scores the predicted variance together with the mean. Both are pooled over the combined subdomains. Overlap RMSE (oRMSE) and overlap NLPD (oNLPD) restrict the same two scores to the shared strips, where the agents interact. 

B. Results and Baselines 

The IPD-GP is compared against four estimators: (1) Self-only, where each agent regresses solely on its own measurements without exchange; (2) DEC-gPoE and (3) DEC-grBCM, which are decentralized aggregation rules implemented via discrete-time average consensus using 50 iterations per grid node; and (4) Centralized GP, providing the all-data upper bound. VII. D ISCUSSION 

As detailed in Table II, the communication cost of IPD-GP is light, requiring only 0.024 million scalars to be transmitted over the entire run because IPD-GP senders transmit asmall, targeted batch of inducing points and their posterior moments. In contrast, DEC-gPoE and DEC-grBCM require 

3.0 and 4.5 million scalars, respectively, because they rely on iterative consensus broadcast across every query point on the grid. Furthermore, the computation cost of the IPD-GP ap-proach is approximately half that of the BCM approaches. The execution speed of IPD-GP is faster due to the fact that much less data exists to be processed, and far fewer rounds of calculation are required to assimilate the compact packets compared to running dense average consensus at every spatial node. The predictive accuracy of IPD-GP is close to what the aggregation baselines reach. Over the combined subdomains it sits between DEC-gPoE and DEC-grBCM, and the gap to the centralized GP reference is small. DEC-grBCM is the more accurate of the two decentralized rules, but it reaches that accuracy at more than 150 × the transmitted scalars per unit of error reduction against self-only regression. These results offer evidence that overlap-targeted packet exchange with persistent assimilation recovers much of the accuracy of consensus-based aggregation at a fraction of its commu-nication. They rest on a single realization, so repeated trials over independent field and measurement draws are needed before the margins can be stated with confidence. VIII. C ONCLUSION 

An Inducing Points Decentralized Gaussian Process (IPD-GP) framework is developed for scalable, multi-agent scalar-field mapping. By exploiting spatial discrepancies across overlapping subdomains, agents exchange compact, geometry-targeted data packets rather than computing glob-ally fused summaries. The developed method utilizes areceiver-specific transmission rule to select inducing points over shared boundaries, coupled with a receiver-side ad-mission gate. Assimilating any packet lowers the receiver’s modeled variance, so the gate does not test whether uncer-tainty falls but whether the sender reports a sharper posterior at that location, which raises the chance that the reduc-tion reflects better information rather than the conditional-independence model under which it is computed. Simulation results confirm that IPD-GP reduces overlap estimation error and matches the predictive accuracy of data-heavy aggrega-tion methods like DEC-grBCM. This accuracy is reached while transmitting two orders of magnitude fewer scalars than either aggregation rule. Future research will expand this framework across several interconnected areas. A primary focus involves establishing a formal theoretical foundation for the IPD-GP architecture, including convergence guarantees for the persistent pseudo-measurement recursion and analytical error bounds for as-similating correlated neighbor packets. The current variance-based admission rule will be extended into a comprehensive trust mechanism resilient to untrustworthy or Byzantine agents that might broadcast corrupted statistical moments.
