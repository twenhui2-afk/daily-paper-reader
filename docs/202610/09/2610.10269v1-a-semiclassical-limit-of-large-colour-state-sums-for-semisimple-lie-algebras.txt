A SEMICLASSICAL LIMIT OF LARGE-COLOUR STATE SUMS FOR SEMISIMPLE LIE ALGEBRAS 

ATSUHIDE NAGASAKA 

Abstract. The Melvin–Morton–Rozansky theorem relates the large-colour semiclassical limit of the coloured Jones polynomial to the inverse Alexander polynomial. Bar-Natan and Garoufalidis [BNG96] proved the the-orem using weight systems and, more generally, established the corresponding formula for arbitrary complex semisimple Lie algebras. We give a direct proof of this semisimple Lie algebra generalization by analysing a state sum. For a complex semisimple Lie algebra g and a dominant integral weight λ, we use the embedding Vdλ ,→ V ⊗dλ to analyse the state sum. Under the specialization q = eh/d , we show that, as d → ∞ , only the identity terms and those proportional to Eα ⊗ Fα contribute at leading order. It follows that the leading-order contribution decomposes over the positive roots, with each root contribution evaluated as in the sl 2 case. Writing tα = e−2( λ,α )h, the limit is therefore given by the product of ∆ K (tα)−1 over the positive roots α.

We give a direct proof of Theorem 4.9 by analysing a state sum for the Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant in the regime q = eh/d , without passing to weight systems. There is also a physical motivation for this relation in the sl 2 case. Witten interpreted the Jones polynomial as a Wilson-loop expectation value in SU (2) Chern–Simons theory [Wit89]. Starting from this picture, Rozansky studied the perturbative contribution of the trivial connection and related the corresponding torsion to the Alexander polynomial [Roz96]. In this way, the perturbative expansion of Chern–Simons theory suggests the appearance of the Alexander polynomial in this limit. Several approaches have given quantum or combinatorial descriptions of the relation in the sl 2 case. Rozansky later analysed the universal R–matrix by regarding it as a perturbation of the Burau representation [Roz98]. Lin and Wang interpreted a cabling state sum for the coloured Jones polynomial in terms of random walks on a knot diagram, with the inverse Alexander polynomial appearing as a graph zeta function [LW01]. Garoufalidis and Loebl expressed the coloured Jones function as an evaluation of the inverse of a noncommutative fermionic partition function, again making the relation with the Alexander polynomial visible directly in the formula [GL06]. The higher-rank case presents an additional difficulty. For sl 2 there is only one positive root. For a general complex semisimple Lie algebra, the universal R–matrix involves an ordered product indexed by the positive roots, in which the factor corresponding to α has summands proportional to Enα ⊗ F nα for n ≥ 0. A direct state-sum argument must therefore determine which of these summands contribute at leading order and explain why the contributions associated with the positive roots factorize at leading order. There have also been recent works on related higher-rank large-colour problems. Gruen constructed a large-colour R–matrix for symmetric representations of sl N [Gru22], and Gruen and San Mart´ ın Su´ arez subsequently extended this construction to arbitrary irreducible representations of sl 3, obtaining the invariant F sl 3 

> K

for positive braid knots [GSMS25]. In the latter case, the classical limit is the inverse of a product of three Alexander polynomials. Their result also contains higher-order terms in the perturbative expansion, whereas the present argument concerns only the semiclassical limit under the specialization q = eh/d and applies to an arbitrary complex semisimple Lie algebra, an arbitrary dominant integral weight, and an arbitrary knot.
