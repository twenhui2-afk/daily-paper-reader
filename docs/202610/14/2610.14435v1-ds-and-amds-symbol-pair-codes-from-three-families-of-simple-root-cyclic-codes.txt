# MDS and AMDS Symbol-Pair Codes from Three Families of Simple Root Cyclic Codes 

Jiawei He, Zihan Zhang, Xiaoqiang Wang ∗, Zhao Lin 

Abstract 

Symbol-pair distances of simple-root cyclic codes are important for evaluating error correction performance under overlapping pair read channels. In this paper, we construct three families of simple-root cyclic codes over finite fields of odd characteristic via generator polynomials. For length 2q + 2 ,the minimum symbol-pair distance is 10 for every odd prime power q ≥ 7, established by excluding invalid support configurations. For length 4q − 4, when q ≡ 3 (mod 4) and finite field constraints hold, it is 9 or 10 . For length 2( q − 1) , reciprocal-polynomial analysis shows it is 11 or 12 . All obtained codes are MDS or AMDS symbol-pair codes.

Keywords Symbol-pair code; MDS code; AMDS code; BCH bound. I. I NTRODUCTION 

Modern communication and storage systems often read data in blocks of consecutive symbols. In a symbol-pair read channel, the basic read unit is an overlapping pair rather than an isolated symbol, making the model natural for storage devices and communication systems in which adjacent positions are coupled. Cassuto and Blaum introduced this framework and defined the corresponding pair weight and pair distance [1]. Let Fq be a finite field and let C ⊆ Fnq be a q-ary code. The symbol-pair metric is induced by the cyclic pair-read map π(x) = (( x0, x 1), (x1, x 2), . . . , (xn−1, x 0)) for x = ( x0, . . . , x n−1). Since

each coordinate appears in two consecutive pairs, the pair distance reflects both the locations of nonzero entries and their cyclic adjacency. Consequently, codes with identical Hamming parameters may have different symbol-pair distances. A central problem is to construct codes with the largest possible pair distance for prescribed length and dimension. Chee, Ji, Kiah, Wang, and Yin established the Singleton-type bound for symbol-pair codes and constructed infinite families attaining it [2]. For a linear [n, k ] code, 

dp(C) ≤ n − k + 2 . A code attaining equality is called a maximum distance separable (MDS) symbol-pair code. Analogously, a linear code with dp(C) = n−k+1 is called an almost maximum distance separable (AMDS) symbol-pair code. MDS symbol-pair codes provide optimal pair-error correction, whereas AMDS symbol-pair codes describe the first suboptimal layer and often exist for parameter sets where MDS constructions are impossible. Several algebraic constructions have been developed. Chen, Lin, and Liu obtained lower bounds for constacyclic symbol-pair codes and constructed MDS codes with pair distances seven and eight via repeated-root methods [3]. Dinh et al. completely characterized the symbol-pair distances of repeated-root constacyclic codes of prime power length ps over finite fields [5], and Dinh et al. further investigated repeated-root cyclic symbol-pair codes of length 2ps over 

Fpm [6]. In 2019, Dinh et al. investigated repeated-root λ-constacyclic codes of length 2ps over 

Fpm [7]. Ma and Luo constructed MDS symbol-pair codes from repeated-root cyclic codes with pair distances ten and twelve [14], and later obtained six classes of AMDS symbol-pair codes from repeated-root cyclic codes, including one unbounded length family and one family with pair distance thirteen [16]. Tang and Luo derived further MDS and AMDS families from repeated-root cyclic codes and determined the MDS and AMDS cases of a length 3p family when the generator degree is at most ten [18]. Matrix-product methods have also produced new MDS and AMDS symbol-pair codes [12]. Simple-root cyclic codes provide a complementary algebraic setting. When the characteristic does not divide the code length, the roots of xn − 1 are distinct, and the defining zeros can be analyzed through finite-field conjugacy. In 2015, Kai et al. utilized cyclotomic cosets over finite fields to construct simple-root cyclic MDS symbol-pair codes[9]. Qiu and Fang recently constructed three infinite families of MDS symbol-pair codes from simple-root cyclic codes with parameter pairs (4 q + 4 , 7) , (4 q − 4, 8) , and (2 q + 2 , 9) under suitable field conditions [17]. In this paper, we construct three families of simple-root cyclic codes over finite fields of odd characteristic. The first family has length 2q + 2 ; for every odd prime power q ≥ 7, its minimum symbol-pair distance is ten, established by excluding invalid support configurations. The second family has length 4q − 4; when q ≡ 3 (mod 4) and the relevant finite field constraints hold, its minimum symbol-pair distance is nine or ten. The third family has length 2q − 2, and its minimum symbol-pair distance is 11 or 12. All resulting codes are MDS or AMDS symbol-pair codes. The proofs combine the BCH bound, parity decomposition, root evaluations, Frobenius relations, support-pattern analysis, and symbolic elimination. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. A. Linear codes and the symbol-pair metric 

Let q = pt, where p is prime and t ≥ 1, and let Fq be the finite field with q elements. An [n, k ]

linear code C is a k-dimensional subspace of Fnq . For x = ( x0, . . . , x n−1), its Hamming weight is 

wt H (x) = |{ i ∈ Zn : xi̸ = 0 }| . The minimum Hamming distance of C is denoted by dH (C). For 

x ∈ Fnq , define the cyclic symbol-pair read vector by π(x) = (( x0, x 1), (x1, x 2), . . . , (xn−1, x 0)) ,

where all subscripts are taken modulo n. The symbol-pair weight of x is 

wt p(x) = |{ i ∈ Zn : ( xi, x i+1 )̸ = (0 , 0) }| .

For x, y ∈ Fnq , the symbol-pair distance is dp(x, y) = |{ i ∈ Zn : ( xi, x i+1 )̸ = ( yi, y i+1 )}| . For a code C, its minimum symbol-pair distance is dp(C) = min {dp(x, y) : x, y ∈ C , x̸ = y}. When 

C is linear, translation invariance gives dp(C) = min {wt p(c) : 0̸ = c ∈ C} .

Lemma 1. [1] For a linear [n, k ] code C with 0 < d H (C) < n , then 

dH (C) + 1 ≤ dp(C) ≤ 2dH (C).

Lemma 2. [6] Let C be a q-ary code of length n and minimum symbol-pair distance dp. Then 

|C| ≤ qn−dp+2 . If C is linear of dimension k, then dp ≤ n − k + 2 .

An [n, k, d p] symbol-pair code is called MDS if dp = n − k + 2 , and AMDS if dp = n − k + 1 .

c(x) = c0 + c1x + · · · + cn−1xn−1.

III. MDS SYMBOL -PAIR CODES OF LENGTH 2q + 2 

Let q ≥ 7 be an odd prime power, and put n = 2 q + 2 . We construct a q-ary cyclic code of length n and dimension n − 8 whose minimum symbol-pair distance is 10 . The main ingredient is a decomposition of each codeword into its even and odd indexed components. The roots of the generator polynomial impose a common BCH constraint on these two components, yielding a useful restriction on the supports of codewords of small Hamming weight.

We can now prove the lower bound. 

Theorem 16. For every odd prime power q ≥ 7 satisfying q ≡ 3 (mod 4) , the cyclic code defined by (3) satisfies dp(C2) ≥ 9.

Proof. Let c ∈ C 2 be nonzero. By (12), wH(c) ≥ 5. Suppose, for contradiction, that wp(c) ≤ 8.
