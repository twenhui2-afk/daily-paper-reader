ANALYTIC CRITERIA FOR DETECTING BERNSTEIN-SATO ROOTS 

YIFAN CHEN AND HUAIQING ZUO 

Abstract. We study roots of Bernstein-Sato polynomials of ideals. Our first main result gives a general partial differential equation criterion for detecting roots of the Bernstein-Sato polynomial of an ideal. For homogeneous case, we recast this criterion as an analytic condition, connecting the algebraic problem of locating roots with analytic properties of the defining data. As an application, we give a series of roots for the general determinantal ideal prove the strong monodromy conjecture for them. Keywords. Bernstein-Sato polynomial, determinantal variety, monodromy conjecture. MSC(2020). 14F10, 14M12, 13C40, 16S32.

The Bernstein-Sato polynomial of a nonzero regular function f is the monic polynomial of least degree for which there is a differential operator P (s) satisfying 

P (s)f s+1 = bf (s)f s.

Although defined by a differential equation, bf (s) is a subtle invariant of the singularities of f .Its roots are negative rational numbers; their exponentials describe eigenvalues of local Milnor monodromy, and the largest root determines the log canonical threshold. Thus the polynomial connects the theory of D-modules with the topology of Milnor fibres and birational invariants [Be71, Ka77, Ka83, Ma83]. Budur, Mustat ¸˘ a, and Saito extended this invariant to coherent ideals and proved that it is independent of the chosen generators [BMS06]. Our first main result gives a polynomial PDE test for an arbitrary root of the Bernstein-Sato polynomial of an ideal.

The monodromy conjecture is one of the most important and influential problems in singular-ity theory of algebraic varieties. It connects many different fields including topology, analysis, geometry and number theory. It predicts that exponentials of the poles of motivic zeta func-tions are eigenvalues of local monodromy. Its strong form asserts that every pole of the motivic zeta function is a root of the Bernstein-Sato polynomial. For ideals, Verdier monodromy pro-vides the relevant monodromy action, and the Bernstein-Sato polynomial of the ideal records its eigenvalues after exponentiation [DL98, Ve83, PV10, BMS06]. As an application of Theorem A and Theorem B, we give a series of roots for the Bernstein-Sato polynomial of determinantal ideals and prove the strong monodromy conjecture for de-terminantal varieties. Note that the (weak) monodromy conjecture for determinantal varieties was proved in [CZ25] and the strong monodromy conjecture was solved for maximal minors in [LRWW17]. Moreover, the authors of [LRWW17] gave the Bernstein-Sato polynomial of the maximal minors of a generic m × n(m ≤ n) matrix as Qni=n−m+1 (s + i).
