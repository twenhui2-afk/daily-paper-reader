GRAFTINGS AND COGRAFTINGS FOR SPECIES 

PIERRE CLAVIER, YANNIC VARGAS 

Abstract. We introduce graftings and cograftings for vector species, modelled on the ele-mentary operations of adjoining and deleting a root in a rooted forest. We relate these maps to operads, up and down operators, and obtain nontrivial balanced pairs, admitting pair of creation/annihilation operators with commuting relations. We establish universal proper-ties for the species of rooted forests. Using Fock functors, we recover the classical Connes– Kreimer Hopf algebra, its noncommutative planar analogue, and Foissy’s q-infinitesimal Hopf algebra. 

Keywords: combinatorial species, grafting, Hopf monoids, rooted forests, up and down operators, Fock functors. 

MSC Classification: 05C05, 16T05, 16T1016T30, 18A40, 18M80, 81Q12, 81S05.

Every rooted forest is a product of rooted trees, and every rooted tree is obtained uniquely by grafting the roots of a forest below a new vertex. The Connes–Kreimer Hopf algebra exploits precisely this decomposition, with a coproduct that records admissible cuts of trees [5, 8]. More precisely, let HCK the free algebra generated by non-planar rooted trees. Monomials are naturally associated with (commutative) forests, that is, a set of non-planar rooted trees. This space is endowed with the linear extension of the grafting map B+ : HCK → HCK .For every monomial f = {t1, t 2, . . . , t r}, B+(f ) is the tree obtained by grafting the roots of 

t1, t 2, . . . , t r on a common new root. The pair ( HCK , B +) satisfy the following universal property (see Section 3.1 in [19]) 

Theorem (Kreimer-Moerdijk) . Let A be a commutative algebra and L : A → A a linear map. Then there exists a unique algebra map ϕ : HCK → A such that ϕ ◦ B+ = L ◦ ϕ.Additionally, if A is a Hopf algebra, then ϕ is a map between Hopf algebras. 

The universality of ( HCK , B +) allows to construct the coproduct for the Connes-Kreimer Hopf algebra as follows: consider the linear map L : HCK ⊗ HCK → HCK ⊗ HCK given by 

L(x ⊗ y) := B+(x) ⊗ (ε(y) · 1) + x ⊗ B+(y). (1.1) Hence, there exists a unique algebra map ∆ : HCK → HCK ⊗ HCK such that ∆ ◦ B+ = L ◦ ∆. (1.2) The resulting algebra map ∆ : HCK → HCK ⊗ HCK is the classical coproduct of the Connes-Kreimer Hopf algebra, defined in term of admissible cuts on trees and forests. Our aim is to express this recursive structure at the level of combinatorial species 

[13, 3]. A species p assigns a vector space p[I] to every finite set I, naturally with respect to relabeling. Its derivative p′ records an additional ghost label; hence a map into p′ can represent the attachment of a new, distinguished vertex. Write a for the species of non-planar rooted trees, and E ◦ a for the species of their forests. The root-grafting map is then 

B : E ◦ a → a′.

1.2. The universal property of rooted forests. Our first result explain why the grafting operation is so well suited to universal constructions. Suppose that a commutative monoid 

p in species has a natural map u : p → p′ (an up operator ). To assign to each rooted forest an element of p, there are only two rules to follow: products of trees must be sent to products in p, and adjoining a new root must be sent to the operation u. The recursive decomposition above makes these rules sufficient to define the assignment, and also forces its uniqueness. This is the starting point of the paper.

The free commutative monoid E◦a, equipped with the up operator induced by root grafting, is initial among commutative monoids with an up operator. We now ask which aspects survive if rooted forests are replaced by other combinatorial structures. For a species p, a grafting is a natural map 

B : E ◦ p → p′.

It combines a family of p-objects, on pairwise disjoint label sets, into a new object with an additional ghost point. An up operator only acts on one p-structure, so the two notions are not literally the same. When p is already a monoid with product μ, however, an up operator u : p → p′ produces a grafting by first multiplying the inputs and then applying u:

Bu(x1 · · · xr) = u μ(x1, . . . , x r).

This factorization is exactly what identifies the appropriate subcategory of graftings and leads to an adjunction for the free commutative monoid construction. 

Theorem C (Categories of grafting maps and up operators) [Theorem 4.4]. The cat-egory of commutative monoids with up operators is isomorphic to the subcategory of monoids with graftings that factor through the monoid product, with the prescribed decomposable mor-phisms. The free commutative monoid construction moreover gives the adjunction between the corresponding categories of up operators and graftings. 

This result, proved in Section 4.2, explains why one can sometimes replace a grafting map by an up operator without losing any information, and also identifies when the larger, partition-sensitive notion of a grafting is necessary. 1.5. From operads to grafting operations. There is another, conceptually different, source of graftings. An operad organizes the substitution of operations into one another. For rooted trees, one can think of its composition as replacing a vertex by another rooted tree while respecting the attachment of subtrees. The NAP operad is the basic example that produces ordinary root grafting. More generally, operadic composition endows the derivative of an operad with a monoid structure, so an up operator can be extended from one input to an entire forest of inputs. 

Theorem D (Grafting from an operad) [Theorem 3.8, Proposition 3.9]. Let q be a positive symmetric operad and let u : q −→ q′ be an up operator. The monoid structure GRAFTINGS AND COGRAFTINGS FOR SPECIES 5

induced by the operad on q′ extends u uniquely to a monoidal grafting Bu : E ◦ q −→ q′. For the NAP operad, this construction recovers the usual grafting of rooted forests.
