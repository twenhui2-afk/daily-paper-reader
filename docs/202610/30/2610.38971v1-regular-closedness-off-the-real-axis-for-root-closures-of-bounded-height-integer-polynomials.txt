REGULAR CLOSEDNESS OFF THE REAL AXIS FOR ROOT CLOSURES OF BOUNDED-HEIGHT INTEGER POLYNOMIALS 

BERNAT ESPIGULÉ 

Abstract. For each integer h ≥ 1, let Zh be the closure in the complex plane of the roots of nonzero integer polynomials whose coefficients have absolute value at most h,with no degree restriction. We prove that Zh is regular closed off the real axis: every non-real point of Zh is a limit of interior points. The root-closure identification of Espigulé and Juher, for which we include a self-contained proof, shows that the same closure arises from monic polynomials and that outside the closed unit disc it is the connectedness locus of h + 1 equally spaced collinear similarities. The main step proves that this locus is regular closed off the real axis; this was known for h = 1 by Calegari, Koch and Walker, for h ≥ 19, and inside an explicit lens. The symmetry z 7 → 1/z then gives the full-plane statement. The proof adapts the method of traps of Calegari, Koch and Walker, using a neighboring-piece criterion, short chains through consecutive digits, and Rouché’s theorem. We also classify the convex attractors. 

1. Introduction 

Fix an integer h ≥ 1. The height of a nonzero polynomial P (z) = ∑mj=0 aj zj ∈ Z[z] is 

H(P ) := max 0≤j≤m |aj |. Allowing arbitrary degrees, define the planar root closure (1.1) Zh := cl C{z ∈ C : P (z) = 0 for some 0 ̸ = P ∈ Z[z] with H(P ) ≤ h},

(1.2) Zh \ R = cl C

Equivalently, Zh \ R is regular closed in C \ R.

Thus every non-real point of the root closure can be approximated by interior points; the theorem concerns both sides of the unit circle. Espigulé and Juher [9, Proposition 1.1] identify Zh with the closure obtained from monic polynomials and express it through the connectedness locus Mh+1 of the collinear family introduced below and its image under 

z 7 → 1/z . The new geometric step is regular closedness of Mh+1 throughout its non-real part. We state this step as Theorem 1.2 and deduce Theorem 1.1 in Section 5, where Proposition 5.2 also gives a self-contained proof of the root-closure identification. The restriction to non-real points is necessary: 0 is an isolated point of Zh, and for h ≥ 2 the real points z with 1 + √h < |z| ≤ h + 1 lie in Zh but are not limits of interior points, because near them Zh coincides with Mh+1 (Proposition 6.1(iii)). Connectedness loci, also called Mandelbrot sets, record the parameters for which an iterated function system has connected attractor. We also determine the convexity locus. Convexity occurs exactly on rational-direction radial segments: if c = ρe πip/q with p/q reduced, then the attractor is convex if and only if ρq ≤ n. Convexity does not occur in irrational directions. For n = 2, this specializes to [7, Lemma 7.2.3]. For general n, the necessity of ρq ≤ n and the absence of convexity in irrational directions also appear in Silvestri and Pérez [20, Section 3.5], which describes the extreme points of planar self-affine attractors with a common linear part by sofic systems; Theorem 3.4 adds the converse and the equivalence with convexity of the filled hull (defined below), which is the form the trap argument uses. For a compact set L ⊂ C, its filled hull ̂ L is the union of L and all bounded components of C \ L. A continuum is a nonempty compact connected set; it is full if its complement is connected. The proof of Theorem 1.2 is organized as follows. The annulus 1 < |c| < √n lies in int( Mn) by a similarity-dimension argument (Lemma 2.7). For |c| > √n, the convexity theorem of Section 3 implies that a non-real connected attractor has nonconvex filled hull. The planar trap lemma (Proposition 4.1, proved in Section A) then provides a stable crossing configuration for a translate of that hull. A short-hop argument converts this configuration into overlap of adjacent cylinders (Section 4), and Rouché’s theorem realizes the required cylinder translation at parameters arbitrarily close to the original one (Lemma 2.10). Section 5 completes the proofs of Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, and Section 6 combines them with the closure of polynomial roots and with the capture levels of [9] to describe the internal structure of Mn.2.
