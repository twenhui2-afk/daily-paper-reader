# A Dolbeault complex for the contact quantised flag manifolds of type C2 and D4Antonio Del Donno ∗1 1Mathematical Institute of Charles University Sokolovská 49/83, 18675 Prague 8, Czech Republic. October 30, 2026 

Differential calculi on non-irreducible quantised flag manifolds of the A-series have recently been investigated using Lusztig positive root vectors, revealing that several features of classical differential geometry persist in the noncommutative setting. We extend this investigation to quantised flag manifolds associated with the lowest-rank quantum groups of types C and D whose classical spaces are holomorphic contact manifolds. Building on the Heckenberger–Kolb framework, we construct covariant first order differential calculi. The maximal prolongations of calculi restricted to contact directions have lower dimensions than their classical counterparts. In type C 2, however, adjoining the Reeb direction yields a three-dimensional calculus on the contact flag manifold whose maximal prolongation has classical dimension. Adjoining the remaining simple-root direction yields a four-dimensional calculus on the full flag manifold, again with a maximal prolongation of classical dimension. For both calculi, we explicitly present the exterior algebras and identify them with those of the corresponding framing calculi on the quantum group. In type D 4, we show that adjoining the Reeb direction yields a nine-dimensional calculus on the contact flag. We conjecture that its maximal prolongation also has classical dimension. 

Subject Classification: 58B32, 16T05, 20G42, 46L87. 

Keywords: noncommutative differential geometry, Hopf algebras, quantum groups.

1arXiv:2610.40076v1 [math.QA] 29 Oct 2026 Introduction 

The theory of quantum groups, initiated in the mid-1980s by the foundational work of Drinfel’d [Dri87] and Jimbo [Jim85] on quantum universal enveloping algebras, and by Woronowicz’s parallel development of compact quantum groups via the formalism of C∗-algebras [Wor98], has by now reached considerable maturity from a purely algebraic and representation-theoretic standpoint. In parallel, and largely independently, the framework of noncommutative differential geometry, as systematised by Connes [Con90] and, in the Hopf-algebraic setting, by Woronowicz [Wor89] and by Majid [Maj88], provides a general apparatus for extending differential calculus to noncommutative algebras. The natural problem of reconciling these two traditions, that is, of equipping the coordinate algebras of quantum groups with geometrically meaningful, covariant differential calculi, has proven to be substantially more difficult than one might initially expect. Indeed, a fundamental obstruction already appears at the level of the simplest quantum group Oq (SU 2): Woronowicz’s three-dimensional calculus fails to be bicovariant [Wor89]. This behavior is not confined to the particular construction, as subsequent classification results establish the nonexistence of classical dimension bicovariant calculi for general quantum groups [SS95, HS98]. Nevertheless, rich and geometrically meaningful covariant calculi of classical dimension do exist. The paradigmatic example is again the three-dimensional left-covariant calculus on Oq (SU 2) [Wor87], which restricts to the celebrated two-dimensional calculus on the Podleś sphere [Pod89], and whose behavior closely mirrors that of the classical geometry of SU 2 and S2 respectively. In view of these difficulties at the level of the quantum group itself, a more tractable approach is to shift attention to quantum homogeneous spaces, and in particular to the quantised flag manifolds. This strategy is further motivated by the classical fibration G → G/P , which expresses a semisimple Lie group in terms of its flag varieties, so that a systematic understanding of the differential geometry of the latter provides essential structural information about the former. A covariant first order differential calculus on a quantum homogeneous space must be compatible with the coaction of the underlying quantum group. The general classification of such calculi is well understood [Her98], and follows essentially from the equivalence of categories established in [Tak79]. However, singling out those covariant differential structures that carry meaningful geometric content remains a substantially more delicate problem. Significant progress has nonetheless been made in the setting of the irreducible quantised flag manifolds, that is, the q-deformations of those classical flag varieties that are of compact Hermitian symmetric type. The foundational work of Heckenberger and Kolb [HK03] established a complete classification of all covariant first order differential calculi of classical dimension over these spaces, and subsequently investigated their maximal prolongations and the resulting higher-order differential geometry [HK06]. This family includes, as its most studied members, the quantum projective spaces CP nq and the quantum Grassmannians of the A-series, which have been the subject of intensive investigation by numerous authors [Mat22, CMO23, DDL08]. More recently, progress has been made towards the understanding of quantised flag manifolds in the non-irreducible setting. The full flag manifolds of the A-series were studied in full generality by Ó Buachalla and Somberg in [OS25], who demonstrated that their noncommutative geometry is remarkably faithful to the classical picture, in particular the exterior algebra of the maximal prolongation recovers the Dolbeault complex, and a noncommutative version of the Borel–Weil theorem holds. Moreover, restricting such calculi to the corresponding contact quantised flag manifold, namely the one associated with the parabolic subset obtained by removing the first and last simple roots, is shown to yield a differential calculus of classical dimension [GO26]. Along the same lines, in [COR25] the authors carry out a detailed study of the noncommutative complex geometry of the full flag manifold of Oq (SU 3). A similar case study is [VY15], where a quantum analogue of the BGG resolution for the full quantised flag manifold of Oq (SU 3) is employed to verify the Baum–Connes conjecture with trivial coefficients for the dual discrete quantum group. Matassa [Mat25] provides a complementary construction of covariant differential calculi on quantised flag manifolds through equivariant quantisations of the positive nilradical, recovering the Heckenberger–Kolb calculi in the irreducible case. Beyond the A-series, the irreducible rank-two setting has also been studied from a spectral perspective: in [WDO22], the quantum BGG resolution is used to construct a Dolbeault–Dirac operator for the B 2-irreducible quantised flag manifold, yielding a spectral triple. More directly related to the non-irreducible setting considered here, Carotenuto, Ó Buachalla, and Pagani [COP26] investigate a Lusztig differential calculus on the type C 2 quantised flag manifolds. Despite these developments, the covariant differential geometry of non-irreducible quantised flag manifolds remains comparatively little explored, particularly outside the A-series. These spaces are q-deformations of partial and full flag varieties whose classical counterparts are not of Hermitian symmetric type. The geometric and algebraic complications arising beyond the Hermitian symmetric setting are substantial even classically, while quantum deformation introduces additional constraints on the construction of differential calculi and their higher-order extensions. The existing examples therefore motivate a broader investigation of how much of the classical differential geometry survives quantisation, especially for non-irreducible flag manifolds in other Lie types. The present paper constitutes a first step in this direction. We consider the quantisations of those non-irreducible flag manifolds that carry a holomorphic contact structure [CS09] in the lowest-rank examples of 2the C-series and D-series of complex simple Lie algebras, namely those associated to sp 4 and so 8, respectively (and, by the exceptional isomorphism B2 ∼= C2, the B-series as well in the rank-two case). Following the general strategy of Heckenberger–Kolb [HK06], we construct quantum tangent spaces corresponding to the antiholomorphic contact directions and use them to produce covariant first order differential calculi. We then study their maximal prolongations, comparing the graded dimensions of the resulting exterior algebras with those of the classical exterior algebras on the corresponding cotangent spaces. Additionally, for the case of D 4,we construct a differential calculus directly at the level of the quantised coordinate algebra Oq (SO 8), induced from an antiholomorphic tangent space generated by the Lusztig root vectors associated to a distinguished reduced decomposition of the longest element of the Weyl group. Already for such low-rank examples, directly adapting the Heckenberger–Kolb construction to the contact directions produces a picture different from that of the previously studied A-series calculi: the exterior algebras of the maximal prolongations have graded dimensions strictly smaller than their classical counterparts in certain degrees. The same phenomenon occurs for the calculus considered on Oq (SO 8). These examples highlight the need for a careful, case-by-case investigation when extending constructions beyond the irreducible setting, since the recovery of classical dimension cannot be taken for granted. In type C 2, however, the dimension drop can be overcome by adjoining the tangent direction corresponding classically to the Reeb direction. The resulting three-dimensional first order calculus has a maximal prolongation of classical dimension. We give an explicit presentation of its exterior algebra and establish that the natural identification of the cotangent spaces of the flag calculus and its framing calculus extends to an isomorphism of their exterior algebras. Moreover, adjoining the remaining simple root vector to the corresponding tangent space yields a four-dimensional calculus on Oq (Sp 4) whose maximal prolongation also has classical dimension. The constructions are compatible with restriction to contact and the full quantised flag manifolds respectively, and classical dimension is preserved. A related four-dimensional calculus with a maximal prolongation of classical dimension was constructed in [COP26] using Lusztig root vectors. That construction, however, does not admit a restrictions to the contact flag manifold that is of classical dimension. The calculi here analysed, on the other hand, combine the recovery of classical dimension with compatibility with the quantum homogeneous space structure, in particular with both the contact and full flag manifolds. This compatibility provides a geometric distinction between the constructions and makes the calculi of interest natural candidates for studying the noncommutative differential geometry of type C 2 quantised flag manifolds. At the same time, our computations indicate that the dimension drop for calculi built from the contact directions alone should not be understood as a peculiarity of the Lie types outside the A-series. Indeed, when the analogous construction is carried out for the full flag manifold of Oq (SU 3), whose underlying classical variety is also of contact type, a comparable collapse of the exterior algebra occurs. Together with the extended C 2 calculus, this suggests that the recovery of classical dimension depends on how the contact structure is incorporated into the quantum tangent space, rather than on Lie type alone. The results presented here suggest several directions for further investigation. A forthcoming study will carry out a similar analysis in type D 4, examining whether the Reeb adjoined calculus that we discuss in this paper has a maximal prolongation of classical dimension. In the contact setting, a natural next step is to explore whether the differential calculus can be adapted to construct a quantum analogue of the Rumin complex [Rum94], reflecting the contact structure more closely than the full de Rham complex. Exploring the relationship between differential forms and quantum BGG resolutions [HK07], together with the full flag example studied in [VY15], is another promising direction of research. Finally, extending the present analysis to higher-rank contact quantised flag manifolds would provide a broader testing ground for the phenomena identified here. After introducing the algebraic framework underlying our approach, we now turn to one of the fundamental structures of noncommutative differential geometry, namely differential calculi. For the reader’s convenience, we briefly recall the basic notions and constructions that will be needed in the sequel.

# 2. Differential calculi on Oq(Sp 4) and its contact quantised flag manifold 

We now specialise the preceding constructions to the contact quantised flag manifold corresponding to the symplectic Lie algebra sp 4.In Section 2.1 we recall the Lie algebra sp 4, its quantised enveloping algebra, and the associated quantum group. In Section 2.2 we construct the two-dimensional contact calculus on Oq (Sp 4/LS ) together with its framing calculus on Oq (Sp 4), and examine the dimension drop in its maximal prolongation. In 2.3 we adjoin the Reeb direction, obtaining a three-dimensional calculus whose maximal prolongation defines a Dolbeault complex of classical dimension on the contact flag manifold. Finally, Section 2.4 extends this construction by adjoining 

E2, producing a four-dimensional contact-adapted calculus on Oq (Sp 4) and its full flag manifold. For ease of reference, the dual pairings used throughout this section are collected in a table displayed in Appendix A.

# 3. Differential calculi on Oq(SO 8) and its contact quantised flag manifold 

Building on the results of the previous section, we now turn to the special orthogonal Lie algebra so 8. As the first non-trivial case in the D n series, the quantum group Oq (SO 8) already exhibits considerable complexity, while on the other hand remaining feasible for explicit computations with the aid of its triality symmetry. In Section 3.1, we recall the Lie algebra so 2ℓ, its quantised enveloping algebra, and the associated quantum group. In Section 3.2, we construct an eight-dimensional FODC associated with the contact directions on the 34 corresponding contact quantised flag manifold, supply a framing calculus at the level of the quantum group, and compute the corresponding maximal prolongation. Finally, in Section 3.4, we extend this calculus to a nine-dimensional one by adjoining the Reeb direction and show that the resulting FODC cannot be framed by any Lusztig FODC on Oq (SO 8). For ease of reference, the dual pairings used throughout this section are collected in a table displayed in Appendix A.
