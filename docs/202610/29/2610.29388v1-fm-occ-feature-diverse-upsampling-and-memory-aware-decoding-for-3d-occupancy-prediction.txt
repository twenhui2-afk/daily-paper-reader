ABSTRACT 

3D semantic occupancy prediction estimates voxel-wise occupancy and seman-tic labels throughout a scene. Our experiments reveal that conventional sparse transposed-convolution upsampling yields poorly differentiated voxel responses and that successive decoder layers discard previously correct semantic predic-tions. To address these issues, we present Feature-Diverse Upsampling and Memory-Aware Decoding for 3D Occupancy Prediction ( FM-Occ ), comprising two components. First, 3D Sparse Subvoxel Shuffle ( 3S ) mitigates feature ho-mogenization by constructing candidate child features in the channel dimension, jointly transforming and normalizing them, and then rearranging them onto a finer sparse grid with explicit feature–coordinate correspondence. Second, Entropy-Regulated Layer-wise Memory ( ER-LM ) retains semantic information across de-coder layers by maintaining a layer-wise memory for each query through gated recurrent updates and using prediction entropy to regulate the historical infor-mation propagated to the next layer. During training, a class-weighted entropy loss Lcwe provides label-guided regularization for matched semantic queries. On SemanticKITTI, 3S increases the spatial coefficient of variation of normalized voxel-wise root-mean-square (RMS) responses from 0.41% to 4.03% , while ER-LM reduces semantic degradation and improves error correction across decoder layers. The combined FM-Occ improves mIoU from 12 .16% to 12 .73% with negligible memory overhead. ER-LM further improves mIoU in the BEV-based DISC framework from 16 .78% to 17 .17% , supporting its applicability across 3D voxel-based and BEV-based architectures. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

3D occupancy prediction (Cao & De Charette, 2022; Wei et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023) aims to infer voxel-wise occupancy states and semantic categories in 3D space, providing a dense and struc-tured scene representation for autonomous driving and robotic perception. Compared with 3D object detection (Yan et al., 2018; Lang et al., 2019; Li et al., 2024), which outputs sparse bounding boxes, occupancy prediction explicitly models scene geometry and spatial continuity. Following sparse oc-cupancy approaches such as SparseOcc and OPUS (Tang et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024a), we study an image-based encoder–decoder architecture that constructs sparse 3D features and progressively predicts semantic occupancy through feature upsampling and iterative Transformer decoding. We further examine these two key stages and identify two overlooked issues: weak spatial differ-entiation of upsampled voxel features and progressive loss of semantic information during iterative decoding. 

Observation 1: Limited differentiation of upsampled voxel features. Our analysis shows that sparse transposed convolution followed by group normalization produces voxel features with limited spatial variation in their response magnitudes. This observation suggests that the organization of feature construction and normalization deserves attention alongside the expansion of the voxel grid. ER-LM generalizes across both 3D voxel-based and BEV-based frameworks.

2D feature upsampling has been explored through learned kernels, content-aware reassembly, adap-tive sampling, and channel-to-space rearrangement (Zeiler et al., 2010; Wang et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2023; Shi et al., 2016). These methods operate on dense feature maps with spatial positions implic-itly defined by regular tensor grids. Unlike dense 2D feature maps, sparse 3D representations store only active voxel features together with explicit coordinates. Sparse 3D upsampling must therefore expand both features and coordinates while maintaining their correspondence. 3S addresses this requirement through channel-to-space feature rearrangement and matched coordinate expansion. 2.3 INFORMATION RETENTION IN OCCUPANCY DECODING 

Query-based occupancy methods employ stacked decoder layers to progressively refine semantic predictions (Zhang et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024b; Oh et al., 2025). In these decoders, successive layers repeatedly update the query representations, with information from earlier stages carried for-ward implicitly through the evolving queries. As a result, previously acquired semantic information may be altered or lost during subsequent refinement, motivating explicit mechanisms for cross-layer information retention. 

3 METHOD 

The overall framework of FM-Occ is illustrated in Fig. 2. Latent Feature Extraction first maps image features into a sparse 3D representation. The Sparse Latent Diffuser then performs scene comple-tion, followed by a multi-scale sparse feature pyramid. 3S is used to recover higher-resolution sparse features during multi-scale feature fusion. Finally, the Transformer decoder predicts seman-tic occupancy, where ER-LM propagates information across decoder stages under entropy-guided regulation. The class-weighted entropy loss Lcwe is additionally applied during training. 3.1 3D S PARSE SUBVOXEL SHUFFLE

4 EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

We evaluate FM-Occ on the SemanticKITTI (Behley et al., 2019) dataset. SemanticKITTI is a large-scale dataset for 3D semantic occupancy prediction in autonomous driving scenarios, based on the raw KITTI sequences. The dataset contains 21 classes, including 19 semantic categories, one free-space class, and one unknown class. We train FM-Occ for 30 epochs on four NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs using AdamW with an initial learning rate of 1 × 10 −4 and a total batch size of 4. We adopt a step learning-rate schedule. We adopt commonly used evaluation metrics for 3D occupancy prediction, including voxel-wise In-tersection over Union (IoU) and mean Intersection over Union (mIoU). To provide a more systematic analysis of model performance across different semantic structures, we further propose category-grouped evaluation metrics, namely ComplexIoU, TailIoU, and RegularIoU. Detailed definitions and the category grouping of these metrics are provided in Appendix A.1. 4.2 COMPARISON WITH STATE -OF -THE -ART

We examine two alternative explanations for the upsampling results: whether the transposed-convolution reference is disadvantaged by the absence of ReLU after normalization, and whether moving normalization after spatial rearrangement improves 3S. As shown in Table 5, adding ReLU to transposed convolution does not improve mIoU, suggesting that ReLU alone does not account for the gains of 3S. Applying GN after rearrangement gives similar mIoU to the original 3S, suggesting that normalization order has a limited effect on this metric in our experiments. 4.4.3 SUBVOXEL FEATURE ALLOCATION VARIANTS 

As shown in Table 6, permute–reshape interprets the expanded channels as r3 × Cout , whereas 3S uses Cout × r3. Its mIoU improvement from 12 .16% to 12 .32% supports channel expansion and rearrangement, while the 12 .42% achieved by 3S validates the proposed feature allocation. Softmax improves mIoU and RegularIoU, but its sum-to-one competition reduces occupancy IoU. Sigmoid improves ComplexIoU and RegularIoU but lowers TailIoU, possibly because it favors dominant classes under imbalanced supervision. 5 CONCLUSION 

We presented FM-Occ for sparse 3D semantic occupancy prediction, addressing feature homog-enization during upsampling and semantic degradation across decoder layers. The proposed 3S constructs and jointly normalizes child features before spatial rearrangement, preserving greater variation in voxel feature responses. ER-LM retains historical query information through gated re-current memory and entropy-guided state modulation, complemented by the class-weighted entropy loss Lcwe . Experiments on SemanticKITTI demonstrate improved occupancy and semantic predic-tion, while the cross-layer analysis shows better preservation of correct semantic predictions and correction of existing errors on average across classes.
