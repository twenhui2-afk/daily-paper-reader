# Precision-Aware Hierarchical Spectrum Search for Finite-Precision MUSIC DOA Estimation 

Linyun Yue, Chuang Yang, Member, IEEE, Jierui Chen, Mugen Peng, Fellow, IEEE 

Abstract —Dense-grid spectrum search is computationally costly for multiple signal classification (MUSIC) direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation, and low-precision evaluation can introduce numerical errors that distort candidate selection. This paper proposes precision-aware hierarchical MUSIC (PAH-MUSIC), a multistage framework that coordinates arithmetic precision, angular grids, and candidate windows in spectrum evaluation, while covariance estimation and eigendecomposition remain in FP64. Stage-dependent precision is assigned from coarse candidate discovery to final DOA estimation. Under bounded perturbations and local spectral growth, a conditional displacement bound gives a sufficient window-width condition for retained, correctly associated candidates. For a two-stage continuous cost model with fixed precision weights and window coefficients, a closed-form optimal coarse step and a cost-gain condition are derived. Simulations with two separated sources and 500 frames at each tested signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) from -10 to 15 dB show that FP8, FP16, and FP32 coarse searches followed by FP64 refinement achieve DOA root-mean-square errors close to a baseline using FP64 at both search stages. Relative to that baseline, the selected configurations reduce weighted spectrum-evaluation cost by 28.36%–44.66% under the adopted precision weights. 

Index Terms —Direction-of-arrival estimation, MUSIC, mixed precision, hierarchical search, finite-precision arithmetic, spec-trum search. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# DIRECTION-OF-ARRIVAL (DOA) estimation serves as a key enabling technology for perception and localization across multiple domains. As sensing systems evolve from single-target detection toward the resolution of dense multi-target scenarios, super-resolution DOA estimation is increas-ingly important for automotive radar and autonomous driving [1], as well as for massive MIMO and future 6G sensing systems [2]. Subspace-based methods such as MUSIC [3] and ESPRIT [4] provide high-resolution DOA estimation through subspace orthogonality and rotational invariance, respectively. However, MUSIC still requires eigendecomposition of the covariance matrix and spectral peak search over the entire angular domain, which two computational burdens render it a bottleneck for real-time processing. Efforts to reduce the computational burden of MUSIC have addressed three complementary aspects: the computational for-mulation, subspace estimation, and spatial spectrum search. At

the formulation level, real-valued methods replace complex-valued operations with real-valued computations, reducing the arithmetic cost of subspace decomposition and spectrum eval-uation [5]. Beamspace processing projects element-space ob-servations onto a smaller set of beams, reducing the processing dimension from the number of array elements to the number of retained beams [6]. At the subspace-estimation stage, the propagator method (PM) and its variants exploit linear rela-tionships between partitions of the array response to construct subspace information without explicit eigendecomposition [7]. Randomized methods instead use matrix sketching and low-rank approximation to estimate the dominant signal subspace at reduced cost [8]. Randomized unitary MUSIC (RU-MUSIC) combines this strategy with real-valued processing [9]. At the spectrum-search stage, approaches either eliminate angular scanning or reduce the cost of spectrum evaluation. Root-MUSIC replaces grid search with polynomial rooting, although its polynomial degree and associated rooting burden increase with the number of array elements [10]. As a related subspace-based alternative, ESPRIT avoids angular scanning through rotational invariance but requires an array structure supporting the necessary shift invariance [4]. Within grid-based MUSIC, compressed search [11], fast Fourier transforms [12], and ran-domized sketching with selective recomputation [13] reduce the search range or spectrum-evaluation cost. Hierarchical methods further reduce the number of evaluated angles by restricting fine-grid search to coarse candidate regions, as in combined MUSIC for coprime arrays [14]. For automotive radar, Kim et al. proposed adaptive grid-refinement MUSIC, using a coarse search over the full angular range followed by fine searches within selected regions of interest to reduce the search burden while retaining high-resolution DOA estimation [15]. Fang et al. developed blockwise mixed-precision and adap-tive precision strategies for RU-MUSIC, using low-precision sums within blocks and higher-precision accumulation across blocks [9]. These strategies allocate arithmetic precision within numerical computations. Hierarchical spectrum search offers a further opportunity to allocate precision across search stages, which serve different purposes. The coarse stage identifies candidate regions for subsequent refinement and therefore need not provide accurate DOA estimates, provided that the relevant spectral peaks remain within the retained regions. This suggests using low-precision arithmetic for coarse search to reduce computational cost, followed by high-precision evalua-tion within the selected regions for accurate DOA estimation. The key challenge is to coordinate coarse-stage precision, angular grid spacing, and candidate-window width so that arXiv:2610.29059v1 [eess.SP] 21 Oct 2026 2

computational savings are achieved while retaining the peaks needed for subsequent refinement. Motivated by the different accuracy requirements of coarse and fine searches, we propose precision-aware hierarchical MUSIC (PAH-MUSIC), an efficient DOA estimation frame-work that coordinates arithmetic precision, angular grid spac-ing, and candidate-window selection across search stages. Specifically, low-precision arithmetic is used in the coarse stage to identify candidate regions at reduced computational cost, followed by higher-precision evaluation on finer grids within these regions for accurate DOA estimation. To guide candidate-window selection, we analyze the effect of numer-ical perturbations on candidate locations and derive a condi-tional displacement bound for retained, correctly associated candidates. Furthermore, we develop a two-stage spectrum-search cost model and derive the optimal coarse-grid spacing and a condition for cost reduction under prescribed precision weights and window coefficients. Finally, simulations show that coarse searches using FP8, FP16, or FP32 followed by FP64 refinement achieve DOA root-mean-square errors close to those of two-stage MUSIC using FP64 at both stages, while reducing the weighted spectrum-evaluation cost by 28.36%– 44.66%. PAH-MUSIC coordinates stage-wise precision, angular grids, and candidate windows in MUSIC spectrum search. A conditional displacement bound relates local numerical perturbations to window coverage. For two-stage search, the continuous model yields an optimal coarse step and a cost-gain condition at fixed precision weights and window coefficients. In the tested separated two-source scenario over −10 to 15 dB, the selected FP8, FP16, and FP32 coarse-search configurations with FP64 refinement achieved RMSE close to FP64 two-stage search while reducing weighted spectrum-evaluation cost by 28.36%–44.66%. 6
