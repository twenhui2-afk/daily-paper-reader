## ABSTRACT 

A vendor may claim that some rule from a predeclared menu—a score threshold or a union of K score intervals—lets its selective classifier keep a fraction κ of inputs while erring on at most a fraction α of them. Refuting such an existential claim means ruling out every permitted rule at once. The intersection–union test (IUT) does this without a multiplicity correction, but its sample cost as the menu grows, and how close that cost is to the best valid audit, are not captured by the level argument. We characterize both. For a fixed interior risk target α, menus of 

K intervals and a risk gap γ, in the small-gap, large-menu regime of our theorem the minimax sample size at each fixed coverage floor κ ∈ (0 , 1) is K(1 − κ)/(κγ 2)

up to logarithmic factors, and the exact IUT attains it: menu richness costs K

times the abstention odds , even though validity costs nothing. The lower bound holds for audits that know the score distribution; the upper bound extends to any menu of VC dimension d. We then explain the linear cost. Every certificate that never rejects while some rule looks feasible on the audit data—the IUT, Bonferroni, DKW and uniform-convergence bounds—pays it even at laws where √K/γ 2

samples suffice, because the best-looking rule is optimistically biased. We then identify when the gap can and cannot be avoided: against constant-risk alternatives a reference-free audit reaches the testing scale, including the square-root rate on the sign family in its stated small-gap regime, whereas over a rich class with unknown heterogeneous risk the linear cost is unavoidable up to a logarithm. In simulation with K = 4 , the raw menu cost changes 7.7-fold between κ = .5 and 

.95 ; after dividing by the abstention odds and the theorem-motivated localized-VC logarithmic normalization used in Figure 1, the two endpoint values differ by about 5%. Held-out model families show that the same feasible-mixture mechanism is not confined to synthetic constructions: across three contemporary VLM judges, all 60 split-specific confidence-gate families have positive exact-hiding windows and 28/30 contracts selected on audit-A remain hidden on untouched audit-B; on the cleaner multi-annotator Polaris target, hiding persists on all 20 splits but is much narrower and transfers in 3/10 resplits. Frozen image and multimodal classifiers separately show boundary behavior consistent with the exact IUT guarantee and strong power relative to the practical certificates we compare. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Before a selective classifier is deployed—in procurement, certification or a regulatory feasibility check—its vendor often claims that it can be configured to meet a standard: some permitted accep-tance rule retains at least 90% of inputs while erring on at most 10% of the accepted ones. The menu of permitted rules is fixed in advance, for example thresholds on a confidence score or unions of a few score intervals. An auditor with fresh labeled data who wants to refute the claim cannot test one rule of its choice: showing that one rule fails leaves the vendor free to pick another. The null hypothesis is a union over the menu, and a rejection must rule out every rule at once. This is the dual of risk control and Learn-then-Test, which certify one rule selected by calibration (Chow, 1970; El-Yaniv and Wiener, 2010; Geifman and El-Yaniv, 2017; Bates et al., 2021; Angelopoulos et al., 2024; 2025). The intersection–union test (IUT) is the natural audit: reject when every rule fails its own valid test. Because a true claim has one fixed witness rule, no multiplicity correction is needed (Berger, 1982;

## 3 THE ABSTENTION -ODDS LAW 

For an arbitrary menu we place the power requirement on alternatives that are robustly infeasible: 

Pγ (A) = {P : rP (A) ≥ α + γ for every A ∈ A with cP (A) ≥ κ − γ}.

Reading the law. The menu cost K(1 − κ)/(κγ 2) is a cost of power , not of validity: the level proof pays nothing for |A| , yet a richer menu gives the vendor more ways to place the abstained region where the audit sample happens to contain few errors. Part (i) holds for any uniform-convergence certificate with radius of order pd/n ; the endpoint DKW certificate (radius of order K/ √n) and policywise Bonferroni (of order pK log M/n over M cells) have weaker guarantees, and part (iii) uses the Clopper–Pearson bounds’ adaptation to small proportions. The logarithmic gaps and constants remain open; at κ = 1 /2, with the constants of our proof, the new lower bound exceeds the elementary Ω( √K/γ 2) obstruction from Proposition 3 only for small γ and large K. The next two sections explain why the cost is linear in K and when an audit can avoid it.

## 7 RELATED WORK AND LIMITATIONS 

Risk control, Learn-then-Test and Pareto testing certify selected rules (Bates et al., 2021; Angelopou-los et al., 2024; 2025; Laufer-Goldshtein et al., 2023); model falsification, subgroup auditing and PAC verification address adjacent goals (Müller et al., 2025; Cherian and Candès, 2024; Mutreja and Shafer, 2026). Intersection–union validity is classical (Berger, 1982; Berger and Hsu, 1996; Lehmann and Romano, 2005); for a fixed number of normal means the IUT is the likelihood-ratio test and non-monotone tests can be uniformly more powerful (Lehmann, 1952; Berger, 1989), at the price of non-monotone rejection regions that can yield counterintuitive inferences (Perlman and Wu, 1999). Moment-inequality and intersection-bound tests (Andrews and Soares, 2010; Chernozhukov et al., 2013; Romano et al., 2014; Bugni et al., 2015) ask whether some parameter satisfies a set of inequalities; with many inequalities, as in reality checks (White, 2000; Hansen, 2005), the cost is logarithmic in their number (Chernozhukov et al., 2019) because that null is an intersection, whereas ours is a union. Inference on winners corrects the selection bias of an empirical optimum (Andrews et al., 2024), the bias that EFC certificates do not correct; sequential tests of whether some arm lies below a threshold address a related existential question (Kaufmann et al., 2018); confidence sequences give anytime versions of the IUT (Howard et al., 2021) (Appendix G). Universal inference gives reference-free composite tests (Wasserman et al., 2020); recent work on testable hypotheses and safe anytime-valid inference provides broader characterizations and tools for composite testing (Larsson et al., 2026b; Ramdas et al., 2023). In property testing, unions of d intervals are testable from O(√d) samples when the input distribution is known but need Ω( d/ log d) distribution-free (Kearns and Ron, 2000; Balcan et al., 2012; Blais et al., 2021); related interval and flat structure also appears in identity testing of structured distributions (Diakonikolas et al., 2015), and optimal risk can be estimated faster than learned (Kong and Valiant, 2018). Minimax L1-distance estimation and tolerant distribution testing exhibit related linear-over-log sample-complexity phenomena (Jiao et al., 2018; Canonne et al., 2022). Recent work studies calibrated uncertainty for VLM judges and releases the score-token features used in our auditing application (Kumar et al., 2026). Our null is a union of convex sets of laws with ratio-type constraints, losses are noisy, and an unknown input distribution alone does not force the linear scale (Theorem 4), whereas unknown conditional risk can force it on the heterogeneous class of Theorem 5. The Supplementary Note relates the one-observation geometry to convex-set testing (Goldenshluger et al., 2015). 

Limitations. Losses are binary, the contract has one coverage floor, and data are i.i.d.; distribution shift is outside our scope. For each fixed coverage floor, the interval-menu bounds are worst-case and match up to logarithms in the stated small-gap, large-K regime; the lower bound is asymptotic in K;the sharp testing–estimation spectrum and the constant-risk result are proved on sign families; an Ω( d)

bound for every VC-d family is open. Theorem 4 is an existence result with an untuned tester. The natural-family and VLM-judge studies are finite-target mechanism diagnostics, not instance-specific minimax lower bounds for Theorem 5; the six VLM gates are illustrative and use released benchmark outputs rather than production traffic. For a fixed policy A, let c(A) = P (S ∈ A) and r(A) = P (L = 1 | S ∈ A). Then TA ∼

Let UA be a one-sided (1 − ζc) exact upper bound on c(A) and LA a one-sided (1 − ζr ) exact lower bound on r(A), with ζc + ζr ≤ δ.G.1 PROOF OF PROPOSITION 1If the existential null is true, choose a fixed population witness A∗ satisfying c(A∗) ≥ κ and 

r(A∗) ≤ α. This witness depends on the population, not on the sample. If the procedure rejects, then either UA∗ < κ ≤ c(A∗), or UA∗ ≥ κ, in which case A∗ participates in the minimization and rejection forces LA∗ > α ≥ r(A∗). The two one-sided bounds fail with probabilities at most ζc

and ζr, respectively. A union bound over these two events gives at most ζc + ζr ≤ δ. No event is required to hold simultaneously for all searched policies. The equal split used in our experiments is therefore a convenience rather than a requirement; any predeclared allocation is valid. More generally, a fixed-policy joint confidence region for (c(A), r (A)) can replace the rectangular pair whenever its simultaneous failure probability is at most δ and the corresponding coverage/risk projections are used in the same screening logic. 

Proposition 10 (Anytime-valid existential certificate) . For each fixed A, replace (UA, L A) by one-sided confidence sequences (UA,t , L A,t ) whose anytime failure probabilities are at most ζc for coverage and ζr for risk. Reject at the first time t for which min A:UA,t ≥κ LA,t > α . Under the existential null, the probability of ever rejecting is at most ζc + ζr ≤ δ, again without a multiplicity correction over A.

G.2 PROOF OF PROPOSITION 10 If the existential null is true, fix a population witness A∗. Let Ec be the event that the coverage upper confidence sequence ever falls below the true c(A∗), and let Er be the event that the risk lower confidence sequence ever rises above the true r(A∗). By assumption, their probabilities are at most ζc and ζr, respectively. If the anytime procedure ever rejects while neither event occurs, then UA∗,t ≥ c(A∗) ≥ κ, so A∗ is in the screened family, while LA∗,t ≤ r(A∗) ≤ α, contradicting rejection. Hence ever-rejection is contained in Ec ∪ E r and has probability at most ζc + ζr ≤ δ. The argument requires simultaneous-in-time validity only for the one fixed population witness, not for all searched policies. 

Why a one-policy split-sample comparator is not a valid existential-null test. Selecting one A

on one split and rejecting that policy on an independent split proves only that the selected member is infeasible. The null is a union over all A ∈ A , so another member may still be the population witness. A valid split-sample existential test would still need to reject every potential witness (or otherwise control the union-null logic), restoring the structural problem addressed by the IUT.
