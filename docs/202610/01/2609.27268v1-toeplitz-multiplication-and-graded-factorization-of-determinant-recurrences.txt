# Toeplitz multiplication and graded factorization of determinant recurrences

Abstract 

Toeplitz matrices are matrices whose entries are constant along each diagonal. When only finitely many diagonals are nonzero, the determinants of successively larger matrices obey a fixed linear recurrence: each new determinant is a fixed linear combination of finitely many preceding ones. We ask whether the recurrence for a complicated band can be built from recurrences for simpler factors, and show that it can. Multiplying two finite banded Toeplitz matrices reproduces the expected product throughout the interior, with discrepancies only near two opposite corners. Shifting the factors relative to the main diagonal redistributes these boundary discrepancies, and the different shifts account exactly for the pieces from which the full determinant recurrence is assembled. For several factors, all allowed shifts are described by a finite system of linear inequalities, giving a systematic decomposition of the recurrence. This viewpoint also leads to a recursive construction that works directly with polynomial coefficients, without solving for their roots. When a factorization into bounded-degree pieces is supplied, a valid recurrence can be constructed using essentially a linear number of arithmetic operations in the number of coefficients that must be output. A five-diagonal example shows how a sixth-order recurrence is assembled from two tridiagonal Toeplitz factors together with two boundary contributions. 

Keywords: Toeplitz matrix; determinant recurrence; finite-section product; exterior power; com-posed product; symbolic computation.

Since Va and Vb are both two-dimensional, the three summands have dimensions 1, 4, and 1. The middle summand accounts for the four modes coming from the termwise product of the two order-two recurrences, while the two one-dimensional summands account for the two remaining modes. We call these summands sectors ; more generally, a sector records how the index of an exterior power is distributed among the factors. This observation suggests replacing a single recurrence attached to a factor by the entire graded family of recurrences obtained from all exterior-power indices. We develop this point of view in two parallel languages. On the recurrence side, a polynomial core is assigned a graded recurrence profile ,namely the list of recurrence polynomials corresponding to all exterior-power indices. Multiplication of polynomial cores becomes a graded convolution: entries with the same total index are combined, and their roots are combined by pairwise products. On the matrix side, multiplication of finite Toeplitz sections is exact in the bulk and fails only through the two corner defects. Opposite Laurent shifts of two factors leave their product unchanged while moving defect rank from one end to the other. We call this shift operation recentring ; its integer shift parameter will be interpreted as a 

boundary charge . The admissible recenterings turn out to be in bijection with the graded sectors. 

1.1 Relation to previous work 

Several ingredients used below are classical. Widom’s determinant formula expresses banded Toeplitz determinants through fixed-cardinality products of zeros of the generating polynomial [2]. Alexandersson placed these roots in a broader recurrence framework for banded Toeplitz minors and showed that Widom’s formula is a Schur-polynomial specialization [3]; see also the later skew-Schur treatment of banded Toeplitz minors by Maximenko and Moctezuma-Salazar [4]. Thus the subset-product roots and the exterior- power decomposition themselves are background rather than new claims here. On the matrix side, the product of finite Toeplitz sections is governed by Widom’s finite-section product identity, in which the discrepancy from the Toeplitz section of the product symbol is expressed through two Hankel (constant-antidiagonal) boundary terms [5]. This identity and the associated finite- section viewpoint are standard in Toeplitz theory; see, for example, [6, 7]. Algebras generated by Toeplitz matrix sequences have also been studied from a finite-dimensional asymptotic perspective [8]. Our finite-band use of the product identity is therefore not a new product mechanism: the new point needed below is the exact support and rank bookkeeping and its coupling to recurrence sectors through Laurent recentering. The five-diagonal case has an extensive earlier literature. Closed formulas and generating functions for five-diagonal Toeplitz determinants were given by Marr and Vineyard [9]; factorization through tridiagonal Toeplitz matrices was used by Diele and Lopez [10]; and homogeneous pentadi-agonal determinant recurrences were studied, among others, by Jia, Yang, and Li [11], in addition to Sweet’s classical recurrence [12]. Our use of this case is different: it exhibits the full recurrence 3as a canonical 1 + 4 + 1 graded sector decomposition and identifies the two one-dimensional sectors with extreme boundary charges. Finally, the pairwise-root product operation used below is classical; Section 2 introduces the terminology and notation used here. The near-linear algorithm used in Section 8 is due to Bostan, Flajolet, Salvy, and Schost [13]. Against this background, the contributions of the present paper are the multiplicative calculus of graded recurrence profiles, the realization of its sectors by recentered finite-section products and boundary charges, the linear-inequality description of all multi-factor charges, and a pruned balanced recursive algorithm with output-sensitive complexity bounds (cost measured relative to the size of the recurrence that is produced). The main results are as follows. First, the graded recurrence profile of a product core is obtained from the profiles of the factors by a multiplicative convolution using pairwise products of roots; Section 2 introduces the precise notation. Second, denote the support intervals of a and b by [ −ra, s a]and [ −rb, s b], respectively. The finite-section defect for these two banded symbols is supported in two opposite corners, with ranks min( ra, s b) and min( sa, r b).

Third, shifting the two factors in opposite Laurent directions produces a finite family of product representations of the same symbol; these recenterings realize exactly the graded recurrence sectors and transfer corner-defect rank between the two boundaries. Fourth, for an arbitrary number of factors, the admissible shifts are the integer points of a bounded polytope described by explicit linear inequalities. Finally, the same factorization calculus gives an output-sensitive recursive algorithm. For a known factorization into bounded-degree polynomial cores over a characteristic-zero field, the arithmetic cost of constructing the expanded recurrence is linear in its output size up to polylogarithmic factors. A balanced tree also gives a linear bound on the total amount of recurrence data retained during the computation, with an explicit constant in the exactly halved case. The five-diagonal case provides a useful prototype. Two tridiagonal Toeplitz factors contribute four modes through their ordinary determinant recurrences, while two one-dimensional boundary sectors supply the remaining modes. The resulting degree-six recurrence polynomial recovers Sweet’s classical recurrence [12] and makes the 1 + 4 + 1 decomposition transparent. The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the graded recurrence profile and proves its multiplication law. Section 3 gives the exact finite-section product formula and the corner-rank calculation. Section 4 identifies recentring with boundary charge and matches charges with recurrence sectors. Section 5 works out the quadratic-core pentadiagonal prototype. Section 6 treats arbitrary products and the bounded polytope of admissible charges. Section 7 records the resulting recurrence- profile algebra. Section 8 develops a pruned factor-tree algorithm and proves its output-sensitive complexity bounds.

Remark 8.9 (What the speedup does and does not say) . Theorem 8.8 assumes that a useful factorization is already available over the coefficient field. Finding such a factorization may dominate the computation. Over Q, coefficient heights can also grow rapidly, so the field-operation count does not by itself control bit complexity. For rational input, a natural exact implementation reduces the profile arithmetic modulo several primes that avoid the input denominators and then reconstructs the rational output coefficients by Chinese remaindering and rational reconstruction; this standard modular strategy limits intermediate coefficient swell [14, Chapter 5]. The number and size of the required primes, however, depend on height bounds for the output coefficients. Establishing such bounds for the present recurrence polynomials lies outside the scope of this paper. Also, if all roots of Q are already available in the working field, direct enumeration of the N

Widom modes followed by a fast product tree is itself quasi-linear in N . The factorized algorithm is most useful as a root-free construction over the original field for structured symbols whose factors have small degrees or reusable graded profiles. Table 1 is therefore a size illustration, not a software benchmark; implementation comparisons depend on coefficient heights, arithmetic representation, and the generic algorithms selected by a computer algebra system. 

# 9 Concluding remarks 

Multiplication of banded Toeplitz data has two complementary finite-dimensional descriptions. Finite sections multiply correctly away from the boundaries and acquire two explicit corner defects, while multiplication of polynomial cores becomes a graded convolution of exterior-degree recurrence 20 profiles. Recentring by opposite Laurent shifts links the two pictures: it preserves the bulk symbol, transfers defect rank between boundaries, and enumerates the graded recurrence sectors. For several factors, the cumulative shifts across internal cuts form a lattice polytope whose integer points are precisely the multi-index sectors. The same factorization calculus is algorithmic. Given a bounded-degree factorization over a characteristic-zero field, pruning to the target exterior degree and using a balanced factor tree yields an ˜O((DS

)) arithmetic construction of the expanded recurrence, without forming the (DS

)-dimensional compound matrix. Thus, up to logarithmic factors, the arithmetic work matches the output size; the two edge degrees are covered by direct coefficient-rescaling formulas. Natural next questions include optimal factor-tree selection for highly nonuniform degrees, bit complexity and coefficient growth over arithmetic fields, and the cost-benefit tradeoff of first obtaining a useful factorization. Structurally, it remains to understand how much of the unperturbed Toeplitz determinant sequence can be recovered directly from the recentered almost-Toeplitz product families, and to extend the boundary-charge picture to controlled finite corner perturbations and the boundary-state formalism of [1].
