# NS 3R: A knowledge-driven nonconvex square-root regularization framework for joint group sparsity and structured smoothness 

Wanling Xie a, Hu Yang b* 

> aCollege of Mathematics and Statistics, Hunan University of Technology and Business, Changsha, Hunan 410205, China

Abstract 

Structured sparse learning is a leading paradigm for high-dimensional data modeling, as it enables rigorous integration of domain-validated structural priors into the learning process. Among these priors, pre-defined grouped structure and piecewise smoothness are ubiquitous in real-world applications. However, most existing structured regularization methods are built on squared loss and convex penalties, rarely jointly model these two complementary structural priors, and suffer from two key limitations. Their optimal tuning parameters are sensitive to the unknown noise level 𝜎 due to the squared loss. Meanwhile, convex penalties induce inherent estimation bias and severe over-shrinkage of large magnitudes. To address these issues, we propose a knowledge-driven Nonconvex Structured Smooth Adjustment for Square-Root Regularization (NS 3R), which unifies the 𝜎 -free square-root loss with a joint nonconvex ℓ2,𝑞 penalty (0 < 𝑞 < 1) to simultaneously encode group sparsity and structured smoothness priors. Theoretically, we establish rigorous non-asymptotic error bounds for the NS 3R estimator under a mild (2, 𝑞 )-group restricted compatibility condition. Computationally, we develop an efficient double-screened IRLS with thresholding (DS-IRLS-th) algorithm, which adopts a nested double-screening strategy to reduce computational cost and improve numerical stability, with provable global convergence for the resulting nonconvex optimization problem. Extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world data demonstrate that NS 3R consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods in finite-sample settings. 

Keywords: Structured sparse learning, Square-root regularization, Nonconvex ℓ2,𝑞 regularization, Structured smoothness, Global convergence 

1. Introduction 

High-dimensional sparse learning serves as a fundamental methodology in modern statistics and applied mathematics. The explosive growth of data dimensionality has created great challenges for statistical modeling and inference across a wide range of real-world scientific domains, including compressed sensing [1], genome-

Throughout this work, we adopt the standard Gaussian noise assumption, where 𝜀 has zero mean and unknown covariance 𝜎 2 𝐼 𝑛 . A core property of high-dimensional scenarios is that the dimension 𝑝 is far greater than the number of observations 𝑛 

(i.e., 𝑝 ≫ 𝑛 ), which renders conventional model selection and estimation techniques ineffective. Building on the sparse learning framework widely used for high-dimensional data, structured sparse learning has emerged as the standard approach to address the limitations of traditional model selection methods under the 𝑝 ≫ 𝑛 setting. It moves beyond the entry-wise sparsity induced by the Lasso-type procedures [9] by leveraging inherent structural priors of the sparse signal we aim to recover, the most common and practically valuable of which are pre-defined group sparsity and fused sparsity. For group sparsity, the most widely used method is the group Lasso [10], which adopts a convex ℓ2,1 penalty to induce group-level sparsity by encouraging elements in the same group to be either all non-zero or all zero simultaneously. Despite its popularity, the convex penalty brings excessive shrinkage on large non-zero coefficients resulting in non-negligible estimation bias, and tends to produce overly dense solutions leading to sub-optimal recovery performance in practice. To address these limitations, a range of nonconvex group penalties have been proposed, including the widely used group SCAD [11, 12, 13], group MCP [12, 13], and capped folded concave penalties [14, 15]. Also, the nonconvex ℓ𝑟,𝑞 regularization has drawn extensive attention for its superior flexibility in modeling group sparsity [4, 6, 16], with its general form given by

As shown in [4, 6, 16], the nonconvex ℓ𝑟,𝑞 penalties achieve a more favorable trade-off between sparsity induction and estimation bias reduction than convex alternatives, and empirical evidence from their simulation studies further shows that ℓ𝑟,𝑞 regularization with 𝑟 = 2 outperforms its general ℓ𝑟,𝑞 counterparts in most cases. Fused sparsity for ordered features is another widely used structural prior in high-dimensional statistical modeling. The foundational method for this prior is the fused Lasso [17], which adds a total variation penalty 2to the standard Lasso for modeling piecewise constant signal patterns over ordered features. It can adaptively identify contiguous blocks of identical coefficients without pre-specified group information, and has been widely adopted in signal processing, genomic analysis, and image denoising. Despite its popularity, the fused Lasso has clear limitations that restrict its practical utility. Its core design is tailored to capture step-function patterns rather than smooth local variations, which not only incurs a tremendous computational cost as the dimension increases, but also renders it fundamentally unsuitable for signals with smoothly varying coefficients. To tackle these limitations, multiple extensions have been developed. The Smooth Lasso [18] replaces the original ℓ1

fusion penalty with a squared ℓ2 term to better capture gradual smooth shifts in coefficients, whereas the Spline Lasso [19] draws on cubic spline smoothing ideas to model more flexible coefficient trajectories. Subsequent works have further extended this framework in model development, theoretical analysis and applied extensions [20, 21]. Nevertheless, most existing structured regularization methods focus only on either group structures or ordered structures, and cannot simultaneously incorporate both, which are ubiquitous in real-world applications. Nearly all the structured sparse learning methods discussed above are built on the squared loss function. A critical limitation of these penalized least-squares is the challenge of regularization parameter tuning, as the theoretically optimal penalty values are closely tied to the unknown noise level 𝜎 . Accurate estimation of 𝜎 is notoriously difficult in high-dimensional regimes, introducing practical hurdles and a heavy tuning burden in practical use. To address this critical issue, the square-root regularization framework was originally proposed. The classic square-root Lasso [22] replaces the squared loss with a square-root loss function, enabling scale-independent parameter tuning as the optimal regularization parameter no longer depends on the unknown noise level. This 𝜎 -free tuning property has motivated a series of extensions to structured sparse settings. The group square-root Lasso [23] extends the framework to group-level feature selection; the square-root fused Lasso [24] adapts the framework to capture local constant signal patterns; the SSASR model [5] combines the square-root loss with a convex ℓ2,1 penalty to jointly model group sparsity and piecewise smoothness. Recent studies have further advanced this line of work by refining statistical theory and developing more efficient numerical algorithms [25, 26, 27, 28]. However, the vast majority of existing square-root regularized methods adopt convex penalty terms, leading to unavoidable estimation bias and sub-optimal recovery performance. To the best of our knowledge, no existing work has proposed a nonconvex square-root regularization framework that unifies group sparsity and structured smoothness priors. Meanwhile, this challenging nonconvex optimization problem lacks systematic theoretical guarantees and efficient convergent algorithms. Motivated by the aforementioned limitations in existing literature, we develop a novel nonconvex structured smooth adjustment for square-root regularization (NS 3R) framework, which combines the square-root loss function with a joint nonconvex ℓ2,𝑞 penalty for high-dimensional linear regression. It simultaneously encodes pre-defined group sparsity and structured smoothness priors in the sparse signal, while alleviating the common 3drawbacks of 𝜎 -dependent tuning and over-shrinkage from convex regularization. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows. 

• Based on a joint ℓ2,𝑞 penalty, we propose a unified nonconvex square-root regularization framework to fill a critical gap in structured sparse learning. This framework enables joint modeling of group sparsity and structured smoothness while retaining the inherent 𝜎 -free tuning property of square-root regularization. It further mitigates the inherent over-shrinkage and estimation bias of conventional convex regularizers, and thus achieves more reliable recovery of weak signals in high-dimensional regimes. 

• For the NS 3R estimator, we derive non-asymptotic Oracle inequalities under one of the mildest regularity conditions in existing literature. These results provide reliable theoretical guarantees for its practical application, and ensure stable finite-sample performance in high-dimensional regimes. 

• To solve the challenging nonconvex, nonsmooth, and non-Lipschitz optimization problem, we develop an efficient double-screened IRLS with thresholding (DS-IRLS-th) algorithm. The proposed algorithm not only comes with a rigorous proof of global convergence under mild initial value conditions, but also reduces computational cost and improves numerical stability via its nested double-screening design. Comprehensive numerical experiments on both synthetic and real-world data further validate its fast convergence and favorable performance in 𝑝 ≫ 𝑛 settings. The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we present the nonconvex structured smooth adjustment for square-root regularization (NS 3R) model and analyze its key theoretical properties. In section 3, we propose a double-screened IRLS with thresholding (DS-IRLS-th) algorithm admitting provable global convergence. In section 4 and section 5, we conduct simulation studies and an application to a microarray gene expression dataset to demonstrate the superior finite-sample performance of the proposed method compared with competing approaches. We conclude with final remarks in section 6. All technical proofs are deferred to the Appendix.

3.2. Double-screened IRLS with thresholding algorithm 

The objective function 𝐹 (𝛽 ) is nonconvex, nonsmooth, and non-Lipschitz, so most existing nonconvex algorithms either fail to guarantee convergence, or only ensure subsequential convergence under stringent con-ditions. Before investigating the finite-sample performance of the NS 3R, we detail the parameter selection proce-dure for our algorithm. All numerical experiments are implemented in R and run on a personal desktop (Intel Core i7-14700K, 3.40 GHz, 64 GB of RAM). We describe the general experimental setup below. We set the sample size 𝑛 = 250, the dimension 

𝑝 = 1000, the number of groups 𝐽 = 100, and the size of each group 𝑇 𝑗 = 10 for all 1 ≤ 𝑗 ≤ 𝐽 . The coefficients within each active group are set to a constant value of 3, while all coefficients in inactive groups are fixed to 0. The covariates (𝑋 1, 𝑋 2, . . . , 𝑋 𝑝 ) are independently generated from the standard normal distribution 𝑁 (0, 1), and the response vector 𝑦 is generated by

21 5. An application to gene expression microarray analysis 

We apply our analytical framework to a gene expression dataset previously studied in [36]. This dataset has been deposited in the European Bioinformatics Institute (EMBL-EBI) Array-Express repository, ID: E-MEXP-1618. It contains expression microarray profiles of 22815 probe sets and bone biopsies from 84 women, with raw data normalized via the RMA method as detailed in [36]. For our analysis, bone mineral density (BMD) is the outcome of interest, quantified as hip T-scores from the participants and standardized to have zero mean and unit standard deviation. Gene expression levels are analyzed on a log2 scale. To incorporate domain-specific prior knowledge of bone metabolism into the model, we group all genes according to two sources of biological evidence. These include functionally validated bone metabolism-related genes from published studies [2, 3], and well-characterized bone metabolic pathways including bone resorption, osteoblast differentiation and Wnt signaling cascades. We discard only the group of unannotated genes while retaining all other biologically informed grouping information, and sort all genes by their marginal importance relative to the bone density outcome. This strategy integrates prior biological insights into the leaning process, and improves the biological interpretability of the feature sets identified by our penalized regression model. To illustrate the advantage of incorporating the structured smoothness prior, we compare our proposed NSR approach with two competing methods. These are the square-root fused Lasso (srfl) [24] and the external information tuned (xtune) Lasso [3]. For xtune Lasso, we use the xtune and glmnet [37] packages in R, and construct the external information matrix 𝑍 based on the predefined gene grouping structure. Each column of 

𝑍 is a binary indicator for a specific group, with entries set to 1 for genes in the corresponding group and 0 otherwise. Regularization parameters for all methods are selected via the SCV-BIC criterion [38] to ensure consistency and fairness of the comparison. We randomly split the full dataset into a training set containing 80% of the 84 total observations (68 samples) and a held-out test set with the remaining 20% (18 samples). We fit all methods on the training data, and evaluate their predictive performance on the test set. This random split procedure is repeated for 100 independent simulations. Table 2 reports the mean number of selected genes and mean PMSE, with corresponding standard deviations in parentheses. Across all simulations, NS 3Rconsistently selects a substantially smaller feature set than both xtune Lasso and srfl, with a markedly lower standard deviation that demonstrates superior stability in feature selection. Correspondingly, NS 3R achieves the lowest mean PMSE and outperforms both competing methods. This indicates that the sparse, stable feature sets identified by NS 3R yield better predictive performance, while the larger, inconsistent gene sets selected by xtune Lasso and srfl f ail to deliver comparable improvements in prediction accuracy. Notably, genes frequently selected by NS 3R with a selection frequency of ≥ 30% across simulations are well-validated regulators of bone metabolism, and include core components of the Wnt signaling pathway such as DKK1, SOST, and WIF1. These core genes are robustly identified across all 100 simulations, confirming 22 that NS 3R can stably and reproducibly identify biologically meaningful signals relevant to bone metabolism. In comparison, xtune Lasso and srfl select a larger number of genes with lower selection frequencies. Most of the low-frequency genes selected by these competing methods have no validated functional link to bone metabolism, showing that these methods are more likely to capture false-positive features in high-dimensional data. In summary, these results demonstrate that NS 3R achieves a favorable balance among sparse feature selection, predictive performance, and biological interpretability in high-dimensional genomic data analysis. By incorporating a structured smoothness prior adapted to the inherent grouping structure of genes, NS 3R provides a robust and biologically meaningful approach for feature selection in high-dimensional gene expression studies.

6. Conclusions and future works 

In this work, we develop the nonconvex structured smooth adjustment for square-root regularization framework (NS 3R) for high-dimensional structured sparse learning. It couples a joint nonconvex ℓ2,𝑞 penalty with a 𝜎 -free square-root loss to simultaneously encode group sparsity and structured smoothness priors. This design mitigates the inherent over-shrinkage estimation bias from convex regularizers, and the dependency of tuning parameter selection on the unknown noise level 𝜎 , which greatly facilitates the work of parameter tuning. We establish rigorous non-asymptotic estimation and prediction error bounds for the NS 3R estimator under a mild regularity condition, providing reliable theoretical guarantees for its application in 𝑝 ≫ 𝑛 settings. For implementation, we develop an efficient double-screened IRLS with thresholding (DS-IRLS-th) algorithm for the resulting nonconvex, nonsmooth and non-Lipschitz optimization problem, with a rigorous proof of its global convergence under mild initial value conditions. Its nested double-screening design implements a 23 support-shrinking strategy, which guarantees a rapid reduction of computational complexity across iterations. Numerical experiments show that the algorithm converges within few iterations, with a runtime below 1 second for moderate-sized datasets. Comprehensive experiments on synthetic and real-world gene expression datasets confirm that NS 3R delivers consistently better finite-sample performance than state-of-the-art regularization methods. In our future work, we will extend the proposed framework to accommodate more flexible structural priors and complex data settings. We will generalize the structured sparsity-inducing penalties to enable bi-level selection, enhancing its robustness against mis-specified grouping structures. We will also explore a broader class of nonconvex penalties, including group SCAD, group MCP and capped folded concave penalties, to achieve more accurate signal recovery in high-dimensional regimes. Further, we will extend the framework to graph-structured data to model label dependency across nodes via oriented incidence matrices, beyond simple piecewise smoothness assumptions. These extensions will provide a unified tool for high-dimensional data modeling in bioinformatics, signal processing, financial econometrics, image processing and other related application areas.
