> YUQIN GUO AND DONGMEI ZHANG
> Abstract. We prove that the complete flag manifold SU( m)/T m−1, with m≥3 and its standard complex structure, admits no invariant Hermitian metric with constant mixed Chern curvature when the coefficient of the holomorphic sectional curvature is nonzero. This extends the preceding nonexistence result for a particular Wallach metric on SU(3) /T 2to all invariant Hermitian metrics and to complete type-A flags of arbitrary dimension. Curvature evaluations on holomorphic root spheres contradict a necessary trace identity, covering both K¨ ahler and non-K¨ ahler invariant metrics and excluding both zero and nonzero curvature constants.

1. Introduction and statement of result 

Complex geometry harbours a long–standing open problem concerning classification of com-pact Hermitian manifolds endowed with constant holomorphic sectional curvature. In the K¨ ahler case, it is a well established fact that every compact (or complete) K¨ ahler manifold of constant holomorphic sectional curvature belongs to the family of complex space forms. In other words, their universal covers are one of the standard models: complex projective space CP n, complex Euclidean space Cn, or complex hyperbolic space CH n.For non-K¨ ahler metrics g, the curvature tensor R fails to satisfy the symmetry identities valid in the K¨ ahler framework. Consequently, the holomorphic sectional curvature H alone cannot recover full information encoded within R. Nevertheless, the following statement remains widely conjectured to hold for compact manifolds. 

Conjecture 1 ( Constant Chern holomorphic sectional curvature; original formulation ) . Let 

(M n, J, g ) be a compact Hermitian manifold with n ≥ 2 and H ≡ c. If c̸ = 0 , then g is K¨ ahler; if c = 0 , then g is Chern flat, namely R ≡ 0.

Note that the compactness assumption is necessary for the validity of the conjecture, without it there are counterexamples in the noncompact case. Compact Chern flat manifolds have been classified by Boothby [4] as all the compact quotients of complex Lie groups equipped with left invariant metrics. Moreover, compact Levi-Civita flat Hermitian threefolds have been classified by Khan-Yang-Zheng in [14], but for n ≥ 4, the classification remains an open question. In complex dimension n = 2 , combined contributions from Balas-Gauduchon [2, 3] (1985), Sato-Sekigawa [23] (1990), and Apostolov-Davidov-Muskarov [1] (1996) have confirmed Con-jecture 1. For n ≥ 3 , the first substantive result towards this conjecture originates from Davidov-Grantcharov-Muskarov [12]. Their work shows that CP 3 is the unique twistor space admitting constant holomorphic sectional curvature. More recently, Chen-Chen-Nie [5] verified Conjecture 1 for locally conformally K¨ ahler manifolds satisfying c ≤ 0. Tang [24] validated the conjecture under an extra hypothesis that the metric is Chern-K¨ ahler-like, meaning its Chern curvature Rc obeys all K¨ ahler symmetry rules. Zhou-Zheng [36] obtained another confirmation for compact balanced threefolds with vanishing real bisectional curvature a condition introduced in [32], which is marginally stronger than the condition encoded by Hc. Their argument yields Chern-flatness for such manifolds. Chen–Zheng [8] verified this conjecture for several Lie–Hermitian classes and compact bal-anced BTP threefolds. Chen–Tang [9] studied other Chern–Ricci contractions, and Tang–Wang [27] proved nonzero-constant rigidity for compact LCK metrics with constant kth-mixed curva-ture. The unrestricted conjecture remains open; zero mixed curvature requires separate treat-ment. Our theorem excludes both zero and nonzero constants on complete flags independently of either conjecture. Compact complex homogeneous spaces constitute a natural framework for studying the constant mixed-curvature condition. Their invariant Hermitian metrics are characterized via isotropy representations, and their first Chern class may be computed using homogeneous line bundles. Even so, mere transitivity does not guarantee that the mixed curvature is independent of tangent directions. On a complete flag manifold, the distinct root directions yield a finite set of geometrically distinguished directions, against which one may verify the constant-curvature condition.
