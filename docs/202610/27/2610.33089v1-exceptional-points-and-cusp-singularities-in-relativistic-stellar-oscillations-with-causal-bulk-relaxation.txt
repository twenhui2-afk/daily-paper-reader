Abstract 

Causal relativistic hydrodynamics promotes dissipative stresses to independent dynamical variables obeying first-order relaxation equations. Coupling such a channel to the second-order wave problem elevates the oscillation spectrum from a quadratic self-adjoint problem to a cubic non-Hermitian one, whose exceptional-point (EP2) curves terminate at an A3 cusp lying in the physically admissible transport domain (Λ > 0, De > 0). This geometry turns out to carry a sharp and transferable admissibility criterion: reaching a degeneracy at all requires ( vFR /v β )2 ≥ 1 + Θ( γ), where vFR and vβ are the frozen and β-equilibrated sound speeds and Θ is the minimum of the control ratio over the exceptional-point locus at radiative rate γ. For the weakly radiating compressive modes to which bulk viscosity couples, Θ ≃ 8and the condition reads vFR ≳ 3vβ —a condition on the equation of state alone, independent of the relaxation time, which the internally consistent nucleonic case misses by more than two orders of magnitude, and which no published dense-matter susceptibility examined here comes close to meeting. We regard that criterion, rather than the negative verdict it currently returns, as the transferable result of this work. Our central result is that this normal form is not merely an effective parametrisation but is exactly realized in a radiating boundary-value problem: a Pöschl–Teller wave equation coupled to an Israel–Stewart relaxation channel, formulated hyperboloidally so that outgoing (Siegert) conditions become endpoint regularity. We prove a sector-decomposition theorem— the complete quasinormal point spectrum of this open, infinite-dimensional problem, for the aligned profile and away from the discrete resonant couplings, is exactly a tower of cubic normal forms, one per overtone, with each radiative damping rate γm an output rather than a prescribed constant. We do not claim, and do not need, the absence of essential spectrum for a closed extension to a larger space. Completeness of the point spectrum is established by showing that regularity forces the Frobenius series to truncate, so the polynomial eigenfunctions exhaust it. The EP2 curve, the cusp and its migration law are therefore satisfied exactly by genuine Siegert eigenvalues. Exceptional points are certified by multiprecision bordered-Newton continuation, and the square-root splitting exponent is sampled over eight decades of detuning, the asymptotic fit 0 .50000 being taken over ϵ ≲ 10 −4,above which the subleading correction lifts the local slope to 0 .5096. We then break the exact solvability deliberately: under a controlled one-parameter family of viscosity-profile deformations—parity-preserving and leaving the endpoint indicial structure intact, not a generic perturbation of the operator—the fundamental exceptional point survives a finite window, annihilates with a partner born from a defective inter-sector crossing, and escapes into complexified parameters, converting the sharp overdamping transition into a smooth crossover. The persistence found in this controlled family shows that the exceptional-point skeleton is not solely a consequence of exact solvability, and we delimit the deformation class and the window in which it persists.

# Summary for Broad Readership 

When two neutron stars merge, they form a hypermassive remnant that oscillates violently, emitting gravitational waves before potentially collapsing into a black hole. Modern simulations show that the extreme temperatures inside this remnant create "causal bulk friction"—a type of viscosity that takes a finite time to react to the fluid’s motion. In this work, we show that incorporating this delayed friction changes the mathematical class of the vibration problem, and we exhibit an exactly solvable radiating system—a simplified wave equation with a relaxation channel, with energy genuinely leaking to infinity as it does in a real star—in which the new mathematics can be verified to twenty significant digits analytically, and reproduced to the same twenty by a direct numerical solve of the full operator. That solvable system is a model problem, not a star: it captures the spectral structure exactly, but the step to a fully relativistic stellar calculation remains to be taken. In that model system, instead of simply damping the oscillations, the friction forces the vibrational modes to collide and merge, creating structures known as “exceptional points” and “cusp singularities”—though, as we find below, ordinary neutron-star matter does not appear to reach the regime in which they occur. We then ask whether real neutron-star matter can reach the regime where these structures appear, and derive a sharp criterion for it. Applied to the best current calculations of viscosity from weak nuclear reactions, the answer is negative by a wide margin: ordinary post-merger matter falls short by more than two orders of magnitude. The criterion is nonetheless the useful output, since it can be applied to any candidate form of dense matter and shows that the structures would be reachable only where the matter is anomalously soft, as near a phase transition. 

# 1 Introduction 

The oscillation spectrum of self-gravitating relativistic fluids is traditionally formulated as a quadratic eigenvalue problem in the mode frequency [1, 2, 3]. In the absence of dissipation, the associated operator can be rendered self-adjoint under appropriate inner products, leading to a real spectrum for stable configurations and a well-defined modal classification [4, 1]. Recently, the non-Hermitian nature of gravitational-wave asteroseismology has received intense attention, particularly driven by the spectral instability of black hole quasinormal modes (QNMs) [5]. Pseudospectrum analyses have revealed that black hole ringdown frequencies are highly sensitive to small environmental or boundary perturbations [6, 7, 8, 9, 10]. This structural fragility can lead to the resonant excitation of modes and the generic emergence of exceptional points in the perturbed black hole spectrum [11, 12, 13]. The spectral instability in relativistic open systems has emerged as a fundamental feature, as widely discussed in recent literature (see, e.g., the comprehensive review by Shen et al. [14]). However, while non-Hermitian degeneracies 2in black holes are typically induced by external far-field modifications, this work asks whether comparable degeneracies can arise intrinsically, from internal causal transport rather than from external modifications. We identify the cubic mechanism in the stellar reduction, and we demonstrate the associated exceptional-point and cusp geometry exactly in an open radiating scalar model with an Israel–Stewart channel; whether the full relativistic stellar operator realises the same organisation remains open, and is stated as such throughout. We then determine the condition under which dense matter could reach that regime at all. Causal relativistic hydrodynamics modifies this structure in a fundamental way [15, 16]. In Israel–Stewart-type theories, dissipative stresses are promoted to independent dynamical variables obeying first-order relaxation equations [16, 17]. When coupled to the second-order Einstein–fluid system, this additional temporal order generically elevates the spectral problem from quadratic to cubic in the complex frequency [18, 19]. The resulting operator pencil is intrinsically non-Hermitian [4, 20, 21]. While the appearance of non-hydrodynamic relaxation modes in causal theories is well established [22, 23], the global geometric structure of the resulting stellar oscillation spectrum has not been analysed. In particular, the organisation of eigenvalues in parameter space and the possible emergence of non-Hermitian degeneracies remain unexplored in the context of relativistic stellar oscillations [24, 20]. The dimensionless cubic normal form governing relativistic stellar oscillations with a single bulk relaxation channel possesses a structured set of exceptional points (EP2), forming a critical curve in parameter space [20]. Moreover, this curve terminates at a higher-order degeneracy characterised by the simultaneous vanishing of the first and second spectral derivatives, satisfying the standard criteria for an A3 (cusp) singularity in the sense of Arnold’s classification [25, 26]. The spectral surface therefore exhibits universal local geometry with square-root splitting near generic exceptional points and cubic-root scaling at the cusp [27, 28]. These non-analytic features arise purely from the structural interplay between second-order gravitational–fluid dynamics and first-order causal relaxation. The aim of this paper is fivefold. First, we provide a systematic derivation of this spectral geometry at the level of the cubic normal form, including its open-system generalisation. Second— and this is the central result—we prove that the open normal form is exactly realized by a genuine radiating boundary-value problem: a hyperboloidally formulated wave–relaxation system with outgoing Siegert boundary conditions whose complete quasinormal point spectrum decomposes into a tower of open cubic polynomials, one per overtone, with radiative damping rates γm arising as outputs of the eigenvalue problem rather than prescribed parameters (section 7). Third, we certify the resulting exceptional points and the cusp numerically with multiprecision methods, at the level of rigour demanded by non-Hermitian spectral analysis. Fourth, we quantify the structural robustness of this skeleton under a controlled one-parameter family of transport-profile deformations that activates inter-mode coupling, locating explicitly the boundary of the parameter window in which the exceptional-point structure survives on the physical domain. Fifth, we derive a criterion, in terms of the frozen and β-equilibrated sound speeds alone, for whether a given form of dense matter can reach the exceptional-point regime, and evaluate it against current microphysical calculations (section 9.6). 

# 2 Causal relaxation and cubic spectral structure 

We consider a self-gravitating relativistic fluid described by the Einstein equations coupled to a causal dissipative hydrodynamic theory of Israel–Stewart type [15, 16, 17]. In such theories, dissipative stresses are promoted to independent dynamical variables obeying first-order relaxation equations [16, 22]. For definiteness, the analysis is restricted to a single bulk viscous channel [29, 30]. The bulk 3viscous pressure Π satisfies a relaxation equation of the form 

τ u α∇αΠ + Π = −ζ∇αuα, (1) where τ is the relaxation time and ζ the bulk viscosity coefficient [16]. The fluid four-velocity is denoted by uα.The equilibrium configuration is assumed static and spherically symmetric [1]. Linear perturbations are introduced in both the metric and fluid variables. The Einstein equations remain second order in time derivatives [1, 2], while the fluid equations derived from ∇μT μν = 0 are also second order in time when expressed in terms of the Lagrangian displacement [2, 31]. The relaxation equation, however, is first order in time [16, 17]. Consequently, after lin-earisation and Fourier decomposition with time dependence e−iωt , the coupled system acquires an additional factor of (1 − iωτ )−1 in the effective pressure perturbation [22, 18]. Because the gravitational–fluid subsystem is second order in time, multiplication by (1 − iωτ ) yields a cubic polynomial eigenvalue problem in ω [4, 32]. The resulting spectral equation can be written schematically as 

P(ω)ξ = 0 , (2) where P(ω) is a cubic operator polynomial in the complex frequency [4]. This elevation of the spectral degree is structural: it follows directly from the temporal differ-ential order of the Einstein–fluid–relaxation system and does not depend on specific microphysical details [18, 19]. The presence of the relaxation variable renders the operator pencil intrinsically non-Hermitian [20, 21], defining a universal framework whose spectral consequences resolve into a dimensionless normal form that captures these fundamental properties. 

# 3 Origin of the cubic spectral structure 

The cubic spectral structure is not a peculiarity of a specific stellar model, gauge choice, or background symmetry. Rather, it follows generically from the differential order of the coupled Einstein–fluid–relaxation system under minimal structural assumptions [16, 17, 4]. It is crucial to clarify that this elevation to a cubic pencil is a specific structural hallmark of extended transient thermodynamics (such as Israel–Stewart or Müller formulations) [16, 23], which enforce strict causality by promoting dissipative fluxes to independent variables governed by relaxation equations. This differs from alternative modern causal frameworks, such as the Bemfica-Disconzi-Noronha-Kovtun (BDNK) first-order theories [33, 34]. In BDNK-type approaches, causality and nonlinear stability are achieved without promoting the dissipative fluxes to independent dynamical variables, but rather through systematic field redefinitions (frame transformations) that absorb the non-equilibrium degrees of freedom into modified inertial parameters [19, 18]. We are careful not to overstate the contrast: “first order” in BDNK refers to the gradient expansion of the constitutive relations, not to the temporal differential order of the resulting evolution equations, which do contain time derivatives of the hydrodynamic variables and are in general second order in time, with gapped non-hydrodynamic modes of their own. Indeed Ref. [18] exhibits frame transformations of BDNK leading to equivalent local formulations with additional non-hydrodynamic degrees of freedom, one of which is third order in time. What is specific to the Israel–Stewart paradigm is therefore not the mere presence of extra temporal order, but the particular coefficient structure that a single scalar relaxation channel imposes on the resulting pencil—the structure analysed here.

3.2 Physical admissibility and open-system coefficient structure 

The cubic polynomial governing the stellar normal modes is not arbitrary; its structure is constrained by strict causal transport and the non-conservative nature of an open gravitational-fluid system. In a realistic astrophysical scenario it is the perturbation problem that is open: a static, spherically symmetric background does not radiate, but non-radial perturbations satisfying outgoing conditions lose energy to gravitational radiation, so the baseline hydro-gravitational operator, considered with those boundary conditions, possesses complex quasinormal mode 5(QNM) roots of the form Ω 0 = ±1 − iγ , where γ > 0 is the radiation damping rate. It must be stressed at the outset that in the genuine boundary-value problem γ is not a free algebraic parameter: it is an output of the nonlinear (Siegert-type) eigenvalue problem, each mode carries its own γm, and radiative and viscous dissipation do not in general factorise. For this reason, in the present section the following one-parameter family of cubics is introduced only as a definition —a candidate normal form whose status must be settled at the level of the full boundary-value problem. The tension between causality, stability, and sensible initial-value formulations in relativistic dissipative fluids has been the subject of rigorous recent formal developments [33, 34, 35]. This alteration is what raises the grade of the pencil to three. Crucially, the A3 cusp lies within the physically admissible parameter plane (Λ ∗ > 0, De ∗ > 0). This is not automatic: had the organizing center fallen at negative coupling or negative Deborah number, the singularity-theoretic structure would be a formal property of the polynomial family with no bearing on admissible transport. Because it does not, the versal unfolding of the cusp governs a region that thermodynamic admissibility permits, rather than a formal corner of the polynomial family; whether dense matter occupies it is a separate question, settled in the negative in section 9.6, and the non-trivial Riemann-surface topology of the spectrum follows from the coefficient structure that a single scalar relaxation channel imposes on the family (7)—not from 6parameter tuning, but not from the presence of causal relaxation alone either, since section 9 gives a cubic family with two real controls and no degeneracy whatever [25, 20].

5.1 Exceptional points and the discriminant locus 

For polynomial families, the condition ∆ = 0 is equivalent to the existence of a multiple root [4]. Therefore, the exceptional-point (EP2) curve derived previously coincides precisely with the discriminant locus of the cubic family. This establishes the EP curve as the global algebraic degeneracy set of the two-parameter family, rather than a sectorial artifact. It is worth situating this structure within relativistic hydrodynamics at large: collisions of hydrodynamic and non-hydrodynamic modes at critical points of complexified spectral curves are known to set the convergence radii of the gradient expansion in Israel–Stewart-type and holographic theories [40, 41]. The exceptional-point curve derived here is the real-parameter incarnation of that mechanism for a self-gravitating configuration, realised in section 7 by genuine Siegert eigenvalues and organised globally by the A3 center. 

5.2 Higher-order degeneracy and cusp condition 

A higher-order degeneracy occurs when, in addition to ∆ = 0, the gradient of the discriminant also vanishes, ∇∆ = 0. In the closed limit this reduces to the polynomial whose discriminant is given above. Note that the discriminant of the cubic in y is ∆ y = −∆, so the familiar real-coefficient sign rule appears here with the opposite sign convention. In terms of the physical modes: • Regions where ∆ < 0: all three roots are purely imaginary in Ω (three distinct real y), i.e. three non-propagating, overdamped modes. • Regions where ∆ > 0: one purely imaginary root together with a propagating pair symmetric about the imaginary axis (one real y and a complex-conjugate pair), i.e. one overdamped mode and one damped oscillatory doublet. Note that no root is ever real in Ω: in the dissipative regime every mode is damped, and the transition encoded by ∆ is between propagating and non-propagating behaviour, not between real and complex frequencies. Crossing the discriminant curve changes the algebraic multiplicity structure and reorganises the branching of the associated Riemann surface [20, 21]. The cusp marks the point at which the EP curve changes curvature and the square-root branching characteristic of generic exceptional points transitions to cubic-root behaviour. 7.3 Multiprecision certification of the exceptional points 

The pencil (41) is discretised by Chebyshev collocation in σ (no boundary conditions imposed; regularity is the outgoing condition). The operator is strongly non-normal: near the degeneracies the eigenvalue condition numbers reach O(10 14 ), and double-precision eigensolvers do not converge under resolution refinement there. Exceptional points are therefore located and certified as regular zeros of bordered augmented systems (Appendix B) solved by Newton iteration in 25–30-digit arithmetic. Two facts legitimate the digit counts quoted below. First, for the aligned profile the discretisation is not merely convergent but exact : by the Proposition and Lemma 2 the eigenfunctions are polynomials of degree m, with Lemma 1 supplying the invariance of PN

that makes this useful, T (s) leaves span {1, . . . , σ N } invariant, and Chebyshev collocation at

The exact solvability above rests on the alignment of the viscosity profile with the potential. The physically decisive question—the one raised by the generic non-factorisability of radiative and viscous dissipation—is what survives when this alignment is broken. We deform along a one-parameter family, and are explicit that this is a controlled sample of misalignment profiles rather than a generic perturbation of the operator: the deformation below preserves the parity symmetry σ → − σ, satisfies c(±1) = 1 and so leaves the indicial structure at the endpoints untouched, and couples only sectors of equal parity with ∆ m = ±2. An odd term would break parity, a profile not approaching unity at the endpoints could alter the Frobenius problem, and a multi-harmonic profile would activate wider couplings. What follows is therefore a statement about this family, not a theorem about generic perturbations—which is nonetheless considerably more than the fine-tuned case η = 0. We therefore deform

The global discriminant analysis shows that the parameter plane ( De, Λ) is partitioned by the curve ∆( De, Λ) = 0 into regions with distinct algebraic multiplicity structure, yielding a direct dynamical interpretation in the context of relativistic stellar oscillations. 

8.1 Mode classification away from degeneracy 

In the non-dissipative limit where both causal relaxation and viscous dissipation vanish ( De →

0, Λ → 0), the system reduces to a quadratic self-adjoint problem describing propagating gravitational–fluid oscillations with purely real stable frequencies [1, 2]. However, for a finite viscous coupling in the purely parabolic Navier–Stokes limit ( De → 0, Λ̸ = 0), the reduced operator becomes a quadratic non-self-adjoint dissipative system where the modes are intrinsically complex. For finite relaxation time ( De > 0), the full cubic spectral surface comprises three sheets: (i) Two sheets continuously connected to the non-dissipative oscillatory modes, (ii) One sheet associated with the non-hydrodynamic relaxation channel [17]. In this regime the operator remains diagonalisable and the dynamical classification of modes is stable under small parameter variations. 

8.2 Crossing the discriminant curve 

Crossing the curve ∆ = 0 corresponds to the coalescence of two eigenvalues and the formation of an exceptional point [20, 21]. At such points: • The algebraic multiplicity exceeds the geometric multiplicity, • The polynomial pencil, restricted to the relevant invariant sector, becomes defective—we assert this for the sector cubic and its companion linearisation, and for the discretised operator, not for a closed infinite-dimensional realisation, whose domain and norm this work deliberately does not fix, • The spectral projection onto individual modes ceases to be well defined. Dynamically, this implies that two previously distinct modes become inseparable under infinitesimal perturbations of transport parameters. In particular, when one of the merging branches is continuously connected to the relaxation mode, the crossing of the discriminant curve signals a reorganisation between: • A propagating gravitational–fluid oscillation, • A relaxation-dominated non-hydrodynamic mode. The classification into “oscillatory” and “relaxational” branches therefore ceases to be invariant across the EP curve. 

8.3 Cusp and change of branching topology 

At the cusp, where ∆ = ∇∆ = 0, three sheets meet tangentially and the local unfolding acquires cubic-root structure [25]: the eigenvalue splitting under transverse perturbations changes from |ϵ|1/2 at generic exceptional points to |ϵ|1/3 there. The spectral sensitivity to transport parameters is correspondingly enhanced, and the local mode structure reorganises according to the A3 geometry; both laws are quantified and verified numerically in section 9. 30 9 Spectral sensitivity and structural implications 

The existence of exceptional points and of a higher-order cusp singularity has direct structural consequences for the spectral problem [20, 25]. These consequences follow from the non-analytic dependence of eigenvalues on control parameters and are independent of detailed microphysical modelling [4]. 

9.1 Non-analytic spectral response 

In analytic spectral problems, eigenvalues depend smoothly on system parameters [4]. In contrast, near non-Hermitian degeneracies the dependence becomes non-analytic [20, 21]. As shown in the previous section, near a generic exceptional point the eigenvalue splitting satisfies

This behaviour reflects the local topology of the spectral surface and is intrinsic to the cubic non-Hermitian structure [20]. 9.2 Reorganisation of spectral sheets 

Away from degeneracies the three sheets admit a continuous labelling inherited from the non-dissipative limit. Along the exceptional-point curve two of them coalesce and exchange analytic continuation, the geometric multiplicity dropping below the algebraic one; at the cusp all three meet tangentially and the local Riemann surface acquires cubic-root topology [25, 20]. 

9.3 Structural consequence of causal transport 

The emergence of this singular geometry is not model-specific. It follows from a structural property: A second-order dynamical system coupled to a single first-order relaxation channel generically produces a spectral problem of cubic grade .The distinction is worth stating in one line, because the two halves of this paper rest on different halves of it: temporal order fixes the grade of the pencil; the coefficient structure fixes the exceptional-point and cusp geometry . The counterexample below shows that the first does not imply the second. It is important not to overstate what the degree count delivers. Temporal order fixes the 

grade of the pencil; it does not fix its discriminant, and a cubic family need not possess any real-parameter degeneracy at all. The one-parameter family λ3 + λ + 1 + a2 + b2, for instance, is cubic in λ with two real controls, yet its discriminant −27(1 + a2 + b2)2 − 4 is strictly negative for every ( a, b ) ∈ R2: no multiple root, no exceptional-point curve, no cusp. What produces the EP2 locus and the A3 organizing center is the specific coefficient structure that a single scalar relaxation channel imposes—the family (7)—and not the temporal order by itself. Within 

that family the exceptional-point curve and its terminating cusp are unavoidable features of the 31 parameter plane [16, 17], and section 7 exhibits an open boundary-value problem that realises the family exactly. In the limit De → 0, corresponding to vanishing relaxation time, the cubic structure collapses into a quadratic non-self-adjoint dissipative problem [47]. Within this parabolic framework, while the higher-codimension A3 cusp singularity is dissolved as the relaxation channel closes, secondary non-Hermitian degeneracies persist, retaining a regular second-order exceptional point (EP2) at Λ = 2 where the roots coalesce into a double defective eigenvalue Ω = −i. Finite causal relaxation therefore modifies not merely the damping rates but shifts the higher-order topological classification of the non-Hermitian spectral problem. 

9.4 Implications for relativistic stellar dynamics 

If a physical system explores regions of parameter space near the exceptional-point curve, the fractional-power branching implies enhanced spectral sensitivity to variations in bulk transport coefficients [29]. To verify whether realistic stellar configurations dynamically access such non-Hermitian degeneracies, the dimensionless normal form must be mapped to physical transport scales.

The condition number of the bordered Jacobian is 

κ2(J) = 5 .45 × 10 12 , 3.34 × 10 14 , 7.67 × 10 15 at N = 24 , 32 , 40 , (94) growing steeply with resolution, and it is this factor—not the residual alone—that sets the attain-able digit count. Because the discretisation is exact on the polynomial sector, the discretisation error vanishes identically and the entire deviation from the exact sector value is arithmetic; the forward accuracy therefore degrades with resolution , tracking κ2(N ) rather than improving with it. At d = 30, with residuals in the range 2 × 10 −29 –3 × 10 −28 , the bordered solve agrees with the exact sector value of s∗ to about 21, 20 and 18 significant digits at N = 24 , 32 , 40 respectively (and to about 22, 21 and 20 in ζ∗ 

> 0

), against local linearised forward-error estimates 

κ2∥F ∥/(∥J∥ | s∗|)—the relative normalisation used in the archived script—of 19–20, 18–19 and 17–18 digits. We call these estimates and not a-priori bounds: they come from the linearisation at the computed point, whereas a rigorous bound for a nonlinear system would require control of the Jacobian and its inverse over a neighbourhood, in the manner of Newton–Kantorovich, which we do not establish. The estimate thus tracks the observed accuracy to about one digit at every resolution, which is the sharpest statement available and is what identifies κ2 as the operative quantity. Agreement between resolutions is about 20 digits in s∗ for N = 24 versus 32 and about 18 for 32 versus 40—in each case equal to the forward accuracy of the coarser run, as it must be when the error is arithmetic and monotone in κ2. At fixed N = 24 the accuracy tracks the working precision one-for-one, again as the error model requires: 16, 21, 31 and 41 forward digits at d = 25 , 30 , 40 , 50, with residuals ∼ 10 −24 , 10 −29 , 10 −39 and 10 −49 . The twenty digits quoted in table 1 are therefore reproduced in full by the archived N = 24, d = 30 runs and are not a limit of the method; d ≥ 30 is used throughout because d = 25 already falls short of them. All digit counts in this paragraph are quoted to the nearest unit and were measured over five Newton seeds perturbed at the seventh digit: since the terminal residual sits at the roundoff floor of the working precision, the counts fluctuate by one to two units between runs and no more resolution than that should be read into them. Finally, at ζ0 = 0 the exact Pöschl–Teller frequencies s = −(m + 12 ) ± ik are eigenvalues of the 

discretised operator to working precision: for m = 0 , 1, 2, 3 at N = 24 and d = 30 the smallest singular value of the real 2 n × 2n embedding of T (s), evaluated at the exact frequency, lies between 10 −30 and 5 ×10 −29 in absolute terms, that is 10 −33 –10 −32 relative to ∥T ∥ ≃ 2×10 3—the level of the working precision. The individual values are not quoted, since at the roundoff floor they are not reproducible digit by digit. This is the numerical counterpart of the exactness of the collocation on the polynomial sector, and confirms that the certification measures arithmetic rather than discretisation error. branches_Ap_B.py ); the branch-identity test of section 7.4(i) ( eigenvector_overlap.py ); the numerical audit reported in Appendix B, including the condition numbers, Newton contraction factors and digit stability against N and the working precision ( audit_appendixB.py ); the symbolic verification of the local structure—the expansion (33)–(35) with its explicit deriva-tive values, the two counterexamples fixing the scope of the degree-counting argument, the compact forms (59), the reality of (24), the common factor (70) and the resonant divisibility (verify_local_structure.py ); the exact-arithmetic verification of the geometric simplicity of the crossings (63), carried out in three independent ways—symbolic, rational, and at the level of the discretised operator ( code/defective_crossings_proof/ ); and the figure generators ( make_ all_figures.py , make_fig4_robustness.py , make_fig5_postmerger.py ).
