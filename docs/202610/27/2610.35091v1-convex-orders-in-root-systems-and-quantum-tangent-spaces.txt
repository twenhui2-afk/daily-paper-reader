> ALESSANDRO CAROTENUTO, CHRISTOPHE HOHLWEG, AND PAOLO PAPI
> Abstract. We classify the reduced decompositions of the longest element w0of the Weyl group of a semisimple Lie algebra gfor which the corresponding Lusztig quantum root vectors span a quantum tangent space in Uq(g). We introduce a combinatorial condition on the convex order induced by a reduced decomposition, tangency, and prove that a reduced decomposition gives rise to a quantum tangent space if and only if it is tangent. The proof relies on a description of the coproduct of quantum root vectors in terms of the convex order, and on a projection in the right weak order attached to each positive root. Up to commutation, tangent reduced decompositions are obtained by concatenating the longest minimal coset representatives along a chain of cominuscule parabolic quotients; in particular there are none in types E8,F4and
> G2.

1. Introduction 

The choice of a differential calculus for a noncommutative algebra A, viewed as the alge-bra of some class of functions on a noncommutative manifold, is a foundational problem in the relatively young field of noncommutative differential geometry, and a key feature of the theory. This constitutes a fundamental difference from classical differential geom-etry, where a manifold comes equipped with its own de Rham complex. When a first order differential calculus Ω 1 on A is given, it always admits a unique maximal prolonga-tion to a differential calculus Ω • over A. In most of the concrete examples, this reduces the problem to the one of finding a good choice for Ω 1, as the right choice of a differential calculus turns out to be its maximal prolongation. There are however some notable ex-ceptions to this rule, such as Woronowicz’s bicovariant calculi for quantum groups [39]. In the setting of Drinfel’d–Jimbo quantum groups, and particularly for the quantum flag manifolds Oq(G/L S ) introduced by Soibelman [36], requiring covariance of the calculus allows for its local description through either the fundamental theorem of Hopf modules or its generalization to quantum homogeneous spaces provided by Takeuchi’s equiva-lence. An influential result in this line of research was established by Heckenberger and Kolb, who gave a q-deformation of the classical de Rham complex of the flag manifolds of Hermitian symmetric type [15], now known as the Heckenberger–Kolb calculi. These are generalizations of Majid’s left-covariant calculus for the Podle´ s sphere Oq(S2) [30], and are the essentially unique differential calculi which exhibit at the same time left covariance with respect to the coaction of Oq(G) and classical dimension of their local part. For these reasons, there is a widespread consensus that these objects are the right quantization of the classical de Rham complex of irreducible flag manifolds. This consen-sus has in turn been reinforced by the many results on their noncommutative differential

With most of the theory for quantum flag manifolds of irreducible type already well established, mathematicians have recently started to address the noncommutative dif-ferential geometry of quantum flag manifolds beyond the irreducible case, unveiling new interesting phenomena in quantum geometry. The authors exhibited two choices of a reduced decomposition of the longest element w0 of the Weyl group of sl n+1 for which the associated sets of quantum root vectors span quantum tangent spaces. They then showed that the associated first order differential calculi admit maximal prolongations of classical dimension. Following the same approach, the first author, ´O Buachalla and Pagani gave a quantization of the holomorphic calculus of the full flag manifold of type 

B2. In this case only one of the two reduced decompositions of w0 gives rise to a quantum tangent space [7]. It is important to note that not every choice of a reduced decomposition of w0 gives a basis of a quantum tangent space. This was the original motivation for the present paper, whose main goal is to understand the combinatorial properties of the reduced decompositions of w0 that give rise to quantum tangent spaces. At the level of the quantum coordinate algebra Oq(G), the notion of a quantum tangent space is very close to that of a right coideal in Uq(g). For this reason, one has to understand how the coproduct of the quantum root vectors of Uq(g) depends on the choice of a reduced decomposition. This problem is far from trivial, since Lusztig’s root vectors are defined by means of an action of the braid group B of W on Uq(g) by algebra automorphisms which are not coalgebra maps. The number of reduced decompositions of w0 grows quickly with the rank of g; so does the number of their commutation classes, which is what matters here, and whose growth is understood only up to bounds even in the relatively simple case of An [12]. For this reason, a direct approach—considering all the reduced decompositions of w0 and computing the coproducts of the corresponding quantum root vectors—is computationally prohibitive, and a more conceptual framework is needed. Uq(so 2n), while in [22] a coproduct formula for the quantum root vectors in type G2 is given. In [25] Kr¨ ahmer expressed the coproduct of quantum root vectors in terms of
