# Minimum orders of clique oddomorphism counterexamples 

Michal Čertík The World bank Group Washington, DC, USA 

michal.certik@gmail.com 

Research manuscript draft 17 October 2026 

Abstract 

An oddomorphism to Kn is a proper colouring with n colours in which every vertex is designated odd or even, every vertex has in each other colour class a number of neighbours of its own parity, and every colour class contains an odd number of odd vertices. We prove that an oddomorphism witness to Kn on at most n + 6 vertices contains a Kn minor with every branch meeting the witness’s specified odd set. The conclusion holds inside any graph containing the witness and therefore applies to weak oddomorphisms. The bound is sharp: for every n ≥ 8 the minimum order of a graph admitting a weak oddomorphism to Kn but having no Kn minor is exactly n + 7 . The extremal example is a fifteen-vertex graph with an oddomorphism to K8, obtained from the doubled theta graph of Kriesell and Mohr by adding one vertex adjacent to the seven roots; we give a structural proof that it has no K8 minor, including their counting argument for the rooted case. A separate technical companion records further results on related root-apex graphs, an attachment theorem and a bounded nine-colour statement, together with their computational provenance; none of it is used here. The results do not resolve the unrestricted clique-target problem.

21 Results and their scope 

The relation between parity-constrained graph homomorphisms and clique minors has two different quantitative aspects. One asks whether a sufficiently large complete target forces a prescribed clique minor without restricting the source. Another fixes the target and asks how small a counterexample can be. This paper studies the latter question through the excess |V (W )| − n of a witness to Kn.Roberson introduced oddomorphisms and their weak variant and related them to homomor-phism counts of parity constructions [10]. The general implication from an oddomorphism to containment of its target as a minor is false: Kristjánsson gives counterexamples, including com-plete targets [8]. His examples for the target Kn come from random 3-lifts of Kn, using a result of Drier and Linial on minors in random lifts [3]; they have 3n vertices and exist for all sufficiently large n [8, Section 3.2]. We determine the least order of such a counterexample exactly: it is n + 7 

for every n ≥ 8.In subsequent work, Kristjánsson refuted the Strong Roberson Conjecture itself and its immer-sion analogue [7, Theorems 1 and 14]. Those counterexamples concern non-complete targets and do not determine the minimum order considered here. 

Theorem 1.1 (Rooted excess six) . Let n ≥ 2. Suppose a finite simple graph F contains a subgraph 

W with an oddomorphism to Kn and specified odd set P . If |V (W )| ≤ n + 6 , then W has a Kn

minor with every branch set meeting P . In particular, the same model exists in F .

This formulation is slightly stronger than a host-order bound: vertices outside W do not count. In particular, a graph of order at most n + 6 with a weak oddomorphism to Kn satisfies the conclusion. A branch is only required to contain a specified odd vertex; it is not required to contain an odd number of such vertices, remain within a fibre, or have a prescribed colour. The word odd refers to the parity conditions of Section 2: on the number of neighbours of a vertex in each other colour class, and on the number of odd vertices in each colour class. It is unrelated to odd minors, which occur in the odd-minor variant of Hadwiger’s conjecture. There a graph contains an odd Kn minor if it has n vertex-disjoint trees, every two joined by an edge, whose vertices can be two-coloured so that the edges of the trees are bichromatic and the joining edges are monochromatic [4]. All minors in this paper are ordinary minors. 

Theorem 1.2 (Sharpness) . For n ≥ 8, let m(n) be the minimum order of a graph admitting a weak oddomorphism to Kn but having no Kn minor. Then m(n) = n + 7 . An extremal example can be chosen to admit an oddomorphism. In particular, m(8) = 15 .

The construction uses the theta-based two-vertex-fibre graph of Kriesell and Mohr [6, Section 3, pp. 8–9, immediately before Theorem 2]. Its rooted obstruction is theirs (their Theorem 2 states it as the failure of a rooted routing property); it already shows that a uniform rooted excess-seven theorem fails at n = 7 , and we include their counting argument in Lemma 5.3. The assertion needed for Theorem 1.2 is stronger and different: adjoining a vertex adjacent to the seven designated roots produces no ordinary K8 minor. The rooted obstruction excludes only the models in which the new vertex forms a singleton branch; an ordinary model may have a different singleton branch or put the new vertex into a larger branch. We exclude every model structurally in Proposition 5.4 using a spanning-model reduction, singleton saturation, and eight elementary cases. 

Relation to previous work. Three ingredients come from Kriesell and Mohr [6]: the doubled graphs Z(B); their routing theorem for graphs B on at most six vertices [6, Theorem 6, Section 5, p. 12]; and the doubled theta graph with completed root pairs, together with its rooted obstruction. 3We use the routing theorem only when the core of a witness consists of at most six double fibres. It is a statement about the graphs Z(B), not about arbitrary six-colour Kempe routing, and the companion gives direct proofs of these cases. Twin deletion and parity-preserving merging also occur in recent work [9, 5]; we make no priority claim for those general ideas. For the target K5, Neuen and Seppelt prove that the class of K5-minor-free graphs is closed under weak oddomorphisms [9, Theorem 5.22 in arXiv version 2]. Hence a weak oddomorphism to K5 forces a K5 minor whatever the order of the source, but this does not give the rooted conclusion of Theorem 1.1. Our contribution is the mixed-fibre excess-six theorem with its rooted conclusion, the exclusion of an ordinary K8 minor in the fifteen-vertex graph (Proposition 5.4), and the resulting exact value of 

m(n). A comparison with the primary sources cited here found no earlier statement of the threshold 

m(n) = n + 7 . That comparison was limited in scope and is not a certification of novelty. Roberson’s Question 7 asks whether, for every t, some complete target KN forces a Kt minor under a weak oddomorphism [10]. This broader problem remains open, as discussed by Neuen and Seppelt [9] and reaffirmed by Kristjánsson [7, Section 7]. A bound on the source order does not settle it. Nor do we determine the analogous minimum orders for n ≤ 7; for n = 5 the closure theorem of Neuen and Seppelt mentioned above rules out every counterexample. 

Organisation. Section 2 sets up witnesses and a root-preserving normalization. Sections 3 and 4 prove Theorem 1.1: the excess-five bound and the seven fibre patterns of excess six, including mixed fibres of sizes up to five. Section 5 constructs the fifteen-vertex graph, proves that it has no K8

minor and deduces Theorem 1.2. 

The technical companion. A separate companion document, whose section and statement numbers continue those of this paper, contains material that is not needed for Theorems 1.1 and 1.2: three extremal families found within a specified thirty-one-member family of root-apex graphs; a criterion for an even vertex attached to eight double fibres to force a K8 minor; a classification of when the apex of the three hard root-apex graphs can remain a singleton branch after one extra edge is added; and a bounded computer-assisted theorem for weak oddomorphism witnesses to K9 of order at most nineteen. For each statement it says whether the evidence is structural, certificate-backed or inherited.

# 4 The excess-six theorem 

We prove the positive statement with respect to the odd set of a specified witness. In particular, the root condition survives passage from a weak oddomorphism witness to its host graph. 

Theorem 4.1 (Excess six) . Let n ≥ 2, and let W ⊆ F be a specified oddomorphism witness for 

Kn, with specified odd set P ⊆ V (W ). If |V (W )| ≤ n + 6 , then W (and hence F ) has a Kn minor whose every branch set meets P . The branch sets are not required to respect the fibres. 

We use the support-tracked normalization lemma and the original-rooted excess-five theorem established earlier. The proof below supplies every additional fibre-pattern case at excess six. The only further general minor theorem used is the classical fact that a finite simple graph of minimum degree at least three contains a subdivision of K4.

Theorem 7.4 (Attachment theorem) . Assume (P7). Let A be a graph on {0, . . . , 7} and let

Remark 7.7 (Counting the exceptions) . The automorphism groups of the three hard graphs are determined as follows. • In 42232 = Θ(2 , 3, 3) the vertices 0, 1 are the only vertices of degree three. G.1 Three and four doubles 

For three doubles, B = K3. The branches {o1, e 2}, {o2, e 3}, {o3, e 1} have active internal edges and pairwise crossing edges e2e3, e 3e1, e 1e2.
