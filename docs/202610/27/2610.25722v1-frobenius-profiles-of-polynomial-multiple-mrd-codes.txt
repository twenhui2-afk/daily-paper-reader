A B S T R A C T 

We study Frobenius intersection profiles of maximum rank distance (MRD) codes arising from ordinary polynomial multiples over finite fields. We prove that every numerically admissible profile is realized within this family over every sufficiently large extension, using a root-exponent pattern independent of the extension degree. A factor-orbit gap formula provides the underlying structural description, while effective extension-degree bounds guarantee realization. We also establish a lower bound valid for all polynomial generators and show that the upper and lower bounds differ by only 𝑂 𝑞 (log 𝑛 ) along an infinite family of parameters. Finally, we characterize the generalized Gabidulin members and obtain non-generalized-Gabidulin realizations of every profile with initial loss at least two over infinitely many extensions.

1.1. Methods and relation to earlier work 

The numerical restrictions on Frobenius intersection sequences are known [1]. Our principal contribution is to prove their sufficiency within the polynomial family (1.1), together with effective bounds on the required extension degree. The structural tool is a factor-orbit gap formula for the degrees 

𝐷 𝑗 (𝑔 ) = deg lcm( 𝑔, 𝜎 (𝑔 ), … , 𝜎 𝑗 (𝑔 )) , 𝜎 (𝑎 ) = 𝑎 𝑞 .

It expresses these degrees through weighted gaps in Frobenius orbits of irreducible factors, including repeated and nonsplit factors. The intersection dimensions are recovered by 

𝑑 𝑗 (𝐶 𝑔 ) = max{0 , 𝑛 − 𝐷 𝑗 (𝑔 )} .

The truncation at zero is important: the observable profile need not determine the complete factor-orbit structure. Our construction uses generalized Moore evaluations on the short power-basis space 

𝑊 𝛼 = ⟨1, 𝛼, … , 𝛼 𝑛 −1 ⟩𝔽 𝑞 .

The evaluation framework is classical [1, 3, 4], as is the quotient of a generalized Moore determinant by the ordinary Moore determinant [5]. The important distinction is the domain of the determinant tests. The global Moore-exponent-set property requires nonvanishing on every independent tuple in 𝔽 𝑞 𝑚 ; for 𝑞 > 5, Bartoli and Zhou show that a fixed non-arithmetic-progression exponent set eventually fails that global property as 𝑚 grows [5]. Our MRD test requires nonvanishing only on independent tuples in 𝑊 𝛼 , where 𝑛 = dim 𝔽 𝑞 𝑊 𝛼 remains fixed while 𝑚 varies. Thus the realization theorem does not assert, or require, the global Moore-exponent-set property. The specialized determinant quotients give exact degree formulas on this restricted space and lead to effective extension bounds. Neri et al. established quantitative abundance results for MRD and Gabidulin codes using subspace-indexed determinant tests and degree counts [6]. Here we apply this viewpoint to prescribed Frobenius root patterns on short power-basis spaces, where specialized Moore quotients provide exact degree bounds and effective extension estimates. Generalized Gabidulin codes [3, 4] provide the natural comparison class; non-Gabidulin MRD constructions are also well established [7]. Using the intersection criterion of Horlemann-Trautmann and Marshall [8], we characterize the Gabidulin members by their Frobenius root chains and prove that every admissible profile with 𝛿 1 ≥ 2 has a non-generalized-Gabidulin realization over infinitely many extensions. Moreover, writing 𝑝 ≤𝑟 (𝑘 ) for the number of partitions of 𝑘 with largest part at most 𝑟 , complete realization gives exactly 𝑝 ≤𝑟 (𝑘 ) attainable profiles and at least that many semilinear rank-equivalence classes. This is a lower bound on equivalence classes, not a complete classification. The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the polynomial model and the necessary profile conditions. Section 3 develops the factor-orbit gap formula. Sections 4 and 5 establish complete realization and effective extension bounds. Section 6 proves the lower bound, while Section 7 treats the Gabidulin comparison.

## 8. Conclusion 

We established that every numerically admissible Frobenius intersection profile is realized by a polynomial-multiple MRD code over every sufficiently large finite extension. We derived a factor-orbit gap formula and obtained effective upper and lower bounds for the every-extension threshold, which differ by only 𝑂 𝑞 (log 𝑛 ) along an infinite family of parameters. We also characterized the generalized Gabidulin members of this family and established non-generalized-Gabidulin realizations of every profile with initial loss at least two over infinitely many extension degrees. Finally, we exhibited semilinearly rank-inequivalent MRD codes whose polynomial generators have identical complete degree hierarchies (𝐷 𝑗 )𝑗 ≥0.
