CROSS-RATIOS AND FIRST ORDER ORDINARY DIFFERENTIAL EQUATIONS

Abstract. We investigate the usefulness of the cross-ratio in the study of first order odes of the form y′ = Pnyn + Pn−1yn−1 + . . . + P1y + P0, (Pi)0⩽i⩽n ∈ K, n ⩾ 1, where K is a differential field of characteristic zero with algebraically closed field of constants. We discover along the way many differential algebraic identity between solutions to that equation. We also explain why the cross-ratio of any four solutions to such an equation is in general not constant when 

n ≥ 3 by exhibiting an obstruction which prevents its constancy when n ≥ 3. Finally to any separable polynomial Pφ(z) = zn+1 + φ1zn + φ2zn−1 + . . . + φn+1, n ≥ 2, over a differential field of meromorphic function on some domain of C with the usual derivation we associate a uniquely defined first order ode of the form y′ = Pnyn + Pn−1yn−1 + . . . + P1y + P0, (Pi)0⩽i⩽n ∈ K such that every root of Pφ(z) is a solution to the said differential equation.

We learn in the old treatises, cf. for instance [3, 5], that the cross-ratio of any four distinct solutions to a Riccati equation is a constant. This property is related to the theory of Vessiot-Guldberg-Lie algebras (Lie superposition) or what is the same in modern parlance to the question of internality to the constants of first order odes y′ = f (y) where f (y) ∈ K[y] and (K, δ ) is a differential field of characteristic zero with algebraically closed field of constants CK = ker δ, say.

In fact the Riccati equation y′ = P2y2 + P1y + P0 and its sister equations y′ = Q1y + Q0, y′ = R0

are the only first order ODEs with polynomial right hand sides which admit a Lie superposition formula aka are internal to the constants, see [4, §11.2] for the classical proof or [11, 16] for a modern algebraic proof. Above the Pi, Qj , Rk belong to K. Since the Riccati equation is the only first order y′ = f (y) where f (y) ∈ K[y] having such a property it is hopeless to search for a similar property for first order odes 

y′ = Pnyn + Pn−1yn−1 + . . . + P1y + P0, (Pi)0⩽i⩽n ∈ K, n ≥ 3, P n /= 0.

But rather than internality to the constants is it possible to exhibit differential algebraic relations between solutions to that last differential equation in some differential field extension which could possibly play the role of internality. One hint in that direction is given by considering the logarithmic derivative of the cross-ratio of any four distinct solutions to a Riccati differential equation with P2 /= 0. Then if we denote still by δ the extension of the derivation on K to a derivation on K(y1, y 2, y 3, y 4) obtained by setting y′

d log ( (y3 − y1)( y4 − y2)(y3 − y2)( y4 − y1) ) = 0.

It is through this route, i.e. by considering logarithmic derivatives of differences of solutions and their differences that we find differential algebraic relations for differential equations 

y′ = Pnyn + Pn−1yn−1 + . . . + P1y + P0, (Pi)0⩽i⩽n ∈ K, n ≥ 3, P n /= 0

which could play the role of the constancy of the cross-ratio for the Riccati equation, cf. .Theorem 2.2. One the other hand what exactly is preventing a generic given differential equation 

y′ = Pnyn + Pn−1yn−1 + . . . + P1y + P0, (Pi)0⩽i⩽n ∈ K, n ≥ 3, P n /= 0

from having a constant cross-ratio. We find in Theorem 3.1 the exact obstruction which pre-vents such an occurrence. This obstruction is expressed as a K-linear combination of certain determinants on the solutions which arise via the use of Schur functions. For the theory of Schur functions we refer to [14, p. 40]. Besides classically since the time of Schwarz [23] and Liouville [13], given a differential equation over a differential field (K, δ ) one seeks whether or not it admits solutions of a given type, for instance one looks for solutions algebraic over K or Liouvillian over K etc, see also [7] and [24] for modern undertakings of such problem. But what about inverting that type of problems? For instance for a given separable polynomial Pa = zn+1 + a1(x)zn + a2(x)zn−1 + . . . + an+1(x) of degree 

n + 1, n ≥ 2 over a differential field (K, ddx ) of meromorphic functions on a domain, and such that 

ker ddx = C, is it possible to find a differential equation of the 

y′ = Pnyn + Pn−1yn−1 + . . . + P1y + P0, (Pi)0⩽i⩽n ∈ K

such that all the roots of the polynomial Pa are at the same time solutions to the differential equation? Formulated as such the problem looks like some kind of Riemann-Hilbert problem. We show that given such a separable polynomial P there is exactly one unique such differential equation

Such a differential field extension is in general denoted K⟨w1, w 2, . . . , w m⟩. The underlying ring of a finitely generated differential field is called a finitely generated differential ring. An element of a finitely generated differential ring is called a differential polynomial. Among the finitely generated differential rings over K, in case m = 1 we have what is called the ring of ordinary differential polynomials denoted

Remark 4.6. Given a separable polynomial Pφ(z) with coefficients in K of the form (4.1) we have exhibited a uniquely defined differential equation of the form (4.18) such that every root of such a separable polynomial is a solution to that differential equation. Assume n ≥ 3 and in the notation above that Pn /= 0. Then it is interesting to investigate whether our differential equation (4.20) has a Liouvillian transcendental solution in a no new constant differential field extension 

L of K, see [22, p. 600] for the definition. Then according to [22, Th. 3.2] our differential equation (4.20) has at least n solutions algebraic of K and its number of algebraic solutions is finite, in case it admits a transcendental Liouvillian solution (solution in the restricted sense: in a differential field extension of with the same field of constants). So potentially our differential equation (4.20) may admit such a Liouvillian transcendental solution since the number of its algebraic over K solutions is at least (n + 1). More generally [22, Th. 3.3] gives precisely when such a transcendental Liouvillian solution should exist. Moreover they express when the number of algebraic over K, solutions to a differential equation of the form 

y′ = Pnyn + Pn−1yn−1 + . . . + P1y + P0, (Pi)0⩽i⩽n ∈ K, P n /= 0

is precisely n, and the differential equation has a transcendental Liouvillian solution, that differential equation in terms of its n algebraic solutions. Our constructions are different and do not intersect since in case of intersection our differential equation (4.20) would have to be a degree (n + 1) polynomial in y on its right hand side, which it is not. Finally in the construction of [22, prop. 3.6] the leading coefficient Pn must be non zero.
