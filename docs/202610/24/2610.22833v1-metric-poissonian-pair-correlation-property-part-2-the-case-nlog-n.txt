METRIC POISSONIAN PAIR CORRELATION PROPERTY PART 2: THE CASE n log n

BO TAN, ZHEN ZHANG, QING-LONG ZHOU 

Abstract. We prove that ( αn log n)n≥1 has Poissonian pair correlation for almost every real α, solving a Problem posed by Aistleitner, El-Baz and Munsch [Open Problem 3, Geom. Funct. Anal. 2021]. Our main contribution is a new four-point counting estimate with a fixed power saving over N 7/3 at tolerance N −1, valid on the ranges left after a first-moment and Borel–Cantelli argument. Its proof reduces the counting condition to the required proximity of an implicitly defined real root to an integer; a Fejér kernel majorizes this condition, and multidimensional van der Corput A- and B-processes supply the saving. A localized Mellin argument then yields the second-moment estimate needed for the metric conclusion. 

1. Introduction 

This is the second of two papers addressing the open problems on metric Poissonian pair correlation for the sequences n + log n and n log n posed in Aistleitner, El-Baz and Munsch [1, Section 9, Open Problems 2 and 3]. Our first paper [11] establishes the metric Poissonian property for n + log n and discusses the relation with uniform distribution and the background of both problems. The present paper completes the treatment of these two sequences by resolving the n log n case. Recall that a real sequence ( xn)n≥1 has Poissonian pair correlation (PPC) if 

RN (s; x) := 1

N #{1 ≤ m̸ = n ≤ N : ∥xm − xn∥R/Z ≤ s/N } −→ 2s (N → ∞ )for every s ≥ 0, where ∥t∥R/Z denotes the distance to the nearest integer. A real sequence (an)n≥1 is metric Poissonian if ( αa n)n≥1 has PPC for Lebesgue almost every α ∈ R.The main theorem of this paper establishes the metric Poissonian pair correlation property for the n log n case, thereby giving an affirmative answer to the related open problem mentioned above.

We first apply a first-moment estimate and the Borel–Cantelli lemma to show that the contribution of pairs ( n, n +h) with n ≤ N/ (log N )4 or h ≤ N/ (log N )4 tends to zero for almost every α along a suitable subsequence. For the remaining pairs, a localized Mellin argument bounds the second moment in terms of four-point counts on dyadic ranges, counting pairs of differences of ( n log n)n≥1 whose absolute difference is at most N −1. A general benchmark for these counts is provided by Bloom’s bound for the additive energy of convex sets [2, Theorem 2], which, after rescaling and discretization, yields an upper bound Oε(N 123 /50+ ε)for each such count. In our framework, the second-moment reduction gives, up to logarithmic factors, N −7/3 times the largest of these counts; see (3.7). To obtain power decay through this reduction, we therefore require a sharper counting bound with a fixed exponent strictly below 7 /3.
