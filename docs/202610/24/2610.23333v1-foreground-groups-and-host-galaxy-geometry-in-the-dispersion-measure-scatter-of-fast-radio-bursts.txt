Foreground Groups and Host-Galaxy Geometry in the Dispersion-Measure Scatter of Fast Radio Bursts 

Jiale Wang 1 and Taotao Fang 1

> 1Department of Astronomy, Xiamen University, Xiamen 361005, China

ABSTRACT Localized fast radio bursts (FRBs) show substantial scatter in dispersion measure (DM) about the mean cosmic DM–redshift relation, also known as the Macquart relation. Both foreground structure and host galaxies contribute to this scatter. In this work, we examine foreground tracers and host galaxy properties using a parent catalog of 136 localized FRBs with measured DMs and spectroscopic host redshifts. Among 77 FRBs within the Gfinder footprint, DM excess relative to a fixed Macquart mean increases with foreground group count excess, with a Spearman coefficient of 0.459. Adding the foreground term to a mean host baseline reduces the leave-one-out root mean square error (RMSE) and mean absolute error (MAE) in extragalactic DM by 13 .0% and 7 .5%, respectively. For 20 of these FRBs with optical host axis ratio measurements, adding a foreground term calibrated on the other 57 FRBs to the host shape model reduces the observer-frame leave-one-out RMSE from 76 .3 to 64 .0 pc cm −3 and MAE from 54 .2 to 50 .2 pc cm −3. And the in-sample residual standard deviation in the host rest frame decreases by 20 .2%. Idealized galaxy models based on the Milky Way electron density distribution produce DM changes with inclination comparable to the fitted amplitude under the adopted assumptions. 

Keywords: Galaxy groups (597) — Galaxy structure (622) — Intergalactic medium (813) — Radio transient sources (2008) 

1. INTRODUCTION At low redshift, a substantial fraction of cosmic baryons is thought to reside in diffuse ionized gas that is difficult to detect directly (J. M. Shull et al. 2012). Fast radio bursts (FRBs) probe this gas through their disper-sion measures (DMs), which trace the free-electron col-umn density along the line of sight (M. McQuinn 2014). The observed DM includes contributions from the Milky Way, intervening cosmic gas, and the host galaxy and lo-cal environment of the burst (E. Petroff et al. 2019). The Macquart relation describes the increase in mean cosmic DM with redshift, allowing localized FRBs to probe the cosmic baryon content (W. Deng & B. Zhang 2014; Z. Zheng et al. 2014; J.-P. Macquart et al. 2020). Localized FRBs show substantial scatter about this mean relation (J. Baptista et al. 2024), and the relative contributions of foreground structure and host environments to this scatter remain uncertain. Intervening cosmic structure introduces additional DM variations between sight lines. Studies of individual

FRB sight lines have inferred DM contributions from foreground halo gas ranging from small to substantial (S. Simha et al. 2021, 2023). At the population level, excess cosmic DM has been found to correlate with fore-ground galaxy overdensity, with a stacked excess ex-tending to megaparsec projected separations from fore-ground galaxies (M. Hussaini et al. 2025). Gas associ-ated with galaxy groups, clusters and filaments can also contribute to these variations (A. E. Lanman et al. 2026; J.-F. Mo et al. 2025). Cosmological simulations suggest that host contribu-tions can dominate the variance in extragalactic DM (W. Zhu & L.-L. Feng 2021). For disk galaxies, the host contribution depends on the ionized-gas distribu-tion, the burst position and the viewing inclination, with higher typical DMs expected for more inclined sys-tems (J. Xu & J. L. Han 2015). A study of DMs cor-rected for the Galactic disk contribution found a cor-relation with host specific star-formation rate, but no significant association with inclination (R.-N. Li et al. 2025). M. Glowacki et al. (2025) reported an associ-ation between optical host axis ratio and the absolute extragalactic Faraday rotation measure. More recently, L. Marnoch et al. (2026) reported a relation between arXiv:2610.23333v1 [astro-ph.CO] 16 Oct 2026 2inferred host DM and optical axis ratio using an ex-panded set of host-shape measurements. Their analy-sis subtracts the mean cosmic DM contribution without applying additional sightline-specific foreground correc-tions. Such foreground variations can also affect the scatter about the host-shape–DM relation and its pre-dictive performance. In this work, we examine foreground tracers and host-galaxy properties to investigate DM scatter about the Macquart relation. Section 2 describes the data, ob-servable definitions, and statistical methods. Section 3 presents the foreground group relation and assesses the additional value of foreground information for host-shape modeling and DM prediction. We discuss the physical implications and limitations in Section 4 and summarize our conclusions in Section 5.

4. DISCUSSION 4.1. Foreground Group Environments and Ionized Gas 

The association between group-count excess and DM links electron-column variations to foreground group en-vironments. The adopted 1 .5R180m aperture includes group interiors and outskirts, where intragroup gas, the circumgalactic media of member galaxies, and correlated intergalactic structure may all contribute. Stacked ther-mal Sunyaev–Zel’dovich measurements detect gas to ap-proximately 3 R500 around nearby groups (C. T. Pratt et al. 2021), while recent kinematic Sunyaev–Zel’dovich measurements provide complementary evidence for ex-tended ionized gas around massive groups (Y. Gong et al. 2026). These observations motivate including group outskirts when tracing foreground gas. The counting aperture nevertheless defines an environmental scale, not a mea-sured gas boundary. The intercepted electron column depends on halo mass, impact parameter, and gas distri-bution, whereas our count assigns equal weight to each intersected group. The fitted coefficient therefore de-scribes an average DM dependence on the adopted en-vironmental tracer; it does not assign the same physical electron column to every group or isolate the contribu-tion of intragroup gas. 4.2. Host Geometry and the Amplitude of DM Variations 

More inclined disks can present longer paths through ionized gas, providing a possible explanation for larger DM residuals toward smaller host axis ratios (J. Xu & J. L. Han 2015). To assess whether this effect can reach the fitted amplitude, we used Milky Way electron-density models as idealized galaxy templates. We com-bined the large-scale NE2001 distribution (J. M. Cordes & T. J. W. Lazio 2002) with the YT2020 hot-gas model (S. Yamasaki & T. Totani 2020), and repeated the calcu-lation with NE2025 (S. K. Ocker & J. M. Cordes 2026) replacing NE2001. For synthetic sources within each template, we varied the viewing inclination toward a dis-tant external observer while keeping the gas distribution and source positions fixed. As the optical axis ratio decreases from q = 0 .8 to 0 .2, our fitted relation implies a host-rest-frame mean DM increase of 204 .6 pc cm −3, with a 95% posterior inter-val of 114 .3–293 .3 pc cm −3. Over the same interval, the NE2001+YT2020 and NE2025+YT2020 templates give mean increases of 181–240 and 158–203 pc cm −3, respec-tively. These ranges span the adopted source distribu-tions and optical geometries (Appendix E). The compa-rable amplitudes support the physical plausibility of an inclination contribution under these assumptions. How-7ever, the templates are not matched to the gas densities, scale lengths, or burst locations of individual hosts, and optical axis ratio need not trace the geometry of their ionized gas. 4.3. Foreground and Host Contributions to DM Scatter 

Accounting for both foreground groups and host shape yields the lowest residual scatter and leave-one-out pre-diction errors among the four models tested for the 20 hosts. Foreground information alone does not im-prove the mean-host baseline, suggesting that unmod-eled host variation may obscure its predictive contri-bution. In this subset, more elongated hosts tend to lie along sightlines with smaller foreground group-count ex-cesses ( ρ = −0.297, nominal two-sided p = 0 .203). This weak trend may contribute to the strengthening of the host-shape relation after foreground correction. This empirical improvement does not uniquely par-tition the DM variance into foreground and host com-ponents. The corrected residuals still include cosmic structure untraced by the group catalog, and the opti-cal measurements sample only part of the diversity of FRB hosts. Moreover, leave-one-out evaluation retains choices of observables and functional forms informed by the inspected sample. Larger, independently assembled FRB samples with more complete foreground catalogs and uniform host imaging can test whether these prediction gains per-sist across different foreground environments and host shapes. Combining FRB electron columns with thermal Sunyaev–Zel’dovich pressure measurements could con-strain the density and thermal state of group-associated gas (J. B. Mu˜ noz & A. Loeb 2018). Spatially resolved host spectroscopy and gas kinematics, together with burst offsets, could test whether optical shape traces the relevant ionized-gas path.

CONCLUSIONS In this work, we compiled a parent catalog of 136 local-ized FRBs with measured DMs and spectroscopic host redshifts, and examined foreground tracers and host-galaxy properties to investigate scatter about a fixed Macquart mean relation. The foreground group analy-sis uses 77 non-PRS FRBs within the Gfinder footprint. Of these, 20 have optical host axis-ratio measurements; the other 57 provide a disjoint foreground-calibration sample for the host analysis. Our main results are as follows: 1. The foreground group-count excess within 1.5R180m is positively correlated with DM excess ,with a Spearman coefficient of ρ = 0 .459. The fit-ted observer-frame coefficient is k = 45 .1 pc cm −3

per unit group-count excess, with a 95% posterior interval of 27 .8–62 .3 pc cm −3. Adding this term to the mean-host baseline reduces the leave-one-out RMSE and MAE in observer-frame DM exgal by 13 .0% and 7 .5%, respectively. 2. For the 20-host sample, foreground correction using the separate 57-FRB calibration strength-ens the correlation between the host-shape in-dicator 1 − q and host-rest-frame DM residuals: the Spearman coefficient increases from 0 .620 to 0.815. Here, q = b/a is the optical minor-to-major axis ratio. Relative to the axis-ratio model alone, adding the foreground term reduces the host-rest-frame in-sample residual standard devi-ation by 20 .2% and the observer-frame leave-one-out RMSE and MAE by 16 .2% and 7 .4%, respec-tively. 3. As the optical axis ratio decreases from 0 .8 to 0 .2, the fitted host-shape relation implies a host-rest-frame mean DM increase of 204 .6 pc cm −3, with a 95% posterior interval of 114 .3–293 .3 pc cm −3.Models based on the Milky Way electron-density distribution produce inclination-induced DM changes of comparable amplitude, supporting the physical plausibility of an inclination contri-bution under the adopted assumptions. FOREGROUND TRACERS AND COMPARISONS We compared foreground counts and galaxy-density measures with observer-frame DM excess , using the subset of the 131 non-PRS FRBs supported by each catalog. Table B1 summarizes representative results. B.1. Foreground Counts 

Gfinder coverage was defined by occupied HEALPix pixels at Nside = 1024, using catalog entries before membership and redshift cuts (K. M. G´ orski et al. 2005). For each FRB, 10,000 group-control positions were sampled without replacement from occupied Nside = 128 pixel centers within the coverage mask. Galaxy counts used 10,000 shared control positions sampled with replacement from the corresponding galaxy mask. Observed and random counts used identical foreground-redshift cuts and apertures. Covered sightlines without an eligible foreground interval were retained with zero observed and random counts. For 2MRS, we adopted the published Galactic-latitude and extinction cuts (J. P. Huchra et al. 2012). Cluster comparisons applied the PSZ2 survey mask to PSZ2, MCXC, and their combined catalog ( Planck Collaboration et al. 2016). 2 Each mask used 10,000 shared random sightlines drawn uniformly in solid angle. Aperture centers were required to lie inside the mask, without a minimum aperture-area coverage requirement. Since individual-galaxy halo radii were not available in the adopted tables, we used a common projected physical aperture of 300 kpc for the Gfinder and 2MRS galaxy-count comparisons. For 2MRS groups and the cluster catalogs, we used apertures of 1, 1 .5, and 2 times their respective R200 and R500 . We retained the published 2MRS group and MCXC radii and derived PSZ2 R500 from the catalog SZ mass proxy using Planck18. The combined catalog retained MCXC entries and added PSZ2 objects without an exact MCXC identifier match; its selection remains heterogeneous. Unless otherwise stated, count excesses subtract the median random count. B.2. Large-Scale Structure Tracers 

For DR1 LSS, we used BGS, LRG, and ELG samples over 0 .10 ≤ z < 0.40, 0 .40 ≤ z < 1.10, and 1 .10 ≤ z < 1.60, respectively, in shells of width 0.05 and 20 Mpc projected physical apertures. Shell contrasts were weighted galaxy counts divided by the expected counts from survey randoms, minus one. Catalog WEIGHT values were used, with random normalization performed separately by tracer, sky region, and shell. WISE ×SuperCOSMOS used shell edges at z = 0 .005 , 0.05 , 0.10 , . . . , 0.40 and 10, 20, and 30 Mpc apertures. Shell contrasts were mask-corrected surface densities divided by the mean density in valid control apertures, minus one, using 10,000 random positions. For both catalogs, angular apertures were evaluated at shell midpoints. Valid measurements required at least one complete foreground shell and support in every eligible shell within the catalog redshift range. Aperture centers had to lie inside the mask, with at least 80% area coverage. DR1 LSS additionally required an effective random count of at least 25, defined as the expected count squared divided by its weighted random-sampling variance. Shell contrasts were summed with weights equal to the increase in the Macquart mean across each shell. For 2M++, we used the galaxy-density reconstruction of J. Carrick et al. (2015), smoothed on 4 h−1Mpc. Using trilinear interpolation, we evaluated the local density at z = 0 .005 for sources with zFRB > 0.010, and a column measure proportional to R δg (r)[1 + z(r)] d r, where r is comoving distance. Column integrals began at z = 0 .005 and retained the 0.05 foreground-redshift buffer. We limited the integration to 113 h−1Mpc, leaving three smoothing lengths inside our adopted common radial limit of 125 h−1Mpc, and excluded sightlines through the Galactic zone of avoidance, where missing galaxy data were filled using neighboring regions. ROBUSTNESS TESTS D.1. Foreground Relation 

The 95% posterior interval for k remains above zero under the tested changes to Galactic subtraction, the cosmic mean, counting aperture, and random-background reference, including imaging-matched controls, and when restricting the sample to z ≤ 1 (Table D1a). To test sensitivity to spatial variations in imaging conditions, we matched random sightlines to each FRB using four DR9 brick-level quantities: z-band galaxy detection depth, z-band PSF size, Galactic reddening E(B − V ), and the natural logarithm of one plus the point-source density in deg −2 (A. Dey et al. 2019). Candidate positions satisfied the Gfinder coverage requirement and had valid measurements from survey-primary bricks with positive z-band exposure counts. Within the northern and southern regions separately, each quantity was centered on its candidate median and divided by 1 .4826 times its median absolute deviation, using the standard deviation if this scale was zero. For each FRB, we drew 10,000 controls with replacement from the 2,048 nearest candidates by Euclidean distance in this standardized space. We recalculated the median random background and refitted the foreground model, keeping the observed counts unchanged. With these imaging-matched controls, the leave-one-out RMSE and MAE are 129 .6 and 107 .3 pc cm −3, respectively, both below those of the mean-host baseline. E. INCLINATION-DEPENDENT DM MODELS Following J. Xu & J. L. Han (2015), we used Milky Way electron-density models as idealized galaxy templates to assess the amplitude of an inclination effect. We retained the thick disk, thin disk, spiral arms, and Galactic center of NE2001 (J. M. Cordes & T. J. W. Lazio 2002), excluding Solar-neighborhood structures, individual clumps, and voids, and used the corresponding NE2025 components for comparison (S. K. Ocker & J. M. Cordes 2026). Both templates included the flattened and spherical YT2020 components to represent the model galaxy’s hot gaseous halo, with gas metallicity Zgas = 0 .3Z⊙ (S. Yamasaki & T. Totani 2020). All components shared the same galactic center, and the disk planes were aligned. We kept the gas-density parameters at their published values.
