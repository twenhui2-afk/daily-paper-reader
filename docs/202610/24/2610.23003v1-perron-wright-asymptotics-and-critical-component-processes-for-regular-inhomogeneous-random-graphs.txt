Wright’s constants play two exact roles in the critical Erd˝ os–R´ enyi graph: they govern the asymptotic enumeration of connected labelled graphs with fixed surplus [38, 39], and they appear in the factorial densities of Aldous’s limiting size and surplus process [3, 4, 22]. The classical evolution theory locates the critical window and describes the sizes and complexity of its components [20, 13, 27, 28, 24]. These enumerative and probabilistic descriptions are joined by more than a numerical coincidence: the Wright enumerator, combined with vertex selection and component isolation, produces the factorial densities of the Brownian excursion process [33, 23, 22, 36]. The earlier Erd˝ os–R´ enyi work [34] develops this enumerative route directly for the homogeneous critical graph. It starts from exact formulas for ordered component tuples, proves finite graph factorial moment bounds uniformly in the factorial order, and uses them to obtain the local marked component process and ordered ℓ2 

> ↓

convergence. It provides the homogeneous prototype for the present work. The question addressed here is what replaces the Wright enumerator and its exact amplitude when a constant edge weight is replaced by a Perron critical inhomogeneous kernel. For a general kernel, the answer is not obtained by inserting an effective variance into the Erd˝ os–R´ enyi formulas. The critical direction is selected by the positive Perron eigenfunction of the mean operator, candidate components carry nonconstant type profiles, and the finite population calculation depends on how those types are assigned. The phase transition for inhomogeneous kernels was developed by Bollob´ as, Janson and Riordan [11]; PERRON–WRIGHT ASYMPTOTICS 3

rank one critical models were subsequently studied in [8, 9, 37, 35, 10]. More recent universality theorems give global ordered or metric space limits in several finite type and graphon settings [6, 7]. These results do not identify the connected graph partition function with kernel weights at fixed surplus, its exact Perron deformation of Wright’s constants, or the fixed size factorial densities derived from that enumerator. Starting from a regular critical kernel on countably many types, Theorem 3.9 constructs a single graph sequence with growing finite supports, integer type counts, and exact critical normalization for which all the fixed size factorial and marked process limits hold simultaneously. The construction is substantive because the constants in the finite type estimates depend on the truncation level; the dimension cannot simply be allowed to grow inside those estimates. Theorem 3.10 also identifies what happens without exact spectral normalization: a displacement of the empirical Perron root on the n−1/3 scale produces the corresponding deterministic shift of the critical window parameter. 1.2. Relation to previous work. The classical relation among sparse connected graph enumeration, Brownian excursion area, and critical random graph components was developed in [38, 39, 33, 23, 3, 22]. The earlier Erd˝ os–R´ enyi paper [34] develops finite 

n factorial measure bounds that are uniform in the factorial order. The present work extends the fixed surplus enumerative ingredient used there, together with its exact ordered component calculation and factorial density argument at each fixed order, to Perron critical kernels. The single type enumerator is an exact specialization at t = 1; the two papers use slightly different, but asymptotically equivalent, finite graph parametrizations of the critical window. The globally ordered critical components and their measured metric space limits in the classical model are described in [1, 2]. The proof begins with one critically normalized field family. Lemmas 4.1 to 4.3 construct the nearby critical pair, select the least rooted tree branch, and continue it jointly on a common dented domain. On that branch, Lemmas 4.4 to 4.6 establish the path resolvent, the determinant factorization, the exact finite multigraph kernel expressions, and the continuation of the connected generating functions. Their coefficients at the zero field are extracted in Proposition 4.7. The normalized field derivative in Lemma 4.8 yields the local and shrinking field estimates of Corollary 4.9. Specialization to the scalar field gt in (4.40) gives the exact radius formula and its Gaussian expansion in Theorem 3.6. Finally, Lemma 4.10 and Corollary 4.11 make the critical radius, coefficient, and field estimates uniform along nearby finite critical models whose parameters vary with n.The probabilistic part begins in Section 5, where the field estimates are converted into concentration and quadratic integrability bounds for empirical type profiles. The estimates used later are collected in Lemma 5.3. Proposition 6.1 then combines the connected component weights with the exact residual factors in the deterministic and annealed models. The factorial moment calculation in Section 7 matches the limiting densities with those in Proposition 3.7. The local factorial moment and mark replacement criteria of Lemmas D.1 and D.2 give the point process and marked limits in Theorem 3.8. Finally, Lemmas 8.1 to 8.3 control truncation, integer approximation, and each fixed finite type stage; the diagonal construction then proves Theorems 3.9 and 3.10. 1.4. Organization. Sections 2 and 3 introduce the spaces, models, and main results. Section 4 develops the Perron–Wright enumeration and proves the coefficient asymptotics. Section 5 converts the resulting field estimates into concentration and quadratic control of component type profiles. 7. Factorial moments and point process convergence 

In this section we prove Theorem 3.8. We combine the Perron–Wright formula with the component count formula to obtain the fixed size factorial asymptotics, and then use factorial moment measures and type profile concentration to prove the unmarked and marked process limits. Related local estimates for the homogeneous model appear in [36]; the finite n factorial measure method is developed in [34]. The uniform finite type form used at fixed countable truncations is stated in Corollary 7.1.
