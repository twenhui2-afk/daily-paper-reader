QUASI-MONTE CARLO BASED MESHFREE METHODS FOR PARABOLIC EQUATIONS ON PARAMETERIZED RIEMANNIAN MANIFOLDS 

ZHIJIAN HE, ZHIYUAN HUI, CHANGTAO SHENG, BIHAO SU, AND TAO ZHOU †

Abstract. We develop and analyze a randomized path sampling framework for the pointwise approximation of linear parabolic equations on parameterized Riemannian manifolds, treating both closed manifolds and manifolds with Dirichlet boundary. Based on the Feynman–Kac representation, we construct a parameter-coordinate retraction-based discretization, referred to as the p-Ret scheme, for the manifold diffusion generated by the uniformly elliptic operator, and use it to formulate the Monte Carlo (MC) and randomized quasi-Monte Carlo (RQMC) methods. We derive the local weak consistency of the p-Ret tran-sition and first-order weak convergence of the temporal discretization on closed manifolds. For fixed h, we show that RQMC sampling yields asymptotically faster variance decay than standard MC. On closed manifolds, the variance is 

o(n−1) and, under additional regularity, satisfies the sharper bound O(n−2+ ε)for any ε > 0. For Dirichlet problems, despite the discontinuities induced by numerical first exit, nested-uniformly scrambled Sobol’ sampling attains 

O(n−1−1/s +ε) variance for any ε > 0 under suitable regularity assumptions, where s denotes the nominal path-space dimension. More notably, we further develop an Array-RQMC strategy based on stepwise low-dimensional random-ized point sets and a Hilbert space-filling curve that maps high-dimensional particle states to a one-dimensional ordering, and establish a variance rate of 

o(n−1) under suitable assumptions. Numerical results demonstrate that both RQMC methods reduce variance and root mean square error (RMSE) relative to standard MC and remain effective for manifolds with intrinsic dimension up to 50. 

1. Introduction 

Diffusion and transport on curved geometries arise in many scientific and en-gineering applications and lead naturally to partial differential equations (PDEs) on surfaces and Riemannian manifolds. A broad range of deterministic methods has been developed for such problems, including surface, adaptive, and trace finite element methods, embedding and closest-point techniques, and meshfree approaches (see, e.g., [4, 7, 8, 11, 26, 35, 37, 40, 48, 49] and the references therein). This motivates a complementary meshfree probabilistic approach that directly approximates the solution pointwise through stochastic trajectories. The Feynman–Kac representation provides a fundamental bridge between PDEs and stochastic processes by expressing their solutions as expectations over diffusion paths (cf. [12, 14, 15, 29, 53]). In Euclidean domains, this viewpoint has led to a broad class of Monte Carlo-type methods [5, 24, 38, 39, 51], which are particularly attractive for complex domains and high-dimensional problems. Extending this framework to Riemannian manifolds is natural, but introduces additional challenges, since the simulated paths must respect the manifold geometry while correctly approximating the intrinsic diffusion. On Riemannian manifolds, the Feynman– Kac representation remains valid once the Euclidean diffusion is replaced by a manifold-valued diffusion [23], whose numerical approximation has been approached through geodesic random walks, intrinsic SDE discretizations, constrained Brownian dynamics, and projection-based schemes (see, e.g.,[1, 2, 25, 28, 34, 54]). More recently, Schwarz et al. [50] developed an efficient retraction-based random walk that replaces costly geodesic computations by second-order retractions while preserving convergence to the correct Brownian motion. From a computational perspective, the Feynman–Kac representation reduces the pointwise evaluation of the PDE solution to the approximation of an expectation over diffusion paths, for which Monte Carlo sampling provides a natural and highly parallelizable approach, although its root-mean-square sampling error decays only at the rate O(n−1/2) (cf. [6, 52]). This relatively slow convergence motivates ran-domized quasi-Monte Carlo (RQMC) methods, which replace independent random samples by randomized low-discrepancy point sets while preserving unbiasedness [17, 19, 43, 44, 46]. In particular, nested-uniformly scrambled Sobol’ point sets achieve variance o(n−1) for square-integrable integrands and faster rates under additional smoothness assumptions [9, 10, 45]. The effectiveness of RQMC, however, depends strongly on the regularity of the integrand and on the transformations used to generate the sampling variables [3]. QMC methods have also proved effec-tive for high-dimensional PDE computations under suitable regularity assumptions (cf. [16, 30, 31]). Applying RQMC to Feynman–Kac discretizations on parameterized Riemannian manifolds introduces two additional challenges. First, with K time steps and 

r − 1 random variables per step, the direct path-space formulation has nominal dimension s = ( r − 1) K, which can become large as K or r increases. This motivates sequential RQMC methods, in particular Array-RQMC (cf. [20, 33]), which apply low-dimensional randomized point sets successively over the time steps rather than to the full path space. For multidimensional state spaces, the particles must additionally be ordered before coupling with the randomized points, for which Hilbert space-filling curves provide a natural mechanism [13, 18]. A second difficulty concerns the regularity of the path-space integrand. While the closed-manifold functional remains sufficiently regular under suitable assumptions, numerical first exit in the Dirichlet case creates discontinuity interfaces in path space, requiring a separate variance analysis for scrambled Sobol’ point sets (cf. These challenges expose a gap between the existing p-Ret analysis and the available RQMC theory. To the best of our knowledge, an RQMC error theory for Feynman–Kac functionals generated by p-Ret discretizations of manifold diffusions has not yet been developed. Existing p-Ret analysis has focused primarily on the geometric approximation of manifold diffusions, while RQMC and sequential RQMC theory has been developed mainly for high-dimensional integration, SDE discretizations, and Markov processes in Euclidean state spaces. In particular, no unified analysis is currently available for direct path-space RQMC, first-exit discontinuities in Dirichlet problems, and Hilbert-ordered Array-RQMC within the p-Ret framework. The main purpose of this paper is to develop a randomized p-Ret framework for the pointwise approximation of linear parabolic equations on parameterized Riemannian manifolds, with the aim of improving sampling efficiency beyond standard Monte Carlo. The key to the efficiency of this framework lies in the Feynman–Kac representation and a parameter-coordinate p-Ret discretization of the associated manifold diffusion, leading to a common discrete Feynman–Kac functional for MC, direct RQMC, and Array-RQMC sampling. Moreover, the framework naturally accommodates both closed manifolds and manifolds with Dirichlet boundary. This construction brings four key computational advantages for the numerical treatment of high-dimensional manifold problems: 

• Meshfree and high-dimensional computation. The p-Ret construction relies only on local geometric information and avoids the costly mesh generation and large scale linear solves inherent in conventional mesh based discretizations, thereby alleviating the curse of dimensionality for high dimensional manifold problems. 

• Pointwise and parallel evaluation. The Feynman–Kac formulation enables pointwise evaluation at prescribed space–time locations. The computation is naturally parallel across such locations, since each can be treated separately. 

• Reduced path-space dimension. Each p-Ret step samples a direction Z ∈ Sr−1

from only r − 1 independent uniform variables, whereas an r-dimensional Euler– Maruyama discretization requires r random inputs per step. Consequently, over 

K time steps, the nominal dimension of the direct RQMC problem is reduced from rK to ( r − 1) K. This removes one stochastic coordinate at every time step and directly lowers the high-dimensional integration cost by direct RQMC. 

• Unified discrete probabilistic structure. MC, direct RQMC, and Array-RQMC are built on the same p-Ret dynamics and discrete Feynman–Kac functional, and hence share the same time-discrete target. This isolates the effect of the sampling strategy from the temporal discretization and enables a consistent comparison of their variance and sampling errors. Beyond these computational advantages, we also analyze the errors arising from the p-Ret discretization and randomized sampling. To the best of our knowledge, no comparable RQMC error theory is available for p-Ret based Feynman–Kac discretizations on Riemannian manifolds. On closed manifolds, we first establish local weak consistency of the p-Ret transition and first-order weak convergence of the temporal discretization. Conclusion 

We have developed a probabilistic framework for the pointwise solution of linear parabolic equations on parameterized Riemannian manifolds. The main feature of the framework is that the manifold diffusion is propagated through local p-Ret dynamics, while the resulting discrete Feynman–Kac functional can be sampled by MC, direct RQMC, or Array-RQMC without changing the underlying time discretization. This separation of geometric approximation from sampling provides a meshfree and naturally parallel approach that remains applicable to manifolds of relatively high intrinsic dimension. The analysis also reveals a marked difference between closed and Dirichlet mani-folds. On closed manifolds, the discrete Feynman–Kac functional retains sufficient regularity for direct RQMC to improve on the standard Monte Carlo variance rate, with nearly quadratic variance decay, up to logarithmic factors, under stronger regularity assumptions. Boundary stopping changes this picture substantially. In the Dirichlet case, numerical first exit creates codimension-one discontinuity interfaces in path space. A layerwise decomposition, together with Owen’s boundary growth condition and geometric control of the switching interfaces, nevertheless recovers a variance rate strictly better than the Monte Carlo benchmark. Array-RQMC provides a different resolution of the high-dimensional sampling problem: it replaces full path-space randomization by sequential low-dimensional randomizations and retains variance o(n−1) for fixed K under the stated stability assumptions. The numerical experiments support these theoretical findings and clarify where the different sampling strategies are most effective. Both RQMC approaches provide clear variance and RMSE reductions in low and moderate intrinsic dimensions, while their advantage decreases as the dimension grows. The experiments also highlight the different sources of dimensional growth: direct RQMC is affected by QMC MESHFREE METHODS ON PARAMETERIZED MANIFOLDS 39 

the full path-space dimension ( r − 1) K, whereas Array-RQMC works with stepwise randomized point sets whose dimension does not grow with the number of time steps. Further improvements may come from sharper control of the numerical first-exit error, verifiable stability conditions for Array-RQMC, and extensions of the present construction beyond globally parameterized manifolds.
