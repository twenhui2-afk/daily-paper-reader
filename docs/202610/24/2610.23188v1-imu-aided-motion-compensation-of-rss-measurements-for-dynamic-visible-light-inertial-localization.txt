# IMU-Aided Motion Compensation of RSS Measurements for Dynamic Visible Light–Inertial Localization 

Xiao Sun, Zhenqi Zheng, Jianzhu Huai, Xingxing Li, Wanwei Zhang, Yuan Zhuang 

Abstract —Visible Light Positioning (VLP) offers high-precision indoor positioning using existing LED infrastructure. However, in VLP systems using Photodiodes (PDs) as receivers, rapid movement of the PD causes measurement inaccuracies as the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) processing yields averaged rather than instantaneous Received Signal Strength (RSS) values. To address this challenge, we propose an RSS correction algo-rithm that integrates Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) data with VLP observations to compensate for motion-induced distortions. Our approach leverages IMU-derived motion parameters to model the temporal variation of optical signals during the DFT window, enabling instantaneous RSS estimation. The algorithm is embedded within both tightly and simply coupled VLP/INS integration frameworks that estimate the pose of the moving receiver. This work targets highly dynamic indoor navigation. Simulations validate the correction model, and total-station-tracked experiments confirm reduced RSS discrepancies against model references. Across eight held-out high-dynamic handheld trials with peak translational and angular speeds of 2.25 m /s

and 284 ◦/s, correction reduces the pooled 3D position and PD-normal inclination root mean square errors relative to the LiDAR/IMU reference with a shared IMU by 56.0% and 71.9%, respectively. Both datasets and source code are publicly available. 

Index Terms —Visible light positioning, optical measurement compensation, inertial sensing, multisensor fusion, state estima-tion. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Visible Light Positioning (VLP) has emerged as a promising technology for high-accuracy indoor localization, leveraging existing Light-Emitting Diode (LED) lighting infrastructure to simultaneously provide illumination and positioning signals. Compared with widely used Indoor Positioning System (IPS) methods nowadays such as WiFi, Bluetooth, Ultra-WideBand (UWB), odometer, vision, and LiDAR, VLP shows multiple advantages [1]–[3] at the same time: high precision, high bandwidth, long lifetime, and low infrastructure cost [4]. Among existing literature on LED-based IPS, photodiodes (PDs) and cameras serve as the primary receiving devices in VLP systems. This study focuses on PD-based VLP systems. Within PD-based frameworks, Received Signal Strength (RSS) represents the most straightforward measurement readily ac-quired from a single PD. In contrast, image-based VLP systems, despite the widespread integration of cameras in modern devices [9], [10], rely on imaging prin-ciples and typically entail higher hardware and computational costs. Although VLP achieves high positioning accuracy with low cost, it suffers from sensitivity to light blockages and receiver inclinations. Integrating VLP with an Inertial Nav-igation System (INS) offers a solution to these challenges [11]. Recent studies have explored both tightly [4], [12]–[14] and loosely [15]–[17] coupled VLP/INS systems. While both approaches leverage IMU benefits, tightly coupled models better resist IMU errors and provide continuous navigation during blockages. Various implementations exist: 2D systems assuming level placement [12], PD position fusion with INS [16], 2D PDR fusion [13], [15], [17], and smartphone ambient light sensor fusion [18]. Sun et al. [4] proposed a 6-DoF tightly coupled VLP/INS system using graph optimization, while Wang et al. [14] developed a data-model dual-drive system. VLP can also be integrated with INS and GNSS for seamless indoor/outdoor positioning [19]. In highly dynamic scenarios such as UAV flight or rapid hand-held device movement, sensor data acquisition often suf-fers from various forms of distortion. In camera-based systems, rapid motion can cause image blurring. GIVL-SLAM compen-sates LiDAR scan distortion using IMU-derived poses before tightly coupled factor-graph estimation [20]. In the context of VLP based on highly dynamic platforms, most existing studies have focused on image sensor systems, primarily addressing issues such as image blurring and high latency caused by rapid translation and rotation. Guan et al. [21] proposed a novel VLP algorithm based on optical flow detection and Bayesian prediction to mitigate image blurring induced by high-speed movement. Lin et al. [22] employed lightweight image processing algorithms to reduce latency in VLP systems during fast motion. Li et al. [23] introduced a deep learning framework based on convolutional neural networks to improve the success rate of decoding user identification information from VLP-LED signals. However, these studies did not address whether their proposed algorithms are applicable to PD-based systems, nor did they suggest how such algorithms could be adapted for PD systems. Here we focus on the motion-induced bias of windowed RSS measurements from a single moving PD. As illustrated in Fig. 1, consider a scenario where an LED emits a sinusoidally modulated optical signal, and a PD rapidly traverses an arc-shaped trajectory within one second. In a VLP system, RSS measurements are typically obtained by applying a Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) to raw photocurrent data collected over a time window [24]. However, during the rapid translation and rotation of a vehicle-mounted PD, changes in position and orientation within this interval cause fluctuations in the instantaneous optical intensity. When this non-stationary signal is processed via a DFT window (typically a one-second interval), the resulting spectral compo-nent corresponds to an averaged amplitude rather than the in-stantaneous optical intensity (shown in Fig. 1). Consequently, the systematic discrepancy between the estimated and true instantaneous RSS degrades positioning accuracy. This study refines the conventional Lambertian model to account for temporal RSS variation within the DFT window and uses inertial data to correct motion-induced RSS distortions before fusion. The contributions are as follows:

Dynamic RSS Formation Model : We identify and for-mulate a previously unmodeled error source in PD-based VLP: DFT demodulation returns a window-averaged RSS, whereas mobile positioning estimators consume it as an instantaneous RSS. This modeling assumption is different from the static per-epoch RSS assumption used in [4].

The module is estimator-agnostic and can be inserted before either the tightly coupled graph of [4] or a simple VLP/INS integration pipeline.  

> •

Open High-Dynamic Datasets and VLP/INS Fusion Code : To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to publicly release high-dynamic VLP datasets containing synchronized PD and IMU measurements together with VLP/INS fusion code, including the proposed RSS cor-rection. C. Simple Integration of VLP and INS 

In the single-PD Lambertian model (1), receiver attitude enters RSS only through the unit normal nu, which has two independent orientation parameters. At a fixed PD position, rotation about this normal leaves all instantaneous RSS ob-servations unchanged, so increasing the LED count cannot recover all six pose degrees of freedom from instantaneous RSS alone. As shown in Fig. 2, simple VLP/INS integration substitutes the INS-derived PD normal into the Lambertian model and solves for the three position unknowns using the available LED observations, subject to adequate LED geometry. This approach relies on the accuracy of the INS-derived PD normal; errors in that normal propagate through the Lambertian model into the position solution.

In this section, we evaluate whether correcting the RSS for-mation stage provides benefits beyond the VLP/INS backend of [4]. We report pose errors against the specified references, RSS correction accuracy, sensitivity to translational and ro-tational motion intensity, DFT window selection, comparison with alternative RSS extraction methods, and physical valida-tion using LiDAR/IMU and independent total-station position references. The data are openly accessible on IEEE DataPort 2.In this study, motion is classified as highly dynamic when the translational speed is at least 1 m /s or the angular speed exceeds 30 ◦/s; satisfying either condition is sufficient. This definition applies to both simulations and physical experiments and includes rapid inclination changes at a fixed receiver position. The RSS correction addresses the discrepancy be-tween window-averaged and midpoint RSS, whose magnitude depends on receiver motion, changes in the PD normal, LED geometry, and window length.

We proposed an IMU-aided RSS correction that com-pensates for the difference between window-averaged DFT measurements and instantaneous RSS during receiver mo-tion. The correction operates before fusion and can be used with both simple and tightly coupled VLP/INS integration. Controlled simulations validate the DFT averaging relation and the correction formula against known RSS references and numerical Lambertian integration. Across eight held-out dynamic trials, the 3D position and PD-normal inclination RMSE relative to the LiDAR/IMU reference decrease by 56.0% and 71.9%, respectively. This reference shares the IMU stream with VLP/INS. Independent total-station posi-tion measurements confirm a 16.8% reduction in pooled 3D position RMSE, alongside reduced RSS discrepancies against the Lambertian reference constructed from measured positions and IMU attitudes.
