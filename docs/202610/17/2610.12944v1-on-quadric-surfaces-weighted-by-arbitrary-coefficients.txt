> RACHITA GURIA, PRAHLAD SHARMA, AND SAURABH SINGH
> Abstract. For a non-singular quadratic form Fin four variables, we study integer solu-tions to F= 0 with an arbitrary weight attached to one of the variables. This allows us to investigate genuinely sparse subsets of the corresponding variety. Our main input is to obtain cancellation in sums of character sums over the moduli arising in the application of the DFI’s delta method to the quadratic hypersurface. This cancellation relies crucially on the equidistribution of quadratic roots and the Weil bound for Kloosterman sums.

1. Introduction 

The study of rational points on varieties has long been a fundamental theme in number theory. Early examples include Lagrange’s four-square theorem [28] and Waring’s problem [36], concerning the representation of integers as sums of powers. The latter became a major motivation for subsequent counting methods. A major advance in the quantitative study of these integral solutions came with the circle method, originating in Hardy and Ramanujan’s work [18] on partitions and further developed by Hardy and Littlewood in their treatment of Waring’s problem [17]. However, the implicit nature of harmonics involved makes it difficult to adapt the circle method as the number of variables decreases. For quadratic forms, the case considered in this paper, at least five variables are required to obtain an asymptotic formula for the number of representations of an integer by the form. The barrier of five variables was overcome and reduced to four by Kloosterman [26] using his refinement of the circle method. Now known as the Kloosterman refinement, his approach exploits cancellation in sums over the numerators of the approximating Farey fractions, giving rise to what are now called Kloosterman sums. This still left the important problem of obtaining an asymptotic formula for the number of integer solutions to the homogeneous equation F = 0, where 

F is a quadratic form in four variables. Heath-Brown [19] later resolved this case using a modern form of the circle method known as the delta method. The delta-symbol approach avoids the major/minor arc dichotomy and is more explicit with the harmonics involved. Heath-Brown exploited this flexibility to obtain cancellation in both the numerators and denominators of certain approximating fractions arising in the method, which can be viewed as a “double Kloosterman refinement”. The works discussed above and related results mainly deal with unrestricted integer solutions, while relatively few address solutions in sparse subsets of the integers. One direction that has been pursued is the study of solutions in primes or product of primes– a subject central to the Waring–Goldbach problem, which have also been investigated in many different settings; see [23, 6, 21, 34, 35, 15, 30, 31, 37, 2, 13, 16]. A principal difficulty for more general sparse sets is that the associated exponential sums require new estimates

that are not readily available. Consequently, one cannot expect to study genuinely sparse solutions using the traditional approach. In this paper, we address this problem for quadratic forms in four variables. More precisely, we obtain asymptotic formulas for solutions to F = 0, with an essentially arbitrary weight attached to the first variable and obtain an error term in which the arithmetic dependence is isolated into simple norms of the weight. Applications include solutions with one variable restricted to perfect powers, short intervals of length essentially as small as 1, or primes, as well as counts of base points whose associated conic fibres are soluble over 

Q. The leading constants in the main term reveal interesting local-global principles in each of these examples. Furthermore, for general multiplicative weights, we give a clean formulation of the main term in terms of the associated L-function. Examples include the indicator function of square-free numbers, divisor functions, the M¨ obius function μ(n), and coefficients of general GL (d) forms. Our main tool is the Duke-Friedlander-Iwaniec delta symbol expansion. It turns out delta symbol packs enough harmonics to provide sufficient cancellation without exploiting the arithmetic properties of one of the variables. More precisely, alongside the three unre-stricted variables, we exploit the sum over moduli in the delta-symbol expansion to obtain cancellation in sums of certain character sums, in place of the cancellation in the fourth variable used in Heath-Brown’s approach. As we shall see, the cancellation in these sums comes from the equidistribution of quadratic roots and the Weil bound for Kloosterman sums. Using the ideas in this paper, one can study quadric surfaces with arbitrary weights attached to more variables as the total number of variables increases. The character sum analysis will be different in each case and will be explored in subsequent works.
