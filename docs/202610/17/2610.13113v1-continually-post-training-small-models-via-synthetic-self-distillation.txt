# ABSTRACT 

Continual post-training faces a dual challenge of acquisition and retention: mod-els must learn new capabilities while preserving prior knowledge. While Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning (SDFT) mitigates this using self-supervision with in-context demonstrations, it relies on strong in-context learning and instruction-following abilities. Consequently, SDFT fails on smaller models critical for low-latency, on-device use. To enable continual learning at smaller scales, we first identify the root cause of failure. We show that poor acquisition and forgetting correlate linearly with the post-trained model’s distribution shift, revealing the ne-cessity of in-distribution learning. We thus introduce Synthetic Self-Distillation (S YNTH SELF ), imitating SDFT’s in-distribution rewrite by outsourcing the task to a minimally larger, same-family aligner. Coupled with off-policy supervision and rejection sampling, this provides the high-quality supervision small models require without breaking their original distribution. Evaluations spanning 4 acqui-sition datasets (including a mixture) and 14 retention benchmarks show S YNTH -SELF consistently outperforms 6 methods (including off- and on-policy distilla-tion) across 3 small model scales. Extensive further analyses confirm S YNTH SELF 

paves the way toward effective small-model continual learning. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Continual post-training of language models (LMs) is a common setting where practitioners aim to inject new capabilities into already-trained models for scenarios such as customizing for specific use-cases and enabling human-like lifelong learning (Shi et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024a; Zheng et al., 2025a;b; Srijith et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026a). Yet, this process is notoriously challenging on both the acquisition (injecting new abilities) and retention (maintaining existing capabilities) axes (Harmon et al., 2026; Luo et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026b). The failure to retain existing capabilities, widely known as catastrophic forgetting (McCloskey & Cohen, 1989; French, 1999; Kirkpatrick et al., 2017), is particularly well-documented in settings where a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) dataset is used for teacher forcing (Kalajdzievski, 2024; Luo et al., 2025; Lai et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026b; Shenfeld et al., 2026b). A recent work showed that self-distillation fine-tuning (SDFT) is an effective way to inject SFT demonstrations in an on-policy manner (Shenfeld et al., 2026a), outperforming recent off-policy continual fine-tuning methods such as re-invoke (Lu & Lab, 2025) and Dynamic Fine-tuning (DFT; Wu et al. (2026)) on both acquisition and retention axes. SDFT leverages the in-context learn-ing (ICL) and instruction-following (IF) abilities of post-trained models to provide demonstrations in-context to the self-teacher model, and applies on-policy distillation (OPD; Ross et al. (2011), Agarwal et al. (2024)) on itself, supervising on an implicit self-rewrite of the demonstrations. However, as models struggle with ICL and IF at smaller scales, SDFT remains infeasible in this regime (see Fig. 3a). We aim to address this limitation, as small models have become critical for applications such as on-device deployment, latency-specialized models, draft models, verifiers, and large-small collaborative methods (Cobbe et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2023; Abdin et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024; Lu et al., 2025; Belcak et al., 2025; Cai et al., 2026). 1arXiv:2610.13113v1 [cs.LG] 9 Oct 2026 IFEval HellaSwag WinoGrande ARC-C TruthfulQA MMLU MMLU-Pro GPQA HumanEval HumanEval+ MBPP MBPP+ GSM8K Minerva

To better understand how to leverage the key principles of SDFT, we first examine why it succeeds in continual post-training ( § 2.2.1 ). We reveal that the distribution shift of the post-trained base model linearly correlates with poor acquisition and forgetting ( Fig. 2 ), indicating that staying in-distribution is crucial for continual learning. While the primary advantage of SDFT over off-policy methods is its ability to staying in distribution, evaluating its self-generated offline dataset reveals that SDFT’s self-supervision quality is inadequate at smaller model scales ( Fig. 3 ). In response, we propose Synthetic Self-Distillation (S YNTH SELF ), which imitates self-distillation’s implicit in-distribution rewrite by outsourcing the distribution-aligned rewrite to a larger model ( §2.4 ). Our method distinctly differs from SDFT in the following ways: (i) choosing off-policy over on-policy supervision allows us to apply rejection sampling, which in turn improves supervision quality for small models that are sensitive to data quality; and (ii) using minimally larger, same-family aligners allows us to simultaneously (ii.a.) provide high-quality supervision ( Tab. 2 ), (ii.b.) 

remain in-distribution relative to the base model ( Fig. 4 ), and (ii.c.) maintain low computational costs. We empirically validate S YNTH SELF (§ 3 ), demonstrating that: 1. S YNTH SELF achieves the best acquisition across 3 domains (science, tool-use, and medicine) and a 4-subject science mixture while preserving prior capabilities on 14 bench-marks, consistently across 3 base model sizes ( 0.5 , 1.5 , 3B ), and against 4 relevant baselines ( Fig. 1, Tab. 1 ). 2. Controlling for the confounding factor of teacher model size, we demonstrate that S YNTH -SELF outperforms both on- and off-policy distillation in acquisition by +5.7%p to 

+16.9%p , averaged over all four settings and three base scales ( Tab. 1 , Fig. 5 ). 3. We show that utilizing a smaller (weaker) in-distribution aligner is more effective than deploying a larger (stronger) out-of-distribution teacher, yielding a 69.9% reduction in alignment computational cost (FLOPs) ( Tab. 2 ). 4. We validate that our improvement over SDFT is not due to prompt differences using prompt-matched and diverse prompts, achieving +18.96%p and +1.68%p gains in acqui-sition and retention over prompt-matched SDFT ( Fig. 6 ). 5. 2.1 PROBLEM SETTING : C ONTINUAL FINE -TUNING 

Continual fine-tuning. Given a post-trained policy π, we continually inject new knowledge from a dataset Dtrain = {(xi, y i)}Ni=1 by further training π. In doing so, we aim to maximize (i) acquisi-tion: performance on a same-domain test set Dtest , while maintaining (ii) retention: performance on existing capabilities, evaluated via Dret .

Self-supervision fails at small model scales. Although on-policy self-distillation (SDFT; Shen-feld et al. (2026a)) successfully preserves the base distribution during continual post-training, its efficacy drops for smaller models. Fig. 3a demonstrates that SDFT’s performance relative to SFT decreases monotonically with student size. We attribute this to poor self-supervision quality. To evaluate this, we generate an offline dataset to approximate on-policy teacher quality. Fig. 3b con-firms that smaller models yield weaker supervision: regardless of the prompt (Appendix A), average accuracy falls to 60%, and CoT traces are dropped 8.2% of the time. 2.4 OFF -POLICY IN-DISTRIBUTION DISTILLATION AS SYNTHETIC SELF -DISTILLATION 

To bridge this gap, S YNTH SELF is designed to (1) remain in-distribution with respect to the base model π, and (2) provide high-quality supervision. We achieve this via two core mechanisms: (i) 

utilizing off-policy distillation to enable rejection sampling, and (ii) selecting a larger aligner model that simultaneously (ii.a.) yields high-quality supervision, (ii.b.) stays in-distribution relative to the base model, and (ii.c.) incurs low computational overhead.

# 4 DISCUSSION 

Additional related work. Huang et al. (2026) highlights the impact of distribution drift in SFT using code-generation data on Qwen3-8B . However, they do not directly address continual learning or small language models, restricting their evaluation to limited scales and domains. Furthermore, Li et al. (2026) combines mechanisms that we instead isolate: SDFT-style self-rewrites with similarity filtering, cross-family fusion, and an on-policy KL regularizer; covered individually by our SDFT, on-policy distillation, and ASFT baselines. Crucially, we differ in the supervision source. Because their rewrites originate from the student, they inherit the small-scale degradation shown in Fig. 3. Our same-family aligner explicitly resolves this limitation. 

Training compute savings and further performance headroom. Rejection sampling reduces training compute by shrinking the dataset | ˆD| (e.g., by 17.1% for tool use; Tab. 2). Despite training on fewer demonstrations than SFT, S YNTH SELF improves acquisition by +4.2%p on average, prov-ing these gains stem from supervision quality rather than quantity. To further boost performance, practitioners can scale k in pass@ k, trading inference compute for larger coverage supervision. 

Limitation and future directions. SYNTH SELF requires a larger, same-family aligner. Fortu-nately, most major open-weight families provide a spectrum of model sizes, naturally satisfying this requirement since smaller variants are often distilled from their larger counterparts (e.g., Qwen2.5 ,

Qwen3.5 , OLMo 2 (Walsh et al., 2025), Llama 3 (Grattafiori et al., 2024), and Gemma 4 ). Nev-ertheless, future work could explore adapting out-of-family aligners to produce in-distribution su-pervision. One promising direction is adding the base model’s answer in-context with an appropriate prompt to generate an in-distribution rewrite. 9AI USE STATEMENT
