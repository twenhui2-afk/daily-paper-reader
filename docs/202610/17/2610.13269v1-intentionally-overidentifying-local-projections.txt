October 12, 2026 ABSTRACT . Local projections (LPs) have become a standard tool for estimating dynamic effects in macroeconomics and are typically implemented using ordinary least squares (OLS) for a given lag order. We propose an alternative approach that exploits additional lags as ex-cluded instrumental variables (IVs). Under a finite-order model with martingale-difference shocks, the resulting (optimally weighted) IV estimator is never asymptotically less efficient than OLS and, as we show, can deliver substantial efficiency gains when data are persistent. For the unit-root AR(1) data with i.i.d. errors, our baseline IV estimator (with one overidenti-fying restriction) has asymptotic variance 2 − h−1, compared with h, the asymptotic variance of the OLS lag-augmented estimator in Montiel Olea and Plagborg-Møller (2021), for an inte-ger propagation horizon h. We then develop IV-based LP inference that is robust to unknown persistence and moderate local model misspecification, whereas the recursive VAR-based infer-ence is generally not robust to the latter. We also characterize the trade-off between the degree of misspecification and the number of additional lag controls. Through simulations and an em-pirical application, we demonstrate that the proposed method can yield meaningfully shorter confidence intervals for impulse responses than OLS in finite samples.

# 1. INTRODUCTION 

A central task in empirical macroeconomics and finance is to estimate how shocks propagate over time (Ramey, 2016; Sims, 1980). Since Jordà (2005), local projections (LPs, hereafter) have be-come a standard tool for this purpose because they estimate horizon-specific impulse responses directly, and can readily accommodate a range of empirically relevant features, including nonlin-earities, instability, and rich sets of controls. 1

In recent work, Montiel Olea and Plagborg-Møller (2021) and Montiel Olea, Plagborg-Møller, Qian, and Wolf (2026) formally prove that, under the finite-order model, the lag-augmented LP-based inference (i.e. using a sufficient number of controlled lags) is robust to persistence of the data, long horizons, and a nontrivial degree of model misspecification, strengthening the case for LPs relative to other methods of inference on impulse responses. On the other hand, it is well known that the LP estimator tends to have large variance and provide wide confidence intervals. The current paper extends this line of research by proposing a new LP estimator which exploits the additional information implied by the model. Concretely, instead of using OLS (ordinary least squares), as is common in practice, we intentionally overidentify the local projection by adding orthogonality conditions based on extra lags of the data to construct the optimally weighted IV (via the generalized method of moments (GMM), Hansen (1982)) estimator of the LP regression. While such an estimator is guaranteed to be no less efficient than OLS, we ask whether and when the efficiency gain can be nontrivial and whether the robustness properties established in the earlier literature on local projections can be retained. Our analysis shows that the new method has considerable potential. Additional orthogonal con-ditions can yield substantial efficiency gains over LP OLS, arising from the specific structure of the

2LP regression. As a teaser, additional lags are not necessarily informative. For the autoregression (namely, the horizon-one local projection), lag augmented or not, with conditionally homoskedas-tic martingale-difference errors (e.g., i.i.d. errors), additional lags are generally redundant excluded instrumental variables; the additional moment conditions do not generate an asymptotically more efficient estimator than OLS. However, such redundancy does not extend to a general LP regres-sion at horizons larger than one, even under conditional homoskedasticity. The optimally weighted GMM can produce a strict efficiency gain over OLS, especially when the data are strongly per-sistent. The efficiency gain arises primarily from the serial correlation of the GMM (residualized) score process, even when the OLS (residualized) score process can be serially uncorrelated (Mon-tiel Olea and Plagborg-Møller, 2021). Under conditional heteroskedasticity, as our general infer-ential theory allows, using additional lags as excluded instruments generally yields non-redundant moment conditions (even for the horizon-one LP regression; see Kuersteiner (2002)). Regarding robustness, we show that controlling for a sufficient number of lags in the LP regres-sion remains crucial for valid inference with our GMM estimator, robust to both data persistence and moderate model misspecification. 

Literature . The terminology of instruments and GMM has appeared in the VAR/LP literature, but their uses and purposes are all different from ours. The instruments used in the current paper are internal lags that improve efficiency. The method applies to reduced-form impulse responses and, under identification, to structural responses as well. Our use of instruments differs from the recent literature on internal and external instruments for structural identification (Lewis, 2021; Stock and Watson, 2018). Inoue, Jordà, and Kuersteiner (2026) similarly refer to their method as GMM LP, but their approach follows the literature on structural identification. Jordà and Kozicki (2011) use GMM and minimum-distance methods to identify and estimate the structural parameter in the solution of a linear rational expectations model. Instruments used for structural identification can be subject to the well-known weak-IV prob-lem (Montiel Olea, Stock, and Watson, 2021), whereas our use of instruments generally is not. 3Under the standard regularity assumptions, the extra weakly-correlated lags can at most reduce or eliminate the efficiency gain (over OLS) from overidentification, and do not themselves threaten the identification or inference. Dufour and Wang (2024) use reduced-form VAR innovations as instruments to conduct two-step estimation and associated inference for the generalized impulse responses and multi-horizon Granger causality in the sense of Dufour and Renault (1998). Their motivation is mostly the simplicity of the (persistence-robust) inference (no HAC variance estimator needed, albeit under an additional assumption on innovations) and the ability to handle a larger class of causal parameters. Their instruments exactly identify the model, thus the resulting estimator of the impulse response can be more or less efficient than OLS. The current paper focuses exclusively on impulse responses, and uses extra lagged outcomes to construct overidentification restrictions which guarantee no asymptotic efficiency loss relative to OLS. Lusompa (2023) also exploits the serial correlation in LP regression errors, by constructing a generated outcome variable, which involves estimates of nuisance parameters (i.e., the full set of impulse responses). Our method is thus simpler to implement: it relies on a standard GMM estimator with readily available instrumental variables and can be carried out using off-the-shelf software. We further develop robust inference and allow for model misspecification, features that are not addressed in Lusompas analysis. 2

Relatedly, recently developed smoothing, shrinkage, and Bayesian methods 3 can improve pre-cision of the LP by imposing structure across horizons and borrowing information across horizon-specific estimates. By contrast, our approach leaves the local-projection estimand unrestricted and obtains efficiency solely from valid overidentifying moments. In this paper we consider the finite-order model, following a strand of the literature (surveyed in Montiel Olea et al. (2026)) that views the low-order models commonly used in practice as approximations to an underlying infinite-order DGP. Recent work by Xu (2025, 2026) establishes

## 2.4. Non-redundancy of the overidentifying restrictions 

While the GMM never has a larger asymptotic variance than OLS with the same set of regressors, it is natural to ask when the overidentifying restrictions provide a strict efficiency gain. # 8. CONCLUSION 

This paper proposes a new method for estimating impulse responses and conducting inference based on local projections. Rather than estimating a local projection by OLS for a given set of lag controls, we exploit additional lags of the data as excluded instruments and optimally combine the resulting moment conditions using GMM. We show that these additional moments can deliver substantial efficiency gains relative to OLS, particularly when the data are persistent. We formally establish the robustness of the proposed inference to data persistence and model misspecification.
