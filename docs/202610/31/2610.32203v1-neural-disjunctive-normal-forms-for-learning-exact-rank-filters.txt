Neural Disjunctive Normal Forms for Learning Exact
Rank Filters

1 Mathematical Sciences Institute and France-Australia Mathematical Sciences and Interactions
ANU-CNRS International Research Lab, The Australian National University

Abstract. Gradient-based learning of morphological operators is hindered by
locally sparse derivatives with respect to the structuring elements. We develop a
method for learning rank filters that retains exact operator evaluation while dis-
tributing the derivatives across the window. Using threshold decomposition, we
construct a neural disjunctive normal form representation and establish a suffi-
cient condition for exactness with integer-valued images and structuring elements.
We then derive a smooth surrogate from the unique root of a smooth threshold
equation. Implicit differentiation gives closed-form derivatives with normalised
local weights, whose distribution is controlled by a smoothing parameter. These
derivatives are used in a straight-through procedure: the forward pass evaluates
the exact rank filter, while the backward pass uses the surrogate. For fixed ranks,
the procedure learns real-valued structuring elements of any rank filter, including
the median, and extends to compositions, infima, and suprema of rank filters.
Experiments on image denoising using MNIST and Fashion-MNIST and retinal
vessel segmentation show that, starting from a generic random initialisation, the
proposed approach consistently improves performance over training with exact
subgradients in every setting. In image denoising, our best network also outper-
forms smooth morphological networks in all settings. On vessel segmentation, the
learned erosions recover structuring elements that fit the vessels, as theoretically
expected, whereas most of those trained with exact subgradients remain close to
their initialisation.
Keywords: Mathematical morphology · Morphological neural networks · Rank
filters · Neural disjunctive normal form · Straight-through estimator

Introduction
Rank filters, including erosions and dilations, describe image transformations through
local order statistics and structuring elements [12]. Learning the structuring elements
from paired images adapts these transformations to a task, and the resulting operator is
determined by its window, rank, and structuring element. Preserving exact evaluation
keeps this description valid throughout training and inference. However, at each pixel
where the selected order statistic is attained at a unique position, the derivative with
respect to the structuring element is nonzero only at that position. This local gradient
sparsity can hinder optimisation and make training sensitive to initialisation [3,6,7].

Morphological networks have been trained with the exact operators and their sub-
gradient [8], which inherits this sparsity. Recent works keep this setting and address
it through guidelines on initialisation and learning rates [3], and, for a single supre-
mum of erosions, through data-driven initialisations and denser descent updates [6,7].
Alternatively, smooth layers can replace the operators by differentiable approximations
[4,10], so that the trained operator only approximates a morphological one. As a com-
promise, older alternatives keep the exact operator in the forward pass and modify only
its derivative. Salembier [16] adapted rank filters through an implicit formulation of the
rank by sign functions, and Pessoa and Maragos [15] replaced the rank indicator vector
by smoothed impulses that spread the gradient over the window. This smoothing is a
heuristic design choice rather than the derivative of a function representing the filter.
In this paper, we rely on the threshold decomposition [12,17] of rank filters to
represent them as a sum, over thresholds, of order statistics of Boolean variables, and
adapt the semi-symbolic layer of pix2rule [5] to obtain a neural disjunctive normal
form representation of these order statistics, which yields a smooth representation of
rank filters that is exact on the integer grid when the smoothing parameter is large
enough. Implicit differentiation of the root of a smooth threshold equation yields a
surrogate derivative with normalised weights over the window which have the form of
the smoothed impulses of Pessoa and Maragos [15]. However, unlike their heuristic
approach, our weights are obtained as the exact derivatives of a mathematically defined
smooth surrogate of the filter, addressing gradient sparsity in a principled way.
For fixed ranks, we use these derivatives in a straight-through procedure [2] that
learns real-valued structuring elements: the forward pass evaluates the exact filter, while
the backward pass uses the surrogate. The smoothing parameter controls the distribution
of the local derivative across the window, while the evaluated filter remains exact and
independent of that choice. The procedure applies to any rank, such as the median,
which smooth layers do not usually cover, and to compositions, infima and suprema of
rank filters. It thus, in principle, extends to grey-level images the framework of discrete
morphological neural networks [13], which learn exact binary operators combined by
composition, infimum and supremum in architectures designed from prior knowledge.
We evaluate the procedure on image denoising and retinal vessel segmentation, com-
paring it with direct differentiation (subgradient) of exact operators and, for denoising,
with smooth morphological networks. These comparisons assess whether redistributing
the derivatives improves learning while retaining exact operator evaluation. Starting
from a generic random initialisation, not tailored to the data, our method consistently
improves on the exact subgradient in every setting, without requiring the specialised ini-
tialisation strategies employed by recent exact-training approaches [3,6,7] and our best
network outperforms the smooth networks in all denoising settings, while the trained
operator remains exact. Moreover, just as the layers of discrete morphological neural
networks specialise into the expected steps of a task, such as denoising followed by
boundary extraction, without being constrained to do so [14], the erosions learned by
our method for retinal vessels recover structuring elements of different sizes and ori-
entations that fit into the vessels, as theoretically expected [19], whereas most of those
learned with the exact subgradient remain close to their random initialisation.

Our main contributions are therefore: (i) a theoretically grounded approximation of
rank-filter derivatives, rather than a heuristic redistribution of gradients [15]; (ii) exact
forward evaluation combined with smoother backward gradients, mitigating the depen-
dence on specialised initialisation strategies of recent exact-training methods [3,6,7];
and (iii) the demonstration of effective training on non-trivial image processing tasks,
starting from generic random initialisations, improving upon exact-subgradient training
and smooth morphological layers.

Final remarks
In this paper, we proposed a principled way to train exact rank filters with gradient
descent via a straight-through estimator, in which the backward pass relies on the
threshold decomposition combined with the DNF neural layer. The results show that
our method is superior to training with the exact subgradient and is competitive with, if
not better than, smooth approximations of erosions and dilations, underscoring that it is
possible to efficiently train morphological operators via gradient descent without losing
the exactness. In particular, our method applies to any rank filter, while, in general,
the smooth layers proposed in the literature approximate only erosions and dilations.
Since compositions, infima and suprema of rank filters are trained in the same way, the
procedure extends to grey-level images the framework of discrete morphological neural
networks [13,14], in which the architecture is designed from prior knowledge and the
trained operator remains exact and interpretable.
We leave many topics to be explored in future research. First, it would be interesting
to learn the rank 𝛿so the architecture is not fixed beforehand, which is closer to the
framework of [4,10]. Second, the experiments illustrate that our method is less sensitive
than the exact subgradient to the initial parameters, which are selected independently and
with high variance, a fact that needs further investigation. Finally, the proposed frame-
work may be extended to non-increasing operators by representing them as suprema
of sup-generating operators, following the Banon-Barrera decomposition [1], and other
surrogates, such as smooth morphological layers, could be used in the backward pass.
