ABSTRACT
Nonlinear activation evaluation remains a major bottleneck in neural inference
under fully homomorphic encryption (FHE). FHE supports efficient addition and
multiplication, but lacks native operations for exponential functions, reciprocals,
and value-dependent selection. High-degree polynomial approximations on fixed
intervals are therefore commonly used to replace nonlinear activations. They can
achieve small errors but often incur substantial multiplicative depth and latency.
We observe that several widely used nonlinear activations share the algebraic
structure A(x) = xg(x) with g(−x) = 1−g(x). This class includes GELU, SiLU,
fixed-parameter Swish, and the scalar nonlinear branches of GEGLU/SwiGLU.
We propose SPA (Structure-Preserving Approximation) to exploit this shared
structure. SPA retains the exact odd component x/2 and approximates only the
even residual as a polynomial in x2, yielding a comparison-free arithmetic kernel.
This construction removes redundant odd degrees of freedom and enables lower-
cost polynomial evaluation at a given error target. Plaintext experiments with
Gemma-4-E2B and Llama-3.2-3B show that SPA largely preserves downstream
task performance without weight updates. Fixed-interval CKKS benchmarks show
favorable error–latency trade-offs. On [−5, 5], SPA-balanced achieves a 3.25×
speedup over MOAI (ICLR’ 26) for erf-GELU with comparable error. On the
same interval, SPA-accurate achieves 79.22% lower root mean squared error
(RMSE) and a 10.40× speedup over Euston (S&P’ 26) for QuickGELU. For SiLU,
SPA-accurate achieves 19.02% lower RMSE and a 2.06× speedup over THOR
(CCS’ 25).

INTRODUCTION
Fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) enables neural inference on encrypted inputs, but does not
directly implement nonlinear activations such as GELU and SiLU. Their evaluation is a major
challenge in large language model (LLM) feed-forward blocks (Cheon et al., 2017; Ebel et al., 2025;
Nam et al., 2025). The goal is to find accurate, low-cost replacements that balance polynomial
accuracy and depth against lookup, selection, or communication costs (Hao et al., 2022; Pang et al.,
2024; Gupta et al., 2024; Kei & Chow, 2025).
We study the scalar GELU/SiLU branches in Figure 1 and other activations with the shared structure
A(x) = xg(x),
g(−x) = 1 −g(x),
(1)
where g is a scalar gate function that modulates x, not an additional learned projection. GELU, SiLU,
and fixed-parameter Swish have this structure, which implies A(x) −A(−x) = x: the odd part is
known exactly, and only the even remainder requires approximation. Replacing this scalar branch
leaves the other projection, elementwise product, and trained weights unchanged.
We propose SPA, Structure-Preserving Approximation, which retains the linear odd part and approxi-
mates the even remainder after the change of variable u = x2. This structural restriction preserves the

EVALUATION
5.1
EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
We evaluate frozen-weight Gemma-4-E2B-it and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct (Gemma Team, 2026; Meta,
2024), separately from ciphertext kernels. Gemma’s Exact-erf reference replaces tanh-GELU at 35
decoder-MLP sites; SPA, MOAI, and NEXUS approximate erf-GELU at the same sites. Native-
tanh remains a control. Other gates, attention, normalization, and weights are unchanged. Llama’s
reference uses native SiLU.
Tasks include multiple-choice questions (MCQ) from HellaSwag, MMLU, Social IQa, and RACE-
high, and language modeling on WikiText-2 and C4-en (Zellers et al., 2019; Hendrycks et al., 2021;
Sap et al., 2019; Lai et al., 2017; Merity et al., 2017; Raffel et al., 2020). MCQ choices are ranked by
conditional sequence likelihood, not free-form generation. Calibration data are disjoint from evalua-
tion data, and calibration is repeated for Exact-erf. The backbone uses BF16; activations are evaluated
in FP32 and cast back to BF16. Appendix C gives splits, range assignment, scoring, and paired
statistics. CKKS kernel timings use the Intel Xeon Gold 6530 CPU with a SEAL 4.1-compatible
implementation; plaintext model evaluations use the NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Workstation
Edition GPU. Code: https://github.com/Anonymous000papers/Code-For-SPA.

LIMITATIONS AND CONCLUSION
SPA preserves the known odd part and best uniform approximation error for activations with the
structure in (1). The measured error–cost trade-offs do not establish superiority over all optimized
even-polynomial evaluators. Model results are empirical, with task-specific calibration in two LLMs;
Gemma uses Exact-erf. Scalar guarantees exclude unverified configurations and out-of-range inputs.
Kernel speedups do not establish end-to-end gains.

C
PLAINTEXT EVALUATION PROTOCOL
Calibration and range assignment for Gemma are performed on the Exact-erf model, not copied from
the original tanh trajectory. Each method independently replaces the activation at the same sites; its
approximation is applied to the original activation input, not to an erf-GELU output. The static plan
and coefficients are frozen before evaluation. Test labels are not used to choose them.
Inputs and scoring.
The main results use zero-shot conditional sequence scores with plain prompts.
HellaSwag and Social IQa have 512-token input budgets, MMLU has 1,024, and RACE-high has
2,048. MMLU uses raw continuation log-likelihood; the other three tasks divide by the UTF-8 byte

CKKS precision instead keeps the full coefficients and varies scale/interior-prime bit sizes. The
balanced R20 degrees are k = 31 for erf-GELU, k = 39 for QuickGELU, and k = 23 for SiLU.

COEFFICIENT CONSTRUCTION AND EVALUATION ALGORITHMS
H.1
RECORDED NUMERICAL CONSTRUCTION
For each target, radius, and candidate k, Equation (10) is solved using SciPy linprog
(method="highs") in float64 with library-default tolerances. All k + 1 coefficients are free.
This is a finite-node minimax problem, not a least-squares problem.
During refinement of the ciphertext coefficients, the error is evaluated on a uniform 300,001-point
grid in t. Endpoints and local extrema satisfying |e| ≥0.80 max |e|, together with a maximum-error
point, are candidates for addition. Refinement stops if no new nodes are added, if the sampled
maximum is at most (1 + 2 × 10−8)ϵLP, or if the iteration cap is reached. A separate 300,001-point
grid in the original input interval checks the activation error.
The degree search fixes m = 4 for R5 and m = 8 for R20/wide, then increases the block count
B = 1, 2, . . . with candidate degree k = Bm −1. Thus B = ⌈(k + 1)/m⌉, matching Section 3.3;
the lowercase b in that section is only a block index.
