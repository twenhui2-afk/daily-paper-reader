Material criteria for angular-momentum transfer from twisted photons to
photoelectrons
A. A. Shchepkin1, 2, ∗and D. V. Karlovets1, 2
1School of Physics and Engineering, ITMO University, 197101 St. Petersburg, Russia
2Petersburg Nuclear Physics Institute named by B. P. Konstantinov of
National Research Centre “Kurchatov Institute,” 188300 Gatchina, Russia
We study the material requirements for transferring the total-angular-momentum (TAM) pro-
jection of a twisted photon to the orbital angular momentum (OAM) of a photoelectron emitted
from an n-type semiconductor surface. Within a single-valley effective-mass model of surface pho-
toemission, we calculate OAM-resolved spectra for GaAs, InSb, InAs0.34Sb0.66, and Hg0.76Cd0.24Te
irradiated by twisted photons with Laguerre–Gaussian–like momentum profile. The OAM trans-
fer is characterized by the mean electron OAM, its root-mean-squared width, and the fraction of
electrons with OAM-projection equal to the photon TAM-projection. Efficient transfer requires the
transverse momentum of the conduction electron to be much smaller than the transverse momentum
width of the photon, which favors low temperatures, small conduction-band effective masses, and
shallow donor levels. GaAs is less favorable within the present model because of its larger effective
mass and deeper donor levels.
We also discuss the demanding photon-wave packet parameters,
possible extensions to Dirac and Weyl semimetals, and post-emission OAM selection. Although the
generation of vortex electrons with a vanishing OAM uncertainty via photoemission seems challeng-
ing, we argue that it is still possible to generate single-electron vortex states with a finite OAM
dispersion.
Key words: Photoelectric effect, photocathode, semiconductors, twisted photon, vortex electron,
angular momentum.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Structured states of light and matter have become im-
portant tools in quantum optics, electron microscopy,
condensed-matter physics, and accelerator science. Vor-
tex, or twisted, states are distinguished by a phase sin-
gularity and a well-defined projection of angular momen-
tum onto a selected propagation axis [1–3]. For a free
electron, a state with an azimuthal phase factor exp(ilφ)
carries an orbital-angular-momentum (OAM) projection
Lz = ℏl. This additional degree of freedom can be used to
probe magnetic and chiral properties of matter, charac-
terize spatial symmetries, and introduce new observables
in scattering and radiation processes [3, 4]. Broader re-
views and recent developments of vortex-particle physics
can be found in [5–9].
Several methods for producing vortex electrons have
already been demonstrated.
These include nanofabri-
cated amplitude and phase holograms [2, 3, 10], mate-
rial and magnetic phase plates [11], aberration-controlled
electron lenses [12], and astigmatic mode converters
[13–15].
Although these methods have enabled exten-
sive studies in transmission electron microscopy, none of
them simultaneously provides arbitrary OAM, high cur-
rent, high transmission, ultrashort pulse duration, and
straightforward compatibility with an accelerator pho-
toinjector. The development of an intense and scalable
source of vortex electrons therefore remains an open ex-
perimental problem.
∗alexander.shchepkin@gmail.com
Photoemission driven by twisted light provides an at-
tractive alternative, as also suggested by the demon-
strated transfer of the photon total-angular-momentum
(TAM) projection to the electron OAM in atomic pho-
toionization [16]. If the azimuthal phase structure of the
electromagnetic field is transferred to an emitted elec-
tron, the photocathode can act as the first stage of a
vortex-electron source.
Such a source could generate
short electron pulses synchronized with the drive laser
and subsequently accelerate them to relativistic energies.
Unlike intercepting electron-optical elements placed af-
ter acceleration, the transfer would occur directly at the
cathode surface [17].
An accelerator implementation requires structured ra-
diation in the deep-ultraviolet (DUV) range, because the
photon energy must exceed the relevant photoemission
threshold. Recent experiments at the Joint Institute for
Nuclear Research (JINR) in Dubna, Russia, have demon-
strated the generation of optical vortices at wavelengths
of approximately 260–266 nm using amplitude and phase
spiral zone plates [18]. A phase spiral zone plate pro-
duced a beam with the topological charge lγ = 1 and a
conversion efficiency of approximately 40% [18]. More re-
cently, fork gratings, a spiral phase plate, and binary axi-
cons were integrated into an RF-photoinjector laser line.
The spiral phase plate generated a high-purity mode with
lγ = 64 and a measured optical conversion efficiency of
approximately 80% [19]. These developments establish
experimentally relevant DUV wavelengths and accessible
topological charges, although the tightly focused broad-
band packets used in the calculations remain more de-
manding than the demonstrated optical states.

Lightly doped n-InSb was identified as a
favorable material because of its small conduction-band
effective mass and shallow donor levels. Dirac and Weyl
semimetals were also suggested as prospective candidates
owing to their linear low-energy dispersion [17].
The material with the smallest effective mass is not
necessarily the most suitable experimental photocath-
ode.
A realistic assessment must also account for the
density of conduction states, donor freeze-out, surface
preparation, electron affinity or crystal-vacuum energy
offset, chemical stability, and the optical response at the
incident wavelength. In particular, increasing the doping
can provide more conduction electrons but also increases
the Fermi momentum and may broaden the OAM distri-
bution. Conversely, extremely light doping can improve
OAM selectivity while reducing the emitted charge.
In this work, we apply the theory from the work [17] to
a wider range of photocathode materials and determine
how the effective mass, donor binding energy, carrier con-
centration, and temperature control OAM transfer. InSb
is compared with the narrow-gap alloys InAs0.34Sb0.66,
Hg0.76Cd0.24Te, and with GaAs under a common set
of photon-wave-packet parameters. The results demon-
strate that the mean emitted OAM alone can substan-
tially overestimate the selectivity of the transfer and
should be considered together with the fraction in the
desired OAM state and the spectral width.

III.
RESULTS
A.
Calculation parameters
We consider four representative n-type semiconduc-
tors: GaAs, InSb, InAs0.34Sb0.66, and Hg0.76Cd0.24Te.
These materials are selected because they combine prac-
tical availability with favorable electronic properties rel-
evant to OAM transfer.
GaAs is included as a tech-
nologically mature photocathode material widely em-
ployed in electron sources, providing a practical ref-
erence despite its comparatively large conduction-band
mass. InSb serves as an established narrow-gap reference
material and allows a direct comparison with previous
calculations.
InAsxSb1−x and Hg1−xCdxTe are widely
studied compositionally tunable infrared materials whose
conduction-band effective masses can be substantially
smaller than those of most conventional semiconductors.
Their shallow donor levels also make low-temperature
population of the conduction band more feasible.
Other n-type semiconductors could, in principle, be
treated within the same formalism.
However, many
widely available alternatives possess larger conduction-
band effective masses, often M∗≳0.1M0, and substan-
tially deeper donor levels.
A larger effective mass in-
creases the characteristic momentum of the initially oc-
cupied conduction-electron states, whereas deeper donor
levels suppress their ionization at low temperature and


5
generally require operation at higher temperatures to
obtain an appreciable conduction-electron population.
Both effects make the hierarchy (13) more difficult to sat-
isfy and, therefore, lead, already at the qualitative level,
to broader photoelectron OAM distributions and poorer
desired-OAM selectivity.
We consequently restrict the
quantitative comparison to the four materials that both
offer particularly favorable band parameters and could
be of direct practical relevance as photocathodes.
The theory assumes a single approximately parabolic
conduction-band valley characterized by a finite, nearly
constant band mass M∗.
The materials are compared
using the same photon TAM projection mγ and the same
wave packet parameters ¯kz, σ⊥, and σz.
The narrow-
gap materials are studied at T = 0.5 and 4 K, whereas
GaAs is considered at T = 2.5 and 4 K because of its
larger donor ionization energy and the stronger freeze-
out of conduction electrons at lower temperatures. The
final-electron momentum components pf,⊥and pf,z are
chosen near the maximum of the photoelectron detection
probability.
The donor concentrations nd adopted for some of these
materials were deliberately chosen near the lower end of
their experimentally reported ranges in order to remain
within the weak-doping regime assumed by the present
model. In this regime, the donor-bound electron states
are treated as spatially isolated: their wave functions
have negligible overlap, and the discrete donor levels
do not merge into an impurity band. The applicability
of this approximation can be expressed approximately
through the Mott criterion (see, for instance, [22, 23]):

1.
GaAs
Gallium arsenide is one of the most widely used semi-
conductor photocathode materials.
GaAs-based nega-
tive electron affinity (NEA) cathodes are widely used for
polarized-electron sources and can provide high quantum
efficiency and low emittance [24, 25]. Their practical op-
eration, however, requires atomically clean surfaces, acti-
vation by Cs/O or related layers, and ultrahigh vacuum.
In this work GaAs is therefore treated as a common n-
type semiconductor.
Figure 2 shows the OAM-resolved photoelectron emis-
sion probability at T = 2.5 and 4 K. Under the pa-
rameters considered here, the spectra are broad already
at 2.5 K and become less selective as the temperature
is raised.
For bulk GaAs, the band-edge conduction-
electron mass is approximately M∗≃0.065 −0.070 M0
[26], while the binding energies of shallow donors are
typically about εd ≃5.8 −7 meV [27]. Donor concen-
tration is chosen to be nd ∼1016 cm−3, which lies in
the range of practically feasible values for GaAs nd ∼
1015 −1017 cm−3 [28, 29].
Donor freeze-out therefore
suppresses the conduction-electron population at suffi-
ciently low temperature. Heating ionizes a larger fraction
of the donors, but simultaneously increases the charac-
teristic transverse momentum of the initial electrons and
broadens the emitted-electron OAM spectrum. Within
the present n-type conduction-electron model, GaAs is
unfavorable for selective OAM transfer because its rela-
tively large effective mass broadens the initial momentum
distribution, while its comparatively large donor binding
energy strengthens carrier freeze-out at low temperature.
Increasing the donor concentration can improve the avail-
able electron population, but it also raises the Fermi mo-
mentum and further reduces the OAM selectivity. This


6
conclusion applies to the model considered here and does
not directly describe the usual activated NEA operating
regime of practical GaAs photocathodes.
2.
InSb
Indium antimonide provides the reference case for
the present comparison.
It is a technologically estab-
lished narrow-gap semiconductor used in infrared detec-
tors and magnetotransport devices [26, 30]. Among con-
ventional narrow-gap semiconductors, InSb has an ex-
ceptionally small band-edge conduction-electron mass,
M∗≈0.014 M0 [26], and very shallow donor levels,
εd ≈7×10−4 eV [31], which reduce the characteristic mo-
mentum of the thermally occupied conduction-electron
states.
This helps to satisfy the momentum hierarchy
pi,⊥≪pf,⊥≃k⊥underlying the approximate selec-
tion rule le = mγ, and, therefore, makes InSb a natural
benchmark for achieving high-fidelity transfer of angu-
lar momentum from the incident twisted photon to the
emitted photoelectron. Shallow donor concentration is
nd ∼1014 cm−3 [32].
The first pair of rows in Fig. 3 shows the calculated
InSb spectra at T = 0.5 and 4 K. At 0.5 K, the mγ = 1
distribution is strongly concentrated in the desired OAM
channel: η1 = 87%, ⟨le⟩= 0.9, and ∆le = 0.4.
For
mγ = 5 and 16, the mean electron OAM remains close
to the incident-photon TAM projection, with ⟨le⟩= 4.8
and 15.7, while the desired-OAM fractions are 55% and
35%, respectively. The nonmonotonic change of ηm be-
tween these two values shows that the detailed spectrum
is governed not only by a single thermal broadening scale
but also by the structure of the transition amplitude. For
mγ = 64, the mean remains large, ⟨le⟩= 63.0, but the de-
sired OAM channel contains only 16% of the normalized
probability and the rms width reaches ∆le = 2.5. Thus,
a nearly correct mean OAM does not imply preparation
of a nearly pure le = 64 state.
At 4 K, all four InSb distributions broaden and become
substantially less selective. The mean values decrease to
0.5, 4.2, 14.7, and 62.5 for mγ = 1, 5, 16, and 64, respec-
tively, whereas the rms widths increase to 0.9, 1.4, 2.1,
and 3.1. The corresponding desired-OAM fractions fall
to 45%, 24%, 18%, and 8%. The downward displacement
of the means indicates preferential redistribution toward
channels with le < mγ. The mγ = 64 case is especially
illustrative: its mean OAM is still close to the photon
TAM projection, but only 8% of the detected electrons
occupy the desired OAM channel.
3.
InAsxSb1−x
InAsxSb1−x is a compositionally tunable narrow-gap
III–V alloy widely studied for infrared detection [33]. Its
conduction-band mass can remain small, while its band
gap, dielectric response, and carrier properties can be
adjusted through the As/Sb ratio.
The present calcu-
lations use InAs0.34Sb0.66, a composition close to the
minimum-gap region of the alloy system [33]. We adopt
M∗= 0.009 M0, consistent with the band-edge masses
reported for Sb-rich and near-minimum-gap InAsxSb1−x
alloys [26].
The donor concentration was set to nd =
1014 cm−3 as a weak-doping model parameter.
Back-
ground dopant or carrier concentrations in the 1014–
1015 cm−3 range have been experimentally reported for
high-quality InAsSb epilayers [34].
The shallow-donor
ionization energy εd = 6×10−4 eV is estimated within the
hydrogenic effective-mass model.
Finally, the crystal–
vacuum offset U0 = 4.7 eV is based on the composition-
dependent electron-affinity interpolation tabulated for
InAsxSb1−x [35].
Thus, these quantities should be
regarded as a consistent parameter set for the cal-
culation rather than as universal properties of every
InAs0.34Sb0.66 sample.
The middle pair of rows in Fig. 3 demonstrates the
strong localization of the spectra around the desired
channels for mγ = 1 and 5 at T = 0.5 K. Their desired-
OAM fractions are 91% and 66%, with mean OAM val-
ues of 1.0 and 4.9 and rms widths of 0.4 and 0.7, re-
spectively. For mγ = 16, the mean remains close to the
incident value, ⟨le⟩= 15.7, but η16 decreases to 36% and
∆le = 1.2. For mγ = 64, the spectrum has ⟨le⟩= 63.4,
∆le = 2.1, and η64 = 17%.
At 4 K, the mean values decrease to 0.6, 4.4, 15.0, and
62.7 for mγ = 1, 5, 16, and 64, while the rms widths
increase to 0.9, 1.2, 1.9, and 3.0. The desired-OAM frac-
tions are 55%, 28%, 16%, and 15%, respectively. The
modest decrease of η64 from 17% to 15% should not be
interpreted as thermal stability of a pure OAM mode:
over the same temperature interval the rms width grows
from 2.1 to 3.0 and the mean moves farther below 64.
This behavior confirms that ηm must be considered to-

IV.
DISCUSSION
A.
General trends
The results reveal the common behavior shared by the
narrow-gap semiconductors considered in this work. In-
creasing the photocathode temperature from 0.5 to 4 K
broadens the photoelectron OAM distributions, increases
their rms widths ∆le, and generally reduces the desired-
OAM fraction ηm. This loss of selectivity becomes more
pronounced as the TAM projection, mγ, of the incident


9
photon is increased. Moreover, the mean electron OAM
shifts below the desired value mγ at 4 K for all the con-
sidered materials.
Within the current model, twisted-
photon photoemission therefore cannot generally be re-
garded as the direct preparation of a pure electron OAM
eigenstate with le = mγ.
The mean OAM alone is not a reliable measure of the
modal purity of the emitted electrons. For example, at
T = 0.5 K and mγ = 64, the mean values for the three
narrow-gap materials remain between 63.0 and 63.4, al-
though only 16 −17% of the electrons are found in the
desired le = 64 state. At 4 K, the corresponding mean
values remain between 62.5 and 62.8, whereas the frac-
tion in that desired state decreases to 8 −15%. Thus,
a mean electron OAM close to the photon TAM projec-
tion can coexist with a broad distribution over neigh-
boring OAM channels. The quantities ⟨le⟩, ∆le, and ηm
must therefore be considered together when evaluating
the quality of OAM transfer.
The broadening originates from the finite momentum
distribution of the initially occupied electronic states in
the crystal. The photoemission probability is averaged
over this thermal ensemble (2), and electrons with differ-
ent initial transverse momenta acquire different correc-
tions to the ideal selection rule le = mγ, as seen from
(12). This, therefore, gives an incoherent mixture of dif-
ferent OAM components in the final state, rather than
a coherent superposition of OAM modes. The quantity
∆le thus characterizes the width of this OAM distribu-
tion. A coherent superposition would imply nonzero off-
diagonal elements of the emitted-electron density matrix,
which are not described by the present calculation. Effi-
cient OAM transfer requires the characteristic transverse
momentum pi,⊥of the initial electrons to remain small
compared with the transverse momentum width of the
incident photon packet,
pi,⊥≪σ⊥.
(21)
This condition is favored by low photocathode tem-
peratures, small conduction-band effective masses, and
shallow donor levels. It also shows that the transfer effi-
ciency is determined jointly by the material parameters,
temperature, and momentum-space structure of the in-
cident twisted-photon wave packet.

GaAs is less favorable within the
present bulk n-type conduction-electron model because
its larger effective mass produces a wider thermal mo-

surface preparation,
crystal–vacuum

A finite OAM width is not necessarily a drawback. Co-
herent occupation of several OAM modes can provide
a high-dimensional degree of freedom, as demonstrated
for OAM-entangled photons [37], and wave-packet phases
and transverse coherence can play an essential role in
scattering observables [7, 38].
Thus, if photoemission
preserves off-diagonal coherence between OAM compo-
nents, or if the emitted electron becomes entangled with
another degree of freedom or another particle, a con-
trolled nonzero ∆le may become a useful resource rather
than merely a loss of mode purity. However, the present
OAM populations Ple do not establish such coherence
or entanglement.
Testing this possibility requires a
density-matrix calculation and correlation-sensitive mea-
surements.
The incomplete OAM selectivity of semiconductor pho-
toemission suggests two complementary directions for
further development. The first is to consider materials
with more favorable low-energy band structures, includ-
ing Dirac and Weyl semimetals, although their descrip-
tion requires an extension beyond the parabolic effective-
mass approximation. The second is to separate the emit-
ted OAM components after photoemission and postselect
the desired channel using an electron OAM sorter. These
possibilities are discussed below.

C.
Preparation of OAM-selected electron states
with mode sorters
The photocathode does not need to emit electrons ex-
clusively in the desired-OAM state. If several OAM com-
ponents are produced, a downstream OAM sorter can
map them onto different spatial channels. An aperture
can then transmit the desired component le = mγ and
block the others.
This makes it possible to obtain an
electron beam with high OAM selectivity, although at
the cost of reducing the total current. In the ideal case,
the fraction of electrons available for postselection is lim-
ited by the desired-OAM fraction ηm calculated above.
Such postselection becomes particularly useful at finite
temperatures and for photons with large mγ, when the
emitted-electron OAM distribution broadens.
Sorting alone does not increase the purity of the elec-
tron state: it only separates its OAM components spa-
tially. Purification is achieved conditionally by transmit-
ting one output channel. Moreover, selection of a defi-
nite le does not necessarily select a unique radial mode,
electron energy, or transverse momentum.
The result-
ing beam should therefore be described as OAM-selected
rather than as a completely pure transverse quantum
state.
A magnetic-quadrupole Laguerre-Gaussian–Hermite-
Gaussian (LG–HG) converter [13–15] provides one possi-
ble method for analysing electron vortex modes. It uses
astigmatic focusing to transform a Laguerre–Gaussian
mode into a Hermite–Gaussian mode of the same total
transverse order,
N = 2n + |le| = sx + sy,
(25)
where n is the radial index of the Laguerre–Gaussian
mode and sx and sy are the transverse mode indices
of the Hermite–Gaussian mode [13–15].
This method
is not well suited to the present problem because the
photoemitted electrons occupy a mixture of OAM, ra-
dial, and transverse-momentum states. The correspond-
ing HG-like intensity patterns would overlap, and dif-
ferent (p, l) states can have the same value of N. The
converter therefore does not map each value of le onto a
unique spatial channel.

A log-polar conformal sorter provides a more direct
method for separating the OAM components. It imple-

Log-polar sorting of electron

each output channel a nonzero width, so neighboring

electron-optical design.
2.
Alternative OAM analyzers
A Dammann vortex analyzer [44] performs several pre-
determined OAM projections simultaneously.
A mul-
tiplexed grating sends the incident electron wave into
multiple diffraction orders, each containing the conjugate
phase of a selected OAM state. If the incident beam con-
tains the corresponding component, its azimuthal phase
is cancelled and a compact correlation peak appears in
that diffraction order. Electron OAM components from
−10ℏto +10ℏhave been measured using this method
[44]. The analyzer is compact and suitable for parallel
measurements, but it probes only the finite set of OAM
values encoded in the grating, divides the available inten-
sity among several diffraction orders, and has a response
that depends on the radial profile of the incident beam.
A Stern–Gerlach-like OAM analyzer instead uses the
interaction of the electron orbital magnetic moment with
a cylindrically symmetric inhomogeneous magnetic field
[45]. Different OAM components acquire different focal
lengths and can, therefore, be distinguished through their
radial distributions. This noninterferometric method can
operate on incoherent mixtures and, because it preserves
the azimuthal phase, may not require an inverse transfor-
mation after postselection. Its main limitations are the
weak OAM-dependent magnetic dispersion expected for
realistic fields and its sensitivity to electron energy, beam
size, divergence, and radial-mode content. The lower ki-
netic energies considered in the present work may en-
hance the OAM-dependent focusing, but a separate low-
energy electron-optical analysis is required to establish
whether this method can provide sufficient resolution.

The complete photoemission-and-sorting scheme pro-
vides a conceptually different route to vortex-electron
generation from established electron-optical methods.
Electron vortex beams have already been produced using
spiral phase plates [2], nanofabricated diffraction holo-
grams [3, 10], magnetic-needle “chopstick” phase plates
[11, 52], and tunable electrostatic spiral phase plates [53].
These techniques are experimentally mature and can pro-
vide high efficiency, high mode purity, and very large
OAM.
Its main potential advantage is that the OAM is intro-
duced already at the electron-generation stage. The elec-
tron inherits angular momentum, as well as energy and
linear momentum components and their widths, from the
structured optical field during photoemission rather than
acquiring it subsequently from an electron-optical phase
element. The photon TAM projection and other photon
characteristics therefore become external control param-
eters of the source, which can in principle be changed op-
tically without replacing an electron hologram or phase
plate. Moreover, because the electrons are generated by
a pulsed optical field, their emission is intrinsically syn-
chronized with the driving laser, making the approach
naturally compatible with ultrafast pump–probe config-
urations.
The photocathode does not need to produce a per-
fectly pure le = mγ state. Instead, it generates an OAM
distribution Ple, while the sorter spatially resolves its
components and postselects the desired one. This makes
it possible to control the trade-off between transmitted
current and OAM purity. At the same time, measuring
Ple as a function of temperature, carrier density, photon
TAM projection, mγ, and photocathode material directly
probes the microscopic transfer of angular momentum
in photoemission, whereas conventional holograms and
phase plates simply impose a prescribed phase on an al-
ready formed electron beam.
The JINR program provides a concrete route for test-
ing these ideas: structured DUV light with large OAM
has already been integrated into the RF-photoinjector
laser line [19], while the test-bench beam-dynamics stud-
ies explicitly target relativistic vortex-electron genera-
tion [46].
If OAM-carrying photoelectrons are subse-
quently accelerated, the preservation and manipulation
of their angular momentum in accelerator fields become
relevant, as discussed in [54]. Besides the near-eigenstate
regime, experiments could deliberately explore electron
states with a finite OAM width and test whether inter-
mode coherence survives emission and acceleration.
The price for this additional functionality is substan-
tial experimental complexity: a suitable photocathode,
a stable structured UV source, tight focusing and trans-
verse coherence, operation in a material regime favorable
for OAM transfer, and a downstream OAM analyzer are
all required, while postselection inevitably reduces the
usable current. Therefore, the proposed scheme should
be viewed as a complementary vortex-electron source ar-
chitecture in which electron OAM is optically controlled
at emission and subsequently purified by mode sorting.
V.
CONCLUSION
We have studied the transfer of the angular-momentum
projection of a twisted photon to photoelectrons emitted
from weakly doped n-type GaAs, InSb, InAs0.34Sb0.66,
and Hg0.76Cd0.24Te. Within the single-valley parabolic-
band model, selective population of the channel le = mγ
requires the characteristic transverse momentum of the
initial electrons to be small compared with the transverse
momentum scales of the photon wavepacket. This condi-
tion is favored by low temperature, a small conduction-


13
band effective mass, and shallow donor levels.
Under the adopted parameters, InAs0.34Sb0.66 and
Hg0.76Cd0.24Te produce OAM distributions comparable
to or narrower than those obtained for InSb, whereas
GaAs is less selective because of its larger effective mass
and stronger donor freeze-out. The comparison should
not be regarded as a universal ranking of photocathodes,
since the present calculations do not determine the ab-
solute photoemission yield and do not describe activated
negative-electron-affinity cathodes.
The mean electron OAM alone does not characterize
the selectivity of the transfer. At T = 0.5 K and mγ =
64, the mean OAM remains between 63.0 and 63.4 for
the three narrow-gap materials, although only 16–17%
of the normalized probability occupies the desired OAM
channel. At 4 K, this fraction decreases to 8–15%, while
the rms width reaches approximately 3. The mean OAM,
rms width, and desired-OAM fraction must therefore be
considered together.
A proof-of-principle experiment is most realistic for
small or moderate photon TAM projections, such as
mγ = 1 or 5, and would require a cryogenic photocathode
together with a broadband, tightly focused DUV vortex
pulse. If several OAM components are emitted, a down-
stream log-polar sorter and a mode-selective aperture can
isolate the desired le = mγ channel, at the cost of re-
ducing the electron current. Dirac and Weyl semimetals
may provide more favorable carrier-momentum scales,
but their quantitative treatment requires a multiband
photoemission theory and cannot be obtained by simply
setting M∗= 0 in the present model.
The JINR RF-photoinjector project [19, 46] offers a di-
rect experimental setting for testing twisted-photon pho-
toemission, OAM analysis, and subsequent acceleration,
for which the OAM dynamics discussed in [54] becomes
relevant. The presently used copper cathode is unlikely
to provide narrow OAM spectra because of its large Fermi
momentum, but it can serve as a benchmark for deliber-
ately broad distributions. Beyond postselection of a sin-
gle OAM channel, future JINR experiments could there-
fore also investigate electron states with a controlled fi-
nite OAM dispersion and, with correlation-sensitive diag-
nostics, test whether such multimode states can be used
for generating OAM-entangled states.
