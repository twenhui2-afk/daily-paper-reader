Cox-Ingersoll-Ross Equation

Abstract
We introduce a positivity-preserving drift-implicit square-root Euler method for the
free Cox–Ingersoll–Ross (CIR) equation driven by free Brownian motion. The method
is obtained by discretizing the square-root equation implicitly in the drift and preserves
strict positivity of the numerical solution without any step-size restriction. Under the
Feller condition, we establish the uniqueness of the global strictly positive solution and
derive an upper bound for its operator norm. A key ingredient for the error analysis is
a free stochastic differential representation of the inverse square-root process. Another
key ingredient is an a priori grid-point error estimate for sufficiently small step sizes that
yields uniform upper and strictly positive lower spectral bounds for the numerical solution
on any fixed finite time interval. These results support the strong convergence of order
1 and improve the classical results. Moreover, both the exact and numerical solutions
are exponentially stable with respect to perturbations of the initial value. The numerical
stability holds for arbitrary step sizes, and the associated discrete decay rate converges to
its continuous counterpart as the step size tends to zero. Numerical experiments confirm
the theoretical results.
Key Words: free Cox–Ingersoll–Ross equation, drift–implicit method, positivity preserv-
ing, strong convergence, exponential stability

The free CIR equation is an important type of free stochastic differential equations (SDEs).
They describe the evolution of operators in free probability, where classical independence is
replaced by free independence [32,33]. The theory of free SDEs was developed through the free
stochastic calculus of Biane-Speicher [4, 5] and subsequently in the work of Kargin [22]. One
important motivation for studying free SDEs comes from random matrix theory: free SDEs
can be regarded as limits of matrix-valued SDEs. For instance, suitably normalized Hermitian
matrix-valued Brownian motions converge to free Brownian motion [31], while related random-
matrix limits include free Wishart processes [6], free diffusions with convex potential [14], and
general free SDEs with suitable assumptions [24]. In this sense, the free CIR equation can be
viewed as the limit of matrix-valued CIR processes [24], which provides potential applications to
the spectral analysis of high-dimensional stochastic covariance models in multivariate finance.
The classical CIR equation is widely used in models of interest rates, stochastic volatility,
and credit risk [8, 12, 18]. There is a sizable literature dedicated to CIR-type numerical ap-
proximations within the classical framework (e.g., [1–3, 9, 11, 15–17, 20, 26, 28]), with various
schemes constructed specifically for its square-root diffusion coefficient. In particular, Dereich,
Neuenkirch and Szpruch [11] established strong convergence of order 1/2 at the grid points un-
der the strict Feller condition 2a > σ2. Alfonsi [3], Neuenkirch and Szpruch [28] subsequently
established strong convergence of order 1 under stronger parameter restrictions than the strict
Feller condition. Recently, following this line of research, Liu, Neuenkirch and Wang [23] im-
proved the convergence order to 3/2 under additional assumptions. This naturally raises the
question of whether one can develop a numerical method in the free setting that preserves the
strict positivity of the solution.
However, due to the noncommutativity of the operators, the classical methods cannot be
directly adapted to the free setting. To the best of our knowledge, only two works [29,30] have
developed numerical schemes for free SDEs with locally operator Lipschitz coefficients. In [30],
the authors established strong convergence of order 1/2 in L1(φ) for the Euler–Maruyama
scheme, while the authors of [29] established strong convergence of order 1/2 in L2(φ) for the
θ-method. However, the free CIR equation lies outside those frameworks, and their numerical
schemes, when applied to the free CIR equation, do not in general preserve the positivity. In this
paper, we construct a drift-implicit Euler method. Under the Feller condition, we prove strong
convergence of the method and establish long-time exponential stability in L2(φ) with respect
to perturbations of the initial value for the exact and numerical solutions. More importantly,
the proposed method preserves strict positivity of the solution; see Theorem 10. Beyond the free
CIR setting, the numerical construction and analytical techniques derived here may be capable
to further studies of structure-preserving numerical methods for free SDEs with non-Lipschitz
coefficients.
