LIE ALGEBRAS IN SYMMETRIC TENSOR CATEGORIES 

I. ANGIONO, A. CZENKY, P. ETINGOF, V. OSTRIK, J. PLAVNIK, AND G. SANMARCO 

Abstract. We develop the general theory of Lie algebras in symmetric tensor categories. We prove categorical versions of classical results such as Engel’s theorem, Cartan’s criterion, and that all derivations of a strongly semisimple Lie algebra are inner. For finite-dimensional graded Lie algebras, we construct a graded category O, classify its graded-simple objects, and prove the generic graded irreducibility of Verma modules. Lastly, we develop the theory of root systems for contragredient Lie algebras.

The guiding question of this paper is how much of the architecture of classical Lie theory survives in symmetric tensor categories over fields of arbitrary characteristic. Many classical arguments depend on properties of vector spaces that need not persist in this setting. For instance, in positive characteristic, a skew-symmetric bracket satisfying the Jacobi identity need not have the PBW property [E1]. Thus, extending Lie theory from vector spaces to symmetric tensor categories requires more than a formal translation. There is a particular reason to ask this question now. Over a field of characteristic zero, Deligne proved that every pre-Tannakian category of moderate growth admits a fiber functor to supervector spaces [De], and hence can be reconstructed from ordinary or super group theory. The direct analogue fails in characteristic p. The fundamental

Moreover, Ver p plays an important role: Ostrik proved that every symmetric fusion category in characteristic p admits a symmetric tensor functor to Ver p [O], and Coulembier, Etingof, and Ostrik subsequently extended this result to semisimple pre-Tannakian categories of moderate growth [CEO]. The study of algebraic structures in symmetric tensor categories has expanded considerably in recent years. Much of the recent work has focused on the Verlinde categories, including affine group schemes and Harish–Chandra pairs [V2, M2], representations of general linear groups [V1, K, U], categorical Chevalley–Eilenberg cohomology [EH], and the construction and classification of Lie algebra objects [APS1, EKO, N]. Contragredient Lie algebras have also been defined in arbitrary symmetric tensor categories [APS2]. The present paper develops some of the general tools needed for this program. Let C be a symmetric tensor category over an algebraically closed field k, and let g be an operadic Lie algebra in C. Although the natural map g → U (g) need not be injective [E1], its kernel is always central. If C is quasi-semisimple, meaning that symmetric algebras preserve monomorphisms, then the quotient by this kernel has the PBW property; see Proposition 2.3. In particular, every simple operadic Lie algebra in a quasi-semisimple symmetric tensor category has the PBW property. Our first main result is a categorical version of Engel’s theorem: if the underlying object of a finite-dimensional Lie algebra g is semisimple and every simple subobject of g acts nilpotently on a finite-dimensional representation V , then V admits a finite filtration strictly lowered by g; see Theorem 3.3. We then study invariant bilinear forms. We call an operadic Lie algebra quadratic if it admits a symmetric invariant nondegenerate form, and strongly semisimple if its Killing form is nondegenerate. We prove that a finite-dimensional quadratic operadic Lie algebra with no non-zero abelian ideals is semisimple. Applied to the Killing form, this yields a categorical Cartan criterion, see Theorem 4.10. We also show that every derivation of a strongly semisimple Lie algebra is inner. We next establish a rigidity result for Lie algebra homomorphisms. In classical theory, the vanishing of the first Chevalley–Eilenberg cohomology gives rigidity only up to conjugation. In C, however, a non-zero object X may be invariantless , meaning that Hom C (1, X ) = 0. For an invariantless a, we prove that the affine scheme of Lie algebra homomorphisms g → a

is finite and reduced when the relevant first cohomology vanishes. In particular, this holds when g is linearly reductive. This result has no nontrivial analogue in ordinary vector spaces, where the only invariantless object is zero. We then define a graded category O and Verma modules ∆( X, d ). A pseudo-tensor category over k is a k-linear Karoubian rigid monoidal category whose tensor product is k-bilinear, with finite-dimensional Hom spaces and End( 1) = k. A tensor category is a pseudo-tensor category that is abelian and in which every object has finite length. When a pseudo-tensor category is endowed with a braided structure, we say the category is braided. If the braiding satisfies that the square of the braid is trivial for every pair of objects, we call the category symmetric. For the general theory of tensor categories, we follow [EGNO]. Unless stated otherwise, C is a symmetric tensor category with simple unit over the algebraically closed field k; it is not assumed semisimple. Tensor product and duality are exact. We suppress associativity constraints and symmetry morphisms, and write Cind for the ind-completion. An object of Cind is called finite-dimensional if it belongs to C.We call C quasi-semisimple if every monomorphism X → Y induces a monomorphism 

S(X) → S(Y ) [E1, Definition 7.5]. Every semisimple symmetric tensor category is quasi-semisimple. In characteristic zero, every symmetric tensor category is quasi-semisimple, since symmetric powers are the images of symmetrizer idempotents on tensor powers. In positive characteristic quasi-semisimplicity may fail, although all known counterexamples are in characteristic 2 [E1, §7.3]. 2.1. Schematic notation.
