Examples include Stirling numbers of the second kind [2], truncated exponential sums [1], Eulerian numbers [3], and a sequence arising from a rational integral [8]. Givens and Moll [5] constructed a tree structure for the FiboLaplace sequence that organizes the congruence classes of indices according to the p-adic valuations of the corresponding terms. A vertex of such a tree represents a congruence class of indices. It is called terminal when the valuation is constant on that class and nonterminal when further refinement is required. An internal branch is called simple when every level contains exactly one nonterminal vertex. For even indices, Givens and Moll established a lifting criterion that guarantees the simplicity of an internal branch. They subsequently asked whether every even internal branch must be simple. The first objective of this paper is to answer this question. We show that the answer is negative by giving an explicit counterexample for p = 971. We also observe that the analogous statement for odd internal branches already fails for p = 3. Our second objective is to provide an algebraic interpretation of the lifting criteria of Givens and Moll. We associate polynomials Pp(x) and Qp(x) with the even and odd subsequences, respectively. Their roots modulo p correspond to the internal roots of the associated valuation trees. Thus, the nonvanishing conditions in the Givens–Moll criteria are equivalent to the corresponding roots being simple modulo p. It follows that a simple root of Pp(x) or Qp(x)gives rise to a simple even or odd internal branch, respectively. Finally, we use the lifting congruences constructively. Along a simple branch, they determine the unique surviving residue class at each successive level and therefore provide a recursive method for computing the corresponding p-adic valuations. We illustrate this procedure in detail for p = 13, where the four even internal branches are simple and their successive surviving residues can be computed one base-13 digit at a time.
