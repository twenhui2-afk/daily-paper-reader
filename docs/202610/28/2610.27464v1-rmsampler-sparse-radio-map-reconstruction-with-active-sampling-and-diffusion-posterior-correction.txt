# RMSampler: Sparse Radio Map Reconstruction with Active Sampling and Diffusion Posterior Correction 

Nan Cheng and Xiucheng Wang 

Abstract Reconstructing a radio map (RM) from sparse received-power measurements supports non-cooperative interfer-ence monitoring and spectrum management. However, sparse measurements leave uncertainty about both the global RM and the interfering emitter location, making informative sampling and accurate reconstruction challenging. To address these chal-lenges, a novel joint sampling and reconstruction method named RMSampler is proposed in this paper to improve measurement selection and RM reconstruction under limited measurement budgets. For measurement selection, RMSampler evaluates global RM error reduction using the covariance of diffusion-generated RMs and localization error reduction using candidate emitter locations. Because these errors have different units, their expected relative reductions are combined into a scale-invariant sampling criterion, with each acquired measurement updating both un-certainty estimates. After sampling, the updated emitter-location probabilities guide a separate conditional diffusion ensemble for final reconstruction. To improve measurement agreement, an effective sample size (ESS) target guides robust posterior cor-rection, followed by a minimum-distortion projection enforcing measurement and building constraints. For the reconstruction ensemble, analysis establishes that increasing likelihood power reduces expected robust measurement loss, while pretrained neu-ral parameters and architecture remain unchanged. Experiments using simulated and real-world measured data demonstrate improved reconstruction under matched measurement budgets. In the Chongqing real-world measured data experiment, root mean square error (RMSE) decreases from 3.54 to 2.73 dB relative to random sampling under a common reconstruction method. 

Index Terms Radio map reconstruction, diffusion model, measurement-location optimization, training-free inference, pos-terior correction, active sampling. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Reconstructing a radio map (RM) from sparse received-power measurements enables non-cooperative interference monitoring across a region [1]. By estimating received power at unmeasured locations, the RM identifies interference-affected areas and supports spectrum management [2]. In non-cooperative scenarios, the interfering emitter location and propagation parameters can be unavailable, making receiver measurements and environmental information central to recon-struction [3]. Acquiring these measurements consumes sensing time and equipment resources, so exhaustive measurement campaigns are costly [4]. A limited measurement budget there-fore requires informative sampling locations and an inference method that effectively uses the acquired values. However, sparse measurements leave uncertainty in both the global RM and the interfering emitter location, creating

a joint estimation problem for measurement selection. Build-ing blockage and multipath propagation produce received-power variations that simple distance-based models cannot capture [5]. Learned propagation priors help reconstruct these variations from sparse observations [6], and diffusion-based in-verse estimation reconstructs the RM when the emitter location is unknown [3]. For selecting further measurements, predictive uncertainty guides Gaussian process (GP) active learning [4] and flow-matching-based RM sensing [7]. Evaluating the resulting reconstruction benefit across the RM additionally requires the spatial dependence between a candidate measure-ment and unmeasured locations [8]. Mobile GP sensing also addresses RM reconstruction and emitter localization through confidence-based acquisition [9]. These estimation objectives motivate a common criterion that evaluates how the same measurement reduces global RM error and emitter-location error. Normalizing the expected reductions in these errors allows both estimation objectives to guide the next sampling deci-sion on a common scale. Candidate RMs from a pretrained diffusion model [10] provide spatial covariance between a candidate measurement and unmeasured RM locations. Updat-ing the candidate weights with acquired measurements makes this covariance reflect the current reconstruction uncertainty. Under a Gaussian approximation, it predicts the candidate measurement’s reduction in global RM error. For emitter lo-calization, received-power predictions under candidate emitter locations quantify how the same observation could reduce expected coordinate error. Dividing each predicted reduction by the corresponding current expected error accounts for their different units and magnitudes. The normalized sum therefore ranks sampling locations by their joint estimation benefit, and each acquired value updates the RM weights and emitter-location probabilities used for the next decision. To implement this joint criterion, a novel sampling and RM reconstruction method named RMSampler is proposed in this paper to improve measurement selection and reconstruction under a limited measurement budget. The criterion guides sequential sampling while the acquired values update the probabilities of candidate emitter locations. These probabilities then condition a separate diffusion ensemble for final RM reconstruction, connecting the information obtained during sampling to the reconstruction prior. To improve agreement with the acquired measurements, the generated RMs are reweighted by a robust measurement likelihood. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows. 1) We formulate measurement-budgeted RM reconstruction as a joint sequential estimation and sampling problem with an unknown interfering emitter location. Each acquired measurement updates RM reconstruction un-certainty and emitter-location uncertainty, allowing both estimation objectives to inform subsequent sampling and final reconstruction. 2) We derive a sampling criterion from fractional reduc-tions in expected global RM error and emitter-location coordinate error. Spatial covariance evaluates the recon-struction contribution, while candidate emitter locations evaluate the localization contribution. We establish in-variance to changes in received-power scale and offset when the corresponding variances are rescaled. Under Gaussian and finite-hypothesis approximations, maxi-mizing the criterion minimizes a clipped normalized one-step joint estimation error. 3) We develop ESS-guided robust posterior correction for a separately generated conditional RM ensemble. II. R ELATED WORK 

RM reconstruction combines sparse measurements with spatial dependencies learned or specified before inference. Interpolation and graph regularization exploit these dependen-cies directly [1], while neural completion learns propagation patterns from other environments [6]. Low-rank neural models extend completion to multidimensional radio data [13]. For urban RMs, RadioUNet learns the effect of geometry on re-ceived power [5], and DeepREM estimates unmeasured values from sparse observations [14]. Conditional adversarial learning provides another data-driven reconstruction approach [15]. These methods supply reconstruction mechanisms, while ac-tive sampling additionally requires the expected benefit of a measurement before its value is observed. Generative priors represent multiple reconstructions consis-tent with incomplete observations. Learned generative repre-sentations constrain inverse recovery [16], [17], and diffusion and score-based models generate samples through iterative denoising [18], [19]. Latent diffusion performs this generation in a learned compressed representation [20]. In RM construc-tion, RadioDiff supplies a geometry-conditioned propagation prior [10]. Its extensions address three-dimensional RMs [21], multipath structure through the Helmholtz equation [22], and Bayesian recovery from noisy sparse measurements [3]. The broader relationship between forward prediction, inverse re-construction, and physical information is reviewed in [2]. Diffusion inverse solvers incorporate measurement consistency through manifold corrections [23], denoising restoration [24], likelihood-guided posterior sampling [25], or pseudoinverse guidance [26]. These approaches use observations at specified locations, motivating an additional decision rule for selecting the next measurement. Active sampling assesses a measurement before acquisition through a specified estimation objective. Bayesian experimen-tal design formalizes this decision [27], with expected-error reduction [28], mutual-information sensor placement [8], and upper-confidence acquisition [29] providing different criteria. Adaptive source localization uses observations to reduce loca-tion uncertainty [30]. For RM reconstruction, GP ensembles supply predictive uncertainty [4], and GP upper-confidence-bound sampling with cross-entropy planning (GP-UCB-CE) 3

incorporates receiver motion [9]. Flow matching also uses generative diversity to guide measurement selection [7]. Geometry-aware Cholesky projection (GACP) uses environ-mental geometry, and GACP with autoencoder reconstruc-tion (GACP-AE) combines that selection with learned RM completion [31]. Energy-modified leverage sampling (EMLS) exploits low-rank structure [32]. RMSampler evaluates the expected RM and emitter-coordinate error reductions of the same candidate observation, with normalization making their contributions comparable. Real-world measured data allow these reconstruction pri-ors to be evaluated under propagation conditions that differ from training. The Vienna dataset combines city-scale fourth-generation (4G) and fifth-generation (5G) radio measurements with high-resolution building and terrain models [11], and the Chongqing macro-cell dataset provides ground and aerial measurements with complex-terrain descriptors [12]. Their recorded receiver measurements and environmental data com-plement complete simulated RMs by supporting evaluation at actual receiver locations. III. S YSTEM MODEL AND PROBLEM FORMULATION 

Sequential sampling and RM reconstruction are linked through the posterior updated by each acquired measurement. The current posterior predicts a candidate measurement’s expected benefit, while its acquired value updates the infor-mation used for subsequent sampling and reconstruction. We express this dependence through expected RM reconstruction error and expected emitter-location error. The building map is known throughout inference, and noisy outdoor received-power measurements arrive sequentially without access to the true emitter coordinate.

J OINT ACTIVE SAMPLING AND RM R ECONSTRUCTION 

RMSampler links sequential sampling to final RM recon-struction through the measurement-updated emitter-location posterior, using separate diffusion ensembles for the two tasks, as illustrated in Fig. 2. Spatial correlations from an unconditional pixel-domain ensemble support sampling, with each acquired value updating its weights and the finite location probabilities. Once sampling ends, the retained location modes condition a latent-diffusion ensemble for reconstruction. Mea-surement agreement then determines its posterior weights under ESS control, and projection enforces known values. Both networks retain their pretrained parameters.

E XPERIMENTAL EVALUATION 

The evaluation isolates sampling gains by applying the same conditional diffusion reconstructor to RMSampler, Uniform, and Random on simulated RMs. External baselines retain their complete pipelines. On real-world measured data, common-location and common-reconstructor controls separate recon-struction quality from sampling effectiveness. 

A. Protocol, Baselines, and Metrics 

The simulation study uses dominant-path-model RMs from RadioMapSeer [5]. A fixed scene-level permutation with seed 42 partitions the data into 500 training, 100 validation, and 100 test scenarios, with no scene overlap between partitions. We evaluate all 100 test scenarios, which are unseen during training, using frozen diffusion models for zero-shot RM reconstruction. Each scenario provides 80 Tx positions with a separate single-Tx RM for each position. We use the first provided Tx configuration in each test scenario, giving one 

256 × 256 RM per scenario with only that Tx’s contribution. The exact building map is available at inference as stipulated in Section III, while the true Tx coordinate and unmeasured outdoor received-power values are reserved for evaluation. We evaluate 8 and 16 outdoor measurements, denoted by B8 and B16, with normalized additive-noise standard deviation 0.02 .All methods share the test scenarios, budgets, masks, and noise convention. Known building-pixel values are excluded from the sampling budget. The three sampling strategies use the same conditional reconstruction settings, with measurement sets generated using seed 42031. An unconditional pixel-domain diffusion model supplies 12 sampling particles with 16 reverse steps. The global RM criterion uses a four-pixel target stride and at most 96 representative emitter-location hypotheses. Final recon-struction generates four conditional latent-diffusion particles per retained Tx mode, with three reverse steps and at most four modes. Each mode uses a peak-normalized Gaussian raster with one-pixel standard deviation. The Student-t likelihood has four degrees of freedom, discrepancy standard deviation 

0.1, and a nominal ESS target equal to the larger of two particles and 0.35 times the ensemble size. Posterior correction and projection use each strategy’s acquired measurements and fixed scene-specific latent-noise streams. Conditional inference uses an NVIDIA RTX 3060 Ti, four-particle batches per mode, and global weight normalization. External methods are evaluated on an RTX 4090. All neural parameters and architectures remain fixed. The external baselines are independent paper-constrained reimplementations that retain published sampling and recon-struction mechanisms with the dataset and budget adapta-tions specified here. Ensemble GP active learning (EGP-AL) uses 11 radial-basis random-feature GP experts, predictive-likelihood weighting, Gaussian-mixture variance (GM-Var) sampling, and mixture-mean reconstruction [4]. Its initializa-tion is adapted to the sparse budget, whereas the published experiment starts from 100 labeled points. GP-UCB-CE retains online GP fitting, the published upper-confidence reward, constrained unicycle motion, a five-step horizon, and gradient-enhanced cross-entropy planning [9]. The geometry and low-rank baselines use different struc-tural information for sampling and reconstruction. GACP-AE retains its 32 × 32 lattice, geometry-aware covariance, pivoted Cholesky projection, and published autoencoder [31]. The geometry uses the documented building-footprint adap-tation in place of unavailable obstacle heights. After 100 training epochs, the model reaches validation mean squared error 0.00407 without projection onto the measured values. EMLS with matrix completion (EMLS-MC) retains its two-round sampler and uses singular value thresholding (SVT) for reconstruction [32]. Its published protocol spans 5% to 45% 

sampling, while B8 and B16 expose roughly 0.01% and 0.02% 

of the grid. After the fixed 600 iterations, its observed-entry residuals remain 0.6075 and 0.6099 , exceeding the unchanged 

10 −4 threshold. Table I therefore records non-convergence (NC) under this stopping rule. External methods use their own sampling and reconstruction pipelines, alongside the stated shared-reconstruction controls. Unmeasured received-power values remain excluded from every method’s inference inputs. Simulation reports outdoor NMSE and SSIM [36], convert-ing NMSE to decibels per scenario before averaging. Peak ratio compares predicted and reference maximum normalized RM values within five pixels of the true emitter, with an ideal value of one. Top-1% recall is the fraction of reference top-1% outdoor pixels also identified among the predicted top-1% pixels. For localization, the three sampling policies use the MAP estimate from corrected Tx-mode probabilities, while external methods use the outdoor RM peak. Errors measure Euclidean distance to true antenna coordinates, with hit rates within 5, 10, and 20 pixels. Means cover the same 100 scenarios. Figures use 5000 scene-bootstrap resamples for mean intervals [37], while paired differences use 20000 resamples and 100000 sign flips. Holm adjustment covers both budgets and the two sampling comparators for each metric, with external-pipeline comparisons forming a separate family. C ONCLUSION 

RMSampler improves sparse RM reconstruction by cou-pling next-location selection with measurement-conditioned inference when the interfering emitter location is unknown. Normalized reductions in global RM and emitter-location uncertainty guide sampling, while ESS-guided correction and minimum-distortion projection use the collected measurements without changing pretrained neural parameters or architecture. Evaluations on simulated RMs and Vienna and Chongqing real-world measured data demonstrate improved reconstruc-tion, including lower RMSE than random sampling under the common-reconstructor Chongqing protocol.
