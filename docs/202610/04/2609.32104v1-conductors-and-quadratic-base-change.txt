# Conductors and Quadratic base change 

Siddharth Ramakrishnan Cherukara 

Department of Mathematics University of Oklahoma e-mail: siddharth.ramakrishnan@ou.edu 

Abstract. Let F be a totally real field and K/F a totally real quadratic extension. For a Hilbert cusp newform f over F with base change fK to K, we determine the exact level of fK in terms of the level of f, using only local representation theory: for each place p of 

F we compute how the conductor of the local component of f changes under quadratic base change. The tame case (odd residue characteristic) follows from existing base change theory, but the case p = 2 requires a finer ε -factor analysis that does not appear in the literature. We give a complete treatment of the dyadic imprimitive representations and, more delicately, the exceptional supercuspidal representations, for which no closed-form base change formula was previously known; in particular we determine, in terms of local root numbers, the base change of every exceptional supercuspidal representation of GL (2 , Q2) with trivial central character and conductor 3. This yields an explicit formula for the level of fK , specialized here to F = Q,

along with a partial converse.

The Doi–Naganuma lifting was extended by Saito in [Sai75] to the case where K is a totally real cyclic extension of prime degree over Q satisfying some assumptions. This was seen as an instance of Langlands’ functoriality principles. Let F be a totally real number field, and let K be an extension of F, 

and let p and P be finite places of F and K respectively such that P lies over p. Locally, base change is a map from irreducible admissible representations of GL (n, F p) to irreducible admissible representations of GL (n, K P), and globally is a map from cuspidal automorphic representations of GL (n, AF ) to those of GL (n, AK ). The complete description for GL (2) was given in [Lan80], following [Sai75], using a local interpretation due to [Shi79]. The case of GL (n) was established in [AC89]. Both works dealt with the cases where K/F is a Galois extension of prime degree. In [BH99] and [BH05], the authors extended the lift to cover the cases where K, F are local fields and K/F is any tamely ramified extension, and the notions agree with [AC89] when the domains overlap. Our main problem is as follows. Let F be a totally real field, oF be its ring of integers and let N be an ideal in oF . Let K be a totally real quadratic extension of F.

The behaviour of the conductor under base change is completely understood in the case of tamely ramified extensions in [BH99] and easily follows from [AC89]. However the wildly ramified case is more delicate and requires finer analysis. Since our focus is on applications to modular forms, we will do a finer analysis in the case p = 2 .

This is difficult for several reasons. 

• We can study the conductors via ε-factors attached to these representations. These are not easy to describe when p = 2 .

• There are exceptional supercuspidal representations in this setting, whose base changes do not admit descriptions as simple as those of the other cases. 

• There are 2[Fp:Q2]+2 − 1 quadratic extensions of Fp when p is dyadic, giving more cases and calculations to consider. These cases are dealt with in Sections 4 and 5, which form the heart of the paper. Although the results in the odd prime case follow easily from the existing literature, the p = 2 case does not appear in the literature. In particular, the exceptional supercuspidal cases are especially difficult since there is no closed-form expression of base change of these representations. In Section 5 we also determine the explicit base change of an exceptional representation of GL (2 , Q2) with trivial central character and conductor 3, in terms of the ε -factors associated to these representations by Langlands. Both sections mentioned above are novel and to the best of our knowledge, have not appeared in the literature before. Using explicit constructions such as that in [Kud78], bounds on the level of Hilbert modular forms obtained by Doi–Naganuma lifts have been obtained. However, the exact level of the newform associated via base change has not been addressed before. In [KM19], the authors study the problem of lifting elliptic modular forms of level N to a real quadratic field K of discriminant D , under the assumptions (N, D ) = 1 and D ≡ 1 (mod 4) .Their results agree with ours in this setting; however, our results apply in greater generality. We also remark that similar problems about conductors under functorial transfers of modular forms have been considered before by [Roy22] and [BMM25] in the case of Sym 3 transfer. In particular, some of our notation follows [BMM25]. We also remark that a similar problem could be studied regarding base change of representations of GL (n, F p). In this case the existence of the conductor of a representation π, c(π) was dealt with for n > 2 by [JPS81] when π is generic and [AKY22] for π non-generic. Our results in Section 3 completely deal with the case where KP is a tamely ramified extension of Fp.

2.1. Local representation theory. Let F be a non-archimedean local field with an additive valuation vF , oF be the set of elements with non-negative valuation, p be the set of elements with positive valuation and qF , the residue cardinality. This valuation induces a topology on F

with pi forming a basis of open neighbourhoods of 0 . We induce a metric on F, by defining 

|x|F = q−vF (x).

Let WF denote the Weil group of F . We have a canonical map, the Artin homomorphism 

from WF to F ×, which we denote by Art , given by local class field theory. In this section, we recall some basic preliminaries of the representation theory of GL (2 , F ) , the group of 2 × 2

invertible matrices with entries in F. By the Local Langlands Correspondence (abbreviated as LLC), these representations correspond to 2 -dimensional semisimple Weil–Deligne representations of WF . Thus, we briefly describe both objects. A representation of WF is a continuous homomorphism 

σ : WF → Aut C(V ),

where V is a finite-dimensional vector space over C. A Weil–Deligne representation is a pair 

(σ, N ) such that σ is a representation of WF and N is a C− endomorphism of V such that for each x in WF we have 

σ(x)N σ (x)−1 = |x|N, 

where |x| = |Art −1(x)|F .

To these representations we associate what are called the ε -factors. We do not need the full gen-erality of these factors; however, we note some of the important properties of them. ε(σ, s, ψ ) is a Laurent polynomial in qs , for a complex number s. This function satisfies the following properties 

• We have (1) ε(σ, s, ψ ) = cq (nc (ψ)−a(σ)) sF ,

for some constant c. The integer a(σ) is uniquely determined, and is called the Artin conductor of σ. 

• Let σ = σ1 ⊕ σ2....σ n, then a(σ) = a(σ1) + a(σ2)... + a(σn).

• If σ is irreducible, then a(σ) ≥ dim σ.
