Abstract 

Transformers are deployed as a special model class. Neural processing units (NPU) add dedicated operators for attention and normalization, and quantization relies on Transformer-specific algorithms. We show the computation does not force this. Every Transformer block, with attention, LayerNorm and a GELU MLP, compiles exactly into a generic deep neural network built from fixed affine maps, routing and the square, sigmoid and inverse square root, plus GELU or its sigmoid approximation. Without activation–activation products, every transform-wise compilation adds at least N 2 scalar nonlinear evaluations per head for dense scores. Keeping that one operation type removes this cost; its elementwise form, present on every surveyed NPU, suffices with the sigmoid and inverse square root. For top-k

attention with exchangeable tail scores, we prove that exact or conditional-mean tail mass gives an unbiased, minimum-mean-square-error estimate of the attention distribution, while tail drop is conditionally biased. When queries or agents cluster, centroids choose the keys first, reducing the number of computed scores from N 2 to N k per head. On DeiT-Tiny, MambaIRv2-Light and Pythia-70M the compiled models match their references with all keys, standard post-training quantization applies, and the exact tail-mass estimate beats tail dropping with half as many keys on DeiT-Tiny and, for k ≤ 8, on Pythia-70M. 

# Introduction 

Transformers [1] are treated as a model class apart from the deep neural networks (DNNs) that accelerators and tools were first built for. This view has two practical consequences. The first is hardware. Accelerators built for convolutional networks run a fixed operator set and hand everything else to the host. The Edge TPU compiler cuts the model at the first unsupported operation and runs the rest on the CPU [2]. NVIDIA’s Jetson DLA has no matrix-multiply layer [3]. On phones, llm.npu runs the linear layers on the NPU and keeps LayerNorm and attention on the CPU or GPU [4]. Vendors answer with Transformer-specific operators and units for attention, softmax and normalization [5–7]. The second is quantization. Post-training quantization (PTQ) of Transformers relies on dedicated algorithms. Liu et al. search quantization scales with a ranking-aware objective [8], and PTQ4ViT adds twin-uniform quantizers for the post-softmax and post-GELU activations [9]. FQ-ViT adds power-of-two factors for LayerNorm and a log-integer softmax [10]. RepQ-ViT 1arXiv:2610.28958v1 [cs.LG] 20 Oct 2026 reparameterizes quantization scales [11], IGQ-ViT groups channels per instance [12], I-BERT replaces the nonlinearities by integer polynomials [13], and SmoothQuant moves activation outliers into the weights of language models [14]. We show that the computation does not force this split. The argument has four steps. 

Exact compilation. Every Transformer block, with LayerNorm, scaled dot-product attention, softmax and a GELU MLP, compiles exactly into a generic DNN of fixed affine maps, scalar nonlinearities and routing, with no product of two activations (Theorem 1). We call it the strict graph. Squares replace products, through ab = 12 [( a + b)2 − a2 − b2] where the squares of the factors are shared and through polarization otherwise, and no exponential or logarithm is needed: squares, the sigmoid and the inverse square root suffice, with GELU or its approximation 

x σ (1 .702 x) as the MLP activation. 

The cost of strictness. Since the strict graph has no dedicated layers, standard DNN tools apply to it. But removing products of two activations has a price. For free arguments, any compilation without them adds at least N 2 scalar nonlinear evaluations per head at QK ⊤ and 

N d at P V , whatever the scalar functions, depth or width (Theorem 2). 

One more operation type. Adding activation–activation products removes the price. Their elementwise form, the Hadamard product, is on every NPU we survey, and it suffices: with it, a block needs only the sigmoid and the inverse square root besides its MLP activation, and only softmax adds work, 2 N 2 evaluations per head (Proposition 1). With the exponential and the reciprocal it adds no pointwise work (Proposition 2), and with matrix multiplication of two activations the extended compilation adds no work of either kind (Theorem 3). On a GPU, for 

k ≤ 64, the Hadamard and strict graphs run at 1 .0 to 1 .3 and 1 .2 to 1 .5 times the latency of this extended graph. Theorems 2 and 3 make this operation type necessary and sufficient for compilation without added pointwise work. Practice already uses this operation set. ONNX defines LayerNormalization and Softmax as functions over it [15, 16], current NPUs expose it [6, 17, 18], and convolutional networks with squeeze-and-excitation gating already use the elementwise product [19, 20]. 

Clustered attention. The attention weights of a query cluster on a few keys [21–23] when its scores have a gap (Lemma 1). Queries cluster too [24, 25], and queries close to one point share their top keys (Lemma 2). Top-k attention keeps the k largest scores of each query [21, 22], the topological rule of bird flocks [26]. We add an estimate of the omitted probability mass, spread uniformly over the dropped keys. For exchangeable tail scores, exact or conditional-mean tail mass gives the unbiased, minimum-mean-square-error attention-distribution estimate; tail drop is conditionally biased (Theorem 4). Centroids of the query clusters [25] choose the keys before the scores, so the strict graph forms only N k scores per head (Corollary 2, Proposition 5). One step of the Motsch–Tadmor flocking model is one attention head (Proposition 6). We test the compilation on three model families: DeiT-Tiny [27] for image classification on Oxford-IIIT Pet [28] and ImageNet-1k [29], MambaIRv2-Light [30] for super-resolution, and Pythia-70M [31] for language modeling. 

Related work. Universal approximation gives some network within each error on a compact set [32, 33]. Forming products from squares is classical: tables of quarter-squares multiply by polarization [34], Winograd’s inner product forms the self-terms of each row once and halves the products of a matrix multiplication [35], and expressivity proofs for ReLU networks use 

ab = 12 [( a + b)2 − a2 − b2] [36]. Compilers such as Glow map a model onto the operators a device provides [37]. Privacy-preserving inference decomposes attention, softmax and LayerNorm into 2primitive operations for cryptographic protocols, usually with polynomial approximations [38–40]. Multiplication-free training replaces every product by an approximate piecewise-affine operation and trains on that arithmetic [41]. Training-time alternatives simplify LayerNorm [42] or remove it [43, 44]. Top-k attention saves memory on long contexts [21–23], usually by renormalizing the kept scores. Scatterbrain adds a low-rank estimate of the non-selected attention to a sparse one [45], Combiner attends to the rest of the sequence through region summaries [46], and MagicPIG replaces top-k selection by sampling to avoid its bias [47]. Clustered attention chooses the kept keys by query centroids and gives the other keys the weights of the centroid [25]; we use its key selection with calibrated centroids. Lookup tables, piecewise-linear units and integer approximations implement scalar nonlinearities in hardware [7, 48, 49]. 

What is new. Unlike universal approximation, the compiled graph is exact on all inputs, reuses the trained weights and has a known size. Unlike compilers and privacy-preserving decompositions, we ask which small operator set expresses a whole block exactly. New are the lower bound that prices product-free compilation and the extended construction that removes the price. Unlike multiplication-free training, we compile a trained model, keep LayerNorm, and remove only products of two activations. Our tail-mass estimate is simpler than those of Scatterbrain, Combiner and clustered attention. It is a fixed affine map for a constant mass. For clustered queries, new are the cost bounds for the selected scores and the flocking step as one attention head.

A generic DNN vocabulary 

We describe a DNN by the types of operations its nodes perform. Three types cover plain convolutional networks and multilayer perceptrons. 

Definition 1 (Strict basis) . Fix a set Φ of scalar functions. A graph over the strict basis 

Bstrict (Φ) is a finite acyclic graph whose nodes are of three types: 1. weight–activation contraction : a fixed affine map x 7 → Ax + b, where A and b are parameters of the graph and do not depend on the input; 2. unary map : x 7 → ϕ(x) applied entrywise, with ϕ ∈ Φ; 3. routing : copy, reshape, permutation, concatenation, broadcast, masking, and selection of entries by comparisons of computed values (max, TopK, gather), with a fixed tie-breaking rule. No node multiplies two quantities that both depend on the input; a square is a unary map. For every Φ, we call Bstrict (Φ) a product-free basis and its graphs product-free graphs. Convolutions, fully connected layers, batch normalization at inference, residual sums and average pooling are fixed affine maps; max pooling is routing. ReLU, GELU and other activations are unary maps. Gating, as in squeeze-and-excitation blocks [19], multiplies two activations and is not in this basis. The set Φ is a parameter. Every Transformer block compiles exactly 

Theorem 1 (Exact compilation into the strict basis) . Every finite Transformer block composed of transforms in TTr is computed exactly by a finite graph over Bstrict (Φ strict ).

We call the graph of Theorem 1 the strict graph . A compilation is transform-wise when it replaces each transform of the block by its own graph that computes that transform exactly; the graphs then compose. The strict graph is transform-wise: Lemmas 3–6 in Methods give the graph of each transform.

Theorem 2 (Lower bound for product-free compilation) . Fix one attention head and any set Φ

of scalar functions, and let the entries of Q, K, P and V range freely over R. Every product-free graph that computes Score = QK ⊤/√d exactly has PW ≥ N 2, and every product-free graph that computes Mix = P V exactly has PW ≥ N d .

On inputs where its routing is fixed, a product-free graph is an affine function of its unary outputs (Methods, Lemma 9). It therefore needs at least as many unary evaluations as its target has coordinates that stay linearly independent modulo affine functions; for Score these are all N 2 entries. The compiled models match their references 

We compile three pretrained models with the extended graph and top-k tail-mass attention, and evaluate them before training. With all keys kept, the compiled models match their references: 91 .36% against 91 .36% on Pet, 72 .01% against 72 .02% on ImageNet, 32 .51 dB against 32 .51 dB on Set5, and a token negative log-likelihood (NLL) of 4 .5496 against 4 .5496 on WikiText-2. The strict and extended graphs compute the same model exactly (Theorems 1 and 3). Only model changes, such as top-k attention, and rounding change accuracy. 

The strict graph pays the predicted cost 

Table 2 measures the cost of the strict graphs of Theorem 1 and Proposition 3 on a GPU. The reference calls fused attention and LayerNorm kernels. The extended graph runs at 2 .5 to 5 .6times the reference latency in Tables 2 and 4. The strict graph takes 1 .2 to 2 .4 times the latency and, at top-64, 2 .3 times the peak memory of the extended graph. The GPU has κ ≈ 2 for squares (Methods), far below the threshold of Corollary 1, so this slowdown does not come from the bound of Theorem 2. It comes from the construction: d times more pointwise work than the bound, and N 2d operands per head that the implementation writes to memory. A per-module profile puts 73 to 77% of the added time in Score and Mix (Supplementary Table S2). A merged GPU kernel avoids these writes; no transform-wise product-free graph avoids the bound of Theorem 2. With the same GELU approximation, the strict graph stays within 0 .6 points of the Hadamard graph and of its polarization variant with top-k support, and within 0 .4 points routed. Two runs of one strict configuration differ by up to 0 .5 points with top-k support and 1 .5 points routed, since the tail mass and centroids are calibrated on augmented training images. Squares cost no accuracy beyond run-to-run variation. With top-k support, polarization makes 1 .9 times the unary evaluations and, for k ≤ 64, runs 13 to 14 times slower with 5 .5 times the memory (Supplementary Section S5). In integer arithmetic both identities are exact (Methods). 

The elementwise product removes most of the strict cost 

With top-k support and k ≤ 64, the Hadamard graph of Proposition 1 runs at 1 .2 to 1 .3 times the latency of the extended graph, against 1 .4 to 1 .5 for the strict graph, at similar memory (Table 2). Its measured unary counts equal Table 1: 2 .2 × 10 6 per image at k = 16, against 1.1 × 10 8 for the strict graph. Its remaining cost is the N 2d products of QK ⊤ per head, which this implementation writes to memory (Supplementary Table S2). With top-k support, the approximation of GELU costs the Hadamard graph 0 .7 to 2 .6 and the strict graph 0 .3 to 2 .3points; with GELU, the Hadamard graph stays within 0 .1 points of the extended graph.

# Discussion 

An NPU needs no attention, softmax or normalization unit to run Transformers. The elementwise product of two activations removes the pointwise cost that Theorem 2 prices, and a MAC array that accepts two activation operands only keeps the N 2d products of QK ⊤ and P V out of memory. Theorem 2 applies to transform-wise compilation, which keeps the attention contractions as subgraphs; a compilation that never forms QK ⊤ is outside it (Supplementary Section S1). Corollary 1 needs hardware whose MAC array outruns its pointwise unit by more than 2 N d/ (N +

d). The construction extends to other blocks. RMSNorm [42] compiles as LayerNorm without the mean. A gated MLP such as SwiGLU [60] multiplies two activations, and its SiLU is x σ (x), so both take a polarization graph in the strict basis and a Hadamard node in the Hadamard and extended bases. For autonomous and multi-agent systems, the Hadamard graph lets a robot or drone run a Transformer policy on an NPU built for convolutional networks. By Proposition 6, top-k

attention is the topological rule of starling flocks, where each bird follows a fixed number of nearest neighbors [26], and the tail-mass term describes the far agents by their total weight and mean velocity, as cluster-level models of multi-agent control do [61]. 

Future work. The latency and memory reported here are GPU measurements, and the NPU placement of Supplementary Table S1 comes from documented operator support. Compiling the three graphs and their quantized integer units with the NPU toolchains, and running them on the devices, is the next step. Post-training quantization 

Precision. We target edge NPUs, so DeiT-Tiny and MambaIRv2-Light run in bf16, which stores each value in half the memory of fp32 with 8 significant bits. With all keys, bf16 changes the accuracy of every DeiT-Tiny graph by at most 0 .46 points and the PSNR of MambaIRv2-Light by at most 0 .04 dB against fp32 (Supplementary Section S8). An 8-bit PTQ code drops the 8-bit exponent of a bf16 value and keeps its sign and 7 more bits: all values of a tensor share one scale. Near the largest value of its tensor the code is as fine as bf16, and smaller values lose bits. A square doubles the exponent range of its input: a value at 2 −4 of the largest has a square at 2 −8

of the largest square. Bit widths are those of the input codes. The fixed affine nodes of the strict graph read squares and sums of squares, so their inputs take 12 bits; weights, the logits and the inputs of linear, convolution and product nodes, including the two scalings by the calibrated tail mass in each block, take 8 bits. 

Quantized graph. Quantized nodes are the linear and convolution layers, the products of two activations or of an activation and a parameter, and the fixed affine nodes with a coefficient tensor, including the residual and position-embedding additions. Each quantized node has one quantizer per input, and this quantizer is also the output quantizer of the preceding node, so every boundary is quantized once. An identity node at the model output gives the last layer an output quantizer too, so the logits are 8-bit codes with one scale, and the model is quantized from the image to the logits. Unary maps, routing, and plain sums and means over an axis have no quantizer of their own. A sum or mean reads integer codes with fixed scales, or their squares, so its result is an integer with a fixed scale. It keeps its full integer range, with no rounding to a specific bit width; the next quantizer sets the width. The graph between two quantizers is thus a unit with integer inputs and integer outputs: a quantized node with its output map, followed by any unary, routing and summing nodes. Every tensor that passes from one unit to the next is an integer code, so the quantized graph is an integer graph however its units are evaluated. On an NPU, a unit is an integer multiply–accumulate followed by a requantization, a fixed multiplier and offset [53], and the lookup-table or piecewise-linear units of the NPU approximate its unary nodes over the calibrated input range [48, 49]. 

Simulation and calibration. We simulate each unit in fp32 [54], with the integer codes held in fp32 tensors. fp32 holds every integer up to 2 24 ≈ 1.7 × 10 7, so the integer sums are exact. The run checks this for every node with integer inputs: over the calibration data, the sum of the 21 absolute products of the node stays below 4 .5 × 10 6, a factor 3 .7 below this limit. The output maps and unary nodes run in fp32. The bf16 float model is the reference. Calibration runs it for two forward passes over the full Pet training split: the first records the maximum absolute value of every node input, the second fits the output maps to its outputs. Activations use one symmetric max-abs scale per input tensor. Linear and convolution weights use one symmetric scale per output channel; affine coefficient tensors use one scale. A linear or convolution node takes its output scale as the product of input and weight scales and fits a per-channel output bias; other nodes fit an affine output map by least squares. A node that combines several inputs of different range, as the norm expansion does with v2 and ( v − p)2 or with u2 and a2, quantizes each input on its own grid and rescales it before the sum, as an integer add with per-input multipliers does. Quantizing the logits changes accuracy by −0.16 to +0 .30 points. Supplementary Section S9 gives PTQ of the strict graph with exact GELU. 

Integer products from squares. For integer operands both identities hold exactly when no intermediate step overflows or requantizes. For signed 8-bit operands in [ −128 , 127], the sums and differences lie in [ −256 , 255]. A shared table of squares needs 512 signed-index entries, or 257 magnitude-index entries, with 17-bit unsigned outputs, and the difference of two squares fits in 18 signed bits. Dividing ( a + b)2 − (a − b)2 by four, or ( a + b)2 − a2 − b2 by two, before accumulation gives the integer product exactly. Each product has magnitude at most 2 14 , so a signed 32-bit accumulator holds a dot product of every length d < 217 .

Timing and unary counts 

Latency is the GPU time of the forward pass alone, divided by that of the reference, on one NVIDIA RTX 4070 Ti SUPER. Ten batches of 32 images from the held-out Pet test split are first stored on the GPU. Each model runs in bf16 under torch.compile in the default, reduce-overhead and max-autotune modes, and we report its fastest mode: the median of five timed runs over the ten batches, with CUDA events after a warm-up. Peak memory is the maximum allocated GPU memory during an eager evaluation pass over the held-out Pet test split, with only the measured model on the GPU; Supplementary Section S10 gives the tensor sizes behind it. Unary evaluations are counted with forward hooks on every unary node over one test minibatch. With all keys, the DeiT-Tiny count per image equals twelve block totals of Table 1 plus the final LayerNorm: 6 .02 × 10 6 for the Hadamard graph and 1 .96 × 10 8 for the strict graph with GELU. The implementation of the extended graph forms the squares u ⊙ u of its 25 LayerNorms with a unary square node, so its count of 4 .17 × 10 6 adds 25 N hd = 9 .46 × 10 5

to that sum. With x ⊙ σ(1 .702 x) the strict graph forms this product from two squares, 8 N hd

The GPU ratio. The RTX 4070 Ti SUPER has 66 Ada streaming multiprocessors at a boost clock of 2 .61 GHz. Each multiprocessor retires 128 FP32 operations per clock on its CUDA cores and 256 dense bf16 multiply–accumulates per clock with FP32 accumulation on its tensor cores [63]. This gives Rpw ≈ 22 T/s, Rmac ≈ 44 T/s and κ ≈ 2. 

Numerical conventions 

The compiled LayerNorm uses the ε of each reference: 10 −6 for DeiT-Tiny and 10 −5 for MambaIRv2-Light and Pythia-70M. Softmax normalization divides by the max-shifted par-tition sum Z. The DeiT-Tiny and MambaIRv2-Light checkpoints store their weights in bf16; Pythia-70M runs in fp32 (Supplementary Section S8). The bf16 runs evaluate the reference and every compiled graph under PyTorch CUDA autocast, which keeps means and sums in fp32; a 22 node that adds several inputs stores its result in the precision of its first input. In float64, against the reference with its weights rounded to bf16 the same way, the extended and strict DeiT-Tiny graphs and the Hadamard graph with exact GELU, all with all keys, reproduce the reference logits on 16 Pet test images to 6 × 10 −8. The differences between the compiled and reference models in Table 2 therefore come from bf16 weights and arithmetic. The implementation of the strict graph evaluates ( x + ε)−1/2 as a single kernel; it equals the inverse square root after a fixed affine map.

Tightness and scope of the bound 

Squares only. With Φ = {(·)2} and one nonlinear layer, PW is within a factor two of the bilinear rank of the matrix multiplication tensor, since polarization turns each product into two squares and each square is one product. Fast matrix multiplication [64, 65] then gives strict graphs with PW = O(n2.373 ) for n × n products, so no Ω( N 2d) bound holds for this Φ, and the factor 

d + 2 d/N between the strict graph of Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 is not forced. Conversely, on the set of Lemma 8 a graph over {(·)2} with PW squarings is an arithmetic circuit with PW nonscalar multiplications, and nonscalar multiplications never exceed the total arithmetic cost [66]. For d = N , a lower bound of order N 2+ δ for this Φ would therefore prove that the exponent of matrix multiplication is at least 2 + δ, an open problem. For fixed d, N 2d = O(N 2)and the theorem is tight in order. MAC arrays and tensor units run the classical product, and the strict graph follows it. 

Unrestricted Φ. On a bounded box, Score is computed by a product-free graph with N 2 +2 N d 

unary evaluations, so no argument that ignores the regularity of Φ can improve Theorem 2 beyond the additive 2 N d . Map the n = 2 N d inputs affinely into [0 , 1). For p = 1 , . . . , n let ψp send 

x ∈ [0 , 1), written in binary with infinitely many zero digits, to the number whose digit at position 

rn + p is the r-th digit of x and whose other digits are zero. The affine sum z = ∑ 

> p

ψp(xp)interleaves the digits of all inputs, has infinitely many zero digits, and so determines every 

xp. One further unary map per output, ϕij (z) = Sij , decodes the score. The total is n + N 2

evaluations. The construction reaches the bound by first encoding its inputs; one evaluation of a linear form alone cannot compute a product on an open set, because ϕ(a · x + b) is constant on hyperplanes. 

Transform-wise scope. Applied to a whole block as one map, the nonlinear rank is at most the number of outputs, N hd = 3 .78 × 10 4 for DeiT-Tiny, below the pointwise work of the source block. A compilation that never forms QK ⊤ is therefore outside Theorem 2. The theorem concerns the attention contractions as transforms, which is the form in which every Transformer implementation and every NPU operator set presents them.

# S5. Polarization and squares 

The strict graph forms each product of two activations from squares, by one of two classical identities: 

ab = 14

The first, polarization, is the method of quarter-squares [34]. The second, the norm expansion, forms products in expressivity proofs for ReLU networks [36]. 

Cost. Polarization pays two squares per product. The norm expansion pays one square per product plus the squares a2 and b2 of the factors, so an isolated product costs two squares 32 by polarization and three by the norm expansion. In a contraction each factor enters many products, and its square is formed once. QK ⊤ of two N × d matrices then costs 2 N 2d squares by polarization and N 2d + 2 N d by the norm expansion, and the ratio to the bound of Theorem 2 falls from 2 d to d + 2 d/N . This is Winograd’s precomputation of the self-terms of each row [35], with squares in place of products. The strict graph uses the norm expansion where the squares of the factors are shared, in QK ⊤, P V , LayerNorm and the exact tail term, and polarization elsewhere, in softmax and the GELU approximation.

# S8. bf16 against fp32 

Table S7 compares bf16 with fp32 for every network with all keys. The bf16 column evaluates the saved models: weights in bf16 and arithmetic under CUDA autocast, as in the main tables. The fp32 column uses the fp32 reference weights and IEEE fp32 arithmetic. Each compiled graph is rebuilt in fp32, so its fixed coefficients are fp32 too. With all keys, no calibrated quantity is active. The DeiT-Tiny and Pythia-70M results repeat exactly between runs. MambaIRv2-Light uses nondeterministic kernels, and two runs differ by up to 0 .01 dB. In fp32, every compiled graph with exact GELU reproduces its reference (Theorems 1 and 3, Proposition 1). On Pet, the extended, strict and Hadamard graphs match the accuracy and loss of DeiT-Tiny to four digits. The extended graph matches on ImageNet-1k and WikiText-2, and on Set5 within the run-to-run variation. With x σ (1 .702 x), the strict and Hadamard graphs compute the same model and give the same fp32 accuracy. bf16 changes the accuracy of DeiT-Tiny by at most 0 .46 points and the PSNR of MambaIRv2-Light by at most 0 .04 dB. Pythia-70M runs in fp32. In bf16 its validation NLL rises from 4 .55 to 6 .68 nats, for the reference and the compiled graph alike. Only bf16 weights with fp32 arithmetic give 4 .56. The degradation cause is the logits. They share a large offset that softmax ignores: their median is 1061, while the top two differ by a median of 0 .73. Near 1000, bf16 resolves steps of 8, so it loses the ranking of the tokens. The output projection causes 1 .7 of the 2 .1 nats and attention the other 0 .4.
