Abstract. The precise origin of critical points in rational functions remains a longstanding open problem. This paper elucidates the mechanism underlying critical point generation in rational functions. For over two centuries, a conjec-ture attributed to Gauss and Fourier has remained unresolved: every real zero of ( p′

(s)/p (s)) ′

is associated with a pair of non-real zeros of the real polyno-mial p(s). The real zeros of ( p′

(s)/p (s)) ′

that are multiple real zeros of p′

(s)are termed second-kind zeros of ( p′

(s)/p (s)) ′

. By developing the root locus method, we establish a necessary and sufficient condition for the emergence of second-kind zeros: a second-kind zero M arises if and only if at least two root loci of 2 qπ or 2 qπ + π degree, emitted from at least one pair of non-real con-jugate zeros of p(s) (in the extended complex plane, excluding the real axis), directly intersect another root locus at M on the real axis. Our results resolve one conjecture of the Gauss-Fourier conjectures and reveal the fundamental mechanism by which critical points of rational functions are generated. 

Introduction 

In 2004, Borcea and Shapiro proposed a conjecture [1], motivated by their efforts to prove the Hawaii Conjecture put forward by Craven, Csordas, and Smith [2]. The Hawaii Conjecture asserts that for a real polynomial p(s) of degree n ≥ 2, the number of real zeros of ( p′(s)/p (s)) ′ does not exceed the number of non-real zeros of 

p(s). Tracing its roots to foundational work by Gauss and Fourier—with historical details documented by Edwards and Hinkkanen [3]—this conjecture has long been a focal point in the mathematical community. In the correspondence G¨ ottingische gelehrte Anzeigen (G.G.A.) dated February 25, 1833 [4, 5], Gauss described observations and a conjecture by Fourier. Gauss explained that Fourier believed for each real zero of ( p′

(s)/p (s)) ′

(termed ”crit-ical points” by Fourier), there exists an associated pair of non-real zeros of the polynomial p(s). The Hawaii Conjecture represents an effort by Craven, Csordas, and Smith to quantify the ideas explored by Gauss and Fourier, though it does not explicitly reference the above-mentioned association between zeros. This conjecture was re-solved in 2011 by M. Tyaglov [6]. Subsequently, B. Shapiro proposed three new conjectures related to the Hawaii Conjecture (Conjectures 11, 12, and 13 in [7]).

When root loci have distinct unit complex values, they cannot intersect. When unit complex values coincide, intersection is possible. 2. Continuity of Root Loci 

In prior literature, the continuity of the root locus has often been treated as self-evident, with no rigorous proof provided. However, proving the continuity of the root locus is not a trivial matter and cannot be overlooked. It is only under the assumption that the root locus is fully determined by the phase angle condition equations that we can rigorously establish its continuity. The continuity of the root locus is a fundamental property in root locus theory, and providing a rigorous proof of this property is essential. Prior to our work, there were no established results regarding the relationship between the argument of complex functions of one variable and their critical points.
