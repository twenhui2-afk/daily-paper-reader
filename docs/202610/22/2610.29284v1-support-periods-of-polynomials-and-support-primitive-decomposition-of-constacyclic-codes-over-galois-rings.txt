# Support periods of polynomials and support-primitive decomposition of constacyclic codes over Galois rings

Abstract 

Let A = GR( pe, r ) have residue field Fq , and let λ ∈ A×. We relate coefficient compression of monic divisors of XN − λ to support-primitive decomposition of constacyclic codes. At a simple-root length, the support period of every nonconstant monic divisor is preserved at each coefficient precision and equals the order of the multiplicative stabilizer of its root set. For a cyclic Frobenius-orbit polynomial, the multiple equal-difference structure determines its support period, core degree, and least relative degree of an unramified extension over which all its basic-irreducible factors are binomials. Coordinate projections give every constacyclic code over a finite commutative ring a unique maximal interleaving into copies of one support-primitive code. At simple-root lengths over A,the intrinsic period of a code is the greatest common divisor of the periods of its torsion codes. The decomposition yields distance and duality formulas and the arithmetic Singleton bound for free codes. At every length, a nonzero code over A has the same minimum Hamming distance as its highest torsion code, which gives a refined bound. At repeated-root lengths, identical torsion filtrations can occur with different intrinsic periods, while a class of nonzero codes obtained by substitution from simple-root generators retains the same core and minimum distance. Trace matrices recover the coefficients of basic-irreducible cores using unit pivots, and local Hensel corrections lift compressed factors directly. For Xn − 1 with p ∤ n, lifting and output have quasi-linear bit cost in the compressed representation when the arithmetic data and residue factorizations are given. 

Keywords. Galois ring; constacyclic code; support-primitive decomposition; multiple equal-difference representation; Hensel lifting; arithmetic Singleton bound.

# 1 Introduction

with s prime to the characteristic has two equivalent descriptions: its positive support exponents are divisible by s, and its roots, counted with multiplicities, are invariant under multiplication by the s-th roots of unity. Over a Galois ring, these descriptions involve Hensel lifting, the torsion filtration, and the distinction between simple and repeated roots. Let A = GR( pe, r ), with residue field Fq, where q = pr. The inverse (N λ )−1Xf ′ of the complementary factor modulo f gives a local correction that lifts the core directly. For a basic-irreducible core, a trace matrix with unit determinant recovers its coefficients even when Newton’s identities involve nonunit multipliers. At a simple-root length, the support period also equals the order of the multiplicative stabilizer of the root set. Normalization of the principal-unit part of λ reduces this description to Teichmüller roots. For a cyclic Frobenius-orbit polynomial, the MED index determines the core degree and the least relative degree of an unramified extension over which all its basic-irreducible factors are bino-mials. For primitive-order layers, the identity Φd(X) = Φ D(Xμ) gives a factorwise correspondence; layers with the same reduced order D share their core factors. For codes, stability under projections onto coordinate classes modulo s defines an intrinsic support period. The admissible divisors of N are closed under least common multiples. Hence every constacyclic code over a finite commutative ring has a unique maximal interleaving into copies of one support-primitive code, for arbitrary lengths and without a freeness assumption (Theorem 5.3). For a monic-divisor code, this period agrees with the polynomial support period, with an ambient-length convention for the full code. For an arbitrary simple-root ideal over A, it is the greatest common divisor of the periods of all torsion codes (Theorem 5.9). Equivalently, it is the stabilizer order of the function assigning to each root the exponent of its Chinese remainder component. The interleaving gives formulas for minimum distance, weight enumerators, covering radii, duals, and hulls. The Singleton bound for the core yields the arithmetic Singleton bound for free codes. The highest torsion code, which determines minimum distance, can have a larger period than the full code and therefore give a stronger bound (Theorem 6.6). At repeated-root lengths, a nonzero code generated by a pa-power substitution from a simple-root generator has the same support-primitive core, minimum distance, and arithmetic Singleton value as the original code. The intrinsic decomposition also applies to arbitrary ideals, but residue multiplicities and even the full torsion chain need not determine their intrinsic periods. Explicit examples show how these periods depend on the lifting coefficients. At lengths dividing q + 1 , quadratic trace factors determine hulls explicitly. Pairing factors with opposite traces at length p + 1 gives exact support periods and minimum distances. Section 2 reviews Galois rings, constacyclic codes, and MED representations. For a nonconstant monic divisor at a simple-root length, coefficient compression, the multiplicative stabilizer of its root set, and the interleaving of the associated constacyclic code determine the same support period. Hensel uniqueness preserves this period at every coefficient precision. For cyclic Frobenius-orbit polynomials, the MED invariant gives the support period and core degree. For arbitrary simple-root ideals, the intrinsic period is the greatest common divisor of the periods of the torsion codes. The coordinate-projection decomposition remains valid at arbitrary lengths, and the highest torsion code determines the minimum Hamming distance of every nonzero code over A. At repeated-root lengths, nonzero free codes obtained by a pa-power substitution from simple-root generators preserve the support-primitive core, distance, and arithmetic Singleton value. For general repeated-root ideals, even the complete torsion filtration need not determine the intrinsic period.
