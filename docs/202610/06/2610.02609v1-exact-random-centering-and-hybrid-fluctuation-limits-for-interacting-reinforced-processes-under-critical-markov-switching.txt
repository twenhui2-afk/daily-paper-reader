ABSTRACT
We study two interacting reinforced occupation processes driven by a common two-state Markov
environment whose switching probabilities decrease at the same rate as the stochastic-approximation
gain. At this critical scale, the environmental motion persists in the first-order limit, which is a
telegraph-driven piecewise deterministic Markov process. Consequently, centering at a deterministic
equilibrium does not separate the order-one environmental response from the intrinsic square-root
fluctuations. To overcome this obstruction, we introduce an exact discrete random centering that
removes the accumulated environmental forcing before rescaling. An exact decomposition into
collective and synchronization coordinates then separates the two contraction mechanisms of the
system. Under the contraction condition corresponding to the square-root regime, we prove joint
functional convergence of the randomly centered coordinates and the first-order background to a sta-
tionary hybrid PDMP–diffusion system. Its stationary entrance law is characterized through stochastic
convolutions over the remote logarithmic past. Conditionally on the complete environmental path, the
two fluctuation modes are independent centered Gaussian processes with a common state-dependent
volatility and different contraction rates. We also determine the invariant distribution of the first-order
component, derive explicit stationary second moments, and obtain a closed-form correlation formula
that recovers interaction information not visible in the synchronized first-order limit.

telegraph process · piecewise deterministic Markov process

Introduction
Fluctuation limits for stochastic approximation are commonly obtained by centering the recursion at a deterministic
equilibrium or at an almost surely convergent random target. Once this first-order behavior has been removed, the
remaining martingale noise can often be analyzed at the square-root scale. This approach becomes inadequate when
the driving environment evolves on the same asymptotic scale as the stochastic-approximation gain. In that case,
the environmental response may survive in the first-order limit as a nondegenerate stochastic process. Deterministic
centering then leaves an order-one component that dominates the intrinsic fluctuations, while direct centering at the
weak continuous-time limit does not provide the approximation accuracy required at the square-root scale.
The purpose of this paper is to resolve this centering problem for a pair of interacting reinforced occupation processes
subject to critical Markov switching. The central device is an exact discrete random centering that reproduces the
accumulated environmental response of the original recursion. Subtracting this response before rescaling separates the
persistent environmental dynamics from the intrinsic martingale fluctuations and provides the basis for the second-order
analysis developed below.

Reinforcement and environmental persistence are distinct mechanisms through which stochastic systems retain informa-
tion from the past. Reinforced random walks and Pólya urns are classical models in which past observations influence
future sampling probabilities [1, 2]. Their asymptotic analysis is closely connected with stochastic approximation,
martingale methods, and techniques from dynamical systems [3, 2].
Interacting reinforced processes provide a natural framework for repeated binary decisions in which individual behavior
is shaped both by past experience and by interaction with other components [4, 5]. Such mechanisms may be interpreted,
for instance, in models of collective choice, coordination, or opinion formation [6], where repeated decisions reinforce
previous tendencies while social interaction may promote alignment across individuals. When all components are
additionally exposed to a common changing environment, however, similarity between their occupation proportions
may arise not only from interaction but also from shared external forcing. Distinguishing these two mechanisms is
therefore important, since apparent agreement at the first-order level need not reveal whether it is primarily associated
with cross-interaction or with a persistent common environment. This motivates the study of whether the corresponding
fluctuation structure can retain information that is not visible from the synchronized first-order behavior.
The critical phenomenon considered here arises from the balance between the stochastic-approximation gain rn ∼n−1
and the switching probability qn ∼cn−1 of a common Markov environment. At this critical switching scale, the
environmental response remains dynamic in the first-order limit rather than averaging out or freezing. Consequently,
deterministic centering does not remove the order-one environmental contribution, which dominates the intrinsic
square-root fluctuations. This leads naturally to the exact random-centering procedure developed below and, ultimately,
to a hybrid PDMP–diffusion limit rather than an autonomous Gaussian fluctuation process.
These questions are closely related to the literature on synchronization and fluctuations in interacting reinforced
systems. Mean-field synchronization was studied in [7], while fluctuations around the synchronized regime were
analyzed in [8]. Network-based interaction models and their asymptotic behavior were developed in [9, 10], whereas
functional fluctuation limits for interacting reinforced random walks were established in [4]. Related second-order
analyses have also been used to develop statistical procedures for estimating interaction structures and strengths
[5, 11]. Subsequent work has addressed nonlinear reinforcement and possible failure of synchronization [6], complete
first-order classifications on general networks [12], mechanisms combining individual and collective reinforcement
[13], phase transitions in nonlinear or power-reinforced interacting urns [14, 15], and competitive or cooperative
interaction structures [16]. The question addressed here is how this fluctuation structure is modified when the interacting
components are driven by a common environment whose switching rate decreases over time.
A complementary line of research concerns stochastic approximation driven by time-inhomogeneous Markov noise.
Decreasing-step tracking under Markov noise with slowly varying statistics is studied in [17]. For constant-step schemes
with regime switching, continuous-time interpolations may converge to switching ordinary differential equations, while
rescaled tracking errors converge to switching diffusions [18]. Related limits for least-mean-square algorithms are
obtained in [19]. Central limit theorems in this setting are typically formulated after convergence to a stable target and
under ergodicity assumptions that permit a Poisson-equation decomposition of the Markov noise [20].
When the off-diagonal transition probabilities of a finite-state Markov chain decay as n−γ, its occupation behavior
exhibits averaging for 0 < γ < 1, a nondegenerate critical regime for γ = 1, and freezing for γ > 1 [21, 22]. Related
changes of scale occur in the coin-turning model [23]; at the critical order n−1, the associated walk has a nonclassical
continuous, piecewise-linear limit with Beta-distributed marginals [24]. More generally, the theory of asymptotic
pseudotrajectories connects time-inhomogeneous Markov chains with homogeneous continuous-time Markov processes
[25]. For freezing empirical measures, this leads to exponential zig-zag piecewise deterministic Markov processes and
functional convergence [22].
The setting considered here lies at the intersection of these two lines of research. The spectral gap of the environmental
transition kernel vanishes at the same order as the stochastic-approximation gain. Hence, the environment neither
averages out nor freezes on the logarithmic time scale; instead, it generates a nondegenerate stochastic first-order limit.
This is precisely why the conventional fluctuation argument breaks down. Deterministic centering retains an order-one
environmental component, and direct substitution of the weak PDMP limit is not sufficient because weak convergence
alone does not control the centering error at the square-root scale.

5
Concluding remarks
We have studied the fluctuation behavior of two interacting reinforced occupation processes when a common Markov
environment evolves on the same asymptotic scale as the stochastic-approximation recursion. At the critical switch-
ing scale, the environmental response survives in the first-order limit as a nondegenerate telegraph-driven PDMP.
Consequently, centering at a deterministic equilibrium does not isolate the intrinsic square-root fluctuations.
The main contribution is an exact discrete random centering that removes the accumulated environmental response
before rescaling. Under a > 1/2, the centered collective and synchronization coordinates converge jointly with the
first-order background to a stationary hybrid PDMP–diffusion system. Its two fluctuation modes have contraction rates
a −1/2 and b −1/2, respectively, and share an environment-dependent volatility. The two-sided stochastic-convolution
representation specifies the stationary entrance law inherited from the complete prelimit history.
The first-order analysis provides the averaging–critical–freezing classification associated with the competition between
the stochastic-approximation gain and the environmental switching probability. The synchronization coordinate
vanishes in all three regimes, whereas the collective coordinate averages to zero for 0 < γ < 1, remains random on the
logarithmic time scale for γ = 1, and converges to an environment-dependent equilibrium for γ > 1. In the critical
regime, the invariant marginal distribution of the first-order PDMP is an affine transformation of a symmetric Beta
distribution.
Conditionally on the complete environmental path, the collective and synchronization fluctuation modes are independent
centered Gaussian processes. Their recombination yields the correlation in (47), which is positive precisely when κ > 0.
Since the first-order dynamics depend on (ρ, κ) only through a = 1 −ρ −κ, while the second-order correlation also
contains the difference between the two contraction rates, the pair (a, χ) provides an asymptotic identifiability relation
for the self-interaction and cross-interaction coefficients.
The value 1/2 is the natural boundary of the square-root Gaussian regime for each contraction mode. Further questions
include the fluctuation behavior at this boundary, the corresponding nonstandard scalings in the subcritical contraction
regime, and extensions of the random-centering principle to larger interaction networks and finite-state switching
environments.
