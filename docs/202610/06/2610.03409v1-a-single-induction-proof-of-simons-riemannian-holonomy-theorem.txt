A single induction proof of Simons’ Riemannian holonomy theorem
Lei Ni
Abstract. We give a detailed and complete algebraic proof of Simons’ Riemannian holonomy theorem by
a single strong induction on the dimension of the linear span of the curvature orbit. The proof uses the
construction of flats and root centralizers, but replaces the detailed analysis of the common zero-weight
space, the most technical core of previous algebraic proofs, by a trace-vanishing lemma.
The lemma
applies to curvature operators contained in an ideal that annihilates the curvature module. Restriction
to a common geodesic subspace produces a proper invariant kernel. Induction makes this kernel fixed by
the holonomy action, after which the trace lemma shows that the root centralizers span the ambient space.
Their intersection recovers the maximal flat, and irreducibility completes the proof. For the completeness
we also include the detailed derivation of the local symmetry of the underlying Riemanian manifold from
the algebraic theorem.

1. Introduction
Berger’s holonomy theorem and Simons’ algebraic proof are fundamental results in Riemannian geometry
[2, 8]. We begin with the geometric statement.
Theorem 1.1 (Berger–Simons). Let (M n, gM) be a connected Riemannian manifold, n ≥2, whose
restricted holonomy group acts irreducibly on a tangent space. If this group is not transitive on the unit
sphere, then M is locally symmetric and has rank at least two.
A Riemannian holonomy system is a triple S = (V, R, G) consisting of a Euclidean space V of dimension
n ≥2, a connected compact subgroup G ⊂SO(V ), and an algebraic curvature tensor R such that
Rx,y ∈g := Lie(G) for all x, y ∈V . The system is irreducible if G acts irreducibly on V , and symmetric
if g(R) = R for every g ∈G. The action on curvature tensors and our sign conventions are specified in
Section 2 (cf. Section 2 of [4]).
Theorem 1.2 (Simons). An irreducible Riemannian holonomy system (V, R, G) is symmetric if G is
not transitive on the unit sphere of V .
For a holonomy system, write
M := G(R) = spanR{g(R) : g ∈G},
gR := spanR{Qx,y : Q ∈M, x, y ∈V }.
(1.1)
Thus G(R) is a linear space spanned by the orbit. The subspace gR is an ideal of g. Its orthogonal
complementary ideal is denoted by gR. The connected analytic subgroup with Lie algebra gR is denoted by
GR; in the irreducible setting it is closed and compact. These facts shall be proved in Section 2.
Theorem 1.3 (Simons). Let S = (V, R, G) be an irreducible Riemannian holonomy system. If GR is
not transitive on the unit sphere, then S is symmetric and has rank at least two.

The result below is standard. We include its proof for the completeness.
Lemma 2.3 (Closedness of subgroups whose Lie algebras are ideals). Let G ⊂SO(V ) be connected
and compact and act irreducibly on V . Every ideal of g integrates to a closed connected subgroup of G. In
particular, GR is compact.
Proof. First dim z(g) ≤1. Indeed, if 0̸ = A ∈z(g), the symmetric endomorphism A2 commutes with
G. Its eigenspaces are invariant, so irreducibility gives A2 = −a2 id with a > 0. If B ∈z(g), then AB is
likewise symmetric and commutes with G, hence is scalar. Thus B is a scalar multiple of A.
Use the elementary decomposition of a compact Lie algebra into its center and compact simple ideals:
g = z(g) ⊕g1 ⊕· · · ⊕gs.
An ideal is a sum of some gj and a subspace of the center. Since the center has dimension at most one, this
last subspace is either zero or the whole center. The simple factors integrate to compact connected subgroups,
and the connected central subgroup is a torus of dimension at most one. Their product is compact and with
the prescribed Lie algebra. Uniqueness of the connected analytic subgroup proves the assertion.
□

Corollary 2.4. A connected Lie subgroup H ⊂SO(V ) acting irreducibly on V is closed and compact.
Proof. Its closure H is compact, connected and irreducible. Since Adh Lie(H) = Lie(H) for h ∈H,
continuity gives the same invariance under H. Thus Lie(H) is an ideal of Lie(H). Lemma 2.3 shows that
the corresponding analytic subgroup, namely H, is closed in H.
