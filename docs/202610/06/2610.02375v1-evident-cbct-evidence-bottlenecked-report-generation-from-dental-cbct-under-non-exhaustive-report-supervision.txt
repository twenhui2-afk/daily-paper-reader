EviDent-CBCT: Evidence-Bottlenecked Report Generation from Dental CBCT
under Non-Exhaustive Report Supervision
Ruiyang Hao, Zhi Qin Tan, Yulan He, Owen Addison, Yunpeng Li∗
King’s College London
{ruiyang.hao, zhi_qin.tan, yulan.he, owen.addison, yunpeng.li}@kcl.ac.uk
Abstract
Dento-maxillofacial cone-beam CT (CBCT) reports may con-
tain dozens of tooth-specific, anatomical, and spatial findings
from a single 3D scan. Learning to generate such reports
from limited clinical data is challenging because routine re-
ports may not exhaustively document image findings, and a
non-mention may reflect either absence or non-reporting. We
present EviDent-CBCT, an evidence-bottlenecked framework
designed for this incomplete supervision. An anatomy-aware
network maps each CBCT scan to a discrete record of tooth-
level, global, and tooth-IAC evidence. A dental-logic consis-
tency projection reconciles incompatible evidence before a de-
terministic renderer and an image-blind local language model
generate the report using only this record. For tooth-level evi-
dence, reliability-aware training uses eligible non-mentions as
reduced-weight negatives, while unreported global and tooth-
IAC labels remain unknown. A metal-sensitive input chan-
nel preserves intensity cues from dental materials. Across
three validation runs, EviDent-CBCT achieves 0.666±0.006
merged evidence set-F1 and 0.402 ± 0.003 RadFact-Lite-
Dental logical-F1, versus 0.371±0.018 for the strongest con-
trolled direct baseline. In the ODIN 2026 challenge, it ranked
second in automated evaluation and third in blinded clinical
Arena comparison on the hidden test set. These results sup-
port the discrete evidence record as an effective and auditable
interface for CBCT report generation.

Introduction
Cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) provides three-
dimensional views of dento-maxillofacial structures and
is widely used for dental diagnosis and treatment plan-
ning (Kaasalainen et al. 2021; Reddy et al. 2024). A single
scan may reveal findings involving individual teeth, the jaws,
sinuses, scan coverage, and spatial relations to the inferior
alveolar canal (IAC), often requiring assignment to a specific
anatomical region or FDI tooth position. CBCT reporting is
therefore more than image captioning: a system must recover
case-specific findings, preserve their anatomical identity and
polarity, and express them coherently.
Routine reports, however, record what was documented
in clinical practice rather than exhaustively annotating the
image. A non-mention may indicate true absence, report-
specific omission, habitual non-reporting, or oversight, and
these mechanisms cannot be distinguished from the report

alone (Pandharipande et al. 2016; Hartung et al. 2020). Nev-
ertheless, aggregate non-mention patterns may provide use-
ful negative signal without establishing absence in an indi-
vidual case. We refer to this setting as non-exhaustive routine
report supervision; its scope and interpretation are detailed
in supplementary Sect. A.
This setting is particularly challenging for direct image-
to-text generation. Recent radiology systems connect visual
encoders to large language models (Wang et al. 2023; Li
et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2024a; Xiao et al. 2025), but with
limited CBCT-report pairs, a direct generator must jointly
learn three-dimensional anatomy, visual grounding, report-
specific content selection, and language generation from in-
complete targets. These functions remain entangled, obscur-
ing whether candidate-reference discrepancies arise from vi-
sual prediction, incomplete documentation, or realization.
Canonicalization reduces linguistic variation (Vosshenrich
et al. 2023; Bannur et al. 2023) but not sparse visual supervi-
sion; auxiliary evidence losses do not ensure that generation
depends on predicted evidence; and fixed templates provide
control at the cost of expressive flexibility (Delbrouck et al.
2025; Li et al. 2025). These limitations motivate an explicit
content interface that separates evidence prediction from lan-
guage realization and prevents the decoder from bypassing
predicted findings.
We propose EviDent-CBCT, a three-stage framework built
around a discrete evidence bottleneck (Figure 1). Stage 1
segments individual teeth and key anatomical structures and
extracts structure-specific features. Stage 2 uses anatomy-
aligned branches to predict tooth-level, global, and tooth-IAC
evidence; eligible tooth-level non-mentions provide reduced-
weight negative supervision, unreported global and tooth-
IAC labels remain unknown, and a metal-sensitive chan-
nel preserves dental-material intensity cues. A determinis-
tic dental-logic projection then reconciles incompatible tu-
ples using anatomical semantics and confidence. Stage 3
renders the corrected record into a deterministic draft and
supplies only this draft to an image-blind language model.
Thus, unlike direct generators conditioned on continuous vi-
sual representations, EviDent-CBCT makes the consistency-
projected record the sole source of case-specific informa-
tion, preventing bypass while keeping content selection and
rewrite-induced deviations auditable. Across three validation
runs, EviDent-CBCT achieves 0.402 ± 0.003 RadFact-Lite-

Related Work
Medical report generation.
Medical report generation has
evolved from recurrent and joint disease-report models (Jing,
Xie, and Xing 2018; Wang et al. 2018) to memory-based,
retrieval-based, and region-guided architectures (Chen et al.
2020, 2021; Endo et al. 2021; Tanida et al. 2023). Recent sys-
tems increasingly connect visual encoders to large language
models through frozen-LLM adaptation, diagnosis prompts,
knowledge augmentation, and flexible token fusion (Wang
et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2024a; Jin et al. 2024; Li et al. 2024;
Wang et al. 2025; Goswami, Subedi, and Chakraborty 2025;
Shi et al. 2026). Most prior work targets chest radiographs,


while CT2Rep extends direct generation to volumetric CT
(Hamamci, Er, and Menze 2024). Despite these advances,
report decoders generally remain conditioned on continuous
visual features, retrieved text, or soft diagnostic guidance.
Factuality and evidence interfaces.
Factual correctness
has consequently become a central concern. Prior work op-
timizes clinical coherence and entity, relation, or semantic-
level agreement (Liu et al. 2019; Miura et al. 2021; Delbrouck
et al. 2022); RadGraph supports structured clinical extrac-
tion (Jain et al. 2021), and RadFact evaluates reports through
bidirectional proposition entailment (Bannur et al. 2024).
Grounding, preference optimization, and reasoning further
improve visual attribution and factuality (Bannur et al. 2024;
Xiao et al. 2025; Jing et al. 2026), but do not require genera-
tion to proceed through an explicit record of predicted find-
ings. Structured approaches such as KERP, region-guided
generation, and concept bottlenecks instead separate content
prediction from verbalization (Li et al. 2019; Koh et al. 2020;
Tanida et al. 2023). EviDent-CBCT makes this separation a
hard inference-time constraint: an auditable evidence record
is the sole case-specific input to an image-blind realizer.
Dental CBCT Analysis.
Dental CBCT learning has pri-
marily focused on anatomical segmentation. nnU-Net pro-
vides a strong general framework (Isensee et al. 2021);
connectivity-aware methods address the thin inferior alveolar
canal (Liu et al. 2024b; Bolelli et al. 2025); and recent work
expands multi-structure segmentation through large dental
benchmarks and state-space models (Bolelli et al. 2026; Tan
et al. 2025). Moving toward clinical interpretation, Lumetti et
al. formulate dental CBCT reporting as ontology-grounded
prediction of finding presence, anatomical allocation, and
clinical qualifiers from clinician-validated structured targets
(Lumetti et al. 2026). Their system treats structured predic-
tion as the final output and does not address narrative real-
ization; its task-specific schema and structured metrics are
therefore not directly comparable to free-text report genera-
tion. EviDent-CBCT instead learns tooth-level, global, and
tooth-IAC evidence from selective, non-exhaustive routine
reports and makes the predicted discrete record the required
case-specific input to an image-blind narrative realizer.

Let x denote a 3D CBCT volume and y its observed clini-
cal report. Direct generation estimates p(y | x). We instead
derive a finite evidence record

deviations directly auditable and allows evidence prediction
to be evaluated separately from language realization.
4
Method
Overview
As illustrated in Figure 1, the method isolates these measur-
able interfaces: anatomical localization, report-derived ev-
idence recovery, deterministic consistency correction, and
textual realization of the selected evidence. Stage 1 supplies
anatomical masks and reusable structure tokens. Stage 2
maps anatomy-aligned inputs to a partially observed mul-
tilabel record. Stage 3 converts the record to prose while
keeping the content interface discrete.
Reference Evidence Construction
Before model training, we construct reference evidence from
each observed report with a two-pass, dental-knowledge-
guided workflow. The first pass GPT-5.5 agent identifies
coverage statements, tooth-indexed findings, tooth-IAC rela-
tions, global clinical facts, and normal or explicitly negative
findings, while retaining the supporting report span for ev-
ery item. A review-and-correction agent implemented with
Claude Opus 4.8 then verifies each structured item against its
source text, confirming 98.3% on the first pass and directly
correcting the remaining 1.7% before Stage 2 label con-
struction. Schema definitions and verification procedures are
provided in Sect. B of supplementary.
The resulting JSON is deliberately richer than the com-
pact Stage 2 interface: it combines normalized entries with
free-form facts and source text, whereas Stage 2 projects
recurrent, learnable content into 11 tooth, 12 global, and
8 relation categories. The complete prediction schema and
label frequencies are listed in supplementary Sect. B. The
workflow structures the observed report; it does not claim to
adjudicate the report or create exhaustive clinical truth.
Stage 1: Anatomy Segmentation and Feature
Exposure
Stage 1 is an nnU-Net-v2-style 3D U-Mamba2 segmenter
(Tan et al. 2025) trained on ToothFairy3 to predict 46
segmentation classes, including FDI-position tooth masks,
mandible, maxilla, sinuses, canals, and dental-material
masks. It supplies anatomical masks for crop construction
and semantic channels, together with pooled encoder tokens
for individual structures. The segmenter achieves a macro
Dice of 0.899 over present structures and a case-averaged
Dice of 0.880; training and feature-extraction details are pro-
vided in Sect. C of supplementary.
Stage 2: Reliability-Aware Evidence Prediction
Branch-specific views.
The schema is decomposed into
three multilabel branches with separate anatomical inputs
and classifiers. The tooth branch predicts 11 findings at


each of 32 FDI positions from tooth-centered crops. The
global branch predicts 12 volume-level findings from a down-
sampled full volume. The relation branch predicts eight
canal-related findings from left and right posterior-mandible
views. This decomposition aligns each receptive field and
output space with the corresponding clinical unit.
Anatomy-aligned fusion.
Each branch contains a from-
scratch 3D encoder that produces an appearance feature r.
The global branch uses r directly. The relation and tooth
branches project the corresponding Stage 1 token into a con-
text feature c and use an anatomical embedding s to identify
the tooth or region. Their gated hybrid representation is
g = σ(Wg[r; c; s] + bg),
z = (1 −g) ⊙r + g ⊙c. (3)
where Wg and bg are the learnable projection matrix and bias
of the element-wise fusion gate. The hybrid representation
can exploit segmentation context while retaining raw inten-
sity patterns that Stage 1 may discard. The main system uses
a scratch global branch and a hybrid relation branch. The
corresponding global-relation interface sweep is reported in
supplementary Sect. F.
Metal-sensitive intensity channel.
Standard preprocess-
ing compresses high-intensity CBCT values, obscuring dif-
ferences among metallic restorations and prostheses. We
therefore add a raw-intensity channel to each tooth crop,
with its window estimated from high-density dental-material
regions within the tooth-centered crop. It suppresses lower-
density tissue while preserving normalized variation among
high-density materials and artifacts, complementing the con-
ventional anatomical channel.
Reliability-aware tooth-level non-mention supervision.
Report parsing assigns each tooth-class pair an observed
positive, an observed negative, or an unmentioned state.
We apply non-mention supervision only to eligible tooth-
level labels; unreported global and tooth-IAC labels remain
unknown and are excluded from the corresponding losses.
Motivated by the hypothesis that eligible tooth-level non-
mentions are predominantly negative in aggregate, while re-
maining uncertain at the individual-case level, we compare
three policies: explicit excludes it from the loss, hard
assigns it a unit-weight negative label, and pu-weak assigns
it a negative label with reduced, class-dependent weight.
This reliability-weighting strategy is inspired by positive-
unlabeled (PU) learning (Kiryo et al. 2017). The weighted
tooth-branch objective is

Tooth-level sampling and missingness conditioning.
Within the tooth branch, per-class epoch sampling retains
all supervised positives while limiting exposure to abun-
dant negatives. A detached missing-tooth probability condi-
tions the remaining attribute head, allowing it to distinguish
present and likely-absent tooth contexts without propagating
attribute loss into missingness prediction. The sampling and
conditioning details are given in Sect. C of supplementary.
Stage 3: Evidence-Bottlenecked Report Realization
Dental-logic consistency projection.
Before rendering,
deterministic constraints derived from dental anatomical se-
mantics map bE to eE = Πdent( bE). Rather than treating se-
lected labels independently, the projection removes combina-
tions that violate predefined dental compatibility constraints,
using predicted confidence only to resolve mutually exclu-
sive alternatives. It operates solely on predicted tuples, in-
troduces no positive finding, and leaves the corrected record
inspectable at the evidence interface. The complete constraint
definitions are provided in supplementary Sect. C.
Deterministic evidence rendering.
A non-parametric ren-
derer converts the consistency-projected evidence set eE
into a canonical draft. Findings are grouped by compatible
anatomical region and finding type, with compact templates
used for repetitive patterns such as a fully edentulous arch.
The resulting draft explicitly enumerates the content selected
by Stage 2 while leaving sentence structure, ordering, aggre-
gation, and clinical phrasing to the learned realizer.
Content-preserving teacher rewrites.
For each training
case, we construct the draft from consistency-projected pre-
dicted evidence, thereby exposing the realizer to inputs re-
sembling those encountered at deployment. GPT-4o (OpenAI
2024) then rewrites the draft into natural clinical language.
The corresponding clinical report is provided only as a lin-
guistic reference, and GPT-4o is instructed to preserve the
factual content of the draft without adding, removing, or cor-
recting findings. The resulting rewrites serve as realization
targets rather than corrected evidence labels.
Image-blind
behavioral
distillation.
We
distill
the
teacher’s evidence-to-report realization behavior into a lo-
cal Qwen2.5-7B rewriter adapted with low-rank adaptation
(LoRA) (Hu et al. 2022). The student receives only the de-
terministic draft during both training and inference; it has
no access to the CBCT volume, the reference report, or a
parallel visual representation through which it could bypass
the evidence bottleneck. It is therefore not explicitly trained
to identify or correct Stage 2 prediction errors. We retain the
deterministic draft for direct comparison, and inference re-
quires no external model call. Decoding details are reported
in supplementary Sect. C. We empirically evaluate the trained
student rewriter’s content preservation and rewrite fidelity in
Auditability and Rewrite Fidelity, with additional analyses
provided in supplementary Sect. D.

Evaluation Metrics
Merged Stage 2 logical set-F1.
For each case, positive tu-
ples parsed from the reference report are pooled across the
tooth, global, and relation branches within the evaluation
mask. Predicted and reference-derived tuples match only
when their structured keys are identical. We compute per-
case precision and recall, average each quantity over cases,
and take the harmonic mean of those two averages. This
RadFact-style aggregation is an LLM-free measure of struc-
tured agreement with the reports. Merged Stage 2 logical
set-F1 is computed on the selected tuples before consistency
projection, isolating the learned evidence predictor from de-
terministic correction. Its formal definition and aggregation
conventions are given in supplementary Sect. G.
Internal report metrics.
RadFact decomposes each narra-
tive report into clinical propositions and evaluates entailment
bidirectionally (Bannur et al. 2024). Candidate propositions
supported by the reference determine logical precision, while
reference propositions supported by the candidate determine
logical recall; logical F1 is their harmonic mean. We build
on RadFact-Lite (Lumetti 2026) with a dentist-guided dental-
atomic parser, FDI normalization, and dental entailment in-
structions. Exact and rule-guaranteed matches are resolved
deterministically, while comparisons are judged by GPT-5.6-
Luna. The original bidirectional aggregation is unchanged.
We report these metrics together with BLEU-4 (Papineni
et al. 2002) and METEOR (Banerjee and Lavie 2005). Main-
paper metrics use the same fixed reference report per volume;
supplementary Sect. E provides a mean-over-references ro-
bustness analysis. Full details and an author audit of RadFact-
Lite-Dental are provided in supplementary Sect. G. The of-
ficial ODIN evaluation uses a different RadFact protocol.
We therefore report its scores separately and do not directly
compare their absolute values with RadFact-Lite-Dental.
Paired uncertainty analysis.
For the primary comparison,
we average case-level logical metrics across runs, jointly
bootstrap matched cases, recompute corpus-level logical F1,
and report the percentile 95% confidence interval of paired
differences. Details are provided in supplementary Sect. E.

Auditability and Rewrite Fidelity
The deterministic draft makes learned realization auditable
at the proposition level. An author reviewed 30 evalua-
tion cases for additions, omissions, polarity changes, FDI
or anatomical-side reassignment, finding substitutions, and
redundancy. All template propositions were preserved in
28 cases (93.3%), with no polarity reversal, FDI reassign-
ment, or anatomical-side reassignment. The two substantive
changes both aligned the rewrite more closely with the ref-
erence: one removed a crown prediction where the reference
described a restoration, and the other changed a restoration
to a crown. Rewriting is therefore not guaranteed to be loss-
less, even though individual changes can regularize imperfect
predicted evidence. Its modest aggregate improvement is re-
ported in Table 2, and retaining the pre-rewrite draft keeps
case-level changes inspectable. The full audit protocol and
case-level deviations are reported in supplementary Sect. D.

Within the tooth branch, treating every eligible non-

Conclusion and Outlook
We presented EviDent-CBCT, an evidence-bottlenecked
framework for learning dento-maxillofacial CBCT reports
from non-exhaustive routine-report supervision. Its discrete
record separates evidence prediction from language real-
ization, enables deterministic dental-consistency correction,
and makes selected report content auditable. For tooth-level
evidence, eligible non-mentions receive reduced-weight neg-
ative supervision, while unreported global and tooth-IAC
labels remain unknown. Across three validation runs, the
system achieves 0.402 ± 0.003 RadFact-Lite-Dental logical-
F1, compared with 0.371±0.018 for the strongest controlled
direct generator. It further ranked second in the ODIN 2026

Supplementary Material of EviDent-CBCT
A
Scope of the Report-Derived Supervision
EviDent-CBCT starts from the clinical reports available in Tooth-
Fairy4. We do not interpret these reports as exhaustive image an-
notations or construct an independently adjudicated medical gold
standard. A non-mention may reflect true absence, report-specific
omission, habitual non-reporting, oversight, or disagreement across
readers. These mechanisms are not adjudicated and cannot be dis-
tinguished from the observed reports alone. Explicit report inaccu-
racies are also possible, but their prevalence is not measured in this
study.
The three policies in the main paper apply only to eligible tooth-
level non-mentions. explicit excludes these entries from the
loss, hard assigns them unit negative weight, and pu-weak as-
signs class-dependent reduced negative weights. Unreported global
and tooth-IAC categories remain unknown and are excluded from
supervision because their positive labels are sparse. None of these
policies adjudicates explicit report inaccuracies, and our experi-
ments do not estimate their prevalence. Accordingly, the reported
Stage 2 metric measures structured agreement with the reports
rather than clinical accuracy against an external standard.
B
Reference Evidence Construction and
Coverage Audit
Construction and independent verification
The full reference evidence JSON is produced before Stage 2 pre-
processing. GPT-5.5 (medium effort) receives the original report
and a dental-specific extraction prompt covering FDI notation, arch
and quadrant expressions, missing and treated teeth, prostheses and
implants, bone findings, scan coverage, and tooth-IAC relations.
It returns structured sections for coverage, tooth labels, relation
labels, additional clinical facts, and normal or explicitly negative
statements. Every item includes the report text that supports it, so
the normalized entry remains traceable to the observed wording.
A second agent implemented through Claude Opus4.8 indepen-
dently reviews each JSON against the report. The reviewer checks
support, polarity, tooth identity, anatomical assignment, complete-
ness, and the correspondence of each attached span; it either accepts
the entry or requests calibration. The reported 98.3% first-pass con-
firmation rate is the proportion of entries that this Opus4.8 reviewer
accepted without requesting calibration. The remaining 1.7% are
flagged and corrected before constructing Stage 2 labels. This is
agent-review agreement with the report, not dentist validation or
correctness against an independently read CBCT volume.
Full JSON versus the prediction schema
Two representations should not be conflated. The full evidence
JSON preserves categorical fields, free-form facts, normal/negative
statements, and verbatim source evidence. The Stage 2 interface is
a task-oriented projection containing 11 tooth-level, 12 global, and
eight relation categories. Rare details can therefore remain auditable
in the full JSON without becoming dedicated output classes for
image-based prediction. The ceiling row “verbatim source clues”
evaluates the former; the template and Qwen ceiling rows evaluate
the latter after projection and realization.
Complete Stage 2 evidence schema
Tables 5-7 enumerate the complete prediction schema. Counts are
positive reference-derived labels pooled across the fixed train-
ing and validation partitions. Tooth counts denote FDI-position-
category instances; global and relation counts denote case-category
instances. They are label frequencies in the observed reports, not es-
timates of clinical prevalence. For sparse global and relation labels,
an unreported category remains unknown. For tooth labels, non-
mentions eligible for positive-unlabeled supervision are assigned
the reliability weights in Table 5; explicit positive and negative
statements receive weight 1, while unavailable positions and con-
tradictory statements receive weight 0.
Coverage protocol
For each evaluation case, we recursively collect the attached text
fields from the coverage, tooth-label, relation-label, free-form fact,
and normal/negative sections. After whitespace normalization, du-
plicate clues and shorter clues wholly contained in longer ones are
removed, and the retained spans are restored to their order in the
original report. This yields 555 unique clues. Every retained clue is
an exact substring after whitespace normalization.
We report two complementary coverage measures. Literal cov-
erage is the fraction of alphanumeric report characters contained
in the union of retained source spans; its case-averaged value is
96.5% and its median is 100%. Semantic coverage concatenates the
retained clues and compares them with the report using RadFact-
Lite-Dental. The resulting logical precision, recall, and F1 are 0.986,
0.929, and 0.957, respectively. Literal coverage measures retained
wording, whereas logical recall measures whether the selected spans
support the propositions recovered from the report.
Residual content
We inspect report text outside the union of all retained source
spans after excluding section headers, punctuation, and connective
fragments. Table 8 summarizes the substantive residuals. They are
concentrated in low-frequency metadata, classifications, and broad
normal statements rather than recurring core dental findings.
The audit supports a precise interpretation: major report con-
tent is comprehensively captured, and the supporting text preserves
most fine detail verbatim, but the normalized structured fields inten-
tionally emphasize macroscopic, recurrent concepts. The residual
concepts are too sparse in this corpus to justify separate super-
vised classes. The decrease from 0.957 for source clues to 0.597
for reference-derived evidence after template rendering and Qwen
rewriting should therefore be attributed primarily to post-extraction
projection and realization, rather than to wholesale failure of the
initial evidence extraction.
Coverage-to-ceiling interpretation
The coverage audit and ceiling study examine successive repre-
sentations of the same observed reports. Source spans retained in
the full evidence JSON preserve fine-grained statements verbatim,
reaching 96.5% mean literal coverage and 0.957 logical-F1 when
reassembled. Together with the two-pass verification results, this
indicates that the extraction workflow captures the major content of
the observed reports.

cise distribution and degree of atrophy, treatment quality, bridge
topology, and lesion morphology or size. Rare free-form findings
and normal statements outside the schema are likewise unavail-

Implementation Details
Stage 1.
The 46-class nnU-Net-v2-style U-Mamba2 segmenter
uses 128 × 256 × 256 patches and a 1,500-epoch schedule. In ad-
dition to segmentation masks, it exposes 1,408-dimensional pooled
encoder tokens for individual teeth and anatomical structures.
Stage 2.
The fixed tooth configuration performs label-wise sam-
pling at the tooth-instance level. For every class and epoch, all
supervised positives are retained and eligible negatives are sam-
pled without replacement. The target negative-to-positive ratio is
0.5 ≤n−/n+ < 2 when enough negatives are available; otherwise
all available negatives are retained. Sampling determines the active
label-instance pairs, while their explicit or pu-weak reliability
weights remain in the loss. The PU-weak weights are predefined
reliability weights under the guidance of the clinical routine, not
learned or tuned on validation data. Unmentioned negatives receive
class-dependent weights reflecting how reliably non-mention im-
plies absence. For example, if a mask-present tooth is not reported
as missing, it is highly likely to be present, so missing receives a
high weak-negative weight. In contrast, non-mention of caries is
less reliable because subtle caries may be overlooked or selectively
omitted, so caries receives a lower weight.
For positions with a Stage 1 tooth mask, the mask center and
principal axes define the oriented crop. Mask-absent positions re-
ceive only a fixed crop centered by interpolation from teeth in the
same quadrant and no tooth-branch supervision; their missing status
is inherited directly from the Stage 1 mask during branch merging.
For a classifiable position with tooth feature u, the missing proba-
bility m = σ(hm(u)) conditions the remaining attributes through
[u; mdetachϕ(u)], allowing missingness to coexist with residual
or prosthetic findings without propagating attribute loss into the
missing head. Each branch is trained independently. The main con-

An author manually reviewed 30 evaluation cases selected by sim-

each case, the deterministic template and Qwen rewrite were com-

all template propositions in 28 of 30 cases (93.3%). Table 10 sum-

Both substantive departures moved the output toward the refer-

finding. In case A014, the rewrite omitted a predicted prosthetic
crown at tooth 24 and redundantly repeated endodontic treatment at

RadFact-Lite-Dental
RadFact-Lite-Dental is a dentist-guided evaluation extension built
from RadFact-Lite v0.1.0 (Lumetti 2026), a lightweight implemen-
tation of the text-only RadFact procedure (Bannur et al. 2024). It
retains RadFact’s bidirectional proposition-entailment protocol and


corpus aggregation while adapting proposition construction and en-
tailment assessment to dento-maxillofacial reports. GPT-5.6-Luna
first parses each report into complete, image-verifiable dental propo-
sitions. The parser atomizes tooth lists but preserves qualifiers such
as laterality, severity, extent, uncertainty, visibility, treatment status,
and anatomical relations; bridge spans remain relational proposi-
tions rather than independent tooth labels.
Before LLM assessment, conservative normalization maps
equivalent dotted and undotted FDI notation to a common form,
removes semantic duplicates, and resolves exact or closed-form
positive entailments. The deterministic layer never rejects an unre-
solved comparison. Remaining hypotheses are assessed jointly for
each case and direction with dental-specific instructions that require
agreement in tooth position, anatomy, side, finding type, polarity,
and clinically meaningful qualifiers. Parsing and entailment outputs
are cached for identical normalized inputs, and this final RadFact-
Lite-Dental configuration is used for all report-level evaluations.
For report-level evaluation, the report with the lexicographically
smallest identifier was fixed as the reference for each volume and
used across all systems and evaluator runs.
Official ODIN hidden-test RadFact uses a different evaluation
protocol and is therefore reported separately without direct numer-
ical comparison to RadFact-Lite-Dental.

We conducted an
author audit of 30 candidate-reference report pairs, sampling ten
cases from each tertile of total proposition count. For each pair, the
complete reports, proposition boundaries, and every bidirectional
entailment decision were reviewed to establish author-audited gold
scores. Table 14 compares these scores with the original RadFact-
Lite v0.1.0 implementation using GPT-4o without dental FDI nor-

reduces MAE by 52.7% to .043, increases Pearson correlation to

Thus, its small aggregate offset accompanies substantially closer

Class-wise Stage 2 Branch Analysis
The three supervision policies alter only the tooth branch; the crop
geometry, gated hybrid encoder, metal-sensitive channel, global
branch, and relation branch are otherwise fixed. We therefore com-
pare pu-weak, explicit, and hard directly at the tooth inter-
face. For a tooth category, a tuple is defined by its FDI position
and finding label. Table 15 reports exact-tuple precision, recall, and
F1 on the strict evaluation mask, with unmentioned eligible tooth
attributes treated as negatives.
The branch-level ordering matches the aggregate tooth-branch
ablation in the main paper: pu-weak gives the highest tooth logical
set-F1, followed by hard and then explicit. Its advantage is not
uniform across every rare label, but it obtains the highest class F1 for
six of the 11 categories, including missing teeth, crowns, endodontic
treatment, prosthesis-related findings, impaction/disodontiasis, and
caries. The explicit model often retains recall while losing pre-
cision under the strict mask, most visibly for restorations (P=.161,
R=.943) and caries (P=.014, R=.667), showing that ignored non-
mentions leave many unsupported positives unconstrained. Full-
weight negatives recover much of this precision, while reduced-
reliability negatives provide a better aggregate balance. Root frac-
ture, caries, and root remnant have only 1, 10, and 12 positive vali-
dation instances, respectively; their policy-specific ordering should
therefore be interpreted as descriptive rather than as a stable rank-
ing.
