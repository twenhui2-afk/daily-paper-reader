ABSTRACT
On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) uses reference solutions as privileged hindsight
to supervise student-generated reasoning trajectories. However, reference-based
guidance may explain a correct solution without addressing why the student’s own
reasoning fails. This reasoning mismatch between the guidance provided and the
correction needed can encourage the student to borrow correct conclusions while
leaving its reasoning errors unresolved. Moreover, applying the same hindsight
throughout the trajectory risks a distillation trap, where unnecessary constraints
on valid reasoning compete with correction of substantive errors. To address
these issues, we propose Root-Cause-Guided On-Policy Distillation (RC-OPD),
which uses repairs of the student’s own reasoning to provide guidance that ad-
dresses its specific errors while building on valid progress. For each failed attempt,
RC-OPD locates the earliest substantive error, develops a local correction, and uses
the corrected intermediate result as an anchor for the valid prefix. An iterative
diagnosis–repair–continuation process tests the repairs through student continua-
tion, identifying further errors within a fixed repair budget. For repair chains that
reach a correct answer, root–cause–guided distillation uses failure diagnoses and
corrective goals to supervise the erroneous segments, while anchor-guided distilla-
tion supports the corresponding valid prefixes with reasoning chains leading to the
repaired intermediate results. We evaluate RC-OPD across multiple datasets and
model scales. Extensive experiments and analyses show that it mitigates reasoning
mismatch and the distillation trap, yielding substantial performance gains. Code is
available at https://github.com/Starrylay/RC-OPD.git.

INTRODUCTION
Large language models have made substantial progress in mathematical reasoning by generating
intermediate steps and learning from demonstrations and feedback (Wei et al., 2022; Shao et al.,
2024). A central concern in this development is how to provide useful supervision over a long
reasoning process. Outcome supervision evaluates completed answers, while process supervision
offers feedback on intermediate decisions (Lightman et al., 2023). On-policy distillation provides
dense teacher distributions at the prefixes generated by the student, reducing the mismatch between
training on demonstrations and reasoning at inference time (Agarwal et al., 2024). On-policy self-
distillation (OPSD) extends this approach by using the student itself, conditioned on a reference
solution as privileged hindsight, as the teacher (Zhao et al., 2026a). The student can thus learn on its
own trajectories with guidance informed by a correct solution, making OPSD an appealing approach
to reasoning post-training.
However, reference-based guidance may explain a correct solution without addressing the specific
errors in the student’s own reasoning. The teacher may supply an intermediate conclusion without
explaining how to correct the student’s derivation to reach it. We call this reasoning mismatch:
a misalignment between the guidance provided and the correction needed in the student’s own
attempt. The student may then accept a supplied intermediate conclusion without establishing the
steps that justify it. As shown in the top panel of Figure 1, a Borrowed Reference Segment skips
over the missing reasoning step labeled Unreachable. The response reaches the correct answer

The underlying challenge is to turn knowledge of
a successful solution into a correction that the stu-
dent can use from its own reasoning state. A failed
attempt can contain a valid prefix, an initiating
error, and a suffix affected by that error. Provid-
ing a conclusion from another route can bridge
these segments in the output without explaining
the missing inference. Querying the teacher at
student-generated prefixes does not automatically
resolve this problem, since the teacher still has
access to the reference conclusions. Useful hind-
sight must instead explain the local fault and es-
tablish a corrected intermediate result supported
by the retained prefix. Its value should also be
tested through the student’s continuation: can
the student proceed from this repair, rather than
merely accept a supplied endpoint? This calls for
trajectory-grounded hindsight, distinct supervision
for the erroneous segment and valid prefix, and
renewed diagnosis when the continuation exposes
another error.
Our central insight is to use local repairs of the stu-
dent’s own reasoning to construct guidance for cor-
recting its errors and supporting its valid progress.
Based on this insight, we propose Root-Cause-
Guided On-Policy Distillation (RC-OPD) with three components. First, Diagnose locates the
stage containing the earliest substantive error, termed the Error Stage, and repairs it into an Anchor
Stage: a corrected reasoning step that builds on the retained prefix and provides a valid starting
point for continuation. Diagnosis also produces Failure Reason & Goal, explaining the local fault
and the intermediate result needed to correct it, and COT to Anchor, a concise chain of thought
connecting the problem conditions to the Anchor Stage along the student’s viable route. Second,
Iterative Counterfactual Validation tests the repair by letting the same student policy continue from
the repaired prefix. If the answer remains incorrect, diagnosis proceeds in the new continuation,
retaining earlier Anchor Stages while correcting successive Error Stages. Successful completion
selects the associated diagnosed prefixes and Error Stages for supervision. Third, Differentiated
Distillation conditions the teacher on Failure Reason & Goal for each Error Stage and on COT to
Anchor for the corresponding diagnosed valid prefix. When validation does not reach a correct answer
within the repair budget, supervision falls back to reference-conditioned distillation on the original
trajectory. Together, these components ground hindsight in local diagnosis, test it through student
continuation, and assign it according to each reasoning segment’s role.
Our contributions are threefold: 1) We formulate reasoning mismatch and the distillation trap as two
connected challenges in using reference-based hindsight for on-policy supervision. 2) We propose
RC-OPD, which uses local repairs to construct root-cause-guided supervision for erroneous segments
and anchor-guided supervision for valid prefixes. 3) We introduce iterative counterfactual validation
to address successive errors and select local supervision based on successful student continuation.

RELATED WORK
On-policy self-distillation develops from a central challenge in learning from demonstrations: the pre-
fixes encountered during student generation can differ from those in the training solutions. MiniLLM
and Generalized Knowledge Distillation address this mismatch by querying a teacher along student-
generated trajectories, providing distributional feedback on decisions the student actually makes (Gu
et al., 2024; Agarwal et al., 2024). Building on this principle, OPSD obtains a teaching signal from an
asymmetry in available information: a teacher derived from the student’s base model receives a refer-
ence solution, while the student sees the question alone (Zhao et al., 2026a). Reference-conditioned
predictions then provide dense supervision at the student’s own prefixes, allowing verified reasoning
traces to guide learning beyond direct imitation of their tokens. Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning applies a
related idea to continual learning, using demonstrations as teacher context to acquire new capabilities
while preserving existing ones (Shenfeld et al., 2026). Together, these approaches show how richer
context can supply an on-policy teaching signal from the model itself. The availability of this signal
raises a further question: how should it be adapted to the structure of an individual rollout? DASH
studies the allocation of supervision, using the temporal evolution of teacher–student divergences
to adapt token weights through multi-step aggregation (Hou et al., 2026). This makes supervision
sensitive to how discrepancies unfold during generation. ROSD addresses the semantic content of
feedback by constructing a corrective reflection from a failed attempt and localizing its first erroneous
span; distillation then targets the ensuing suffix while masking the valid prefix (Zhao et al., 2026b).
The resulting progression links the source of teacher guidance to its content and scope. Our work
studies how trajectory-specific hindsight can jointly support local error correction and continued
supervision of valid reasoning.

ANALYSIS
A teacher with access to a complete solution can return the right answer while leaving its own
derivation incomplete. This raises a central question for OPSD: does reference hindsight improve the
teacher’s reasoning, or simply make the correct conclusion easier to reproduce? We examine this
distinction by comparing answer correctness with the reasoning supporting it.
We use a frozen Qwen3-4B on AIME24 and AIME25, each containing 30 problems. We compare
Base responses with responses generated using the OPSD hindsight prompt, which supplies a worked
reference solution and its final answer. We use an external LLM1 to identify instances of unresolved
reference borrowing, in which a response adopts a necessary intermediate conclusion or the final
answer from the reference solution without deriving or independently verifying it anywhere in the
complete response. Further experiment details are given in Appendix B.1.

DIAGNOSE
Diagnose turns an incorrect student response into a local repair and the hindsight needed to learn from
it. It identifies the Error Stage, constructs an Anchor Stage for Iterative Counterfactual Validation,
and prepares distinct guidance for the Error Stage and valid prefix in Differentiated Distillation.

problem x and reference solution href to check correctness while following the student’s reasoning
route. It locates the Error Stage and produces the three outputs in Figure 4(a):

5.2
ITERATIVE COUNTERFACTUAL VALIDATION
Iterative Counterfactual Validation tests whether a local repair enables the student to complete the
solution. It alternates diagnosis and student continuation, using the final answer to determine whether
the accumulated repairs provide hindsight for Local supervision or the original response receives
Fallback supervision.
At round n, we retain the valid prefix un, replace the Error Stage en with the Anchor Stage rn, and
discard the old suffix. The student generates a new continuation from this repaired prefix, giving the

We allow at most M repair rounds, where M ≥1. If τn+1 has a correct final answer, validation stops
and selects the Error Stages, valid prefixes, and corresponding hindsight collected across the chain for
Local supervision. If the answer remains incorrect and another round is available, diagnosis searches
only within cn. The prefix un ⊕rn, including all earlier Anchor Stages, remains unchanged.
If the budget is exhausted without a correct answer, Fallback supervision uses the original rollout
τ0 with reference hindsight. A chain is skipped if diagnosis cannot identify a supported error or
construct a compatible local repair. The next subsection describes how the selected supervision is
used for distillation.
5.3
DIFFERENTIATED DISTILLATION
Differentiated Distillation uses the validation result to assign teacher guidance. Local supervision
provides different hindsight for the Error Stage and valid prefix, while Fallback supervision uses the
reference solution for the original response. Both branches use the same frozen teacher and train the
student through a common distillation objective.
Local supervision.
The Error Stage needs guidance on correcting a mistake, while the valid prefix
needs guidance that connects its existing reasoning to the Anchor Stage. We therefore use Failure
Reason & Goal for the Error Stage and COT to Anchor for the valid prefix.
For a chain that reaches a correct answer after N rounds, with 1 ≤N ≤M, we collect supervision
from each round n = 0, . . . , N −1. Let y(n) be the token sequence of τn before replacing its Error
Stage. Define En and Pn as the supervised token positions in that Error Stage and its valid prefix.
Thus, the corrected response determines whether the repair is accepted, while the teacher and student
are evaluated on the trajectory before that repair. At later rounds, the valid prefix can include earlier
Anchor Stages and valid portions of student continuations.

EXPERIMENTS
6.1
EXPERIMENT SETTINGS
Models and Datasets
We study Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B, and Qwen3-8B (Yang et al., 2025).
Training problems and reference solutions come from OpenThoughts-Math-30K (Guha et al., 2025).
The student sees only the problem when generating a rollout; the diagnostic model and privileged
teacher may use the reference as described in Section 5. For each model size, we screen for
answer-incorrect rollouts before applying the repair pipeline. We evaluate on AIME 2024, AIME
2025, and HMMT February 2025 (Mathematical Association of America, 2024; 2025; Harvard-MIT
Mathematics Tournament, 2025), each containing 30 problems and separate from the training pool.
Baselines and Evaluation
We compare RC-OPD with the unmodified Base model, supervised fine-
tuning (SFT), outcome-reward optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024), and six distillation methods:
OPSD (Zhao et al., 2026a), EOPD (Jin et al., 2026), DASH (Hou et al., 2026), PW-OPSD (Liu
et al., 2026), AVSD (Nguyen et al., 2026), and ROSD (Zhao et al., 2026b). Our benchmark score is
Avg@4, the mean correctness of four sampled responses per problem across all 30 problems; the
reported Mean averages the three benchmark scores equally. Our own evaluations use thinking mode,
a common problem-only prompt, and top-p = 0.95. The sources and evaluation protocols for SFT
and GRPO are specified in Table 1. ROSD Avg@4 results are supplied by a collaborator.
Implementation Details
Each near-online update begins with 32 training problems. Student
rollouts use non-thinking mode, temperature 1.1, top-p = 0.95, top-k = 20, and a 4,096-token
cap; the frozen, same-size Qwen3 teacher uses thinking mode. DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 provides
diagnoses. We use Local supervision when a fresh student continuation after repair is correct. We
train rank-64 LoRA adapters with scaling factor 128 and learning rate 5 × 10−6, using a clipping
threshold of 0.05 for the distillation loss (Appendix B.6) and prefix loss weight of 0.1. Additional
implementation settings are provided in the supplementary configuration file.

OVERALL PERFORMANCE
Table 1 summarizes reasoning accuracy across three model sizes and benchmarks. 1) Overall
Performance. Among methods evaluated with Avg@4, RC-OPD achieves the highest mean accuracy
at all three model sizes. It improves mean accuracy by 9.66%, 5.78%, and 5.70% relative to the OPSD
on Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B, and Qwen3-8B, respectively, showing consistent gains across model
scales. 2) Distillation Trap. Baselines such as DASH and PW-OPSD vary distillation strength across

useful reasoning. Figure 5 further examines whether RC-OPD preserves valid reasoning while

may follow a path the student has not established. ROSD introduces trajectory-specific reflections

further tests repairs through student continuation and provides distinct guidance for Error Stages and
valid prefixes, achieving higher mean accuracy than ROSD across all sizes.

CONCLUSION
We presented RC-OPD, which combines diagnosis, Iterative Counterfactual Validation, and Differen-
tiated Distillation to address reasoning mismatch and the distillation trap. It achieves the highest mean
accuracy among methods evaluated with Avg@4 across three benchmarks and three model sizes.
Analyses show better corrective alignment, stronger preservation of valid prefixes, and improved
recovery.

EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS
B.1
REFERENCE-CONDITIONING ANALYSIS
Data and Evaluation.
We evaluate frozen Qwen3-4B on AIME24 and AIME25, with 30 problems
each. Base receives only the problem; the reference-conditioned variant additionally receives the
worked solution and final answer through the OPSD hindsight prompt. Both generate responses
from scratch. As for the evaluation, answer accuracy counts correct responses; filtered accuracy
counts those that are both correct and free of unresolved reference borrowing. Both divide by the
total number of responses. Base is evaluated without this filter.
Reference-borrowing assessment.
DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 checks complete responses for un-
resolved reference borrowing: adopting a necessary intermediate conclusion or final answer from
the reference without deriving or independently verifying it. Each response is assessed twice, with
disagreements reviewed.
B.2
VARIANT SETTINGS
Table 2 compares variants involving prefix supervision, repair validation, and reference fallback.
Detailed settings are retained in supplementary/ablation settings.md.
1)w/o Anchor supervision removes the Anchor Prefix loss while retaining Error Stage supervision
and the reference fallback. 2) w/o Validation still diagnoses the earliest root error and proposes a
route-preserving local repair, but directly constructs local distillation supervision once diagnosis and

B.3
ERROR-STAGE ALIGNMENT AND PREFIX PRESERVATION
Trajectory selection.
We start with 88 archived, answer-incorrect Base trajectories from AIME24,
AIME25, and HMMT25, with one trajectory per problem. We retain 58 with a reliably identified
first substantive error and a local repair that preserves the original reasoning route: 16, 20, and 22
from the respective benchmarks. The other 30 are excluded for reasons including incompatible
stage formatting or unreliable diagnosis. All 58 enter the Error Stage analysis. Nine have no tokens
preceding the Error Stage, leaving 49 for valid-prefix analysis. Diagnoses are reviewed twice and
adjudicated by the same assistant using the problem, response, and final answer; earlier annotations
were checked against worked references.
Distributional measurements.
We use the Qwen3-1.7B RC-OPD and OPSD checkpoints from the
main evaluation. The corrective teacher is frozen Base conditioned on the diagnosis and corrected-
state goal, without the worked reference or full replacement stage. Teacher forcing supplies identical
archived response prefixes to all models; thinking is enabled for the teacher and disabled for the
evaluated models. We compute forward KL from the corrective teacher at Error Stage positions,
excluding stage markers, and from Base at up to 128 deterministically and uniformly sampled
valid-prefix positions. Both use the full vocabulary, natural logarithms, and no clipping. Scores are
averaged over selected positions within each trajectory, then equally across trajectories.
B.4

Data and protocol.
The candidate pool contains 6,591 questions with incorrect Qwen3-4B Base
responses. After excluding questions used to train either compared model and reviewing eligibility,

reviewed, and the valid prefix preceding that error is retained. Base, OPSD, and RC-OPD receive
the same problem and the exact same prefix, without a reference answer, diagnostic hint, or inserted
repair. All models are based on Qwen3-4B; the RC-OPD model corresponds to the 66.11% mean
result in Table 1 and the OPSD corresponds to the 62.50% mean result.

B.5
ITERATIVE COUNTERFACTUAL VALIDATION SETTINGS
Data and Evaluation. Following the OPSD setup, we use the OpenThought dataset. For the main
experiments, we identify 9,312 candidate training questions by retaining cases that Qwen3-1.7B
answers incorrectly in a single attempt. Of these, 1,288 are used in the corresponding training run.
For the repair experiment, we sample from the remaining 8,024 questions, generate one response per
question using the frozen Qwen3-1.7B Base model, and retain 100 incorrect responses. To prevent
formatting differences from affecting evaluation, we use DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 as an LLM judge
to compare model responses against the reference answers.
Repair procedure. Each trajectory receives up to three repair rounds. At each round, diagnosis
identifies the first substantive error in the editable suffix and proposes a local replacement that
preserves the student’s reasoning route. Diagnosis has access to the worked reference, while Base
receives only the problem and the repaired prefix for continuation. Earlier repairs remain fixed, and
Base generates one continuation per active trajectory per round using the same sampling settings and
a fixed seed per question and round. Repair stops when the answer is correct, the round budget is
exhausted, or a reliable diagnosis or compatible local repair cannot be obtained.
Training comparison. Separately from the frozen-Base repair experiment, we train Qwen3-1.7B
RC-OPD with a maximum of one, two, or three validation rounds per repair chain. We evaluate each
final model using the main Avg@4 protocol and report the mean across AIME24, AIME25, and
HMMT25.
