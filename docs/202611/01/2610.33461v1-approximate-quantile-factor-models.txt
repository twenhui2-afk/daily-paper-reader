# Approximate Quantile Factor Models 

## Xiaochun Liu ∗

## October 27, 2026 

Abstract 

Weakly dependent idiosyncratic errors are standard in mean factor models, but quantile factor models have been analyzed only under independence. The mean-case definition of weak dependence, a bound on the eigenvalues of the error covariance matrix, does not carry over: I show that it neither implies nor is implied by the property that controls quantile estimation, since uncorrelated errors can share a pervasive quantile component. I instead define approximate quantile factor models through a concentration condition on the idiosyncratic array. Under it, the quantile factor analysis (QFA) estimator is consistent at a rate that deteriorates by the square root of a dependence constant, which is min-imax optimal, and the number of factors is consistently estimated. Dependence changes the asymptotic variance, for which I provide HAC and thresholded esti-mators. Monte Carlo experiments confirm these predictions. In FRED-QD and FRED-MD, standard errors ignoring dependence understate uncertainty, most strongly in the tails. 

Keywords: Quantile factor models, approximate factor models, weak depen-dence, concentration inequalities, HAC inference. 

JEL classification: C33, C38, C55.

Large-dimensional factor models summarize the comovement of many series through a few common factors and are a workhorse of macroeconomic and financial modeling (Stock and Watson, 2002; Bai and Ng, 2002). A foundational distinction in that liter-ature is between exact and approximate factor models (Chamberlain and Rothschild, 1983): the exact model requires the idiosyncratic components to be mutually un-correlated, whereas the approximate model permits weak cross-sectional (and serial) dependence among them, with the common part distinguished by diverging eigen-values. The move from exact to approximate is what made factor models usable on real data, where idiosyncratic terms are never perfectly uncorrelated; Bai and Ng (2002) establish consistent estimation and factor-number selection for approximate 

static factor models of the conditional mean, and this approximate-error robustness is now the default setting of the applied literature. Mean factor models are, however, silent about how comovement varies across the conditional distribution: whether the factors that drive the center also drive the tails, and whether the number of pervasive factors differs across quantiles. These features matter for distributional and tail-risk analysis. Chen, Dolado, and Gon-zalo (2021, henceforth CDG) introduce the quantile factor model and its estimator, quantile factor analysis (QFA), which minimizes the check-function loss and allows a quantile-dependent number of factors r(τ ). Korobilis and Schröder (2025b) de-velop a probabilistic counterpart estimated by variational Bayes, and Korobilis and Schröder (2025a) embed quantile factors in a factor-augmented vector autoregression (QFAVAR) to monitor macroeconomic risk. All of these work with exact factor mod-els, in which the idiosyncratic errors are independent across units and time (CDG, Assumption 1(iii)), an assumption that CDG themselves describe as strong (their Re-mark 1.4). The quantile factor literature thus has no counterpart of the approximate factor model. What, then, is an approximate quantile factor model? In the mean case, the answer is a bound on the largest eigenvalue of the idiosyncratic covariance matrix

At the true parame-ters the threshold is s = 0 , which gives the score ψit (τ ) = τ − I{uit (τ ) ≤ 0}, but at any other candidate value the thresholds s range over the gap between the candidate 2and the true common component. The criterion must therefore be controlled through such bounded functions of the errors, and uniformly over a parameter space of dimen-sion (N + T )r. The covariance of the errors is not the relevant object: Proposition 2 shows that the eigenvalue condition neither implies nor is implied by the concentra-tion property on which the theory rests. Uncorrelated errors can share a pervasive quantile component that second moments cannot detect: if a common shock moves the τ -quantile of every error while leaving all means and covariances unchanged, the eigenvalue condition holds, yet QFA can be inconsistent, because the shock acts as an omitted quantile factor. The answer must therefore be sought in the check-function criterion itself. The question matters in practice. In the FRED-QD and FRED-MD panels, which are standard in the factor literature, the QFA scores are serially correlated for 40 to 90 percent of the series, and far more pairs of series are cross-sectionally correlated than independence allows (Section 5). Under such dependence the confidence intervals of CDG are invalid. In the simulations of Section 4, the coverage of their nominal 95 percent intervals for the loadings falls to 0.59 when the errors follow an autoregression with coefficient 0.8. In the FRED panels, the standard errors of the factors that account for dependence are 1.4 to 1.6 times those that assume independence in the tails of the distribution, where quantile factor models are most often used. 

Contribution. This paper defines and analyzes the approximate quantile factor model, and its contributions are four. First, it formulates weak dependence at a quantile as a concentration property of the idiosyncratic array, with a dependence constant CD (Assumption A3); the exact model is the case CD = 1 /2, and primitive conditions cover Markov-driven and mixing errors (Propositions 3 and 4). Second, it shows that consistency reduces to concentration: QFA, without modification, esti-mates the factors and the loadings at the rate LN T /√CD, with LN T = min {√N , √T },and the number of factors consistently (Theorem 1 and Corollary 1). Because the concentration bound may hold conditional on a common volatility path, the theory also covers dependence that A3 excludes (Proposition 2). Third, it derives the limiting distribution under dependence, shows that the vari-ance estimators of CDG are then inconsistent, and provides consistent HAC and thresholded estimators (Theorem 2, Propositions 5 and 6, and Corollary 2), which re-main valid conditional on common volatility (Proposition 7). Fourth, it shows that the rate is sharp: a minimax lower bound of order √CD/L N T implies that dependence 3costs exactly the factor √CD and that uniform consistency requires CD = o(L2

Together, these results play the role for quantile factor models that Bai and Ng (2002) and Bai (2003) play for mean factor models, but the notion of weak dependence on which they rest has no counterpart in the mean case. These results require new arguments rather than a modification of the exact-model proofs. CDG note that their consistency proof uses independence only to derive a sub-Gaussian bound for the criterion; the difficulty lies in finding dependence conditions under which such a bound holds. The bound must hold uniformly over the parameter space, for a nonsmooth loss and with the heterogeneous weights that arise in the criterion, and several standard concentration inequalities for dependent data fail this requirement (Section 3.1). The distribution theory requires control of quadratic terms in the expansion that vanish under independence, as well as long-run variance estimators in which the estimated scores enter through indicator functions, and the lower bound requires a construction in which the dependence constant is explicit. This paper treats the static quantile factor model, the setting of Bai and Ng (2002) in the mean case, so that the role of idiosyncratic dependence is isolated. The results are stated for QFA, but because the asymmetric Laplace working likelihood of Korobilis and Schröder (2025b) corresponds to the check-function criterion, the concentration argument offers a natural route to weakly dependent errors in their setting as well. Extending the approximate theory to dynamic quantile factor models, in which the factors follow a vector autoregression as in the QFAVAR of Korobilis and Schröder (2025a), is left for future research. The paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 sets out the quantile factor model, the exact and approximate assumptions, and the QFA estimator. Section 3 states the concentration condition that defines the approximate quantile factor model, proves consistency under it, compares the concentration condition with the eigenvalue con-dition of the mean case, gives primitive sufficient conditions, derives the limiting distribution with consistent variance estimators, and shows that the rates are mini-max optimal. Section 4 reports Monte Carlo evidence on the predictions of Section 3, Section 5 applies the results to the FRED-QD and FRED-MD macroeconomic panels, and Section 6 concludes. 42 The Quantile Factor Model 

This section presents the model, the exact and approximate quantile factor models, which differ only in their assumption on the idiosyncratic components, and the es-timator. The identification conditions, which are common to both, follow, and the section closes with the QFA estimator. 

## 2.1 Model 

Let xit denote the observation on variable i = 1 , . . . , N in period t = 1 , . . . , T , and fix a quantile level τ ∈ (0 , 1) . The quantile factor model specifies the conditional 

τ -quantile of xit as a linear combination of r(τ ) latent common factors: 

Qτ (xit | λi(τ ), f t(τ )) = λi(τ )′ft(τ ), xit = λi(τ )′ft(τ ) + uit (τ ), (1) with P {uit (τ ) ≤ 0 | F0} = τ , where F0 = ( f01 , . . . , f 0T )′ denotes the true factor path. The quantile restriction is imposed conditional on the entire path, which amounts to strict exogeneity of the factors with respect to the idiosyncratic quantile restriction. Identification requires this form once uit is serially dependent. Equivalently, F0 may be treated as nonrandom, as in CDG, and all probability statements below are condi-tional on F0. In matrix form, xt = Λ ft + ut with Λ = ( λ1, . . . , λ N )′. The factors, the loadings, and the number of factors may all depend on τ . Every object is therefore quantile-specific, estimation is carried out one quantile at a time, and I suppress (τ )

when no confusion arises. The model is static in the sense that no law of motion is imposed on ft. Finally, the quantile restriction in (1) constrains each uit separately and is silent on how the idiosyncratic components depend on one another.

Weak dependence means CD = O(1) , and the exact model is the special case CD = 1 /2.Nor does a second-moment condition on the score at the true quantile, such as summa-bility of |Cov( ψit , ψ js )|, suffice for consistency, for two reasons. First, the criterion must be controlled uniformly over a parameter set of dimension (N + T )r, which requires exponential tail bounds. Second, at parameter values other than the true one, the criterion involves the indicators I{uit ≤ s} at nonzero thresholds s, given by the gap between the candidate and the true common component, and a condition on the score ψit alone says nothing about them. Summability of the score covariances remains the relevant condition for the asymptotic variance (Section 3.4). Section 3.2 justifies this definition: Proposition 2 there shows that A3 and the eigenvalue con-dition are not nested, and that A3 excludes pervasive quantile components in the idiosyncratic term that the eigenvalue condition permits. It also shows that A3 may be imposed conditional on a larger σ-field that preserves the quantile restriction, such as one generated by a common volatility path. The two assumptions differ in their treatment of the dependence that remains after conditioning on the factors. Both are imposed conditional on the factor path, so dependence induced through the common factors is allowed in either case. The exact 6assumption rules out any further dependence, whereas the approximate assumption permits it. Under either assumption, the common component must be identified and must dominate the idiosyncratic component. I turn to these conditions next.

# 3 Asymptotic Theory under Weak Dependence 

This section establishes the asymptotic properties of QFA under the approximate model. Section 3.1 states the assumptions and establishes consistency of the esti-mated factors, loadings, and number of factors. Section 3.2 shows that A3 and the eigenvalue condition of the mean case are not nested, which explains why weak de-pendence at a quantile is defined through A3. For Markov-driven errors the rate of convergence is LN T , and for general β- and α-mixing errors it is LN T up to logarith-mic factors. ## 3.5 Optimality of the rates 

Sections 3.1 and 3.3 establish the rate LN T under A3 with CD = O(1) (Theorem 1 and Proposition 3), the rate LN T /plog( N T ) under β-mixing (Proposition 4(a)), and the rate LN T /{(log N T )(log T )} under α-mixing (Proposition 4(b)). This section applies the results to the macroeconomic panels used in the factor lit-erature. I ask two questions that the theory makes empirically relevant: whether the idiosyncratic errors of a standard panel are consistent with the exact model, and whether allowing for dependence changes inference. I use the quarterly FRED-QD database (McCracken and Ng, 2020) over 1960Q1– 2019Q2, the sample of CDG ( T = 238 ), and the monthly FRED-MD database (Mc-Cracken and Ng, 2016) over 1960:01–2019:12 ( T = 720 ), both from the September 2026 vintage. I end both samples before 2020 to exclude the pandemic observa-tions. Each series is transformed to stationarity with the codes supplied with the data, observations more than ten interquartile ranges from the median of their series are replaced by that median, and series with missing observations in the sample are dropped. This leaves N = 209 quarterly series and N = 121 monthly series. All series are standardized. I estimate QFA at τ ∈ { 0.1, 0.25 , 0.5, 0.75 , 0.9} and select r(τ ) by the rank-minimization estimator with k = 8 , as in Section 4. The theory gives two predictions. # 6 Conclusion 

This paper defines and analyzes the approximate quantile factor model. The eigen-value condition that defines approximate factor models in the mean case does not control quantile estimation and is not nested with the property that does (Propo-sition 2). I therefore formulate weak dependence at a quantile as a concentration property of the idiosyncratic array. Under this condition, QFA consistently estimates the factors, the loadings and the number of factors at the rate LN T /√CD, which is minimax optimal, and dependence changes the asymptotic variances, for which I provide consistent HAC and thresholded estimators. In the FRED-QD and FRED-MD panels, the idiosyncratic errors are dependent over time and across series, and standard errors that ignore this dependence under-state uncertainty, most strongly in the tails. Two extensions are natural: dynamic quantile factor models, in which the factors follow a vector autoregression, and a correction of the thresholded variance estimator for the negative dependence that estimation induces among the scores.
