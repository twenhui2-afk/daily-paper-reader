# Soft factor of quasi-TMDPDFs from the soft limit of the quasi beam function 

Junegone Chay 

Department of Physics, Korea University, Seoul 02841, Korea 

E-mail: chay@korea.ac.kr 

Abstract: The quasi-TMDPDF in large-momentum effective theory is related to the TMD-PDF through a soft factor, which has so far been constructed separately from the quasi beam function. We show that the soft factor is contained in the quasi beam function itself. Com-puting the one-loop quasi beam function and decomposing it by the method of regions, we find that the hard and collinear regions give the matching coefficient and the lightcone beam function. The soft region involves only one lightlike direction, yet, once the two hemispheres of the gluon momentum are folded onto each other, it reproduces the square root of the light-cone soft function together with the reduced soft function. The analysis leads to an operator definition of the soft factor, as the vacuum matrix element of the soft Wilson lines into which the quasi beam function turns. This definition provides the basis for an all-order analysis, and it shows that the Collins-Soper kernel is reproduced to all orders. arXiv:2610.33638v1 [hep-ph] 24 Oct 2026 Contents

1 Introduction 

Transverse-momentum-dependent parton distribution functions (TMDPDFs) describe the dis-tribution of partons in a hadron in both the longitudinal momentum fraction and the transverse momentum. They enter the factorization of processes in which a small transverse momentum is measured, such as Drell-Yan production [1] and semi-inclusive deep inelastic scattering [2], and they carry information on the three-dimensional structure of hadrons that collinear par-ton distributions do not. Their definition is more subtle than that of collinear PDFs [3, 4]. A TMDPDF combines a hadronic matrix element of quark fields joined by Wilson lines, the beam function, with the square root of a vacuum matrix element of Wilson lines, the soft function. Neither is well defined by itself, and only their combination is, with a remaining dependence on a rapidity scale that is governed by the Collins-Soper kernel [4–8]. At small transverse separation bT the TMDPDFs can be computed in perturbation theory in terms of collinear PDFs, but at bT ∼ Λ−1QCD they are nonperturbative, and so is the Collins-Soper kernel. A first-principles determination requires lattice QCD. The Wilson lines of the beam and soft functions are lightlike and extend in time, however, and such operators cannot be computed directly in Euclidean lattice QCD. Lattice studies of TMDs have therefore used equal-time operators with staple-shaped Wilson links and ratios in which the soft factors cancel [9–13]. Large-momentum effective theory (LaMET) [14–16] gets around this obstacle with a simple observation. A lightcone correlation in a hadron can be viewed as the limit of an equal-time correlation in a hadron moving with infinite momentum, since a large boost along ˆ z brings a spacelike separation along ˆ z close to the lightcone. At large but finite momentum P z the equal-time correlation, called a quasi distribution, can be computed in Euclidean lattice QCD. It has the same infrared, nonperturbative physics as the lightcone distribution, and the two differ only in their ultraviolet physics, because the limit P z → ∞ and the ultraviolet regularization are taken in opposite orders. The difference is perturbative and is absorbed into a matching coefficient, up to corrections suppressed by powers of 1 /P z . The simplest example is the quasi-PDF, in which the two quark fields are joined by a straight spacelike Wilson line in place of the lightlike one of the PDF, and which is related to the PDF by such a perturbative matching coefficient. – 1 – For TMDPDFs the quark fields are separated in the transverse direction as well, and a gauge-invariant equal-time operator requires a Wilson line that runs along ˆ z to a distance L,crosses to the transverse position of the other field, and comes back [17–23]. The matrix element of this operator in a hadron, the quasi beam function, inherits the length L of the staple-shaped Wilson line in several ways. Each segment carries a linear divergence proportional to its length [24, 25], the two antiparallel legs exchange gluons over their whole length and produce a term growing like L/b T , as a static potential would, and logarithms of L appear as well. The length L is a parameter of the equal-time construction, bounded in lattice calculations by the size of the lattice, and the dependence on it has to be removed before the quasi beam function can be related to the TMDPDF. This is done by dividing by the square root of the vacuum expectation value ZE of a rectangular Wilson loop of length 2 L and width bT . In the spirit of LaMET one would then expect the quotient ˜f to be related to the TMDPDF by a perturbative matching coefficient. The infrared physics of the two is not the same, however. In the TMDPDF the soft gluons are described by a soft function, a vacuum matrix element of lightlike Wilson lines, while 

ZE is built from spacelike Wilson lines like the staple itself. At bT ∼ Λ−1QCD soft gluons are nonperturbative, so that the mismatch cannot be absorbed into the matching coefficient and has to appear as a separate nonperturbative factor. The difference between the rapidity scales ζz of the quasi-TMDPDF and ζ of the TMDPDF is governed by the Collins-Soper kernel K, and what remains depends only on bT and μ and is called the reduced soft function Sr. Relating the quasi-TMDPDF to the TMDPDF therefore comes down to deciding what the quasi beam function is to be divided by, and the choice has to be justified by what it does to the relation with the TMDPDF. For eq. (1.1) to be useful, every nonperturbative factor in it has to be computable in lattice QCD, and the conditions under which lattice calculations are done determine what this requires. A lattice calculation works at finite spacing a, with Wilson lines of finite length L

and a hadron of finite momentum P z . The linear divergence of the Wilson line then appears as a factor that grows with L/a , and it is the division by √ZE that removes it, before the result is converted from a lattice scheme to the MS scheme [26]. The finite values of L and P z are tolerable only because the dependence on L cancels in this way and the corrections in 1 /(P z bT )are small. Within these constraints lattice QCD has already delivered nonperturbative TMD physics. In the ratio of quasi-TMDPDFs at two momenta the reduced soft function drops out, and the Collins-Soper kernel has been extracted in this way [18, 26, 27]. The reduced soft function does not drop out of the quasi-TMDPDF itself, and it has been obtained from a light-meson form factor [28, 29]. The soft factor is thus the one ingredient that requires a separate calculation, and how it is defined decides what that calculation has to be. This soft factor is where the existing approaches differ. Ebert, Stewart and Zhao [19] follow the lightcone construction and divide by a quasi soft function, a vacuum matrix element of spacelike Wilson lines. The simplest choice, in which the lines from both quark positions run along ±ˆz, leads to logarithms of bT that do not match those of the TMDPDF at one – 2 – loop, and a bent quasi soft function, with lines along two spatial directions, was introduced to restore the agreement. Ji, Liu and Liu [20, 21] keep the rectangular loop and attribute the mismatch to Sr, which they define through the soft function with Wilson lines of finite rapidity and extract from the light-meson form factor mentioned above. The factorization formula has since been proved to all orders in αs by Ebert, Schindler, Stewart and Zhao [23, eq. (1.1)], who relate the quasi-TMDPDF through Lorentz invariance to TMDPDFs defined with Wilson lines off the lightcone. In all these approaches the soft factor is constructed outside the quasi beam function, since it depends on two almost lightlike directions and cannot be obtained by boosting an equal-time operator [19, section 3.4]. An analysis of the leading regions has been noted as a complement to the proof [23, footnote 1]. The quasi beam function at finite L, on the other hand, is a well-defined object, the matrix element of a full-QCD operator in a hadron. Since the open issue is the soft factor, and soft physics is carried by one region of the loop momenta, it is natural to decompose this object by the method of regions [30] and to ask whether the quasi beam function itself determines the soft factor. At leading power in 1 /(P z bT ) the loop momenta divide into hard, collinear and soft regions. We use the lightlike vectors nμ = (1 , 0, 0, 1), along the motion of the hadron, and ¯nμ = (1 , 0, 0, −1), in terms of which ˆ z = ( n − ¯n)/2. Collinear gluons see the legs of the Wilson line as a line along ¯ n, so that the collinear region is expected to reproduce the lightcone beam function once its overlap with the soft region, the zero-bin contribution [31], is subtracted. In the soft region the energetic quark supplies a lightlike Wilson line along n [32, 33], while the legs of the Wilson line along ˆ z, which are long compared with the wavelength of a soft gluon, appear to soft gluons as soft Wilson lines along ˆ z, in place of the lines along ¯ n in the soft function of the TMDPDF. In the frame in which the hadron moves fast, the lightlike direction comes from the state, the energetic quark collinear to n, and the spacelike direction from the operator, which stays at equal time. Viewed from the rest frame of the hadron, the boost brings ˆ z close to the lightcone along ¯ n, not along n, with an orientation that depends on the process, so that the Wilson line takes the place of the lines along ¯ n, as the collinear and soft regions confirm in sections 3 and 4. The soft limit of the quasi beam function is therefore expected to be a definite vacuum matrix element, obtained rather than chosen. It contains a single lightlike direction, whereas the soft function of the TMDPDF contains two, and it is not obvious how the one could account for the other. We answer this question at one loop, in three steps, each of which says something about the question. First, to be sure of the object to be dissected, we compute the quasi beam function at finite L and finite P z bT with momentum-space Feynman rules and reproduce the result of ref. [19]. Every diagram is finite apart from poles in ϵ, and no rapidity regulator is needed, since the eikonal lines are spacelike and the hadron carries a finite momentum. This gives an unambiguous quantity against which every later decomposition can be checked. Second, we decompose the same calculation by the method of regions. The collinear and soft regions contain rapidity divergences, which we regulate with the hemisphere form of the η

regulator [8, 34]. The hard region gives the matching coefficient H, and the collinear region after the zero-bin subtraction gives the lightcone beam function. The matching coefficient H

and the lightcone beam function, the two ingredients of eq. (1.1) that are not in dispute, thus appear directly as parts of the quasi beam function. The collinear region carries a rapidity divergence, which has to cancel against that of the soft region, since the quasi beam function – 3 – itself has none. The third step, the computation of the soft region, takes up this puzzle. In the soft function of the TMDPDF a soft gluon is exchanged between Wilson lines along n and ¯ n, while in the soft limit of the quasi beam function it is exchanged between the line along n and the leg of the staple along ˆ z. We show that the two are related by what we call the folding of the hemispheres. The eikonal factor of the leg distinguishes the gluons moving forward, k3 > 0, from those moving backward, k3 < 0, only by its sign, and interchanging k+ and k− maps one half of the gluon momenta onto the other while reversing that sign. Folding the backward half onto the forward one, we find that the soft region of the quasi beam function adds up to exactly one hemisphere of the lightcone soft function, which at one loop is its square root. The single spacelike direction ˆ z thus does the work of the second lightlike direction, and the soft physics of the TMDPDF, including its rapidity dependence, is contained in the quasi beam function itself. What remains of the soft limit is the self-interaction of the Wilson line. It exceeds that of half of the rectangular loop by a single straight segment, and this segment is the reduced soft function. The reduced soft function is then not a correction for an imperfect choice of the divisor but a definite piece of the quasi beam function. This explains why the simplest quasi soft function of ref. [19], which removes only half of the loop, fails, and why the bent one, whose square root is one open staple at one loop, succeeds. These one-loop results lead to an operator definition of the soft factor. At leading power the quasi beam function factorizes as ˜B = H B n Snˆz , where Bn is the lightcone beam function and Snˆz , the soft limit of the quasi beam function, is the vacuum matrix element of the soft Wilson lines into which the quasi beam function turns. The reduced soft function becomes a ratio of vacuum matrix elements of Wilson lines, and no soft function has to be constructed separately. Because this definition does not rely on the one-loop calculation, it provides the basis for an all-order analysis. It shows that the rapidity divergences of the soft limit are one half of those of the lightcone soft function to all orders, so that the Collins-Soper kernel is reproduced, and it supplies the analysis of the leading regions that complements the proof of ref. [23]. For lattice calculations, Snˆz contains a lightlike Wilson line and does not replace the separate determination of the soft factor. It does support the structure of eq. (1.1), with the division by √ZE and the reduced soft function, and it identifies the separately computed Sr

as a definite part of the quasi beam function. The paper is organized as follows. In section 2 we define the quasi beam function, present the one-loop diagrams and their matrix elements, and give the result at finite L. In section 3 we analyze the momentum regions and the rapidity divergences, and in section 4 we evaluate the collinear and soft sectors with the rapidity regulator, fold the two hemispheres of the soft region, and show that the sum of the sectors reproduces the full result. The connection with the factorization formula at one loop is made in section 5. In section 6 we derive the soft limit as an operator, express Sr in terms of Wilson lines, and extend the factorization to all orders. We compare with the quasi soft functions, discuss the role of the staple length and the open questions beyond one loop in section 7, and conclude in section 8. In SCET a beam function is the matrix element of an operator built from collinear fields, and it describes the collinear modes alone once the soft contribution has been factored out. The quasi beam function in eq. (2.1) is instead the matrix element of a full-QCD operator. In the regime of factorization its loop corrections receive contributions from hard, collinear and soft momenta, and nothing has been factored out of it. Its hard part gives the matching coefficient, its collinear part at leading power in 1 /(P z bT ) is the lightcone beam function in the SCET sense, and its soft part involves the staple-shaped Wilson line and a lightlike one, as shown in section 3. 2.3 Evaluation 

Every contribution is evaluated in the same three steps, the integration over k0, the transverse integration at fixed k3, and the treatment of the remaining dependence on k3, or on x, as a distribution. The order is chosen so that each step produces a definite kind of singularity. The integration over k0 produces none, all poles in ϵ arise in the transverse integration, and the last step deals only with the singular points in k3. The factorization of the quasi-TMDPDF has been proved to all orders in ref. [23, section 3.1] through Lorentz invariance, without an analysis of the momentum regions. The operator form of the soft limit gives the same factorization from within the quasi beam function, and it identifies the soft factor with a definite part of it. The steps leading to the factorization act on the operator and not on individual diagrams. For collinear gluons the replacement ¯ r · An → 12 ¯n · An holds at leading power for any number of gluons, and the collinear sector of the quasi beam function is the lightcone TMD beam function to all orders. For soft gluons the field redefinition ξn → Ynξn decouples them from the quark for any number of gluons, the soft part of the staple keeps its full length because L

is long compared with the soft wavelength, and the multipole expansion sets bz = 0. These are the steps that establish the factorization of the lightcone TMDPDF in soft-collinear effective theory, and here they give ˜B = H B n Snˆz at leading power to all orders in αs, provided that no further region contributes. 7.2 Role of the staple length 

In ref. [19] the finite length is regarded as the regulator of the analog of rapidity divergences, and the term linear in L/b T as its manifestation. For lightlike Wilson lines of finite length this is the case, since the phase ein ·kL regulates the limit n · k → 0. For the spacelike legs of the staple the corresponding singularity at k3 → 0 lies at central rapidity. The term linear in L appears also in the rectangular Wilson loop, which has no lightlike direction, and it is the static potential between the antiparallel legs. The rapidity divergences appear only in the collinear and soft sectors, where they cancel in the sum, and their remnant in the quasi beam function is the dependence on P z through the logarithms Lz that multiply the Collins-Soper kernel. Nor can the length be traded for a rapidity regulator. If the limit L → ∞ is taken before the integration, the eikonal factors of a leg combine into the pinched product 1 /[( k3 − i0)( k3 + i0)], which is singular at k3 = 0. There an on-shell gluon has k+ = k− = kT , at central rapidity, and a regulator acting on k+ leaves the singularity untouched. A finite length is required for the individual diagrams to be well defined, and the rapidity regulator enters only through the division into regions. 

7.3 Beyond one loop 

The operator form of the soft limit settles the factorization of the quasi beam function and the rapidity structure of Snˆz to all orders, as shown in section 6.3. Two observations support eq. (6.8) from a different angle. In the derivation of the Collins-Soper equation [4], the de-pendence of a soft factor on the rapidity of one of its Wilson lines is governed by K(bT , μ )– 36 – once that rapidity is large, independently of the directions of the other lines. In Snˆz only the line along n has an unbounded rapidity, while the direction of the longitudinal legs is fixed, and the rapidity dependence of Snˆz is that of a single lightlike line, one half of that of Sn¯n,as eq. (6.8) requires. The second observation concerns the configurations in which the gluons attached to the Wilson line lie in opposite hemispheres. They are of the type that produces non-global logarithms when two regions are treated differently [46], and here the two regions are the hemispheres. In the evolution of non-global observables the global part is governed by the universal cusp anomalous dimension, and the correlated emission into the two regions adds a separate non-global contribution [47]. By analogy, these configurations are not expected to modify the coefficient of the rapidity divergence, whose dependence on μ is fixed by the cusp anomalous dimension, dK/d ln μ = −2Γ cusp , in accordance with eq. (6.8). What the consistency does not fix is the part of Snˆz free of rapidity divergences. At two loops, webs whose gluons lie in both hemispheres produce such terms, and they enter Sr.Whether Snˆz can be replaced by √Sn¯nZE , or by the bent soft function in fig. 6, is a question about these webs and not about the factorization. An explicit calculation at two loops answers it and tests eq. (6.8) at the same time. It requires the complete set of webs of order CF CA

The two-loop rapidity anomalous dimension with the η regulator [48] and the two-loop quasi-TMDPDF [49] provide the reference values for K and for Sr. We leave this calculation to a separate publication. 

8 Conclusions 

We have computed the one-loop quasi beam function for a staple-shaped Wilson line of finite length L with momentum-space Feynman rules, evaluating each diagram separately. The result is exact in pz bT and bT /L , the logarithms of L cancel identically, and the remaining linear dependence on L is removed by the rectangular Wilson loop of length 2 L. At leading power the result agrees with ref. [19], with the self-energy of the transverse link supplying 1/ϵ + Lb to the coefficient of δ(1 − x). The decomposition by the method of regions assigns each diagram to definite regions. The hard contribution comes entirely from the virtual contribution of the sail diagrams and gives the matching coefficient H. The collinear region, after the zero-bin subtraction, is the lightcone TMD beam function, since collinear gluons see the longitudinal legs as a lightlike line along ¯ n. The soft region, the soft limit of the quasi beam function, consists of the exchange between a lightlike line along n and the Wilson line and of the self-interaction of the Wilson line. The collinear and soft sectors each contain an ultraviolet rapidity divergence, and with the – 37 – hemisphere η regulator the two cancel between the sectors. The regulator has to be symmetric between the two hemispheres, because the eikonal propagator of the longitudinal legs is singular on their boundary. In the soft function of the TMDPDF a soft gluon is exchanged between Wilson lines along 

n and ¯ n, whereas in the soft region of the quasi beam function it is exchanged between the lightlike line along n and the staple along ˆ z. We have shown that the two are related by the folding of the hemispheres, in which the interchange of k+ and k− maps the hemisphere k3 < 0onto k3 > 0 and the two are added. Integrated over the longitudinal momentum of the gluon, the exchange between the lightlike line and the Wilson line is then equal to one hemisphere of the lightcone soft function, which is its square root at one loop. The quasi beam function thus reproduces the soft physics of the TMDPDF without a separately constructed soft function, the direction ˆ z of its Wilson line playing the role of the second lightlike direction. The self-interaction of the Wilson line, compared with half of the rectangular Wilson loop, leaves the contribution of one straight segment, which is the reduced soft function S(1)
