# Perturbation-Based Data Selection for Medical Question Answering 

Yuexin Wu Dayou Yu Vasile Rus 

Department of Computer Science University of Memphis, Memphis, TN, USA 

ywu10@memphis.edu dayou.yu@memphis.edu vrus@memphis.edu 

Abstract —Selecting a compact training subset is an important step in adapting medical language models from existing question-answering banks. We investigate perturbation-based data selection using two signals derived from Gaussian perturbations of the final-block representation: prediction-change frequency and non-flipping margin contraction. After a shared entropy prefilter, Representation-Sensitivity Prioritization (RSP) combines the signals using automatic root-mean-square scaling, while RSP-Drop ranks candidates by contraction alone. Both selectors use only questions and answer options during selection. We evaluate them with MedGemma-4B on five medical QA datasets at retained-set budgets of 64, 128, and 256 examples. At the primary budget of 128, RSP and RSP-Drop obtain dataset-averaged Macro-F1 scores of 47.55 and 48.28, respectively, compared with 45.11 for entropy-pool-matched random selection. Their relative performance varies across datasets and budgets. A selected-set case study identifies contrasting clean-margin profiles among the examples exchanged by the two rules, while a full-pool ablation shows that entropy prefiltering is not uniformly beneficial. These retrospective results characterize the empirical utility and limitations of perturbation-based selection under a fixed adaptation protocol. Evaluation uses one backbone and benchmarks with documented training-family overlap; it does not establish clinical effectiveness. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Developers adapting a medical foundation model may already possess an authorized bank of medical questions, answer labels, and auxiliary explanatory texts, while a planned update can retain only a fixed number B of training examples. This creates an offline data-selection problem: which examples should enter the final update under the same retained-set budget? The selector operates on question-and-option model behavior before auxiliary text is introduced; the downstream adaptation recipe is held fixed when comparing selection rules. The retained-set budget is distinct from the cost of creating, validating, scoring, or tuning the candidate bank. Common selection heuristics emphasize output uncertainty or representation coverage. Entropy and margin identify uncertain predictions; BALD-style criteria use stochastic disagreement [1, 2]; and diversity methods seek broader feature-space coverage [3]. These signals do not directly ask how a local change to a question representation affects the answer bound-ary. We examine two selectors built from these responses:

RSP combines prediction changes with non-flipping margin contraction, whereas RSP-D ROP uses contraction alone after the same entropy prefilter. Their comparison asks whether adding Flip-prioritized examples improves adaptation relative to continuous-drop ranking. Our goal is to characterize when these two operational sensitivity rules help or fail under a fixed protocol, not to establish a universally optimal selector or retrospectively choose a dataset-specific winner. Both selectors are auxiliary-text-blind and label-blind: the raw model receives only a question and its options. For RSP, the mixing coefficient is computed from unlabeled score components in the prefiltered pool. Labels and auxiliary text are introduced only after top-B membership is frozen. a) Auxiliary Explanations During Adaptation: Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting can expose useful intermediate reasoning on multi-step tasks [5], but a generated or collected explanation need not faithfully reflect the process that produced an answer [6, 7]. Our datasets contain heterogeneous auxil-iary text—including reference explanations and an abstract conclusion—so we avoid treating every field as human CoT. We select examples from a pre-existing bank and supervise only the final answer. 

b) Data selection: Entropy, margin, and BALD quantify different forms of uncertainty [1]; batch-disagreement and representation-diversity criteria provide alternative selection rules [2, 3]. Neuron-activation signals provide a complementary representation-based signal [8]. Our comparison also includes explanation-aware RSR-, ASLEC-DROP-, and TEMP-inspired selectors [9–11]. These differ from question/options-only scoring in their access to auxiliary text. We explicitly identify our implementations as protocol-specific adaptations to an existing labeled bank and a common LoRA endpoint, rather than exact reproductions of the cited methods. 

c) Perturbation signals: Prediction stability under noise complements uncertainty and diversity. Our Noise-Stability comparator constructs a hidden-sensitivity representation before diversity selection. RSP instead preserves the direction of the answer-boundary response by separating Flip from non-flipping margin contraction. We treat both as operational heuristics, not calibrated measures of uncertainty or perturbation severity. III. RSP AND RSP-D ROP 

A. Problem Setup 

Let C = {(xi, y i, r i)}Ni=1 be a bank containing a question and its options xi, an answer-position label yi, and auxiliary explanatory text ri. A selector returns S ⊂ C , |S| = B. During scoring for either selector, the model receives only xi: neither 

yi nor ri is read. The UID is used only for bookkeeping and selection-signature checks; it is not a semantic model input. After selection, every retained training example contains ri in its context and places loss only on final-answer target tokens. Evaluation again presents only xi, without ri.The RSP coefficient λ0 uses only unlabeled candidate scores, without adapter fitting or development evaluation. The retained-item budget does not price explanation creation, scoring, or example length.

E. Frozen Acquisition Protocol 

We fix K = 8, the final decoder block, σ = 0.02 ,and entropy-prefilter multiplier four. The primary budget is 

B = 128 , with B = 64 , 256 in the retrospective budget evaluation. For each dataset–budget–acquisition-seed pool, we compute Eqs. (6)–(8), rank by decreasing sRSP or sDrop , and retain B examples. Exact ties resolve by original pool-row index; training restores ascending pool-index order, so ranking changes membership, not presentation order. These settings are fixed design choices: there is no λ-grid, coefficient fitting, or development-based selector choice. Finite-pass Flip has nine possible values and can have sparse realized support; adding Drop supplies resolution without guaranteeing useful rankings. The score is an operational heuristic, not a calibrated or monotone measure of perturbation severity. IV. E XPERIMENTAL SETUP

B. Compared Selectors 

We distinguish information access at selection time. 

Question/options-only selectors comprise Entropy, Entropy-prefiltered Random (Random@ 4B), Margin, BALD-inspired, BatchBALD-inspired, Noise-Stability, SIFT-inspired, NAIT-inspired, RSP, and RSP-D ROP . Random@ 4B is the pool-matched control in both the primary comparison (Table III) and the budget analysis (Table VII). It samples uniformly from the same top-min(4 B, N ) entropy pool used by both proposed methods, whereas Entropy deterministically retains the most entropic items. The random draw does not inspect explanations and is not unrestricted full-bank random when N > 4B.BALD-inspired uses final-block-input hidden dropout rather than parameter dropout; BatchBALD-inspired uses a sampled joint-disagreement criterion from these stochastic predictions [1, 2]. Noise-Stability constructs sensitivity vectors followed by cosine k-center selection. SIFT-inspired combines uncertainty with an embedding-diversity ranking [3]. NAIT-inspired uses first-to-last prompt-activation deltas across decoder layers, ori-ented with unlabeled development questions [8]. The nominal 5,000-example caps in the implemented diversity comparators do not restrict these pools ( N ≤ 1500 ); they should not be confused with the 4B entropy pool. 

RSR-inspired, ASLEC-DROP-inspired, and TEMP-inspired 

are auxiliary-text-aware comparators [9–11]: they score candi-date explanation text, unlike our two selectors. They are not information-matched controls for question-only scoring. Short names in Table III denote study-specific implementations, not exact reproductions of the original papers. All use the same retained-set budget and downstream training recipe, which does not imply equal acquisition cost or information access. BALD-inspired uses eight inverted-Bernoulli hidden-dropout passes with probability 0.1 at the final-block input. Noise-Stability uses eight perturbations with relative RMS scale 0.03, normalized sensitivity vectors, and seeded cosine k-center selection. NAIT-inspired orients one principal component per layer using unlabeled development questions and ranks summed projections. These are operational adaptations of selection criteria. The retained acquisition protocols additionally record token-rank clipping at 100 for RSR-inspired, deterministic new-line/sentence step segmentation for ASLEC-DROP-inspired, and a cosine-diversity weight of 0.5 for SIFT-inspired. TEMP-inspired uses an auxiliary LoRA direction fitted from de-velopment explanations. Thus, candidate information access and auxiliary development access are distinct: describing a candidate scorer does not establish label-blindness of every auxiliary stage.

Each branch starts from google/medgemma-4b-it with no answer-only warm-up. The code did not pin a Hugging Face revision, so retained artifacts establish the repository ID but not the exact resolved model commit. We initialize a fresh rank-16 LoRA adapter with scale 32 and dropout 0.05 and no bias over q/k/v/o/gate/up/down_proj modules [19]. Training uses two epochs, learning rate 10 −4, cosine decay, 3% warm-up, fused AdamW, per-device batch size 1, gradient accumulation 8, gradient checkpointing, and maximum length 1,024. A. RQ1: Overall Performance at the Primary Budget 

At B = 128 , RSP-D ROP obtains the largest dataset-averaged Accuracy/Macro-F1 among the reported selectors (53.60/48.28), followed by RSP (53.00/47.55). These equal-weight averages summarize the five evaluated benchmarks, rather than a pooled patient-level metric. Dataset-level results are heterogeneous. RSP-D ROP has the largest mean Accuracy and Macro-F1 on MedExpQA (53.72/50.82) and MedExQA (54.50/54.28). On AfriMed-QA, RSP has the highest accuracy (53.70), whereas SIFT-inspired has a slightly higher Macro-F1 (51.29 versus 51.26). BatchBALD-inspired leads on PubMedQA (71.67/54.34), and RSR-inspired leads on MedMCQA (40.60/38.80). In the direct comparison, RSP-D ROP is higher on both met-rics on MedExpQA, MedExQA, and MedMCQA, whereas RSP is higher on AfriMed-QA and PubMedQA. On MedMCQA, RSP-D ROP improves on RSP by 1.02 pp accuracy and 2.56 pp Macro-F1, but remains below Noise-Stability, ASLEC-DROP-inspired, and RSR-inspired. The averages therefore support the utility of the two selection rules under this protocol, not uniform superiority across datasets.

Both reported methods use the current budget-evaluation result family; earlier RSP-Drop runs are not mixed into Table III. The MedMCQA B = 128 , seed-13 case study quantifies this exchange (Table V). Define k = |S RSP \ S Drop |. No selection-cutoff ties occur for either rule at B = 64 , 128 .At B = 256 , both rules have zero-score cutoff ties on all datasets except AfriMed-QA (12 dataset–budget–seed settings). Removing Flip therefore does not generally eliminate tie-driven selection. RMS balancing matches empirical component scales; it does not calibrate their downstream utility.

L IMITATIONS AND SCOPE 

a) Causal scope: All selected examples include auxiliary explanations during training, whereas evaluation omits them. Without a matched answer-only adaptation control, neither the selector comparisons nor the single no-update reference (RQ1) isolates the contribution of explanation conditioning. This train–test context shift is shared by the compared adaptation protocols. The prefilter ablation concerns only RSP and RSP-Drop; unrestricted random selection is not separately evaluated on every restricted-pool setting. 

b) Selection validity: Automatic λ0 balances component scales without establishing their downstream usefulness. Finite-pass Flip frequencies are coarse, and the fixed absolute σ

is not normalized to hidden-state magnitude. We study one backbone, one LoRA recipe, three seeds, and three retained-set budgets. Retrospective budget and component analyses on viewed tests do not establish confirmatory validity. RSP-Drop was investigated after earlier results were viewed; its inclusion as a reported method does not make that comparison prospective. We have not validated a rule for choosing between the two selectors on unseen datasets. 

c) Data and generalization: Auxiliary provenance is heterogeneous: dataset-provided human explanations, doctor-authored reference text, an article conclusion, and an expert-explanation field are not interchangeable. The narrow prepro-cessing rules remove some explicit final-answer tails but do not constitute a comprehensive answer-leakage or factuality audit. We do not observe the latent properties that make one selected subset more useful than another, so the component ablation cannot attribute downstream differences uniquely to ambiguity, label correctness, auxiliary-text characteristics, or any other candidate property. MedExpQA has normalized question-text overlap between pool and development/test. We remove pool-overlap questions from its reported test metrics, but this post-hoc filter is not a full pool/dev/test reconstruction or retraining; one within-test duplicate also remains. d) Clinical scope and reproducibility: These public medical-knowledge benchmarks do not measure patient out-comes, harmful clinical errors, calibration, fairness, or de-ployment readiness. Neither selector assesses explanation faithfulness or factuality. Historical model revisions and soft-ware versions were not fully pinned, and GPU classes differ between some branches. Separate retraining runs can exhibit stochastic variation even when selected-example membership is unchanged; identical selections are therefore not treated as independent evidence for a prefiltering effect. Future clinical use would require appropriate governance and independent clinical validation. VII. C ONCLUSION 

We study perturbation-based data selection for medical QA using two ranking rules: RSP combines prediction changes with non-flipping margin contraction, while RSP-D ROP uses contraction alone after the same entropy prefilter. At B =128 , their dataset-averaged Macro-F1 scores are 47.55 and 48.28, compared with 45.11 for pool-matched random selection. Both rules retain positive dataset-averaged differences over that control across the three evaluated budgets, while their relative performance varies across datasets and metrics. The selected-set case study shows that adding Flip exchanges contraction-selected examples for a group with a different clean-margin profile. The full-pool ablation further shows that entropy restriction can help or hurt depending on the setting. Together, these results identify the component choice, candidate-pool restriction, and retained budget as distinct aspects of the selection procedure. They do not establish a universal selector-choice rule. Evaluation on independent data and additional backbones, with fully tracked inference and training configurations, is needed to assess transfer beyond the present retrospective study. REFERENCES
