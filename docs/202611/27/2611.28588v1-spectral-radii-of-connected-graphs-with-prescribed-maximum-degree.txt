# Spectral radii of connected graphs with prescribed maximum degree 

Fengming Dong ∗

National Institute of Education, Nanyang Technological University, Singapore 

Abstract 

For a real number α > 0 and a positive integer r, we give recursive characterizations of the existence of finite simple connected graphs G with adjacency spectral radius ρ(G) = α and maximum degree ∆( G) ≤ r (or = r). Adding one vertex to a graph F at a time to update the Schur-complement q = α − bTB−1b, where B = αI − A(F ) is positive definite and b is a nonzero 0–1 that specifies the neighbours of z in F . Realizability is characterized by admissible constructions with positive intermediate outputs q and a final zero output. We also establish a reduction for a graph G obtained by attaching pairwise disjoint rooted trees, each by a single edge, to a nonempty connected graph H. # 3 Rooted trees and spectral reduction 

Dong and Zhang [4, Theorems 2 and 3] use a scalar recursion to characterize the existence of trees with prescribed adjacency spectral radius and maximum degree at most r or exactly r. Their recursion combines the contributions of the branches at a single root. The same calculation applies when a rooted tree with a positive root label is joined to a larger graph by a single edge: eliminating the tree contributes a diagonal correction at its attachment vertex. This motivates replacing the single root by an arbitrary nonempty connected graph H and attaching rooted trees at its vertices. Theorem 3.2 uses the tree labels to reduce the spectral condition to a matrix indexed by V (H) and gives the corresponding maximum-degree conditions.

# 5 Open problems 

Problem 1 (Effective recognition with a prescribed 2-core) . Let H be a connected graph of minimum degree at least two, let r ≥ ∆( H) be an integer, and let α > 0 be a real algebraic number specified by its minimal polynomial and a rational isolating interval. Is there an algorithm that always decides whether there exists a connected graph G with 

G′ ∼= H, ρ(G) = α, ∆( G) ≤ r?Does such an algorithm exist for ∆( G) = r? In each case, an equivalent question is whether one can compute an order bound for every input ( H, r, α ) that bounds the order of a smallest realizing graph whenever one exists. A concrete first case is H = C4 and r = 3. 

Problem 2 (Square-root spectral radii of unicyclic graphs) . For a nonsquare integer k ≥ 5 and an even integer m ≥ 4, determine all integers r for which a unicyclic graph of cycle length m has spectral radius √k and maximum degree r.Dong and Zhang [4] settle the corresponding problem for trees in [4]. If k is nonsquare, the minimal polynomial of √k over Q is x2 − k. Since this polynomial divides det( xI − A(G)), the number −√k is also an adjacency eigenvalue. For a connected graph with 15 ρ(G) = √k, Proposition 3.4.1(ii) of [2] implies that G is bipartite. Its unique cycle therefore has even length. Moreover, any realizing graph satisfies 

⌈√k⌉ ≤ r ≤ k − 1. (23) Indeed, ρ(G) ≤ ∆( G) = r gives the lower bound. The star formed by the edges incident with a degree-r vertex is a proper subgraph of the connected unicyclic graph G, so strict spectral monotonicity gives √r < ρ (G) = √k, proving the upper bound. The cycle length must be even because −√k is an algebraic conjugate of √k. Since G is connected and −ρ(G) is an adjacency eigenvalue, G is bipartite by [2, Proposition 3.4.1(ii)]. One realizable value is r = k − 2 for every even m. Attach k − 4 leaves to each alternate vertex of Cm.Assign values 1, 2 /√k, and 1 /√k to the decorated cycle vertices, the other cycle vertices, and the leaves, respectively. These values form a positive eigenvector for √k. An example is shown in Figure 6 with m = 4 and k = 6.
