Abstract. We prove that, over every infinite field and for m ≥ 2, the parallel sums N (N +

C)−1C of nilpotent m × m inputs with invertible sum are exactly the matrices of nullity at least two. Every positive split of the target nullity between the input kernels is realizable. Over 

C, we determine the paired derivative image and characterize universal full matrix cancellation at nonzero residues. For analytic input paths, nilpotence is required only at the base. An adjugate identity forces the last two characteristic coefficients to be O(ε2), yielding sharp bounds 

O(|ε|2/3) at nonzero three-dimensional nilpotent residues and O(|ε|1/(m−2) ) at nilpotent residues for m ≥ 4. At every base with residue of type [ m − 1, 1], m ≥ 4, we prove a generic splitting law, also with fixed input sum. For m ≥ 5, it has m − 2 roots on the scale |ε|1/(m−2) and two on the scale |ε|1/2, separating the largest-root and spectral-gap scales. A separate sharpness family keeps both inputs nilpotent and two roots zero. At the nonzero nilpotent three-dimensional boundary, a finite coefficient criterion computes the splitting orders for each exceptional affine fixed-sum direction. For each such path, the punctured spectral gap is at least c|ε|3 near zero for some c > 0, and the exponent is optimal over this class. Over a nonzero unital ring with 2 invertible, we construct two 3 × 3 matrix inputs, each of nilpotency index exactly three, with invertible sum. In the complex unital Banach case, the residue has spectrum {0} ∪ 12 σ(Q) for every coefficient Q. With the common core allowed to vary, a complex matrix is a finite-Drazin Hua output if and only if its nullity is not one.

For matrices, ker and im refer to their actions on column vectors, and Eij denotes a matrix unit. For complex matrices, we use the operator norm induced by the Euclidean norm. The nilpotency index of a nilpotent element is its least positive vanishing power; in a nonzero matrix algebra, ind(0) = 1. A regular nilpotent over a field has a single Jordan block of size m. 3. Exact realization 

The rank restriction is sufficient over every infinite field. We first prove this fixed-dimensional classification, keeping its weighted-cycle special case explicit. Section 6 treats the coefficient-ring construction with its separate index-three and Banach spectral conclusions. The construction uses two of the zero-primary chains to accommodate the invertible primary part. First, a weighted cycle realizes the nilpotent target after its closing edges are divided between the two inputs. We then split the inverse of the invertible part into two cyclic units and make their inverses nilpotent by corrections of rank at most one. Joining these corrections to two chain ends preserves nilpotence of the inputs. The final ordered factorization verifies the prescribed parallel sum. PARALLEL SUMS OF NILPOTENT ELEMENTS 5

Remark 3.2 (A finite-field boundary) . Infiniteness cannot simply be omitted from the theorem. Over F2, no nonzero nilpotent rank-one matrix in M3(F2) is a parallel sum of nilpotent inputs with invertible sum. The incidence and normal-form reduction used here works over any field F.Let N, C ∈ M3(F) be nilpotent with invertible sum and nonzero nilpotent residue ν = P(N, C ). By Lemma 2.1, both inputs are regular nilpotent and rank ν = 1, so ν2 = 0. Let KN , K C be their kernel lines and WN , W C their image planes. Each kernel line lies in its own image plane, and ker ν = KN ⊕ KC , im ν = WN ∩ WC ⊆ ker ν. 

The planes are distinct because N + C is invertible. Exactly one cross-incidence KN ⊆ WC or 

KC ⊆ WN holds. Indeed, if both held the planes would both equal KN ⊕ KC . If neither held, a vector in their intersection written as the sum of a vector in KN and one in KC would have both components zero, contradicting rank ν = 1. After exchanging the inputs, assume KN ⊆ WC . Then WC = KN ⊕ KC and KC ⊈ WN .Thus WN ∩ WC = KN , so im ν = KN ; the exchanged case gives im ν = KC . Put N = J3. A generator of KC has nonzero third coordinate, so an invertible polynomial in J3 sends e3 to that generator. Changing basis by this polynomial keeps N = J3 and makes KC = ⟨e3⟩; it also gives 

WC = ⟨e1, e 3⟩. The zero third column, zero second row and zero trace of C therefore give
