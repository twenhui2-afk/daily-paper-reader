EISENSTEIN COHOMOLOGY AND CRITICAL VALUES OF CERTAIN 

L-FUNCTIONS: A ROOT-THEORETIC COMBINATORIAL LEMMA 

FARID HOSSEINIJAFARI 

Abstract. This paper gives a root-theoretic extension of the Harder–Raghuram combina-torial lemma, relating criticality of adjacent L−values to degrees in nilradical cohomology, to maximal parabolics P = M U of connected split reductive groups with irreducible, essen-tially tempered cohomological inducing data. At real places, the paper classifies the maximal parabolics for which the two Kostant de-grees in the boundary comparison can agree at a regular shift. For each such parabolic, an admissible shift makes both adjacent arguments critical in every Langlands–Shahidi grade exactly when both Kostant degrees equal dim U/ 2. The classification recovers the two-block parabolics of type A with even product of block sizes (Harder–Raghuram, proved by We-selmann) and the isotropic-line parabolic of type D2m+1 for m ≥ 2 (Bhagwat–Raghuram). The only exceptional cases are P1 and P6 of type E6. In all other cases, roots of U fixed by the longest Levi Weyl element confine simultaneous criticality to a singular wall, generaliz-ing the odd-block obstruction for GL N . Their number is the difference of the fundamental ranks of M and G. When the Kostant degrees differ, as in Harder’s GL 2 setting, the paper determines which boundary degrees can still coincide. At a complex place, an admissible shift is simultaneously critical exactly when the two conjugate Levi weights after the shift occur in Kostant degrees summing to dim U . With a common relative center, total degree balance at a regular admissible shift characterizes simultaneous criticality at every archimedean place. For split E6 over Q, a half-spin family yields Eisenstein cohomology classes in degrees 20 and 21, including a degree-20 class with nonzero restrictions to both associate boundary face types. 

1. Introduction 

This paper determines when Kostant degrees in boundary cohomology characterize ad-jacent critical arguments of Langlands–Shahidi L-functions [59]. The question begins with the inverse Kostant problem in Harder’s Eisenstein cohomology program [25]. Given a Levi coefficient, does it occur in nilradical cohomology, and if so, for which dominant ambient weight and in what degree? The combinatorial lemma of Harder and Raghuram gives this question arithmetic significance. In their Rankin–Selberg setting, the shifts that realize the required Kostant degrees are precisely those that give two adjacent critical arguments, no more and no less [27, Lemma 7.14]. The graded formulation of the combinatorial lemma grew out of the author’s work on split G2 over totally imaginary fields. Its two maximal parabolics contribute two and three 

L-factors, respectively, to the Eisenstein constant term. A shift allowed by the adjoint-cube factor can be excluded by the determinant factor, which is evaluated at twice the argument. The combinatorial lemmas for G2 [28, Lemmas 6.1–6.2] thus suggest a general question. For a maximal parabolic of a connected split reductive group, when is equality of the two

Theorem B (Combinatorial lemma at a complex place) . At a complex place, an admissible shift is simultaneously critical if and only if the shifted Levi coefficients at the two conjugate embeddings occur in nilradical cohomology for dominant ambient weights at their respective embeddings, in degrees whose sum is dim U . The two Kostant representatives are then unique and complementary. This equality holds grade by grade: in grade j the two inversion counts sum to dim rj , the number of roots in that grade. 

For two general linear blocks, Theorem 4.1 recovers Raghuram’s combinatorial lemma over totally imaginary fields [46, Lemma 3.16]. Equation (4.2) gives the balance in each grade. For fixed inducing data, the simultaneously critical shifts are exactly the admissible points in an explicit open interval, which may be empty (Proposition 4.6). The proof brings together Saper’s count of Kostant inversions in pairs of roots [50, Lemma 17.6] and Shahidi’s description of archimedean local coefficients in terms of Artin factors [57, §3]. At a real place, a fixed root contributes a real Gamma factor whose parity is determined by the Weil group action on its root line. At a complex place, the paired root coordinates have a common midpoint after division by grade. Thus degree balance forces opposite signs in every pair, precisely the condition for adjacent criticality of the corresponding Gamma factors. 

Remark 1.1 . Theorem 4.1 proves the full combinatorial lemma at complex places. Under its local hypotheses, simultaneous criticality in every Langlands–Shahidi grade is equivalent to the occurrence of the two conjugate Levi coefficients in Kostant degrees whose sum is dim U .4

After completing this work, the author learned that Raghuram and Zou had indepen-dently proved the forward implication at their evaluation point, obtaining complementary Kostant representatives from their hyper-criticality condition [47, Def. 5.8 and Lemma 7.15]. Remark 4.2 compares the normalizations. In the real general linear setting of Harder and Raghuram, balance means that the Kostant representative has length dim U/ 2 [27, Lemma 7.14]. At a complex place, the corresponding condition is that the two conjugate Kostant degrees sum to dim U . The following corollary shows when one equality of total degrees detects these local conditions at every archimedean place. 

Corollary 1.2 (Global balance and simultaneous criticality) . Suppose the relative centers agree at all archimedean places. An admissible shift that is regular at every embedding is simultaneously critical at every archimedean place if and only if the total Kostant degrees on the two associate sides agree. More precisely, in each grade the absolute difference between the Kostant degrees summed over all embeddings on the two sides equals the total pole order of the archimedean factors at the adjacent arguments and of their duals at the reflected arguments. 

The common relative center forces all nonzero local degree differences to have the same sign. Their sum therefore vanishes exactly when each local difference vanishes (Theorem 5.2). The grade-by-grade pole count is Corollary 5.3.

Corollary 6.4 gives the two-face assertion. 1.3. Arithmetic questions beyond equal Kostant degrees. Harder’s program also asks what Eisenstein cohomology can detect when the two Kostant degrees differ [24, §9.3.1]. The degree difference then gives two pieces of information. On the cohomological side, the two boundary contributions meet in a common degree precisely when a difference of Levi cohomology degrees compensates for the difference of Kostant degrees (Lemma 5.10). On the analytic side, at a regular shift with a common relative center, the absolute degree difference in each grade counts the poles of the direct and reflected archimedean factors at the adjacent arguments (Corollary 5.3). For the (3 , 1)-parabolic of GL 4/Q, the symmetric-square lift of Ramanujan’s cusp form gives a common boundary degree 4 = 3 + 1 = 2 + 2. Of the adjacent arguments −2 and 

−1, the first is critical. At the second, a simple archimedean pole and nonvanishing at the reflected argument imply that the finite L-function has a simple zero (Example 5.6). This singles out both a derivative and a boundary degree in which to seek its cohomological interpretation. 5

The Klingen parabolic leads to a different arithmetic question. For Sp 2n over a totally real field, with n ≥ 2, suitable globally generic cuspidal Levi data realize the singular shift as the unique simultaneously critical shift at every real place (Proposition 3.14). For GSp 4/Q,the denominator question arises in the neighboring chamber of Kostant length two. For the algebraically twisted inducing representation at the cohomological parameter, only the upper argument is critical (Example 5.14). The author’s Klingen denominator conjecture relates this critical value, after period normalization, to the denominator of an Eisenstein class [29], following Harder’s conjecture for the Siegel parabolic of Sp 4 [23, §1.5.1, (93)].
