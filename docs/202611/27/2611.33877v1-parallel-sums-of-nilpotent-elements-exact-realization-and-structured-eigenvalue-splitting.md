## Abstract
We prove that, over every infinite field and for $m\ge2$, the parallel sums $N(N+C)^{-1}C$ of nilpotent $m\times m$ inputs with invertible sum are exactly the matrices of nullity at least two. Every positive split of the target nullity between the input kernels is realizable. Over $\mathbb{C}$, we determine the paired derivative image and characterize universal full matrix cancellation at nonzero residues. For analytic input paths, nilpotence is required only at the base. An adjugate identity forces the last two characteristic coefficients to be $O(\varepsilon^2)$, yielding sharp bounds $O(|\varepsilon|^{2/3})$ at nonzero three-dimensional nilpotent residues and $O(|\varepsilon|^{1/(m-2)})$ at nilpotent residues for $m\ge4$. At every base with residue of type $[m-1,1]$, $m\ge4$, we prove a generic splitting law, also with fixed input sum. For $m\ge5$, it has $m-2$ roots on the scale $|\varepsilon|^{1/(m-2)}$ and two on the scale $|\varepsilon|^{1/2}$, separating the largest-root and spectral-gap scales. A separate sharpness family keeps both inputs nilpotent and two roots zero. At the nonzero nilpotent three-dimensional boundary, a finite coefficient criterion computes the splitting orders for each exceptional affine fixed-sum direction. For each such path, the punctured spectral gap is at least $c|\varepsilon|^3$ near zero for some $c>0$, and the exponent is optimal over this class. Over a nonzero unital ring with $2$ invertible, we construct two $3\times3$ matrix inputs, each of nilpotency index exactly three, with invertible sum. In the complex unital Banach case, the residue has spectrum ${0}\cup\tfrac12σ(Q)$ for every coefficient $Q$. With the common core allowed to vary, a complex matrix is a finite-Drazin Hua output if and only if its nullity is not one.
