## Abstract
We address the solution of systems of nonlinear algebraic equations via continuous Newton methods; these methods reformulate the discrete Newton update as an initial value problem for a system of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) whose stationary points correspond to the desired roots. A fundamental challenge arises when the Jacobian of these ODEs becomes singular along the solution trajectory, inducing blow-up in the vector field --or in the state itself-- and causing the numerical integration to break down before reaching the desired root. To overcome this difficulty, we propose a directional self-similar blow-up detection and crossing procedure, wrapped around an adaptive step-size ODE solver with event detection. The method assumes local self-similar behavior near the blow-up; the self-similarity exponent, blow-up time, and amplitude are estimated on the fly from a short sequence of threshold crossings via linear regression, requiring no prior knowledge of the singularity location, its order, or the blow-up regime---in contrast to approaches that rely on explicit analytical continuation or problem-specific coordinate transformations. The estimated exponent distinguishes two qualitatively different scenarios at finite time: one in which only the vector field diverges while the state remains finite, admitting an explicit integral correction, and one in which the state itself blows-up. In addition, infinite-time divergence of the state (the so-called "grow-up" regime) is handled through a suitable time reparameterization. In higher dimensions, we track the norm of the vector field along its instantaneous direction; near a simple singularity, the flow asymptotically aligns with a single dominant direction (see text), reducing detection and estimation to a scalar problem regardless of ambient space dimension.
