Abstract
We obtain a new, sharper upper bound for the determinant of a matrix of or-
der n ≡2 (mod 3) with entries in the complex third-roots of unity. This bound
is an analog of Ehlich’s bound for binary matrices of order n ≡3 (mod 4) and it
is obtained by first applying an appropriate normalization, or balancing, proce-
dure for matrices with root-of-unity entries and then generalizing Ehlich’s argu-
ment. This new bound achieves an asymptotic fraction of approximately 74% of
the Hadamard bound.

Hadamard’s determinant bound [6] states that the determinant of an order n ma-
trix M with entries in the complex unit disk satisfies | det M|2 ≤nn, with equality in
the bound if and only if MM ∗= nIn. In the same paper, Hadamard asked the question
of determining the maximal determinant of a matrix with entries ±1. The ±1 matrices
attaining the Hadamard bound with equality are known as Hadamard matrices, and
they can only exist at orders n = 1, 2 or n a multiple of 4. Currently, the smallest open
case for the existence of an Hadamard matrix is n = 2396, [3]. Sharper upper bounds
have been found for ±1 matrices of order n > 2 with n̸ ≡0 (mod 4). Barba [1] showed
that a ±1 matrix M of odd order n satisfies | det M|2 ≤(2n−1)(n−1)n−1, with equality
if and only if NN ⊺= (n −1)In + Jn. ∗Equality in Barba’s bound implies in particular
that n ≡1 (mod 4), hence this bound can be further refined for n ≡3 (mod 4). In [5],
Ehlich analyzed the maximal determinant of matrices in the finite set
Cm = {C ∈Matm(3 + 4Z) : C = (cij)ij, cii = n, C = C∗is positive definite},
(1)
and maximized the determinant of a matrix in Cm to find a determinantal upper bound
for matrices with entries ±1 of order n ≡3 (mod 4).
The maximal determinant problem for ±1 matrices has been generalized to matri-
ces over the fourth roots of unity in [2] and subsequently to matrices over arbitrary
roots of unity [8, 9]. A generalization of Barba’s bound, depending on the minimal
absolute value of a sum of n roots of unity, was shown in [8, 9]. For ternary matrices,
i.e. matrices with entries in the set of third roots of unity, this generalized Barba bound
is identical to the original, and it can only be met with equality if n ≡1 (mod 3), hence
a sharpening like that of Ehlich could be possible for ternary matrices of order n ≡2
(mod 3). The main purpose of this paper is to obtain such an upper bound.
Here, we will proceed in a similar way to Ehlich’s proof and with as much gener-
ality as we possible, first re-proving results in Ehlich’s paper while at the same time
deriving new results pertaining to matrices with entries in µ3, and finally analyzing
the latter case in detail while referring to [5] for the analogous calculations.
We will often use the following corollary of Sylvester’s Criterion:
Lemma 1. Let A be a Hermitian matrix with det A > 0. If the submatrix of A obtained by
deleting the last row and column is positive definite, then A is positive definite.
Proof. Let A′ be the submatrix obtained from A by deleting its last row and column.
Since A′ is positive definite, all the principal minors of A′ are positive definite by [7,
Cor. 7.1.5]. This, together with the assumption that det A > 0, implies that every
leading principal minor of A is positive. By Sylvester’s Criterion [7, Thm. 7.2.5(b)] we
conclude that A is positive definite.

To prove this result, we will first introduce an appropriate normalization procedure
for matrices with entries in the set of ℓ-th roots of unity µℓ. Normalization will ensure
that Gram matrices belong to a set Kn, for which we will be able to characterize its
maximal determinant matrices as block matrices indexed by partitions of n. We will
then determine the partition shapes associated to maximal determinant matrices and
thus obtain a set of candidate maximal determinant matrices of size n. Finally, for the
case ℓ= 3 we determine a unique maximal determinant of Kn for every n, leading to
an upper bound for matrices with entries in µ3.
