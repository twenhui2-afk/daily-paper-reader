Projecting Fermions into Exact Scar Towers:
Local Protection across Phase Transitions
Wenjie Wei,1 Kun Jiang,1, 2, ∗and Hui-Ke Jin3, †
1Beijing National Laboratory for Condensed Matter Physics and Institute of Physics,

2School of Physical Sciences, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100190, China
3School of Physical Science and Technology, ShanghaiTech University, Shanghai 201210, China

We construct exact scar towers in SO(n) (n ≥3) spin chains by Gutzwiller projection of n-flavor
flat-band Kitaev chains. The projected roots have only singlet and antisymmetric components on
each bond. These local constraints allow a staggered flavor flip to generate an equidistant tower.
The root and its first descendant exhaust the two-site configurations that are visited by the tower,
leaving an orthogonal bond subspace unvisited. Deformations within this subspace can break SO(n)
symmetry and reshape the low-energy physics without altering the exact scar tower. A polarization-
selective deformation drives an exact first-order transition from the symmetry-protected topological
phase to a ferromagnetic phase. The tower retains its wave functions and absolute energies across
the phase transition, including at the gapless point.
Exact scars therefore need not inherit the
symmetries, order parameters, or criticality of the ground state.
Introduction. Quantum many-body scars (QMBS) are
exceptional nonthermal eigenstates embedded in other-
wise thermalizing spectra that violate the eigenstate ther-
malization hypothesis (ETH) [1–6].
First highlighted
by persistent quench oscillations in Rydberg-atom ar-
rays [7] and the kinetically constrained PXP model [8–
13], scarred dynamics have since been placed on an
exact footing through equidistant eigenstate towers in
the Affleck–Kennedy–Lieb–Tasaki (AKLT) chain [14–16],
spin-1 XY magnets [17], and related models [18–23]. As
QMBS have evolved from a phenomenological curiosity
into a general paradigm for weak ergodicity breaking, at-
tention has shifted toward identifying the underlying pro-
tective mechanisms, including projector-embedding con-
structions [24, 25], quasisymmetry-based approaches [23],
commutant-algebra methods [26], and MPS-based frame-
works [27], which enable scar states to coexist with ther-
malizing spectra.
Intriguingly, although these scar towers govern coher-
ent nonthermal dynamics at finite energy density, their
exact constructions are typically built on top of spe-
cific symmetric or topological ground states. This raises
a fundamental question: how are high-energy quantum
scars related to the conventional hallmarks of quantum
phases, such as symmetries, order parameters, and low-
energy criticality [28, 29]? Systematically addressing this
question requires isolating exact scar towers from the
interactions that govern the low-energy phase diagram.
Previous studies have shown that local terms confined
to an unvisited subspace can break global symmetries
and modify the bulk spectrum without affecting the scar
tower [18, 21, 27, 30, 31]. Nevertheless, such construc-
tions are usually tied to the algebraic properties of target
spin wave functions, and their robustness across changes
of aforementioned hallmarks remains largely unexplored.
This leads to a natural question: can microscopic kinetic
constraints completely decouple an exact scar tower from
the low-energy phase structure?
In this Letter, we construct exact scar towers from lo-
cal constraints induced by Gutzwiller projection [32, 33];
see Fig. 1.
Projecting the ground state of n-flavor
flat-band Kitaev chains [34] yields SO(n) symmetry-
protected topological (SPT) roots [35–42], from which
a staggered flavor flip generates an equidistant tower.
The root and its first descendant exhaust the two-site
configurations that are visited by the tower, leaving an
orthogonal bond subspace unvisited. Deformations con-
fined to this subspace annihilate every tower state bond
by bond, preserving the tower regardless of changes in
the surrounding spectrum.
We find that the exact SO(n) scar tower can remain
unchanged despite changes in ground-state symmetry, or-
der, and the low-energy gap. We demonstrate this in the
SO(4) chain, where a simple deformation in the unvisited
subspace breaks SO(4) symmetry and drives an exact
first-order transition between the SPT and ferromagnetic
phases. At the transition point, we construct exact ex-
citations with a quadratic dispersion that establish ther-
modynamic gaplessness, while the tower wave functions
and energies remain unchanged. Exact diagonalization
confirms that the tower states remain low-entanglement
outliers in the energy spectrum.

Conclusion.
Gutzwiller projection of flat-band Ki-
taev chains naturally induces local bond exclusions in
SO(n) spin chains. These constraints restrict the avail-
able two-site configurations, allowing for the construc-
tion of equidistant scar towers. Because the entire tower
visits only a small subset of bond states, deformations
confined to the unvisited subspace preserve the tower
wave functions and energies while driving ground-state
transitions. We demonstrated an exact first-order tran-
sition with a gapless critical point where the scar tower
persists. Our results show that local kinetic constraints
can isolate high-energy exact scars from the low-energy
physics of phase transitions.
For further study, it will be interesting to extend
this Gutzwiller-projection mechanism beyond the flat-
band limit or to higher-dimensional lattices to yield new
scar towers.
It is also worth exploring whether these
projection-induced constraints can protect coherent dy-
namics in driven or open systems[44, 45], or sustain scar
revivals when the tower is weakly coupled to a thermal
background.
