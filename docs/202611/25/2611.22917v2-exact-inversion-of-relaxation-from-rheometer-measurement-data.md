## Abstract
A rheometer is commonly used to measure the input-output relation for analyzing the relaxation of stress for rheological materials. Here, the input is a time-harmonic torque and the output is the angular displacement. This paper aims to propose an exact inversion scheme of the relaxation function from two input-output relations only having different moments of inertia. The relaxation function is represented by a Prony's series of known number of terms, with a positive constant term and terms with distinct relaxation times. The augmented system relates the material parameters to its characteristic roots. These roots are the poles of the transfer function. Here the transfer function is the Laplace transform of the output divided by that of the input, and it is a rational function in the Laplace variable. The poles of the transfer function are recovered using Pade's approximation. By measuring two input-output relations only having different moments of inertia, we can rigorously recover the relaxation function. Thus, we note a clear link between the characteristic roots and the poles. Numerical experiments reconstruct the relaxation function from synthetic angular displacements corresponding to two given moments of inertia based on our inversion method. The results show the effect of measurement noise on the recovered characteristic roots and relaxation function.
