SUPER QUANTUM GROUPS OF DIVIDED POWERS À LA LUSZTIG 

IVÁN ANGIONO AND CRISTIAN VAY 

Abstract. We construct super analogues of the quantum groups of divided powers introduced by Lusztig. We call them super analogues because the Lie-theoretical data governing their structure is comparable to that of contragredient Lie superalgebras. Indeed, we show that they admit PBW-type bases parametrized by root systems of super type, and that they also have Lusztig isomorphisms giving a representation of the corresponding Weyl groupoids. Moreover, we prove that our super objects are equipped with a Frobenius-type morphism whose kernel is the Drinfeld double of a finite-dimensional Nichols algebra of super type, a super analogue of Lusztig’s small quantum group, and whose cokernel is the enveloping algebra of the semisimple Lie algebra determined by the Cartan roots of the Nichols algebra. We carry out our construction within the framework of Nichols algebras of diagonal type, specifically, those whose braiding is obtained by specializing at a root of unity. This allows us to consider a divided power integral form inside a large quantum group of De Concini– Kac–Procesi type and then, via specialization, obtain our desired object. 

1. Introduction 

Since their appearance in the 1980s, quantum groups have spread across various fields of mathematics and theoretical physics. These Hopf algebras were conceived as deforma-tions of the enveloping algebras U (g) of semisimple Lie algebras g. They are defined over the field C(q) of rational functions in one variable and admit various forms over C[q±1],or even over Z[q±1], giving rise, upon specialization, to Hopf algebras over the complex numbers that exhibit different behaviors with regard to their representations. This extension is the algebraic counterpart of the Frobenius morphism of algebraic groups in positive characteristic, and lies at the root of the applications of quantum groups to modular representation theory. The analogues of the first two algebras for Lie superalgebras are already known. In the classical setting, these algebras are defined by explicit presentations in terms of generators and relations, but their generalization to the super setting is quite delicate, as the defining ideal requires higher-degree Serre-type relations whose form depends on the Dynkin diagram. Finding a complete and minimal set of such relations was a substantial task, carried out by Yamane [32]. Moreover, the same superalgebra may admit non-equivalent Dynkin diagrams related by odd reflections rather than by the action of a Weyl group, so there is no distinguished presentation. Nevertheless, these two objects can be treated more uniformly within the theory of Nichols algebras of diagonal type. Following the work of the first author [11], super quantum groups à la De Concini–Kac–Procesi can be constructed by starting with a dis-tinguished pre-Nichols algebra of diagonal type and then applying the Drinfeld double construction. These quantum groups admit a central Hopf subalgebra, and quotient-ing by this subalgebra yields a small super quantum group. The resulting small super quantum groups are also Drinfeld doubles of the corresponding Nichols algebras. The objective of this work is to construct an analogue of the quantum group of divided powers and of the exact sequence (1.1) for simple Lie superalgebras, following the Nichols algebra approach used for the other two algebras. Nichols algebras of diagonal type are fundamental invariants in the classification pro-gram of pointed Hopf algebras with abelian group of group-like elements [9]. The finite-dimensional ones were classified by Heckenberger [21] and can be organized into the classes of Cartan type and super type, the latter consisting of those associated with contragredient Lie superalgebras of classical type, together with finitely many excep-tional examples. The structure of these algebras is governed by root systems and Weyl groupoids [20, 23], as is the case for contragredient Lie superalgebras. These structures underlie the distinction between the aforementioned classes. Moreover, the associated Drinfeld doubles are related by Lusztig isomorphisms [22], which are reminiscent of Weyl group isomorphisms and odd reflections in the theory of Lie superalgebras. Here, we restrict our attention to Nichols algebras of diagonal type determined by braiding matrices belonging to one-parameter families in the sense of [8].
