# Exact Inversion of Relaxation from Rheometer Measurement Data 

Dedicated to the late Professor Anatoly Grigorievich Yagola 

Yu Jiang ∗ Takayuki Kurokawa † Ching-Lung Lin ‡ Gen Nakamura §

Abstract 

A rheometer is commonly used to measure the input-output relation for analyzing the relaxation of stress for rheological materials. Here, the input is a time-harmonic torque and the output is the angular displacement. This paper aims to propose an exact inversion scheme of the relaxation function from two input-output relations only having different moments of inertia. The relaxation function is represented by a Prony’s series of known number of terms, with a positive constant term and terms with distinct relaxation times. The augmented system relates the material parameters to its characteristic roots. These roots are the poles of the transfer function. Here the transfer function is the Laplace transform of the output divided by that of the input, and it is a rational function in the Laplace variable. The poles of the transfer function are recovered using Pad´ e’s approxima-tion. By measuring two input-output relations only having different moments of inertia, we can rigorously recover the relaxation function. Thus, we note a clear link between the characteristic roots and the poles. Numerical experiments reconstruct the relaxation function from synthetic angular displacements corresponding to two given moments of inertia based on our inversion method. The results show the effect of measurement noise on the recovered characteristic roots and relaxation function. 

2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 45K05, 45D05. 

Keywords: inverse problem, relaxation function, viscoelasticity, rotational rheometer, Prony series, Pad´ e’s approximation. 

# 1 Introduction 

The relaxation function describes how a viscoelastic material retains and dissipates stress over time. Identifying this function provides a link between measured mechanical responses and constitutive models [9]. Finite sums of positive decaying exponentials and a positive constant offer a practical representation in terms of relaxation times and moduli. Such representa-tions in terms of Prony series have been used both to approximate relaxation functions and to construct models from creep data [15, 14]. The relaxation times τi, the moduli Gi and the equilibrium modulus Ge are referred to as the relaxation parameters. Recovering the relaxation function is a distinct objective in rheology.

4.1 Reconstruction of relaxation parameters from characteristic roots 

We apply the spectral approach to reconstruct the relaxation parameters. To begin with, for each fixed inertia I, let λIj , j = 1 , . . . , N +2, be the roots of PI . This finite set plays the role of one cluster in [4, 5]. A complete set of characteristic roots means all roots in this cluster. # 5 Conclusion and future works 

This paper develops a method to recover a relaxation function G(t) represented by a Prony’s series with a positive constant term. For known order N and distinct relaxation times τi, two complete sets of characteristic roots λIj and λI′ 

> j

uniquely determine G(t) through (4.1)–(4.3). Pad´ e’s approximation is used to estimate the poles of the transfer function, which are nothing but the characteristic roots from angular displacements. Numerical experiments apply both steps to synthetic angular displacements. They confirm recovery of the relaxation parameters from noise free data. For valid reconstructions from noisy data, Table 1 shows that the median function errors EG are smaller than the parameter errors Ep.Future work will address the following. 

• Study the stability of Pad´ e’s approximation for recovering characteristic roots from noisy angular displacement measurements and regularization of the parameter reconstruction. 

• Examine inertia calibration, synchronized torque and angular displacement measure-ments, and feedback effects in experiments.
