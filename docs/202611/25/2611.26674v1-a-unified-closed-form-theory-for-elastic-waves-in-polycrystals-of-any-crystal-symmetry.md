## Abstract
The coherent elastic wave in random polycrystals is described by the first-order smoothing approximation, whose mass operator remains an integral evaluated only numerically. Here we derive its closed form for equiaxed, untextured polycrystals of any crystal symmetry with an exponential correlation function, giving an explicit dispersion equation at all frequencies. Unlike the integral form, which fails beyond one inverse correlation radius from the real axis, the closed form holds deep in the complex wavenumber plane, where heavily attenuated roots lie. It is compact, reproduces the dispersion equations of Stanke and Kino for cubic grains, extends them to any symmetry and contains the Born and far-field approximations as special cases. It reveals the structure of the roots and their partitions of the coherent field: three roots per polarization, one from the incident wave and one from each of the two scattering channels, without and with mode conversion. It also yields closed-form limits of their velocity, attenuation and partition in the Rayleigh, stochastic and geometric regimes, which show why a single coherent wave has been observed at long wavelengths, whereas more than one can be observed at short wavelengths. These results underpin further theory and quantitative characterization of polycrystalline microstructure.
