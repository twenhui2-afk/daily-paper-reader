## Abstract
Hawking radiation can modify the horizon structure of a black hole through the loss of mass and global charges. Investigating this process through quantum tunneling, we develop a systematic strategy to reconstruct horizon displacements under finite-energy emission, even when their radii are not explicitly known. Inversion of the horizon condition allows to follow roots's displacements along sequences of static geometries. As a concrete realization, we consider a nonlinearly charged extension of the Bañados--Teitelboim--Zanelli (BTZ) family, whose horizon structure offers an ideal setup to explore this process. Of particular interest is an initial configuration with a double inner horizon coexisting with a simple event horizon. We show that neutral emission necessarily splits the inner root into two simple ones, revealing a horizon fine structure. For charged emission, the dependence of extracted charge on emitted energy determines the separation law and allows that preservation or later restore the degeneracy. We construct such channels connecting the same initial and final states through distinct internal histories sharing the same leading semiclassical factor. As emission continues, the larger of the newly separated inner horizons can coalesce with the event horizon, driving the sequence towards extremality with a vanishing temperature but finite tunneling probability. We find that zero temperature cannot be attained in finite boundary time while uniform adiabaticity bounds hold. We also establish a regime where the geometric expansion and the canonical thermal approximation remain controlled near this endpoint. Finally, applying this analysis to the sector reproducing a quantum-backreacted BTZ geometry reveals how the leading semiclassical corrections raise the temperature and enhance the neutral-probe horizon emission factor relative to classical BTZ.
