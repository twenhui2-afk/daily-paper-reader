MINIMAL NORM OF REGULAR VECTORS IN THE ROOT LATTICE OF A CRYSTALLOGRAPHIC ROOT SYSTEM 

MATHIEU DUTOUR SIKIRI ´C

Abstract. For a crystallographic root system Φ with Weyl group W and root lattice L = ZΦ, we determine the minimum norm of a vector of L having trivial stabilizer in W , and we prove that the vectors attaining it form a single orbit of W . Although this is a minimization of a quadratic function over the lattice points of a cone, the minimizer in the dominant chamber is always unique: it is 

ρ+λ, where ρ is the Weyl vector and λ is 0 or the minuscule weight in the class of ρ modulo L. In particular, the optimal vector is the Weyl vector exactly when the Weyl vector lies in L. We give explicit formulas for the families An,

Bn, Cn and Dn, with elementary proofs, and the values for the exceptional types E6, E7, E8, F4 and G2. Finally, we explain the coincidences between the minimal norms by the folding of Dynkin diagrams. 

1. Introduction 

Let Φ be a crystallographic root system in a Euclidean space V with Weyl group 

W = W (Φ), and let L = ZΦ be its root lattice. We refer to Humphreys [1, Chapters 1 and 2] for the definitions and basic properties of root systems, Weyl groups and root lattices; crystallographic root systems, root lattices and weight lattices are treated in [1, §2.9]. For the simply laced types, with roots of norm 2, the root lattices are An, Dn, E6, E7 and E8. For the types Bn and Cn we use the standard realizations in Rn:Φ( Bn) = {± ei, ±ei ± ej } and Φ( Cn) = {± 2ei, ±ei ± ej },

whose root lattices are Zn and Dn respectively. In both cases the Weyl group is the group of all signed permutations of the coordinates, which for Cn is larger than 

W (Dn). For G2 we take, in the plane x1 + x2 + x3 = 0 of R3, the short roots ei − ej

and the long roots ±(2 ei − ej − ek) with {i, j, k } = {1, 2, 3}; the root lattice is A2

and W (G2) is the dihedral group of order 12. For F4 we take, in R4, the short roots ±ei ± ej and the long roots ±2ei and ( ±1, ±1, ±1, ±1); the root lattice is D4

and W (F4) has order 1152. The root lattice alone therefore does not determine the problem considered below: Cn and Dn share the lattice Dn, and F4 and D4 share the lattice D4, but with different Weyl groups. The reflection hyperplanes Hα = {x ∈ V : ⟨x, α ⟩ = 0 }, for α ∈ Φ, partition 

V into open Weyl chambers . Since the stabilizer of a vector v ∈ V is generated by the reflections it contains [1, Theorem 1.12], a lattice vector v ∈ L has trivial stabilizer in W if and only if v lies in the interior of some Weyl chamber, equivalently, 

⟨v, α ⟩̸ = 0 for all roots α ∈ Φ. Such vectors are called regular .We consider the following optimization problem.
