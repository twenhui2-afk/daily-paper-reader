## Abstract
Learning visual policies requires turning interaction experience into guidance for individual decisions. Trajectory outcomes obscure action quality, while unguided exploration can revisit familiar situations. We present \textsc{LightAct}, an experience-learning framework that connects skill-guided exploration with policy internalization. Interaction segments are summarized into experience entries and consolidated into a game-specific skill that guides root collection toward broader state coverage. At each root, candidate actions are compared through short rollouts under a shared frozen policy. Environment-reported score gains are averaged across continuations and normalized into relative action rewards, producing reusable supervision for reinforcement learning. Policy training uses a compact dataset of just 1,024 decision roots collected in a single source game and paired with their action rewards. Across 170 GameWorld tasks spanning 34 games, \textsc{LightAct} improves overall progress by 23.6, 29.2, and 13.0 percentage points at the 2B, 4B, and 9B scales, respectively, with our strongest model competitive with reported proprietary-model results. Ablations show broader root-state coverage with skill guidance and consistent gains across three policy optimizers. Case studies show how rollouts distinguish actions with identical immediate gains.
