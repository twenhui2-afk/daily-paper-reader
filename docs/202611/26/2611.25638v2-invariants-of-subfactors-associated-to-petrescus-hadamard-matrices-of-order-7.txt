INVARIANTS OF SUBFACTORS ASSOCIATED TO PETRESCU’S HADAMARD MATRICES OF ORDER 7 

DIETMAR BISCH AND REMUS NICOARA 

Abstract. Petrescu’s sixth-root 7 ×7 Hadamard matrix defines an irreducible, hyperfinite subfactor 

N ⊂ M with Jones index 7. It is built from the spin model commuting square obtained from the Hadamard matrix by iterating Jones’ basic construction. We prove that the standard invariant of this subfactor is minimal, i.e. the higher relative commutants consists of Temperley-Lieb algebras. Thus, the principal graphs of the subfactor are the Coxeter-Dynkin graph A ∞. This solves a long-standing open problem. The main new idea is a congruence for the profile of the Hadamard matrix that restricts the order of rotation on the lowest-weight spaces in Jones planar algebra description of the standard invariant. Together with a uniform trace bound, this gives bounded annular multiplicities on a subsequence, which forces the principal graph to have norm at most two. We also show that the same result holds for most Hadamard matrices in Petrescu’s one-parameter family. Specifically, the associated subfactors have Temperley-Lieb standard invariant at every transcendental phase and at every algebraic phase with residue ±1 at a prime above three. In each fixed degree the exceptional set is finite and consists of algebraic numbers. Our methods apply to several other families of Hadamard matrices of order q > 4 as well, and we show that the associated subfactors have A ∞ principal graphs. These include real Hadamard matrices of order q ≡ 4 (mod 8), Hadamard matrices over Q(i) of order q ≡ 2 (mod 4) and cube-root matrices of order q with 3 | q and 9 ∤ q. For order six Hadamard matrices, we obtain the same result at every transcendental phase in Diţă’s family through Haagerup’s matrix and at every algebraically independent pair of phases in Karlsson’s two-parameter family. 

1. Introduction 

A Hadamard matrix of order q is a matrix H ∈ Mq(C) such that its entries have modulus one and HH ∗ = qI q. Then U = q−1/2H is of course a unitary matrix. We say that two Hadamard matrices are equivalent if one can be obtained from the other by permuting rows or columns, and by multiplying rows or columns with scalars of absolute value one. Clearly, there is a Hadamard matrix for every order, namely the Fourier matrix (up to normalization). It is an intriguing question how many equivalence classes of non-equivalent Hadamard matrices there are of fixed order. If the order is a composite integer, it is well-known, and not hard to see, that there are always uncountably many non-equivalent Hadamard matrices of that order. For n = 2, 3 and 5, there is just the class of the Fourier matrix [H97]. If n is a prime number ≥ 7, it was shown in [HJ90, MW92] that there is at least one other class of Hadamard matrices. Petrescu showed in his thesis that there is in fact a one-parameter family of non-equivalent Hadamard matrices of order 7, 13, 19 etc. [Pet97]. It is not known what the answer is for order 11. The problem of studying complex Hadamard matrices was initiated by Popa in [P83] as they correspond to pairs of orthogonal MASAs in a matrix algebra. This paper is at the origin of much of the subsequent work on Hadamard matrices in the context of subfactor theory. Every q × q Hadamard matrix defines a so-called spin model commuting square [JS97, Chapter 6] with which one can build an irreducible, hyperfinite subfactor with Jones index q [N10, Section 2]. The collection of higher relative commutants, together with the data describing their inclusions, traces, and Jones projections, is called the standard invariant of the subfactor [P94, Section 1.3.7]. In this picture, an important role is played by the (two-click) rotation that is crucial for our work as well. If a subfactor is constructed from a commuting square, as in the case of spin model subfactors associated to Hadamard matrices, it is known to be hard to compute the standard invariant, or, equivalently, the quantum symmetries the subfactor encodes. In fact, there are only a few constructions where this was achieved, most recently in [BC25]. Since the algebra generated by the Jones projections ek, 1 ≤ k ≤ n, is always a subalgebra of N ′ ∩ Mn, it is particularly interesting to investigate if the subfactor has additional symmetries. If the index is > 4, these subalgebras are the Temperley-Lieb algebras with parameter the square root of the Jones index. Thus, they represent the minimal quantum symmetries that every subfactor has. Popa proved in [P93] that for every number > 4 there is a (non-hyperfinite) subfactor whose standard invariant is minimal. This result was a breakthrough, as it established that Temperley-Lieb algebras with any parameter > 2 can be realized as quantum symmetries of a concrete subfactor. It is unknown for which parameters this can be achieved by hyperfinite subfactors (see e.g. [BC25]). In this paper, we obtain new examples. It has been a long-standing open problem to compute the standard invariant of Hadamard subfactors constructed from spin models. Since Petrescu’s one-parameter family of non-equivalent Hadamard matrices of order 7 is the first continuous family of prime order, it is particularly interesting to study the associated subfactors. Experts have conjectured that their standard invariants should be Temperley-Lieb, and partial progress was obtained for instance in [N06, Introduction and Section 4] and [M21, Example 3.3]. We show in this paper that the Hadamard subfactors of index 7 built from spin models based on Petrescu’s family have Temperley-Lieb standard invariant for most parameters (see below for a precise statement).
