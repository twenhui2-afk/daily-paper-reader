# Joint Cohort Laws and Mixed Cumulants with Spectral Asymptotics for Growing Gaps in Finite-Age Branching Processes 

Mojtaba Moradi a

> aDepartment of Industrial Engineering, Faculty of Technology and Engineering-East of Guilan, University of Guilan, Rudsar, Iran mmoradi@guilan.ac.ir

Abstract 

We investigate the joint distribution and temporal dependence of birth cohorts in a discrete-time age-dependent branching model in which an individual may reproduce at several integer ages. Under an independent age-specific reproduction array, we derive a recursive joint probability generating function, an exact history-dependent transition kernel, and a martingale innovation representation. These identities yield explicit covariance formulae and finite coefficient recursions for mixed factorial moments and mixed cumulants. When reproduction is restricted to a finite maximum age L, the cohort vector admits a finite-dimensional companion-matrix representation. In the primitive supercritical case, we establish an exact Lp criterion: for every integer p ≥ 2, the Perron martingale and the normalized cohort state converge in Lp if and only if all age-specific offspring distributions have finite 

pth moments. The normalized cohorts consequently share a common limit, which satisfies an age-indexed smoothing-transform equation with an explicit triangular cumulant recursion. Our principal asymptotic result concerns two cohorts whose separation increases with time. Under a simple-real-subdominant-root condition, we obtain a three-regime asymptotic equivalent for the mean-square distance between their mean-normalized sizes. The rate is governed by whether the squared modulus of the dominant non-Perron root is smaller than, equal to, or larger than the Perron root. This result quantifies the approach to the common cohort limit and distinguishes the present cohort-level question from first-order Perron convergence alone. 

Keywords: age-dependent branching process, birth cohorts, mixed cumulants, Perron martingale, spectral asymptotics.

1. Introduction 

Branching processes describe populations whose members generate new individuals, but the timing of reproduction matters whenever individuals can reproduce more than once. In a one-generation Galton–Watson process, each individual contributes to the next generation once. In age-dependent models, by contrast, individuals born in different cohorts may contribute offspring to the same calendar-time cohort. The resulting sequence of birth-cohort sizes is therefore generally dependent and need not itself be a first-order Markov chain. The broad framework of general branching processes, including Crump– Mode–Jagers processes, accommodates repeated reproduction at different ages and overlapping generations; a foundational general age-dependent formulation is due to Crump and Mode [2]. Discrete-time formulations and their random-tree representation are discussed by Jagers and Sagitov [5]. The analysis of moments in age-dependent branching processes has a substan-tial history. Sevast’yanov and Chistyakov [12] developed multidimensional renewal equations for branching-process moments. Mode [10] derived discrete-time renewal equations for mean and covariance functions in a generalized one-type age-dependent model. Kimmel [8] used a point-process formulation to obtain symbolic recursions for arbitrary finite-dimensional distributions in age- and time-dependent branching processes. More recently, Janson [6] studied second-order fluctuations in lattice Crump–Mode–Jagers processes and identified distinct regimes governed by the roots of the associated re-newal equation. Cheng [1] established limit and central limit results for supercritical age-structured branching models in which reproduction depends on an individual’s remaining lifetime. That remaining-lifetime framework differs from the discrete repeated-age cohort model considered here, but it is relevant to the current development of age-structured branching limit theory. The model considered here is a deliberately specific member of this broader family. An individual born at time k may produce offspring at ages 1, 2, . . . , and the distribution of the number produced at age t depends on t

but not on calendar time or cohort size. We impose mutual independence of all age-specific reproduction variables, including variables corresponding to different ages of the same individual. A general Crump–Mode–Jagers reproduction point process need not satisfy this last condition. The present assumptions therefore yield a factorization of the conditional generating function by parental age, which is the structural feature used throughout the paper. A closely related discrete-time period-dependent branching model was studied by Fathi Vajargah and Moradi [4], following their earlier age-/period-dependent epidemic formulation [3]. In particular, the 2012 model already allows an individual to contribute offspring at more than one age and is therefore a direct predecessor of the population mechanism considered here. 2The present paper instead makes the independent reproduction array explicit and develops joint-cohort distributional identities, arbitrary-order mixed-moment and mixed-cumulant recursions, and finite-age asymptotic results that were not the focus of those earlier papers. The precise relation and remaining overlap are discussed in Section 7. The dependence between birth cohorts is a central feature of this model. Although offspring contributions scheduled at a fixed calendar time are con-ditionally independent across parental ages, the cohort sizes that determine the numbers of summands are themselves dependent. This distinction rules out a general factorization of the joint cohort pgf into marginal pgfs. Asimple calculation illustrates the point. Under the model assumptions,

These terms are essential whenever the earlier cohorts are dependent. The paper has two complementary parts. The first develops exact finite-time distributional identities: a joint cohort pgf, a history-dependent transi-tion kernel, an orthogonal innovation representation, and coefficient recur-sions for mixed factorial moments and cumulants. The second concerns finite maximum reproductive age. We identify the associated finite-dimensional companion recursion, establish a necessary-and-sufficient age-by-age moment condition for Lp convergence of the Perron martingale, and characterize the common cohort limit through a smoothing transform. The main additional result is a quantitative theorem for growing cohort gaps. It gives a sharp three-regime mean-square asymptotic under an explicit simple-root expansion of the renewal sequence. The threshold is the square-root scale of the Perron root, and the constants are expressed in terms of the offspring variances and the full mean renewal sequence. We do not claim that the Perron martingale or smoothing-transform mechanism is new; the contribution is the explicit 3cohort-level formulation and, in particular, the growing-gap mean-square asymptotic. The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 specifies the reproduction array. Sections 3 and 4 develop the joint law and second-order structure. Section 5 gives arbitrary-order moment and cumulant recursions. Section 6 contains the finite-age Perron theory, the common-limit cumulants, and the growing-gap theorem. Section 7 discusses the relation to earlier work and the scope of the results.

Remark 6.7 (The finite-type branching representation) . The finite-memory state recursion is also a finite-type branching representation. A type-i

Remark 6.17 (What changes for a complex dominant non-Perron root) . The real-root assumption is substantive. Suppose instead that the dominant non-Perron roots of the real renewal sequence are a simple conjugate pair 

ζ, ζ , with ζ = ρe iϑ , 0 < ϑ < π , and that 

ak = cλ k + bζ k + b ζ k + O(ηk), 0 < η < ρ < λ. 

Then the normalized remainder is oscillatory rather than a single real geo-metric term. In the subcritical case ρ2 < λ , the series defining the analogue of C< still converges, because its summands are bounded by a constant times (ρ2/λ )k; the same dominated-convergence argument gives a positive constant after retaining the actual real sequence fk = ak/(cλ k). At the critical bound-ary ρ2 = λ, the leading summands contain a non-oscillatory component and 32 oscillatory components. For a genuinely non-real pair, Cesàro averaging removes the latter, but the resulting constant depends on the conjugate-pair amplitude and must be derived with the corresponding cross terms. In the supercritical case ρ2 > λ , the normalized leading term generally retains a phase depending on mϑ ; consequently a single constant asymptotic equiv-alent need not exist along the full sequence. One should instead expect an oscillatory leading factor, or subsequential equivalents when the phases converge. These cases are not covered by Theorem 6.15; proving a unified complex-root theorem requires a separate formulation that accounts for phase interactions and, for tied roots or Jordan blocks, polynomial factors. We therefore state them as extensions rather than claiming that the real-root theorem already covers them. 

7. Discussion and comparison with related limit theorems 

The results should be interpreted within the assumptions of the inde-pendent age-specific reproduction array. The conditional pgf factorization is a consequence of independence not only between distinct parents, but also between the different age-specific offspring counts of the same parent. Ageneral Crump–Mode–Jagers reproduction point process allows dependence between births at different parental ages; the present formulae do not cover that more general dependence structure without replacing the product of age-wise pgfs by an appropriate joint reproduction transform. The arbitrary-order recursions in Section 5 are finite coefficient identities for the specified cohort model. Their role is to provide explicit recursive access to mixed factorial moments and mixed cumulants while retaining the dependence among cohorts. The general problem of deriving moment equations for age-dependent branching processes is much older and is treated, in broader settings, by renewal and point-process methods; see Mode [10], Sevast’yanov and Chistyakov [12], and Kimmel [8]. Fathi Vajargah and Moradi [4] explicitly studied a discrete-time period-dependent branching process with overlapping generations in which an individual may reproduce at several ages. Their earlier epidemic formulation [3] also made the age/period dependence of reproduction central to the model. The current manuscript is a substantial distributional and asymptotic development of that line of work. Fathi Vajargah and Moradi [4] in their paper focused on the mean, variance, marginal pgf calculations, and simulation, whereas the present paper develops the joint cohort law, the exact history-dependent transition kernel, arbitrary-order mixed factorial-moment and cumulant recursions, and explicit finite-age limit/cumulant formulae. Recent work on discrete multitype branching processes counted with characteristics also establishes law-of-large-numbers and central-limit results in a broader CMJ-related setting; see Kolesko and Sava-Huss [9]. Our finite-age limit theorem is not a replacement for these general results. Its purpose is to exploit the companion-matrix structure to obtain an exact age-by-age Lp moment criterion, direct limits for birth cohorts, and a closed recursive expression for the cumulants of their common normalized limit. The cumulant recursion follows from the model’s independent-age smoothing transform and is stated only under the finite-moment assumptions required at each order. It is useful to compare the asymptotic results with two further develop-ments. First, Janson’s paper on lattice Crump–Mode–Jagers processes was pub-lished online in 2019 and appears in volume 50 (2018) of Advances in Applied Probability [6]. It studies second-order fluctuations of age distributions in a general supercritical lattice CMJ setting. In particular, the asymptotic behaviour depends on the location of the next characteristic root relative to the square-root scale of the Malthusian root, producing Gaussian or oscilla-tory fluctuation regimes under suitable conditions. Our result addresses a different object and a different scale: for a fixed finite maximum reproductive age, it gives an Lp limit for the finite-dimensional birth-cohort state and a mean-square asymptotic for the difference between two mean-normalized cohorts whose observation times and separation both diverge. The three regimes in Theorem 6.15 are determined by the dominant non-Perron root of the scalar renewal sequence. We do not derive a central limit theorem for the population state, and our result should not be read as a replacement for Janson’s fluctuation theory. Second, Cheng’s 2024 preprint on supercritical remaining-lifetime age-structured branching processes [1], subsequently published in 2026, studies measure-valued population states in which an individual’s reproduction law depends on its remaining lifetime. It establishes Malthusian limit results for normalized random measures, gives an L log L-type condition for almost-sure strengthening, and develops a central limit theorem with random normaliza-tion. The state variable, reproduction mechanism, and convergence questions differ from those here. In particular, the necessary-and-sufficient Lp criterion proved in Theorem 6.6 concerns the Perron martingale generated by the finite-dimensional birth-cohort recursion; it is not a replacement for Cheng’s condition for convergence of normalized random measures. The two results are complementary rather than directly comparable in strength. The main limitations are also clear. Dependence between the age-specific offspring counts of a single parent is excluded. The finite-age Perron theorem 35 requires a primitive mean matrix; periodic cases require a separate analysis along residue classes. The three-regime growing-gap theorem assumes that the scalar mean sequence has a simple real dominant non-Perron term with nonzero coefficient. It therefore does not cover complex conjugate roots, ties in the non-Perron spectral radius, or nontrivial Jordan blocks. Those cases can produce oscillatory factors or polynomial corrections and require separate statements. The finite-type representation also makes clear that the Perron direction itself is classical. The main additional asymptotic information here is the explicit mean-square distance between two increasingly separated normalized cohorts, including the transition at the square-root spectral threshold. 

8. Conclusion 

We have studied the joint law and temporal dependence of birth cohorts in a discrete-time age-dependent branching model with repeated reproduction and independent age-specific offspring counts. The finite-time analysis gives a recursive joint pgf, an exact history-dependent transition kernel, and an orthogonal innovation representation. These yield covariance identities and finite coefficient recursions for arbitrary mixed factorial moments and cumulants. For finite maximum reproductive age, the cohort state has a companion-matrix representation. In the primitive supercritical case, the Perron martin-gale converges in Lp exactly when all age-specific offspring distributions have finite pth moments, for each integer p ≥ 2. The normalized cohorts share a common limit; its smoothing-transform equation gives a triangular recursion for all cumulants under the corresponding finite-moment assumptions. The principal quantitative result concerns growing temporal gaps. Under a simple real subdominant-root expansion of the mean renewal sequence, we prove a three-regime equivalent for the mean-square distance between the normalized sizes of cohorts n and n − h, when both h and n − h tend to infinity. The rate changes at the threshold β2 = λ: it is of order λ−(n−h)

below the threshold, ( n−h)λ−(n−h) at the threshold, and ( |β|/λ )2( n−h) above it. The proof uses uniform ratio bounds and a justified passage of limits through the relevant series, including in the supercritical spectral regime. The results rely on independence across parental ages and on a primitive finite-age mean matrix. The growing-gap theorem further assumes a simple real dominant non-Perron root in the scalar mean sequence.
