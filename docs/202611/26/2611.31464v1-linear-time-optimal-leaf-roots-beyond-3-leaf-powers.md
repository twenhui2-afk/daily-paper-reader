## Abstract
A $k$-leaf power for an integer $k \geq 2$ is a graph $G$ that permits a $k$-leaf root, a tree $T$ with leaf set $V(G)$ such that, for all distinct vertices $x,y \in V(G)$, their distance in $T$ is at most $k$ if and only if $xy$ is an edge of $G$. Constructing such trees is motivated by the reconstruction of phylogenetic trees from similarity data. For every fixed $k$, Lafond [ACM Trans. Algorithms 2023] showed that $k$-leaf powers can be recognized in polynomial time. Recently, Dupré la Tour, Lafond, and Ndiaye [SODA 2026] proved that the problem becomes NP-complete when $k$ is part of the input and that deciding whether a graph is a $k$-leaf power for some $k$ is NP-complete as well. Le and Rosenke [J. Graph Algorithms Appl. 2024] proposed a different algorithmic perspective by studying \emph{optimal leaf roots} of graphs $G$, that is, the $k$-leaf roots of $G$ with minimum possible $k$. This smallest $k$-value is called the \emph{leaf rank} of $G$. They showed that optimal leaf roots of chordal cographs, a class of unbounded leaf rank, can be constructed in linear time. However, chordal cographs do not even contain all $3$-leaf powers. This paper extends linear-time optimal-leaf-root construction to three natural superclasses of the $3$-leaf powers, the $(\text{bull},\text{gem})$-free, the $(\text{bull},\text{dart})$-free, and the $(\text{dart},\text{gem})$-free chordal graphs. Except for the latter case, which follows from known results, our methods are based on structural decompositions of bull-free chordal graphs. In particular, the relevant $(\text{bull},\text{gem})$-free graphs decompose into chordal cographs attached to a tree, while the $(\text{bull},\text{dart})$-free case reduces to trees and powers of paths.
