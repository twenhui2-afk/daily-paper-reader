## Abstract
We investigate the joint distribution and temporal dependence of birth cohorts in a discrete-time age-dependent branching model in which an individual may reproduce at several integer ages. Under an independent age-specific reproduction array, we derive a recursive joint probability generating function, an exact history-dependent transition kernel, and a martingale innovation representation. These identities yield explicit covariance formulae and finite coefficient recursions for mixed factorial moments and mixed cumulants. When reproduction is restricted to a finite maximum age $L$, the cohort vector admits a finite-dimensional companion-matrix representation. In the primitive supercritical case, we establish an exact $L^p$ criterion: for every integer $p\geq2$, the Perron martingale and the normalized cohort state converge in $L^p$ if and only if all age-specific offspring distributions have finite $p$th moments. The normalized cohorts consequently share a common limit, which satisfies an age-indexed smoothing-transform equation with an explicit triangular cumulant recursion. Our principal asymptotic result concerns two cohorts whose separation increases with time. Under a simple-real-subdominant-root condition, we obtain a three-regime asymptotic equivalent for the mean-square distance between their mean-normalized sizes. The rate is governed by whether the squared modulus of the dominant non-Perron root is smaller than, equal to, or larger than the Perron root. This result quantifies the approach to the common cohort limit and distinguishes the present cohort-level question from first-order Perron convergence alone.
