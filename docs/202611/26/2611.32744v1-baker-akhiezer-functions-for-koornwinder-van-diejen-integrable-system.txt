Liudmila Bishler 

FIAN/TD-20/26 bishlerlv@lebedev.ru

Abstract 

The Koornwinder-van Diejen integrable system is a counterpart of the trigonometric Ruijsenaars inte-grable system (associated with the root system An) for the root system ( C∨ 

> n

, C n). It depends on the standard Macdonald parameters q and t as well as the Koornwinder (Askey-Wilson) parameters a, b, c, d. Different specializations of the parameters connect it to the other classical root systems from the ( C∨ 

> n

, C n) family. The eigenfunctions of the Koornwinder-van Diejen integrable system are the symmetric Koornwinder polynomials; for certain values of the parameters t, a, b, c, d they coincide with the sum over the Weyl group of the Baker-Akhiezer functions introduced by O.A. Chalykh for the classical root system BC n. The main idea behind this paper is to elucidate Chalykh’s formalism and apply it to other affine root systems. We define Baker-Akhiezer functions for all root systems in the ( C∨ 

> n

, C n) family. We also present some explicit expressions for Baker-Akhiezer functions. 

# 1 Introduction 

The Koornwinder-van Diejen integrable system consists of n commuting Hamiltonians, which were constructed by T. Koornwinder [1] and J.F. van Diejen [2]. It depends on the standard Macdonald parameters q and t as well as the parameters a, b, c, d and is symmetric under the permutations of a, b, c, d. It emerged when Koornwinder looked for a multi-variable analog of the Askey-Wilson polynomials [3] and generalized several Macdonald polynomials corresponding to the classical root systems [4, 5] (except for the root system An) into one construction which is called Koornwinder polynomial. He found the first Koornwinder Hamiltonian, which was later extended to the system of commuting Hamiltonians by van Diejen. Koornwinder polynomials are the Laurent polynomials in variables x1, . . . , x n, they are symmetric under the action of the hyperoctahedral group, so symmetric under the permutation of the variables as well as the inversions x → x−1. They also depend on the parameters q and t and a, b, c, d.The Koornwinder-van Diejen integrable system corresponds to the affine root system ( C∨ 

> n

, C n) (in Macdonald notation [6]) which we denote as C∨C for brevity. The specializations of the Koornwinder parameters allow to get the integrable systems associated with the other classical affine root systems: reduced Bn, Cn, Dn, B∨

He found the more elementary integrable system which consists of Cherednik operators. Their eigenfunctions are the non-symmetric Macdonald polynomials [9–11], which yield their symmetric counterparts upon the weighted symmetrization. The non-symmetric Koornwinder system is described by C∨C DAHA [12–18]. Within the framework of DAHA the non-symmetric Koornwinder polynomials can be constructed recursively with the analog of the Knop-Sahi recurrence [19, 20]. The Koornwinder integrable system is the counterpart [21] of the trigonometric Ruijsenaars integrable system [22,23] which corresponds to the root system An. It can be described by a vertical ray (0 , 1) of the Ding-Iohara-Miki (DIM) [24, 25] algebra and plays a profound role in the description of the integrable systems within DIM. It’s connected to DAHA of type An due to the correspondence of DIM and spherical DAHA [26]. Chalykh formulates his approach for finite reduced and irreducible root systems and non-reduced root system BC n. He studied the poles of the corresponding Hamiltonians and formulated the conditions on their eigenfunctions that cancel those poles out. He showed that these conditions are linear equations. The special place of the parameters (1) and (2) is also evident when we consider Macdonald and Koornwinder polynomials: at these parameters some coefficients in front of monomials go to zero and the initial polynomial splits into a sum of Chalykh’s Baker-Akhiezer functions with the permuted variables. In this paper we consider Chalykh’s approach for the sets of the parameters a, b, c, d corresponding to different affine root systems from Table 1. We go into the details on how Chalykh’s approach works for these affine root systems and calculate some BA functions. There are three components in Chalykh’s formalism: a base function, which consists of monomials corresponding to positive roots. A summation domain which defines the number of coefficients. And finally equations. They determine BA function uniquely up to a normalization coefficient. The paper is organized as follows. In section 2 we give an overview of the Koornwinder-van Diejen integrable system. There are two known types of eigenfunctions of this system: symmetric Koornwinder polynomials and Baker-Akhiezer functions. This paper is devoted to Baker-Akhiezer functions. And in section 3 we consider Chalykh’s definition of Baker-Akhiezer functions for finite reduced and irreducible root systems and non-reduced and irreducible root system BC n. In section 4 we show how Chalykh’s approach works for other affine root systems in C∨C family. We summarize different cases in Table 2 and provide a more detailed description of the rank one case in section 5. In section 6 we consider the first non-trivial case for root system Dn (D1 case is trivial) and present the BA function for root system D2. Section 7 is a conclusion.

O.A. Chalykh [30,32] constructed eigenfunctions for the Macdonald operators and the first Koornwinder Hamil-tonian for the special values of parameters. These eigenfunctions are enumerated not with Young diagrams (as Macdonald polynomials), but with a set of arbitrary complex numbers λ.The idea behind Chalykh’s formalism is to study poles of a corresponding Hamiltonian and to formulate the conditions on its eigenfunctions that cancel those poles out. The conditions are linear equations which with a right base function uniquely define eigenfunctions of corresponding Hamiltonians. First we describe Chalykh’s formalism for the root systems R = An, B n, C n, D n as well as for the exceptional root systems E6, E 7, E 8, F4 and G2. In this case the equations come from poles of the corresponding Macdonald operators. For the root system An these operators coincide with Ruijsenaars Hamiltonians. For detailed description of Chalykh’s approach in case of the root system An see [33]. Apart from reduced and irreducible root systems Chalykh also considers non-reduced finite root system 

BC n. In this case Chalykh’s formalism is built around the Koornwinder Hamiltonian (4). We consider it in the second part of this section. In contrast with Chalykh’s paper [30], we interchanged the space and the cospace in the definitions of base functions (10), (20) and periodicity conditions (12) and (22)-(31) so that the resulting BA functions would match the corresponding Macdonald and Koornwinder polynomials from Macdonald’s definition [4]. There are some explicit expressions in [34–36]. The other difference is that Chalykh’s equations are written for q2.

# 7 Conclusion 

The paper is devoted to Chalykh’s approach to the construction of the eigenfunctions of the Koornwinder Hamiltonian at the special values of parameters, i.e., when the parameters are negative integer powers of q.This case is connected with the affine root system C∨C as well as the other affine root systems in the C∨C

family. We explicitly list all the possible sets of parameters and other ingredients necessary to define the BA functions in Table 2. We also give an explicit formula for the BA function for root system D2 (107). We closely follow the approach developed for the Ruijsenaars integrable system corresponding to the root sys-tem An. This integrable system possesses a triad of eigenfunctions (the Macdonald triad): the Noumi-Shiraishi power series (the universal solution) and its two polynomial reductions, namely Macdonald polynomials and Baker–Akhiezer functions. Koornwinder polynomials are the counterparts of symmetric Macdonald polynomi-als, and in this paper we consider Baker-Akhiezer functions for the root systems in the C∨C family. Even though constructing the universal solution in the C∨C case seems too complex, triads of eigenfunctions may still exist for the root systems in the C∨C family. An important direction for future research is the construction of such analogs of the Macdonald triad, with the Baker-Akhiezer functions serving as one of their elements. 12 Acknowledgements
