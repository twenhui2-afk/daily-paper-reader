Abstract 

Temporal compression reduces the sequence length that motion generators must model, but can 

damage articulated detail and global trajectory. These errors have unequal consequences: root-velocity 

and heading errors propagate through trajectory recovery and affect the placement of the entire body. 

We propose PIAE, a physics-informed continuous autoencoder that allocates a fixed token budget between explicitly retained kinematic signals and complementary learned features. At 8 × temporal compression, each 256-dimensional token contains 224 learned values and a 32-dimensional physical carrier. The complete token conditions neural decoding, while a parallel recovery path constrains 

selected outputs. On the official HumanML3D benchmark, PIAE achieves 8.936 mm global MPJPE, an 

82.0% reduction relative to a same-width learned-only model. The benefit extends beyond trajectory 

restoration: facing-local neural-body error before analytical correction decreases by 76.6% in a frozen-

checkpoint validation diagnostic, and supplying the same true trajectory to both models preserves the body-quality advantage. Targeted root-only and explicit-root hybrid controls establish strong 

trajectory-aware comparisons; alternative carriers reveal that torso references favor position accuracy, 

whereas stance anchors favor support quality. With the original 8 × AEs frozen, matched text-to-motion 

generators predict every carrier value and improve FID from 0.506 to 0.163. Frozen reconstruction also transfers to AIST++ without adaptation. The compressed carrier interface reduces measured generation time by 27.8% relative to its 1 × counterpart. Together, these findings demonstrate that 

selective preservation can use a fixed representation budget more effectively, improving neural articulated 

reconstruction and downstream generation in compact human motion. Code: https://github.com/EviLooo/PIAE 

# 1 Introduction 

A reconstructed character can retain recognizable walking poses yet move along the wrong path. Motion 

errors have unequal consequences: a local joint-coordinate error perturbs articulation, whereas a persistent 

root-velocity or heading error can displace or rotate every subsequent pose. A compact motion autoencoder 

must preserve body detail and global movement despite this unequal sensitivity. Compression matters beyond storage: reducing the temporal sequence also reduces the number of positions and attention 

interactions a downstream generator must model. A useful representation must deliver those savings while 

retaining information that is important for reconstruction and generation. We investigate a simple allocation question: under the same total representation budget, can explicitly 

preserving selected kinematic information improve the remaining neural reconstruction and the usefulness of the representation for generation? Rather than asking the learned bottleneck to approximate every quantity, 

we reserve capacity for signals that define the trajectory or provide shared spatial and support references, 

and learn complementary motion features. The explicit values replace learned dimensions. Any benefit 

must therefore justify their use of scarce capacity, rather than follow from increasing the representation size. Learned motion latents support text-conditioned synthesis in MLD and MotionGPT [ 4, 7], while ParTY 

and PRISM study structured tokenization and latent organization [ 10 , 11 ]. ARDY combines explicit global root information with a learned body code and root-conditioned reconstruction for interactive

We introduce PIAE, a physics-informed continuous autoencoder with an explicit in-token carrier . At 

8× temporal compression, an eight-frame block becomes one 256-dimensional token: 224 learned values and 32 carrier values retaining planar-root and yaw velocities, height summaries, and soft support cues. The full token is mixed and processed by the neural decoder, so the carrier can inform articulated reconstruction as 

well as trajectory recovery. A parallel path reads the carrier to restore velocities and constrain selected 

height/contact outputs. Both paths participate during AE training, before the reconstruction losses are 

calculated. During text-to-motion generation, the generator predicts the entire token from text and noise, 

including all carrier values. 

Three findings connect the design to its practical value. First, the improvement reaches the neural 

body pathway: at 8 ×, facing-local body error falls from 40.011 to 9.352 mm before analytical correction. 

Giving both frozen models the same true trajectory still leaves a large body-quality gap. Second, the representation is useful when its explicit information must be generated rather than extracted from an 

input: a matched 8 × generator improves FID from 0.506 to 0.163. Third, the original frozen AE retains a 

reconstruction advantage on AIST++ without fine-tuning or normalization refitting. Together, these tests 

establish neural reconstruction, downstream-task use, and cross-dataset transfer as distinct benefits of the 

implemented representation. 

The controlled studies also explain how to interpret those benefits. Root/yaw retention provides a 

strong foundation, and the full PIAE design improves on both the root/yaw-only and adapted explicit-root 

controls. In a separate same-width study, torso references give the best positional accuracy, while stance 

anchors best improve the measured support behavior. Exact-height replacement is less effective than the 

original height/support design. These outcomes show why preservation must be considered together with 

the learned capacity it replaces and the recovery rule it supports. The central takeaway is a division of 

representational roles: selected explicit information provides reliable references, while the neural pathway 

reconstructs complementary detail. Our contributions are: • Physically-informed latent tokens (the carrier). We develop a fixed-width continuous repre-

sentation with complementary explicit and learned components. The same carrier informs neural 

decoding and structured recovery; at 8 ×, its 32 values count within the 256-value token budget. • Improved neural reconstruction beyond trajectory recovery. Same-budget ablations and a frozen validation diagnostic establish a 76.6% reduction in neural facing-local body error at 8 ×

before correction. A common-trajectory comparison confirms that post-hoc path repair alone does 

not reproduce the carrier model’s body quality. • Outcome-dependent preservation under a shared budget. Root/yaw-only, adapted explicit-

root, and alternative-carrier studies examine the allocation-and-recovery design. Torso references 

improve positional accuracy most, whereas stance anchors best improve support metrics, identifying 

distinct uses for explicitly retained information. • Generative utility, transfer, and measured efficiency. With the original AEs frozen, predicting 

the complete 8 × carrier token improves matched text-to-motion FID by 67.7%. Frozen AIST++ reconstruction demonstrates cross-dataset transfer, while the compressed carrier interface reduces 

measured generation time by 27.8% relative to its 1 × counterpart. 

# 2 Related Work 

Learned motion representations. Continuous motion representations include Transformer variational 

autoencoders and the VAE used for latent diffusion in MLD [ 3, 4 ]. Discrete systems apply vector 

quantization [ 5]: T2M-GPT and MotionGPT model tokens autoregressively, while MoMask and MMM use 

masked modeling [ 6, 7, 8, 9]. These methods establish useful interfaces for generation but make different 

choices about temporal rate, code organization, and learning objectives. PIAE instead studies deterministic 

continuous autoencoding with a specified division between learned and explicitly retained values. 

2Explicit-root and structured hybrids. ARDY patchifies global root position and heading, combines 

them with a learned body code, and conditions body reconstruction on root information for interactive generation [ 12 ]. PIAE uses per-frame planar/yaw velocities plus height and support summaries within 

a 256-value, eight-frame token, combining neural conditioning with compatible analytical recovery. The 

resulting design supports exact canonical planar-trajectory recovery and provides a setting for studying how 

the allocation affects neural body quality, generated tokens, and frozen transfer. We evaluate an adapted 

ARDY-style root–body autoencoder under the common budget; its implementation and comparison scope 

are specified in Section 4.2. 

ParTY studies part guidance and temporal-aware tokenization, while PRISM decomposes its latent by joint structure [ 10 , 11 ]. Their factorizations differ from PIAE’s explicit/learned split, but share the 

motivation to organize a representation according to the structure of motion. Our experiments distinguish 

common-protocol reconstruction reruns from published reference values, and count all learned and carrier 

values in each continuous token. 

Kinematic constraints and recovery. HumanML3D exposes local pose, root-motion, and contact-related information [ 1]. PIAE uses their physical meaning to select token contents and reconstruction 

rules. Here, physics-informed refers to kinematic and geometric structure and reconstruction objectives, not 

rigid-body simulation or a complete dynamical model. The frozen body-path diagnostic specifically tests 

whether these explicit signals are associated with better neural articulated output in addition to structural 

path retention. 

Downstream generation and transfer. We adapt MotionHiFlow’s hierarchical temporal generator and use its official evaluator [ 13 ], keeping the generative architecture fixed across the four original AE interfaces 

to test their downstream utility. AIST++ provides music-aligned SMPL dance motions [ 14 ]; its official 

pose-test assets supply a separate frozen reconstruction test with unchanged HumanML3D normalization. 

# 3 Method 

PIAE assigns different roles within one fixed token. Root/yaw velocities are retained because integration 

can propagate their errors through the global motion; a learned code supplies articulated detail, conditioned on those values and partial height/support information. Both parts occupy the same budget. The analytical 

path guarantees recovery only of the quantities it explicitly stores and constrains, not of the complete 

motion. At generation time the token must instead be predicted, so input-trajectory preservation is not a 

ground-truth trajectory guarantee for text-to-motion.

# 3.4 Dual-path reconstruction and trajectory recovery 

The neural pathway consumes [ ℓk; ck] jointly. The analytical pathway expands ck into frame-level velocities, 

a height base, and four support priors. It overwrites the predicted planar and angular velocities with the 

stored values. Root height combines a base that preserves the encoded endpoints and mean with a learned 

residual projected to have zero endpoints and zero block mean. The carrier’s height standard deviation 

conditions the neural pathway but is not enforced analytically. Support probabilities provide an additive 

logit prior to the contact head.

# 3.5 Selective preservation under a fixed budget 

More generally, a continuous token can be written as 

where S selects the signals to retain and dc specifies their storage allocation. The selection is an architectural 

design choice; d c is a capacity hyperparameter. At fixed total width D , a larger carrier leaves fewer

Our primary choice D = 256, dc = 32 prioritizes trajectory-defining velocities, supplemented by vertical 

and support summaries. This reflects the different consequences of error: velocity errors accumulate through 

recovery, whereas many local-pose details can be reconstructed directly. The design does not require 

encoding every physical property. More generally, spatial reference signals may have high reconstruction 

impact without being temporally integrated. We test this distinction with torso and support anchors in Section 5.4 . Their extraction and compatible correction rules are specified in Appendix A.3 ; every 

additional carrier dimension displaces a learned dimension rather than increasing total token width. 

The experiments specify S and dc as architectural choices. Each preserved signal has a defined extractor, 

numerical scale, and compatible recovery rule, and every stored value counts toward D. This formulation 

links allocation to use: the complete representation should support accurate neural body reconstruction, 

and a downstream generator must predict both explicit and learned values. We evaluate these properties 

through the body-path diagnostic, alternative-carrier study, and matched generated-token experiments. 

# 3.6 Physics-informed reconstruction objective 

Training combines native-feature reconstruction, global-joint reconstruction, temporal matching, and 

skeletal/contact constraints. Let MSE M denote a component-wise squared-error mean over valid masked

The interval masks require all contributing frames to be valid. These terms match target dynamics rather 

than penalizing acceleration simply for being large. Bone-length error uses an absolute difference. Contact

# 3.7 Compression accounting 

PIAE reduces the number of temporal positions by 8 ×: a 192-frame motion becomes 24 tokens. It stores 

256 /8 = 32 continuous scalars per frame on average. The FP32 latent payload is 24 · 256 · 4 = 24 ,576 bytes 

5(24 KiB) per 192-frame clip, versus 192 · 256 · 4 = 196 ,608 bytes (192 KiB) for the 1 × reference. Relative to 

the 70-channel native representation, the scalar-count ratio is 

Relative to raw 66-channel joint coordinates, the corresponding ratio is 2 .0625 ×. The 8 × storage reduction 

above is specifically relative to our same-width 1 × latent; the native-to-latent reduction is 2 .1875 × at 

equal FP32 precision. These are representation-dependent scalar counts, and a continuous token cannot be 

equated in bitrate with a discrete codebook index without specifying precision and coding. Every carrier 

value is included in the reported count. 

For a full self-attention layer operating on the motion latents, the pairwise score matrix has L2 entries. 

Holding token width fixed while changing the temporal rate from 1 × to 8 × changes this count from T 2 to 

(T/ 8) 2 = T 2/64. For example, a 64-frame motion changes from 4 ,096 possible motion-token pairs to 64. 

This concerns the attention matrix of a comparable layer on the compressed sequence, not the FLOPs or 

memory of the complete system: PIAE’s encoder and decoder still process higher-resolution stages, and 

other generator operations have different costs. We therefore measure end-to-end inference separately. 

# 4 Experimental Protocol 

# 4.1 HumanML3D data and evaluation population 

We use HumanML3D’s official splits and 22-joint motion at 20 FPS [ 1]. The dataset draws on motion-

capture sources including AMASS [ 2]. Training uses only the official training split, and checkpoint selection uses validation MPJPE. The reported autoencoders are exponential-moving-average checkpoints trained for 

up to 300 epochs. 

The official test split has 4,384 identifiers. The shared reconstruction contract requires at least 40 

frames, excluding 174 shorter clips and leaving 4,210 eligible clips. Every model receives the same longest 

centered crop divisible by eight, capped at 192 frames. All 4,210 clips are evaluated with zero skips and 

without padding short motions into eligibility. 

# 4.2 Controlled temporal-rate and carrier variants 

We train four models from scratch under the same optimization and split contract. The 8 × models produce 

one 256-dimensional token per eight input frames, or 32 latent scalars per motion frame. With the carrier 

enabled, that token contains 224 learned and 32 physical dimensions; without it, all 256 dimensions are 

learned. The 1 × references produce one 256-dimensional token per frame. Their carrier split is 248 learned 

plus 8 physical dimensions, because a single frame needs two planar-root velocities, one yaw velocity, one 

height value, and four support values. Thus the carrier/no-carrier comparison at a fixed rate has equal 

width and storage, whereas 1 × versus 8 × is deliberately a compression comparison: the 1 × representation 

stores eight times as many latent scalars. 

We additionally train two targeted 8 × autoencoders from scratch. The root/yaw-only variant retains 

all eight planar-root and yaw velocities (24 physical plus 232 learned values per token), leaving height and contact to the learned decoder. The ARDY-style variant retains each frame’s canonical global root 

xyz and heading sine/cosine (40 explicit plus 216 learned values), with a root-conditioned body decoder. 

This is our adaptation of ARDY’s root–body representation to the HumanML3D pipeline, not its released 

tokenizer or complete generator. All three 8 × explicit variants have the same total width, rate, dataset, crop, normalization, optimization schedule, seed, and loss weights. Both new runs complete 300 epochs; 

EMA checkpoints are chosen by official validation MPJPE before the test evaluation. The same 4,210 test 

clips, coordinate frame, and reconstruction metrics are then used with zero skips. These controls compare 

complete allocation-and-reconstruction designs under a common budget. 

# 4.3 Frozen neural-body diagnostic 

Using the four primary validation-selected EMA checkpoints without any weight update, we observe the 

neural 66-channel pose head before carrier constraints. All 256 token values still enter the decoder. We evaluate the same 1,400 eligible official validation clips, with the standard 40–192-frame centered crops 

6and zero skips. Facing-local body MPJPE averages Euclidean errors over the 21 non-pelvis joints before 

global root translation and heading are applied. It therefore measures neural articulated reconstruction, not 

global-path accuracy or the usual root-relative metric, which retains heading error. In these four primary 

models, analytical recovery does not change the local body coordinates; this equality is checked directly. 

As a separate diagnostic, we recover global joints after giving every model the same ground-truth root 

translation, heading, and height. These common-trajectory scores are oracle controls for comparing body 

outputs, not deployable reconstruction results. The diagnostic uses each checkpoint’s exact training-matched 

runtime and normalization; it neither removes carrier values from decoder input nor selects a new model. 

# 4.4 Matched alternative-carrier reconstruction study 

We train five additional 8 × autoencoders from scratch: a fresh 224-learned/32-explicit primary-design 

baseline, an exact-height replacement with the same split, and torso, stance-anchor, and effector variants 

with 208 learned plus 48 explicit values. All retain the 24 root/yaw values and a total width of 256. The 

latter three retain the original carrier and add 16 motion-derived values; exact-height instead replaces the 

original height/support summaries with eight framewise heights. Extraction and correction rules are given 

in Appendix A.3. 

These five runs share the official data, training-only normalization, loss weights, optimization schedule, 

seed 42, and 300-epoch duration. Shared initialization is copied with carrier-column meanings preserved 

when the learned width changes. Each EMA checkpoint is selected solely by validation global MPJPE, then 

evaluated on the same 4,210 eligible official test clips with zero skips. Because this is a separate matched 

training study, percentage changes use its fresh baseline, not the independent 8.936-mm primary result. 

No new text-to-motion or AIST++ runs are performed for these variants; comparisons concern complete 

allocation-and-recovery designs rather than an isolated signal or carrier-size effect. 

# 4.5 Released reconstruction baselines and common coordinates 

MotionGPT uses its released HumanML3D VQ-VAE at 4 × temporal downsampling [ 7]. MLD-1 uses its 

released HumanML3D VAE with one global latent slot [ 4]. A 192-frame crop produces 24 PIAE tokens, 

48 MotionGPT discrete positions, and one MLD-1 global continuous slot. We retain each model’s native 

representation and token budget, then convert reconstructed joints to metres in a common canonical 

HumanML3D frame. 

A target-derived coordinate transform is shared by target and prediction. No prediction-specific root, 

heading, rigid, or Procrustes alignment is applied. Independent target recovery must agree within 5 × 10 −6

m mean and 2 × 10 −5 m maximum error. This numerical check validates coordinate conversion and never 

adjusts predictions. 

# 4.6 Reconstruction metrics and aggregation 

Global MPJPE is the mean Euclidean joint-position error. Root-relative MPJPE subtracts each motion’s 

own root translation; it does not remove heading error. Velocity and acceleration errors compare finite 

differences at 20 FPS. Contact-foot speed measures reconstructed foot movement during target contact. 

Penetration is the fraction of evaluated joints more than 5 mm below the ground plane. The terminal root 

error is the Euclidean distance between reconstructed and target root positions in all three coordinates at 

the final frame; it includes height error even when the planar path is exact. 

Micro aggregation pools valid elements, so longer crops contribute more joint or temporal samples and 

contact speed is weighted by contact samples. Macro aggregation first computes a metric per clip and then 

weights clips equally. Lower values are better for every reconstruction metric. 

# 4.7 Matched downstream text-to-motion protocol 

We freeze each of the four autoencoders and train a matched hierarchical-flow generator based on Motion-

HiFlow’s temporal architecture [ 13 ]. All generators use a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 text encoder, 256-channel 

input and output, a 384-wide nine-block transformer, effective batch size 64, the same initialization and 

pretrained backbone, and a 400k-step schedule. Every latent channel is predicted from text and noise, including carrier channels; no ground-truth motion or carrier is available at generation time. Latent 

normalization is fitted separately for each frozen autoencoder using training data only.

# 4.8 Cross-dataset transfer protocol 

To test whether the reconstruction effect transfers beyond HumanML3D, we evaluate the four frozen 

autoencoders on the official AIST++ v1.0 pose-test motions [ 14 ]. We use the 137 released fitted motions in the official pose-test intersection, recover the documented SMPL translation, standardize to the HumanML3D 

22-joint skeleton, and subsample exactly from 60 to 20 FPS. HumanML3D training normalization remains 

frozen: no AIST++ sample is used for training, fine-tuning, checkpoint selection, or normalization fitting. 

The reconstruction crop and metrics are otherwise unchanged. 

# 4.9 Optimization 

Autoencoder training uses 300 epochs, batch size 128, AdamW with learning rate 10 −4 and weight decay 

10 −4, five warm-up epochs, gradient clipping at 1.0, EMA decay 0.999, bfloat16 precision, and seed 42. Training-only normalization uses 10,000 sampled training windows. The architecture and complete loss 

configuration are given in Appendix A.

# 5 Results 

The experiments connect four findings: selective preservation improves reconstruction at a fixed budget; 

the improvement reaches the neural body before correction; different explicit references favor different 

reconstruction outcomes; and the compressed representation supports generation and frozen transfer. The 

primary and targeted controls share the official test cohort. The body diagnostic uses validation clips, and 

the alternative-carrier study uses its own fresh matched baseline. 

# 5.1 Selective preservation improves fixed-budget reconstruction 

We first test whether explicit information earns its share of the token budget. In Table 2, the 8 × carrier and 

no-carrier models both store 256 values per eight frames. The carrier reduces global MPJPE from 49.609 

to 8.936 mm (82.0%) and root-relative error by 77.1%, with lower velocity, acceleration, contact-foot speed, 

and penetration errors. Terminal 3D root-position error falls from 48.135 mm to 9 .24 × 10 −5 mm. The 

design therefore improves articulated and temporal fidelity alongside its structural trajectory preservation.

The benefit also appears without temporal compression: at 1 ×, adding the carrier reduces global 

MPJPE from 45.961 to 5.641 mm. Moving from 1 × to 8 × with the carrier then uses 87.5% fewer latent 

scalars at a 3.295-mm increase in global error. Thus, under the matched backbone and training recipe, 

increasing the number of fully learned temporal tokens does not substitute for the explicit structure: the 

compact carrier model reconstructs more accurately than the larger 1 × learned-only reference. 

# 5.2 Trajectory-aware controls clarify the allocation benefit 

Table 3 compares PIAE with two strong trajectory-aware representations. The separately trained root/yaw-

only control reaches 10.765 mm, recovering approximately 95.5% of the absolute no-carrier-to-full-carrier 

error reduction. This ratio describes the complete trained root-aware design, including its body prediction. 

The full 32-dimensional carrier reaches 8.936 mm, a further 17.0% improvement, showing the value of the 

combined height/support allocation and recovery beyond root/yaw alone. The root/yaw-only control has 

an exact planar path but retains 3.494 mm terminal 3D root-position error because height remains learned. 

Its lower penetration also identifies a trade-off between the two designs. The adapted ARDY-style explicit-global-root, root-conditioned-body control obtains 9.717 mm global 

MPJPE. Full PIAE reaches 8.936 mm, a 0.782-mm (8.0%) reduction, with the same 256-value token budget 

and common training/evaluation contract. Its root-relative error is also lower (8.884 versus 9.717 mm), as 

is velocity error (62.678 versus 67.702 mm/s). Both designs preserve the global endpoint to floating-point 

precision. PIAE’s advantage therefore concerns articulated and temporal reconstruction beyond endpoint 

preservation, while using 32 rather than 40 explicit values per token.

# 6 Discussion 

The value lies in how the budget is allocated. The same 256-value token can support substantially 

different reconstruction and generation quality depending on what its values represent. PIAE reserves a 

small fraction of that budget for reliable kinematic references and uses the remaining capacity for learned 

features. The improvement therefore reflects the organization of the representation and its reconstruction 

paths, rather than an increase in stored information. The 1 × learned-only model’s larger temporal payload 

also does not recover the 8 × carrier model’s reconstruction quality under the matched recipe. These 

results identify selective preservation as an effective alternative to assigning all available dimensions to an 

unconstrained learned code. 

A reliable reference accompanies a better neural body prediction. Trajectory recovery and 

neural reconstruction play complementary roles. Root/yaw-only preservation already produces a strong 

reconstruction baseline, and the full carrier further improves articulated accuracy. Crucially, the frozen 

body-path diagnostic observes the neural prediction itself: body error is 76.6% lower at 8 × before correction, 

and giving both models the same true translation, heading, and height retains the advantage. Thus the 

full model does more than place a similar-quality body on the right path; it produces a more accurate 

articulated body. A plausible explanation is that accurate reference information both conditions the decoder 

and prevents trajectory mistakes from contaminating post-recovery global reconstruction errors during 

training. This explains the design intuition behind learning complementary detail around reliable explicit 

references; the diagnostic establishes the body-quality result, while the mechanisms remain a subject for 

further study. 

What to preserve depends on what the reference supports. Root/yaw velocities are important 

because their integration controls global placement over time. Torso and stance information serve a different 

role: they provide shared spatial references for connected upper-body joints or supported feet. Their different outcomes are informative. Torso produces the lowest positional error in the follow-up study, 

while stance anchors give the best contact-foot speed and penetration. The exact-height variant replaces 

13 summarized height, support information, and learned within-block height detail with framewise height 

storage, and performs less accurately overall. The resulting design lesson is to choose preserved information 

together with its neural conditioning, recovery rule, and displaced learned capacity. Greater explicit 

precision on one quantity is not a substitute for a useful division of roles across the complete motion. 

A generative interface must organize predictable information. In reconstruction, explicit values 

are extracted from the input. In text-to-motion, the generator must instead learn their joint distribution 

with the body features. The 8 × FID improvement therefore demonstrates generative usability of the 

complete structured token, with identical token counts and widths in the carrier/no-carrier comparison. The different ranking at 1 × further shows that the best reconstruction interface need not be the best generative 

interface. Redundancy, carrier–pose agreement, and generator optimization are candidate explanations for 

this rate-dependent behavior. The empirical lesson is to assess the representation at its intended temporal 

rate and downstream objective, rather than infer generation quality from reconstruction alone. 

Transfer and efficiency make the representation practically useful. The AIST++ evaluation 

applies the original checkpoints and HumanML3D statistics unchanged to dance motions, demonstrating 

frozen reconstruction transfer to another motion dataset. Separately, the shorter 8 × carrier sequence 

improves generation throughput and peak memory relative to its 1 × counterpart. These are complementary 

advantages: transfer tests whether the representation remains useful on new motions, while efficiency measures the practical benefit of reducing the generator’s temporal interface. Table 10 summarizes the 

evidence supporting these conclusions.

# 6.1 Limitations and future work 

The experiments establish the effect of the complete carrier-conditioned representation under a matched 

backbone and training recipe. Carrier signals and their allocations are manually specified; the study does 

not establish an optimal or automatic selection rule. The body diagnostic retains the carrier as decoder 

input; it does not isolate the learned 224-dimensional subcode or separate conditioning from changes in 

the training-loss pathway. Alternative-carrier corrections also act on body joints, so that study compares 

complete allocation-and-recovery designs. Its additional variants have not been evaluated in T2M or AIST++, and the AIST++ study has no separate pre-correction body diagnostic. These distinctions 

delimit which component-level explanations follow from each experiment. 

The reconstruction studies use one training seed and crops capped at 192 frames; evaluator replications 

quantify sampling variation rather than independent training variation. The learned-only control is a matched carrier-removal ablation, not an independently optimized family of fully learned AEs. Its 1 ×

result does not identify the cause of that model’s remaining reconstruction error. The ARDY-style control 

evaluates an adapted autoencoder, while the published reference rows retain their original protocols. Continuous scalar counts specify equal-precision payload rather than equal entropy-coded bitrate with 

discrete tokenizers. PIAE is kinematic: exact recovery of input-derived trajectory values does not guarantee 

physically consistent generated motion, and lower FID is not a dynamics test. The generation study uses 

one matched generator family on HumanML3D; AIST++ tests reconstruction transfer, not cross-dataset 

generation. 

14 Future work can investigate independent training-seed variation, separate the effects of neural condi-

tioning and structured recovery, and extend predicted-carrier consistency to physical generation objectives. 

The fixed-budget formulation also motivates task-dependent signal and capacity selection, and applications 

in other temporal domains with explicit motion or reference information. The present work provides the 

implemented motion design and experimental foundation for those extensions. 

# 7 Conclusion 

A compact representation can benefit from deciding what to preserve explicitly and what to learn. PIAE 

implements this division within a single fixed-width motion token: selected kinematics provide reliable references, learned features encode complementary detail, and both inform neural decoding alongside 

structured recovery. At 8 × temporal compression, this design improves global reconstruction and neural 

body prediction within the same 256-value budget. The body-quality advantage persists before analytical 

correction and when both models receive the same true trajectory, establishing a benefit beyond path 

replacement alone. 

The representation also supports use beyond encoding its training motions. Matched text-to-motion 

generation improves when the entire compressed token, including its carrier, must be predicted; the original frozen AE transfers its reconstruction advantage to AIST++ without adaptation. Alternative carriers show 

that positional accuracy and support quality favor different explicit references, while the rate-dependent 

generation results distinguish reconstruction fidelity from generative utility. Measured downstream time 

and memory savings demonstrate the practical value of the shorter temporal interface. 

The resulting takeaway is to allocate limited representation capacity according to the role information 

plays in reconstruction and downstream modeling, rather than assume that every value must be learned 

through the bottleneck. PIAE demonstrates this approach through a concrete carrier design, controlled 

motion experiments, and complementary evidence from neural-body reconstruction, generated tokens, and 

frozen cross-dataset transfer.

# A.1 Carrier normalization and constrained decoding 

The 16 planar-velocity values are divided by 2, the eight angular-velocity values by 4, and the four height 

statistics by 2. The four support probabilities are unchanged. These fixed scales are inverted before 

analytical expansion; they do not quantize the values. Native motion channels separately use training-set means and standard deviations. 

For an eight-frame block, height expansion starts with linear interpolation between the stored endpoints. 

An equal correction is added to the six interior values so that the resulting block mean equals the stored 

mean. In normalized native coordinates, the decoder adds 0 .25 Π( u), where u is its predicted height residual and Π sets both endpoints to zero and subtracts the interior mean. This preserves endpoints and mean while 

allowing learned interior variation. The stored height standard deviation has no separate hard constraint. 

For foot j , soft input support is σ((0 .04 − Pt,j,z )/0.01) σ((0 .12 − st,j )/0.03), where st,j is foot speed in 

m/s and heights are in metres. Carrier expansion repeats the four block-average support values over the 

block and clips them to [0 .02 , 0.98]. Their logits are added to the predicted contact logits with weight 1.0. 

Contact output remains learned; this operation is not an inverse reconstruction of the original per-frame 

contacts.

# A.3 Alternative-carrier extraction and recovery 

The follow-up study leaves the primary model unchanged and implements four alternatives. All signals are 

computed from the same unnormalized native input; no external observations or target contact annotations 

enter the carrier. All variants concatenate explicit and learned values before neural decoding and retain 

framewise root/yaw velocities. 

Exact-height replacement (224 learned + 32 explicit). The final eight explicit values store the eight framewise root heights instead of the original four height statistics and four support summaries. Heights are scaled by 1 /2 for storage and recovered exactly. The learned height residual and additive 

17 contact-logit prior are disabled. This is therefore a replacement of both information and recovery rules, not 

a height-only ablation. 

Torso XY (208 learned + 48 explicit). Sixteen extra values store the facing-local planar coordinates 

of the upper-trunk joint (SPINE3) at all eight frames. The difference between that anchor and the predicted 

SPINE3 position is applied with weights 1 /3, 2 /3, and 1 to SPINE1, SPINE2, and SPINE3, respectively. 

The same unit-weight correction is applied to neck, head, collars, shoulders, elbows, and wrists. Vertical 

coordinates are unchanged. Thus a torso anchor affects a set of connected upper-body coordinates rather 

than only replacing one joint. 

Stance anchors (208 learned + 48 explicit). For each foot and each four-frame half-block, the carrier stores the XYZ position at the frame of maximum motion-derived soft support, together with 

that confidence. Two feet, two anchors, and four values per anchor require 16 dimensions. Anchor XY is 

expressed in the block’s initial root frame; Z remains a ground-referenced height. Anchors with confidence 

below 0.1 are set to zero. At decoding, the appropriate half-block anchor is transformed back to the current 

facing-local frame. Its displacement from the predicted foot is norm-clipped to 0.25 m, then multiplied by 

0.5 times stored confidence times predicted foot-contact probability. This bounded correction is added to 

the foot and half of it to the corresponding ankle. The rule provides a soft support reference, not a hard 

foot lock or a guarantee for generated carrier values. 

Effector XY (208 learned + 48 explicit). Sixteen extra values store the first- and last-frame facing-

local XY coordinates of both wrists and both feet. The decoder adds the linearly interpolated endpoint 

discrepancy to those four joints over the eight-frame block. This preserves the stored planar endpoints 

while leaving interior detail and vertical motion learned.
