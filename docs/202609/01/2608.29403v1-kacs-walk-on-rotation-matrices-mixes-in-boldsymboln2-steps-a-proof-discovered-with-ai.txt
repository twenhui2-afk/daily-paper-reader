Abstract 

Let N = (n

> 2

) = dim SO( n). We prove that the coordinate-plane Kac walk on SO( n) has total-variation mixing time of order N : for every fixed 0 < ε < 1, 

t(n)mix (ε) = Θ ε(n2).

The lower bound is the dimensional singularity obstruction before N steps. The upper bound removes the final logarithm from the previously known O(n2 log n) estimate. The proof combines the discrete Malliavin coupling and low-degree pseudo-mixing inputs with a new log-free analysis of the derivative shells. Its static core is a circuit-anchored, arbitrary-spectrum root/pass identity for the physical five-box prime. Keeping one normalization base per original circuit permits simultaneous scalar regluing without paying for artificial cuts. Its temporal core is an exact chronological calculus: passive singleton runs acquire a coboundary/Riesz gain, while root-interrupted components are allocated by vertex-labelled packets before absolute values are taken. The curvature split into pure-Weyl and Ricci parts is kept at its physical tensor type. All-Weyl packets retain a full N −1 resource; mixed packets contain a typed O(n−1/2) Ricci debit; and the final packetless Ricci cell is closed by a joint invariant-column estimate on its two root-hit circuits and an exact causal restoration of the marked root time. These estimates yield an O(n) squared first-derivative shell and a summable all-order marked-shell expansion through logarithmic degree. The resulting score energy is O(n/c 2) after cN steps. A weighted submersion integration-by-parts argument and the Haar log-Sobolev inequality then give the uniform total-variation upper bound. No cutoff profile or cutoff window is asserted. The proof was discovered through an approximately three-day, author-directed collaboration with OpenAI Codex using GPT-5.6 Sol, followed by approximately one day of targeted defect repair and audit. The author has reviewed the statements, cited sources, global proof architecture, and principal reductions, but a complete line-by-line human audit of several technical derivations remains in progress. This version should therefore be regarded as a preliminary proof report.

1.2 Historical context 

Kac introduced the coordinate-rotation process on the sphere as a kinetic model for the Boltzmann equation [Kac56]. Hastings subsequently used the matrix version as a sampler for random orthogonal matrices [Has70]. The two settings share their elementary rotation, but their mixing geometry is different: the sphere has dimension n − 1, whereas SO( n) has dimension N ≍ n2, and a point mass for the group walk remains singular for at least N steps. The spectral theory is mature. Diaconis and Saloff-Coste initiated the quantitative analysis of Kac’s master equation and the associated group walk [DS00]. Janvresse obtained the correct spectral-gap scale [Jan01]; Carlen, Carvalho, and Loss identified the gap exactly [CCL03]; and Maslen computed the spectrum [Mas03]. These results determine relaxation, but do not by themselves control total variation from a singular starting law. That distinction is decisive here: an L2 argument cannot begin before a density has formed, and the creation and conditioning of that density are the difficult part of the group problem. Several complementary approaches clarified the scale. Pak and Sidenko developed compact-group couplings and obtained polynomial weak-mixing bounds [PS07]. Oliveira proved O(n2 log n)convergence in quadratic Wasserstein distance [Oli09]. Jiang gave the first polynomial total-variation estimate for the group walk [Jia17]. Pillai and Smith then introduced a non-Markovian coupling for the singular-to-smooth transition and bounded the group mixing time between order n2 and n4 log n

[PS16]. On the sphere, the analogous coupling yields the sharp order n log n [PS17], improving Jiang’s earlier sphere bound [Jia12]. The sphere logarithm reflects a different coordinate-exposure mechanism and does not force a logarithm on the N -dimensional group walk. Two nearby but more symmetric walks are also instructive. Rosenthal’s fixed-angle random-rotation walk and the uniform-plane walk are conjugation-invariant; character theory yields sharp mixing and cutoff statements in that setting [Ros94; HJ17]. The coordinate-plane Kac walk studied here is not conjugation-invariant, so those Fourier reductions are unavailable. This is why the proof 3works with moving frames, low-degree Haar moments, and the full chronological incidence of selected coordinate planes. The immediate starting point is the 2026 result of Pillai and Smith, which proves the O(n2 log n)total-variation upper bound through a two-stage coupling and a discrete Malliavin analysis [PS26a]. Pillai, Smith, and Vaikuntanathan provide the quantitative low-degree pseudo-mixing estimate used to establish the covariance band after burn-in [PSV26]. Their transvection-walk work gives a useful parallel local-to-global entropy architecture, but it is not an input to this proof [PS26b]. The present paper keeps the first two inputs and replaces the logarithmic derivative-shell estimate by the static and temporal calculus developed below. 

1.3 What removes the logarithm 

At time m ≍ N , the Malliavin covariance is already well conditioned with high probability. The remaining loss in the previous argument came from bounding high-order derivatives after exposing their time order and their tensor contractions too early. A naive absolute value can pay once for every artificial cut, every chronological order, or every appearance of a common coordinate plane; any one of these payments is fatal when the expansion runs to degree L log n.The repair has two inseparable parts. First, the static contraction is based on original circuits rather than on local bends. The physical five-box prime is expanded by an exact root/pass identity while all ten gap variables and the incoming token remain in place. One base is then charged per original circuit, regardless of how many times it was cut for the proof. Second, chronology is summed only after same-time packets and root incidences have been fused. Passive runs gain through an exact coboundary/Riesz identity; mixed Weyl–Ricci packets retain a physical n−1/2

debit; and the unique low-debit packetless Ricci cell is closed on a joint invariant column and then by a summable causal root-time kernel before trace closure. 

The proof never estimates a temporary port as an independent output. Every spatial cut, chronological gap, and selected-plane incidence is returned to one terminal scalar, with its original circuit normalization and packet resource still attached. 

This “intact sum” principle is the quantitative reason the degree summation is exponential in the derivative order rather than factorial, and hence why the L log n truncation is compatible with 

O(N ) time. 

1.4 Detailed proof architecture 

The proof can be read as the following sequence. 1. Coefficient geometry. Decompose every centered Kac coefficient into its pure-Weyl and traceless-Ricci pieces, keeping the normalizations of Λ 2Rn and the probabilistic Kac frame explicit. 2. Circuit-anchored root/pass calculus. theorem 2.4 proves the physical root and pass expansions for the five-box prime in all twenty signed rail states. lemma 2.7 then closes all declared ports with one trace-class base per original circuit. 3. All-order packets. The module-stability and smoothing arguments turn connected coefficient components into positive-credit packets. The scalar packet/ribbon estimate, theorem 2.24, keeps a full N −1 resource at every marked pure-Weyl packet. 44. Pseudo-Haar covariance band. Orthogonal Weingarten calculus [CŚ06; CM09] and quantitative pseudo-mixing control all moments through degree L log n. Matrix martingale concentration [Tro11] then places the Malliavin covariance in a fixed deterministic band after O(N ) burn-in. 5. Chronological renewal. The Kac spectral blocks and exact Haar–residual decomposition turn every finite Green gap into a bounded gate. The singleton coboundary identity supplies a gain on alternate pass vertices, strong enough to absorb all strict chronological orders. 6. Root and Ricci allocation. A vertex-labelled fixed-column presentation allocates every unresolved component without duplicating a packet endpoint or circuit base. Pure-Weyl components close by global renewal. Aggregate-Ricci components split into packet-bearing, scalar dimer, internal-hard, threshold, and one final low-cell family; the last is closed by the normalized invariant-column insertion followed by an exact causal restoration of the marked root time. The latter keeps the native selected gate inside a summable two-rail kernel and uses martingale orthogonality only for the genuine aggregate increment. 7. Marked shells and inverse covariance. The two renewal branches give theorem 3.46. The first squared derivative shell is O(n); the all-order shell bound controls the inverse series through logarithmic degree, while a separate finite-N tail estimate handles the remainder. At time cN 

the score energy is O(n/c 2). 8. Endpoint comparison. A cutoff localizes to the covariance band, and weighted integration by parts transfers the score estimate to the endpoint density. The Ricci curvature of SO( n)yields the Haar log-Sobolev inequality through the Bakry–Émery method [BGL14]; Pinsker’s inequality finishes the total-variation bound. 1.5 AI-assisted discovery and verification status 

The proof was developed through an extended, author-directed interaction with generative-AI systems. The primary system was OpenAI Codex running GPT-5.6 Sol. According to the author’s reconstruction of the workflow, approximately three days of intensive interaction were devoted to proof search, construction, and organization, followed by approximately one additional day devoted to targeted defect repair and consistency audit. The Codex workflow used parallel agents for bounded subproblems and adversarial audits. Its mathematical roles included exploring proof routes, maintaining the proof ledger and dependency map, carrying out tensor and ribbon bookkeeping, searching for counterexamples to proposed intermediate lemmas, producing exact replay calculations where suitable, and revising the exposition. Claude Opus 5 was subsequently used by a collaborator for two additional audit passes. The author supplied the mathematical problem, source papers, target O(n2) invariant, and strategic direction; decided which routes and claims to retain; reviewed the manuscript and cited sources; and checked the theorem statement, global proof architecture, principal reductions, and dependency chain. The AI-generated work was not accepted in a single pass: proposed shortcuts were repeatedly tested, false strengthenings were removed after counterexamples, and the surviving 5argument underwent exact computational replays where its finite algebraic content permitted them. Agreement among AI agents or passage through an AI audit was treated as evidence for further checking, not as a proof by itself. The verification status of this version should nevertheless be stated precisely. A complete independent, line-by-line human rederivation of every lengthy local argument has not yet been completed. In particular, the automated replays and the two external AI-assisted audit passes do not replace such a human audit. Accordingly, this arXiv version is presented as a preliminary proof report, and reports of errors or independent verification are especially welcome. The author takes responsibility for the contents of the submission and will record corrections and the progress of human verification in subsequent versions. No generative-AI system is listed as an author or cited as authority for a mathematical claim.
