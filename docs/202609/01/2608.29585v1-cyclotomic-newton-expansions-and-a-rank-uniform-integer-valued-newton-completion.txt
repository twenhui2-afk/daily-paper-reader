Habiro’s cyclotomic expansion of the colored Jones polynomial gives a hierarchy of integral divisibilities whose multiplicities grow with the color. This is stronger than the existence of a finite-order q-difference equation: the colored Jones function is q-holonomic [8], as are fixed-row colored HOMFLY–PT functions [10], whereas the expansion proved here additionally requires integrality of the corresponding Newton divided differences. For sl 2, Habiro obtained the cyclotomic divisibilities from the integral universal invariant and the completed center [11, 13]. Chen–Liu–Zhu formulated the corresponding expansion for symmetric-power-colored SU (n)invariants [4, Conjecture 1.3]. They verified it for the figure-eight knot and the trefoil [4, Examples 2.5 and 2.6]; subsequent work proved the colored HOMFLY–PT expansion for double-twist knots [5, Theorem 1.2]. Beliakova–Gorsky later constructed a cyclotomic interpolation basis for the center of Uq2 (gl n) and proved Laurent integrality of the universal knot coefficients [2, Theorems 8.2–8.3 and Proposition 8.7]. Their finite dual coefficient formula and rank-reduction identity provide the central input used here. Restriction to symmetric powers initially produces a one-sided factorial basis. The main step is to recover the two-sided Chen–Liu–Zhu kernel by a reflection of the completed Harish–Chandra character and integral descent through z 7 → z + z−1.CYCLOTOMIC NEWTON EXPANSIONS AND RANK-UNIFORM COMPLETION 3

For each fixed n ≥ 2, completed Harish–Chandra reflection and integral local descent convert the one-sided interpolation expansion into the two-sided Chen–Liu–Zhu expansion, proving the conjecture for every zero-framed knot. The figure-eight example shows both that the coefficient ring cannot generally be replaced by 

Z[A±1, q ±1] and that the quantum-factorial denominator length is unbounded. Two structural questions remain. First, does ̂ CA,q admit a canonical realization as a one-row stable quotient of the Beliakova–Gorsky interpolation center, with the basis (178) induced by a stable central basis? Such a result would compare the two constructions of the unified object. Second, rectangular colors lead to a multivariable Harish–Chandra quotient with a stratified ramification locus; compare the explicit conjectural double-twist formulas in [17]. An integral formal-descent theorem for that quotient would extend the one-row method developed here.
