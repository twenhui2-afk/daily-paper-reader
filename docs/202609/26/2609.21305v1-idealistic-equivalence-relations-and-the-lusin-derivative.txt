Abstract. We give a proof, that does not rely on analytic determinacy, of the existence of an idealistic analytic equivalence relation with Borel classes that is not classwise Borel isomorphic to an orbit equivalence relation. This result answers a question of Becker, and Calderoni and Motto Ros. Our proof proceeds through an analysis of Borel complexity of certain sets of countable rooted trees defined via the notion of the Lusin derivative. 

1. Introduction 

The main objects of study in this paper are equivalence relations on standard Borel spaces. We start with recalling two classes of equivalence relations and two methods of comparing equivalence relations. 

Definition 1.1. We say that an equivalence relation E on a standard Borel space 

X is an orbit equivalence relation if there exists a Polish group H and a Borel action a : H × X → X inducing E, that is, 

xEy ⇐⇒ ∃ h ∈ H (a(h, x ) = y).

We note that each orbit equivalence relation is analytic and has Borel equivalence classes. The following notion of idealistic equivalence relation was introduced in [10] and [12]. 

Definition 1.2. We say that an analytic equivalence relation E on a standard Borel space X is idealistic if there is a map assigning to each equivalence class 

C ∈ X/E a ccc σ-ideal IC on C such that C / ∈ IC and the map C 7 → IC is Borel in the following sense: for each Borel A ⊆ X2, the set AI ⊆ X defined by 

x ∈ AI ⇐⇒ { y ∈ [x]E : ( x, y ) ∈ A} ∈ I[x]E

is Borel.

Definition 1.3. Let E, F be equivalence relations on standard Borel spaces X, Y ,respectively. We say that E is Borel reducible to F , in symbols E ≤B F , if there is a Borel function f : X → Y such that for all x, y ∈ XxEy ⇐⇒ f (x)F f (y).

In this case we say that f is a reduction of E to F . If E ≤B F and F ≤B E,then we say E, F are bireducible .Finally, we recall a notion of comparison of equivalence relations that is stronger than the classical Borel reduction above. 

Definition 1.4. Let E, F be analytic equivalence relations on standard Borel spaces X, Y , respectively. We say that E is classwise Borel isomorphic to 

F if there are Borel reductions f : X → Y , g : Y → X of E to F and F to E,respectively, such that their factorings to the quotient spaces ˆf : X/E → Y /F 

and ˆ g : Y /F → X/E are bijections and ˆf −1 = ˆ g. In this case we say that g is a 

classwise Borel inverse of f .In [2], Becker constructed an equivalence relation, later called EB [4], that closely resembles an orbit equivalence relation in that it is analytic, has Borel equivalence classes, and is idealistic. However, assuming analytic determinacy, he proved that 

EB is not an orbit equivalence relation. Calderoni and Motto Ros [4], also assuming analytic determinacy, improved Becker’s result by showing that EB is not even classwise Borel isomorphic to an orbit equivalence relation. Becker [2, Question 1] and Calderoni and Motto Ros [4, Question 4.1] asked if the use of analytic determinacy can be removed. The main result of the current paper removes the assumption of analytic deter-minacy from the above theorems, thereby answering the questions from [2] and [4]. Furthermore, we define a related, yet easier to describe and, perhaps, more natural equivalence relation FB, and show, again without assuming analytic determinacy, that it has the same properties as EB. The equivalence relation 

FB

is defined on the space of (codes of) countable abelian p-groups, for a fixed prime number p. It makes two such groups H1, H 2 equivalent precisely when the quotients of H1 and H2 by their maximal divisible subgroups are isomorphic. 

Theorem 1.5. Relations EB and FB are not classwise Borel isomorphic to an orbit equivalence relation.
