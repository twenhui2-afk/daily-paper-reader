# LOCAL CHORD CORRUPTION IS NOT RECOGNIZER REPLAY: CHORD-CONDITION PROPAGATION IN MIDI-SAG 

# Weiwen Huang 

# Shenzhen University 2510043030@mails.szu.edu.cn 

ABSTRACT 

Synthetic chord corruption provides a controlled stress test for singing accompaniment generation (SAG), whereas complete au-tomatic chord recognition (ACR) replay measures the condition delivered to a deployed system. We compare them by replay-ing CNN–CRF and DeepChroma+CRF predictions through one fixed MIDI-SAG generator, holding track, seed, context, and scor-ing window constant. Across 30 paired tracks and three seeds, a central four-second tritone produced larger changed-target and inside-output effects than CNN–CRF replay in STFT, CQT, and CENS; the CENS target gap was positive on 29/30 tracks (mean 

0.462 ). Matching replay support and relation composition reduced this mismatch, with joint matching giving the lowest replay dis-tance in the full-30 analysis. Relative-root substitutions produced 2.88-fold larger CENS full-window output change than same-root quality flips at near-equal dose. The matched surrogate was closer to replay for both recognizer paths. We conclude that localized corrup-tion tests mechanisms, whereas complete replay evaluates deployed chord-condition propagation. 

Index Terms — singing accompaniment generation, automatic chord recognition, music generation, robustness evaluation, counter-factual analysis 

1. INTRODUCTION 

SAG creates instrumental backing from vocals. Audio-conditioned systems include SingSong and FastSAG [1, 2]; MIDI-Informed Singing Accompaniment Generation (MIDI-SAG) also exposes melody, chord, and MIDI controls [3]. When these controls are estimated, the analyzer becomes part of the generation system. Short synthetic corruption is easy to align and reproduce, yet one concentrated edit cannot stand in for a full ACR sequence. ACR out-put mixes root, quality, and no-chord events with nonuniform tem-poral support. Thus, a robustness conclusion can change with the chord-condition test that is chosen. Existing work establishes chord-conditioned generation and robustness [4, 3]. Here we ask whether a prescribed local edit represents the complete recognizer-derived control sequence seen by a generator under matched conditions. This distinction changes the meaning of a robustness result. A central corruption tests the response to one deliberately concentrated relation. Complete replay tests the response to the heterogeneous chord stream that an analysis front end actually delivers. These are complementary interventions, not interchangeable versions of the same test. Our paired design places them on the same track, con-text, seed, and scoring window so their output consequences can be compared directly. We make three contributions. First, we introduce a paired chord-condition propagation protocol that fixes track, seed, con-text, prompt, scoring window, and generator while contrasting a local edit with a complete recognizer-derived sequence. Second, central and support/profile-matched edits show that a central tri-tone produces larger transfer effects than complete replay, while changed support and relation composition each remove most of the mismatch and their joint construction has the lowest replay distance. Third, this calibration direction holds across CNN–CRF and DeepChroma+CRF, two ACR paths replayed through the fixed MIDI-SAG generator. 

2. RELATED WORK 

SAG spans audio-only and explicitly conditioned generation [1, 2, 3]. AnyAccomp uses a quantized melodic bottleneck rather than a time-aligned chord interface [5]; the present intervention requires the latter. Broader controllable-music systems provide melodic, textual, and time-varying control pathways [6, 7, 8, 9]; MusicGen-Chord adds chord-progressions to the MusicGen family [10, 11]. Gao et al. study robustness of chord-conditioned song generation to inaccurate chord extraction [4]. Their generator-centered question is whether a model tolerates imperfect control. Our evaluation-centered question is whether a localized synthetic condition represents the complete recognizer-derived chord sequence delivered to a fixed generator un-der matched rendering conditions. ACR represents root, bass, quality, and duration; timing and label ambiguity are central evaluation issues [12, 13, 14, 15]. Re-lated substitutions also differ musically [16, 17, 18]. CNN–CRF and DeepChroma+CRF provide distinct ACR paths [19, 20, 21]. We treat their complete outputs as deployed chord conditions. Their downstream consequences reflect label relation, temporal place-ment, and duration. This motivates an evaluation protocol for chord-condition propagation.

4. EXPERIMENTAL PROTOCOL 4.1. Data and generation 

We froze 30 paired 12-s windows from 4,102 candidates in a 34-track evaluation pool before generation. Candidates were ranked by vocal activity, onset density, voiced RMS, and silence, then balanced across register and harmonic-change cells. Each 47.554-s MIDI-SAG context was fixed, CNN–CRF was run on the complete mixture and clipped to context, and only the chord condition varied within a pair [24]. We used seeds 42, 123, and 2027; all 180 central-probe and replay renders passed integrity checks.

4.2. Structure and specificity controls 

An 18-track separate diagnostic compares a four-second relative root-changing substitution with a same-root quality flip at near-equal input-disagreement mass (0.1059 versus 0.1070); three seeds yield 108 renders. These tracks are held out from the 30-track confirmatory contrasts, but are not used in the primary transfer comparison. On a nested 12-track subset, a block-occupancy control replaces exactly the CNN–CRF-changed 0.5-s blocks with a fixed fifth-root shift. It matches temporal support, not chord identity or input dis-tance. 

4.3. Profile-matched synthetic calibration

Construction con-trasts isolate the contribution of matched support and relation com-position to replay fidelity. 

5. RESULTS 5.1. Central corruption magnifies condition propagation 

Synthetic corruption produced larger measured changed-target and inside-output effects than CNN–CRF replay in every representation (Table 2). The CENS target gap was positive on 29/30 tracks (mean 

0.462 , 95% CI [0 .377 , 0.539] , p = 2 .61 × 10 −8); inside-output was positive on 28/30 (mean 0.146 , CI [0 .110 , 0.180] , p = 5 .72 ×10 −7). All six frozen tests survived BH correction ( q ≤ 3.05 × 10 −5). The central probe thus yielded a larger response even though complete replay occupied more than twice as much of the scored window. The gap was not driven by one diffusion realization. Seed-specific CENS means were 0.468 , 0.469 , and 0.450 ; each seed was positive on 29/30 tracks, and track ranks correlated 0.76 –0.77 across seed pairs. We average seeds within track for inference. 

5.2. Relative-root changes induce stronger propagation than quality flips 

Relative root-changing substitutions transferred more strongly than same-root quality flips in all representations. In CENS, the relative-root effect was larger on all 18 diagnostic tracks (2.88-fold mean full-window output change; p = 7 .6 × 10 −6; Fig. 2a), with a paired changed-target advantage of 0.251 (CI [0 .208 , 0.292] ). STFT/CQT ratios were 1.89/3.41. The frozen chord encoder also separated these conditions (distance 0.004085 versus 0.002932). The held-out relation control matches symbolic disagreement mass (0.1059 versus 0.1070), showing that the effect follows chord relation rather than perturbation dose.

5.4. Profile matching resolves the synthetic–replay mismatch 

The support-and-profile construction was closer to CNN–CRF re-play than the central tritone (Fig. 1c): CENS was positive on all 30 tracks (mean 0.384 , CI [0 .314 , 0.457] , p = 1 .86 × 10 −9); CQT was positive on 29/30 (mean 0.328 , CI [0 .268 , 0.390] , p = 3 .73 ×10 −9). The same direction reappeared on all 29 eligible DeepChroma+CRF tracks, with CENS/CQT gains of 0.408 /0.353 (p = 3 .73 × 10 −9 for both). Profile matching also produces lower changed-block output distance without a target template (CENS/CQT gains 0.122/0.080; 28/30 and 24/30 tracks; both exact p < 4.5 × 10 −6). The same di-rection on target-following and target-independent endpoints shows that calibration is a property of the output response, not one scoring template. The full-30 construction analysis shows that changed support and relation composition are both key profile coordinates: mean CENS replay distances were 0.482 (central), 0.115 (exact mask), 0.117 (composition only), and 0.098 (full); CQT gave 0.412, 0.094, 0.108, and 0.085. Exact support reduced central distance on 28/30 tracks in both views (gains 0.367 and 0.318; Holm-adjusted p < 

2 × 10 −7). Composition at central support also reduced it on 29/30 and 28/30 tracks (0.364 and 0.305; Holm-adjusted p < 7 × 10 −8). The joint profile had the lowest mean distance (0.098/0.085), with additional mean reductions of 0.016/0.010 over exact-mask-only and 0.019/0.023 over composition-only (CENS/CQT). Together, these results establish changed support and relation composition as the two key coordinates for replay-matched synthetic evaluation. The condition audit exposes the geometry of each construction. Central and composition-only probes change 4.03 s; exact-mask, full-profile, and replay conditions change 10.03 s. Changed-block raw-template distance is 0.991 for central, 0.658 for composition-only, 0.649 for exact-mask, 0.650 for full-profile, and 0.592 for re-play. Frozen chord-CNN distance is 0.111, 0.071, 0.064, 0.072, and 0.068, respectively. This audit makes the geometry behind each construction visible; target-independent output distance confirms the same calibration pattern. 

6. DISCUSSION 6.1. Implications for evaluation 

A central four-second corruption and a complete recognizer-derived condition are not interchangeable transfer tests. Central corrup-tion magnified the condition-propagation response on 29/30 tracks, whereas profile matching sharply narrowed replay distance on target-following and target-independent endpoints. The full-30 construction analysis identifies changed support and relation compo-sition as the two coordinates that make a synthetic probe replay-like. The result also clarifies the role of local intervention. Relative-root changes produced 2.88 times the CENS full-window output change of quality flips at near-equal dose. A localized edit is there-fore a precise harmonic-mechanism test; complete replay is the operating-condition test. Both should be reported, but they should not be merged into a single robustness score. CNN–CRF and DeepChroma+CRF use different acoustic front ends, yet profile matching improves replay fidelity for both. This consistency shows that the evaluation rule is stable across recognizer paths in the MIDI-SAG conditioner. The factor analysis further shows why a single chord-error rate is not enough for SAG evaluation. Two control streams can contain comparable amounts of disagreement but drive different accompani-ment responses when their changed regions or chord relations dif-fer. Error structure is therefore part of the condition presented to the generator. Support records when the control changes; relation composition records what musical substitutions it contains. Report-ing these coordinates makes the gap between a convenient stress test and a deployed recognizer stream visible. 

6.2. Practical protocol 

Freeze the vocal excerpt, generator context, prompt, seed, and scor-ing window; render the baseline and complete recognizer sequence; then score their paired output change. Add a localized chord edit only when testing a named harmonic mechanism. Reporting the lo-cal response beside replay distance reveals whether a claimed ro-bustness gain reflects sensitivity to an isolated relation or behavior under the chord stream that the deployed analysis pipeline supplies. The practical consequence is simple. A generator can be highly responsive to a single, deliberately chosen chord change and still re-spond differently to the heterogeneous sequence emitted by a recog-nizer. Local probes should therefore be used to identify which har-monic relations matter, while complete replay should anchor claims about the behavior of the deployed chord-conditioned system. This separation also makes robustness results easier to interpret. A large local effect establishes sensitivity to a named musical in-tervention, such as a relative-root change. A small replay distance establishes fidelity to the recognizer-conditioned operating case. Re-porting both quantities disentangles mechanistic sensitivity from op-erating behavior and prevents a concentrated probe from being mis-taken for the deployment condition it does not reproduce. Support-and-profile matching serves as a controlled intermediate condition: it retains intervention control while matching the temporal and rela-tional structure that drives replay. 7. CONCLUSION 

This study shows that a central four-second chord corruption is not a reduced form of complete recognizer replay. Under matched tracks, seeds, contexts, and scoring windows, the local tritone magnified chord-condition propagation on 29 of 30 tracks. Matching support and relation composition closed most of the gap across CNN–CRF and DeepChroma+CRF recognizer paths. The control sequence is a fundamental component of the evaluation, not a neutral carrier for a chord-error rate. Robustness in analysis-conditioned generation depends on the complete control trajectory: support determines where the genera-tor is challenged, and relation composition determines the harmonic nature of that challenge. 8.
