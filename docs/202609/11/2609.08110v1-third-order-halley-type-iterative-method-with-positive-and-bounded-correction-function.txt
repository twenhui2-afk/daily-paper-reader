# Third-order Halley-type iterative method with positive and bounded correction function 

## Hirai Mukasa 1,2 1Department of Creative Design Engineering, National Institute of Technology, Kagoshima College 

> 2

## Graduate School of Mathematics, Kyushu University 

Abstract 

In this paper, we propose a new one-point iterative method for finding simple roots of nonlinear equations by modifying Halley’s method. Its correction function is positive and bounded on R and matches the local expansion of Halley’s correction function near a simple root. The method avoids the singularity and sign reversal of Halley’s correction function while retaining at least third-order local convergence. We also establish sufficient conditions for global convergence. Numerical experi-ments show that the proposed method achieves high convergence success rates and favorable iteration counts over a wide range of initial guesses. Further experiments near the singularity of Halley’s correction function demonstrate robust convergence behavior of the proposed method. An application to the van der Waals equation illustrates the effectiveness of the proposed method. 

Keywords : Global convergence, Halley’s method, Nonlinear equations, One-point iterative method, Third-order convergence

# 1 Introduction 

Solving nonlinear equations f (x) = 0 is a classical problem in numerical analysis with ap-plications in a wide range of scientific and engineering fields (Wissink et al., 1996; Li and Zhang, 2004; Du et al., 2005). Since closed-form solutions are generally unavailable, iter-ative methods are widely used to compute approximate solutions. Among these methods, Newton’s method is one of the most fundamental and widely used. It is given by 

xn+1 = xn − f (xn)

f ′(xn), n = 0 , 1, . . . ,

However, the method may fail to converge when the initial guess is far from the root (Yamamoto, 2000). Halley’s method is a one-point iterative method with a higher local order of conver-gence than Newton’s method. The iteration is given by 

xn+1 = xn − 2f (xn)f ′(xn)2f ′(xn)2 − f (xn)f ′′ (xn),

which converges cubically to α when the initial guess is sufficiently close to the root. Its geometric properties and convergence behavior have also been extensively studied (Hern´ andez, 1991; Melman, 1997). Other third-order iterative methods, including the Chebyshev, super-Halley, Fang, Ostrowski, and Noor methods, have also been proposed and analyzed from the viewpoints of local and global convergence (Hansen and Patrick, 1977; Guti´ errez and Hern´ andez, 2001; Fang et al., 2006; Noor and Noor, 2007; Neta and Scott, 2013; Barrada et al., 2020). # 5 Concluding remarks 

In this paper, we proposed a new one-point iterative method based on a correction function that is positive and bounded on R while preserving the local behavior of Halley’s method. A key feature of the proposed method is that its correction function matches that of Halley’s method to high order near the root and avoids both the singularity at Lf (x) = 2 and the sign reversal for Lf (x) > 2. Moreover, we proved the third-order convergence of the proposed method and its global convergence under certain conditions. In the numerical experiments, the proposed method achieved high convergence success rates and good iterative efficiency over a wide range of initial guesses and exhibited stable convergence behavior even near the singularity of Halley’s correction function. Furthermore, in the application to the van der Waals equation, the proposed method maintained convergence performance comparable to Halley’s method and also showed stable convergence behavior for an initial guess yielding a large value of |Lf (x0)|.
