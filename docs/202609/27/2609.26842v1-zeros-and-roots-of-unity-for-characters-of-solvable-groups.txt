ZEROS AND ROOTS OF UNITY FOR CHARACTERS OF SOLVABLE GROUPS          

> FANG BIAN AND YONG YANG
> Abstract. We prove Miller’s conjectured bound for solvable groups: if Gis a finite solvable group and
> χ∈Irr( G), then χ(g) is zero or a root of unity for at least half of the elements g∈G. We also prove the same bound for monomial irreducible characters of arbitrary finite groups.

1. Introduction 

Let G be a finite group and let χ ∈ Irr( G). Define 

λ(χ) = 1

|G| {g ∈ G : χ(g) = 0 or χ(g) is a root of unity } .

Miller conjectured that λ(χ) ≥ 1/2 for every finite group and every irreducible character [1]. The conjecture appears as Conjecture 7.5 in Navarro’s survey on characters of solvable groups [3]. Moret´ oand Navarro proved the bound for groups with a Sylow tower [2]. Navarro and Sambale later gave a counterexample for arbitrary finite groups [4]; their example is nonsolvable. We prove that the bound holds for every finite solvable group. 

Theorem 1.1. Let G be a finite solvable group and let χ ∈ Irr( G). Then 

λ(χ) ≥ 12 .

Recall that an irreducible character χ of G is monomial if χ = μG for a linear character μ of a subgroup of G.

Theorem 2.2. Let G be a finite group and let χ ∈ Irr( G) be monomial. Then 

λ(χ) ≥ 12 .

Proof. Write χ = μG, where μ is a linear character of a subgroup H ≤ G. Since |μ(h)| = 1 for every 

h ∈ H, the hypotheses of Lemma 2.1 are satisfied. □

3. Solvable groups 

We first note that primitive irreducible characters are quasiprimitive. Let β ∈ Irr( H) be primitive, and suppose that N ◁ H . If βN were not homogeneous, choose θ ∈ Irr( N ) below β. Then the inertia group 

IH (θ) would be a proper subgroup of H, and Clifford correspondence would express β as a character induced from IH (θ), contrary to primitivity. Hence β is quasiprimitive. 

Proof of Theorem 1.1. Choose H ≤ G of smallest order such that 

χ = βG

for some β ∈ Irr( H). We claim that β is primitive. Otherwise β = γH for some proper subgroup K < H 

and some γ ∈ Irr( K). By transitivity of induction, 

χ = ( γH )G = γG,

contrary to the minimal choice of H. Thus β is primitive, and hence quasiprimitive by the preceding paragraph. Since G is solvable, so is H. Therefore the hypotheses of Wilde [5, Theorem B] are satisfied. Hence, if 

h ∈ H and β(h)̸ = 0, then |β(h)|2 is a positive integer dividing β(1) 2. Thus Lemma 2.1 gives
