> Abstract. In this paper, we prove that Zis first-order definable in the ring of integers
> Zab of the maximal abelian extension Qab of Q, which implies that the first-order theory of Zab is undecidable. More generally, writing i=√−1 and Qtr for the field of all totally real numbers, we prove new definability and undecidability results for rings of integers of subfields of Qtr (i), focusing especially on fields which contain infinitely many roots of unity. The key ingredient for these results is that there is a parameter-free positive-existential formula which defines the roots of unity μ(OL) inside OLfor every field L⊆Qtr (i).

1. Introduction 

1.1. A motivating example. Let Qab denote the maximal abelian extension of Q. By the famous Kronecker–Weber theorem, Qab is precisely the extension of Q generated by all roots of unity, i.e., the algebraic numbers ζ which satisfy ζn = 1 for some n. In particular, Qab 

is a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field, meaning it is a subfield of 

Qtr (i) where Qtr is the field of all totally real numbers and i = √−1. The problem of the first-order decidability (in the language of rings) of the ring of integers 

Zab of Qab has been an open problem of interest for several years, as noted in [Koe14, §6.3], [Kar21, Ques. 3.1], [Spr24, §1] and [MRS24, Rem. 2.13]. The guiding motivation for this paper is to resolve this question. 

Theorem 1.1. Z is definable in Zab . Thus, the first-order theory of Zab is undecidable. 

Because Z has undecidable first-order theory, undecidability follows immediately from definability. However, as discussed in the following subsections, there are two more general constructions sitting behind this example, one focusing on definability of Z in rings of integers of algebraic extensions of Q, and the other focusing on establishing undecidability. The core technique of this paper is a unit-group-based method, similar to [MRUV20, MRS24, Spr20, Spr24]. One of the main results of our previous paper [Spr24] showed that the ring of integers OQtr (i) is not definable in Qtr (i), building on work of J. Robinson [Rob62] and Fried, Haran and V¨ olklein [FHV94]. This can also be deduced independently from work of Dittmann and Fehm [DF21]. Currently, as far as we know, Qab is amenable to neither these known techniques of establishing non-definability of a ring of integers, nor the techniques of proving definability such as [Shl18]. We leave the question of the definability of Zab in Qab ,as well as the first-order decidability of Qab itself, as open questions for the future.

1.2. Defining the torsion subgroup of units. Given any set S of algebraic numbers, let 

μ(S) be the set of roots of unity in S. In other words, if R is a ring of algebraic numbers, then μ(R) is the torsion subgroup of R×. The key ingredient which fuels all of our main theorems is that we can define roots of unity within rings of algebraic integers in Qtr (i). The most general theorem of this flavor is the following. 

Theorem 1.2 (Theorem 4.4) . There is a single positive-existential formula Tor( z) which defines μ(OL) in OL for every field L ⊆ Qtr (i).

To prove this theorem, we first use the notion of interpreting formulas in quotient rings (see Lemma 4.3) to reduce to the case of defining the torsion subgroup of units within a ring A ⊆ Qtr (i) of algebraic integers that contains a primitive 20-th root of unity. Then, we define a nonmaximal subring R3(A) := Z + 3 A inside A, following the strategy of [Spr24], so that R3(A)× is totally real. By deploying several formulas throughout Section 3 on this totally real set of units, we eventually obtain an existentially definable totally real subset containing only elements of the form 2 + ζ + ζ−1, where ζ is a root of unity. This lets us define many roots of unity as the roots of polynomials of the form X2 − (ζ + ζ−1)X + 1. 7. JR numbers 

To finish, we make some remarks on the implications of Theorem 6.2 and related construc-tions for the case of totally imaginary quadratic extensions of totally real fields with a fixed JR-number. 7.1. Review of JR -numbers. Let K be a totally real field. Recall the following. 

Definition 7.1. Given a totally real number α and t ∈ R, write 0 ≪ α ≪ t if every conjugate of α lies in the interval (0 , t ). For a totally real subset X, write Xt = {α ∈ X : 0 ≪ α ≪ t}.The JR -number of X is: 

JR( X) := inf {t ∈ R : # Xt = ∞} .

If JR( X) is finite, then we say that the JR -number is attained if the infimum is actually a minimum. 

The usefulness of this definition relies on the following result, which is a straightforward application of Siegel’s four-squares theorem [Sie21]; see [Spr24, Thm. 4.3] for recent exposi-tion. 

Theorem 7.2. There is a single existential formula ϕ(x; a, b ) such that if K is any totally real field and a, b are integers with b̸ = 0 , then ϕ(x; a, b ) holds over OK if and only if 0 ≪ x ≪ ab .

In particular, it was an observation of J. Robinson that if JR( OK ) is either infinite or an attained finite value, then OK is undecidable [Rob62] by an application of J. Robinson’s version of Lemma 6.1 with the parameterized family of subsets given by ϕ(x; a, b ). It is also known that JR( OK ) ∈ [4 , ∞]. For more on JR-numbers, see [Cas18, CVV20, GR19, JV08, MnS24, Spr20, Spr24, VV15, VV16, VV26] as a start. 7.2. Totally imaginary extensions of totally real fields with fixed JR -number. 

First, observe that Theorem 6.2 applies to rings of integers in totally real fields whose JR-number is at the low extreme. 

Corollary 7.3. Let K be a totally real field. Assume that JR( OK ) = 4 is an attained minimum, or equivalently, assume that K contains ζ + ζ−1 for infinitely many roots of unity ζ. Then OL has undecidable first-order theory for all totally imaginary quadratic extensions L/K .Proof. Lemma 2.3 establishes the fact that K contains ζ + ζ−1 for infinitely many roots of unity ζ if and only if JR( OK ) = 4 is an attained minimum. The proof concludes by observing that [ Q(ζ) : Q(ζ + ζ−1)] ≤ 2 for every root of unity ζ, so the hypotheses of Theorem 6.2 are satisfied with D = 2. □

Finally, the analogue of Corollary 7.3 is also true when JR( OK ) = ∞. The proof of this fact does not require the subgroup of torsion units, but is rather a combination of the main results of [MRS24] with the proof strategy seen in [MRUV20, Spr20, Spr24]. In particular, we note that this theorem is strictly stronger than the main theorems of [MRUV20, Spr20].
