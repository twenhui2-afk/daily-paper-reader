1. Introduction 

The unlikely intersection principle asserts that an algebraic variety containing many special points must itself be special . An early example is the following theorem of Ihara, Serre, and Tate (recorded by Lang) [Lan65]: 

Let F pX, Y q P CrX, Y s be irreducible. If the equation F pX, Y q “ 0 has infinitely many solutions in roots of unity, then, up to multiplication by a nonzero constant,

Here the special points are the torsion points of G2

> m

, and the special curves are translates of one-dimensional subtori by torsion points. The Manin–Mumford conjecture, proved by Raynaud [Ray83], predicts that a subvariety of an abelian variety containing a Zariski dense set of torsion points must be a finite union of torsion translates of abelian subvarieties. Laurent [Lau84] established the analogous statement for algebraic tori, while the Andr´ e–Oort conjecture, resolved in [Pil11, Tsi18, KUY22] using the so-called Pila– Zanier strategy [PZ08], provides a further analogue for special points on Shimura varieties; see the survey paper [Tsi24]. Motivated by these results, we consider analogous questions for other collections of algebraic numbers. Given an infinite set S Ď Q, whose elements we regard as special points , we ask which subvarieties of An can contain a Zariski dense subset of Sn.Without additional assumptions on S, the answer can be arbitrarily complicated. For example, if 

S “ Q, then every subvariety of An contains a Zariski dense subset of Sn.
