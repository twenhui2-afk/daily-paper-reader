# Hardy–Littlewood Maximal Operator and Two-Layer Muckenhoupt Weights on Infinite Rooted k-Ary Trees 

Dachun Yang, Wen Yuan and Mingdong Zhang September 14, 2026 

Abstract Let k ≥ 2 be an integer, T an infinite rooted k-ary tree, and M the Hardy– Littlewood maximal operator on T . For any p ∈ (0 , ∞), we characterize the weight w such that M is bounded on Lp(w). To this end, we introduce a two-layer Muckenhoupt weight class Ap and prove that, for any p ∈ ( 12 , ∞), the boundedness of M on Lp(w), w ∈ Ap, and the exponential decay boundedness of spherical averaging operators on Lp(w) are mutually equivalent, and that, when p ∈ (0 , 12 ], there exists no weight w such that M is bounded on 

Lp(w). Moreover, for any p ∈ ( 12 , ∞), we establish the quantitative estimate, with the optimal exponent 1 

> p

of the weight constant, for the boundedness of M on Lp(w). For any p ∈ (1 , ∞), we also obtain two further equivalent characterizations of the boundedness of M on Lp(w), re-spectively, in terms of a global Sawyer-type testing condition and an estimate for the weighted product measure of distance incidence sets. As applications, for any p ∈ ( 12 , ∞), under the assumption that M is bounded on Lp(w), we establish the boundedness of exponentially de-caying kernel operators on Lp(w) and weighted Fe ff erman–Stein vector-valued inequalities.

Since this seminal work of Muckenhoupt, maximal operators and their (weighted) inequalities have been studied in a wide variety of metric measure spaces (see, for example, [1, 14] for spaces of homogeneous type, [11, 16, 17, 29] for non-doubling spaces, and [4, 33, 34] for dis-crete graph settings). It is well known that trees serve as a fundamental and prototypical class of graphs. Recall that a graph is called a tree if it is connected and contains no cycles. Suppose that k ≥ 2 is an integer. A tree T is called an infinite rooted k-ary tree if T has a root vertex and every vertex of T has exactly 

k children. Let T be the infinite rooted k-ary tree, and let M be the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator on T in (1.2). The main purpose of this article is to characterize the weight w such that 

M is bounded on the weighted Lebesgue space Lp(w) on T in (1.3) for any given p ∈ (0 , ∞). We first present a brief history on the study of M on trees. Recall that the degree of a vertex of a tree is the number of its neighbors. A tree is said to be homogeneous if every vertex has the same degree. The study of the boundedness of M on trees can be traced back to the work of Rochberg and Taibleson [31], in which they established the weak type (1 , 1) estimate for the Green operator associated with random walks on trees of bounded degree. When specialized to the isotropic random walk on homogeneous trees, their estimate implies that M is of weak type (1 , 1). In 2010, Cowling et al. [8, Corollary 3.2] gave a direct and geometric proof of the weak type (1 , 1) estimate for M on homogeneous trees. Independently, by means of a combinatorial ex-pansion argument, Naor and Tao [26, Theorem 1.5] proved that M is of weak type (1 , 1) on infinite rooted k-ary trees, which, together with the trivial L∞ estimate and the Marcinkiewicz interpola-tion theorem, yields that M is of strong type ( p, p) for any p ∈ (1 , ∞). The combinatorial ideas of Naor and Tao [26] were subsequently adapted to the study of maximal inequalities in several related settings; see, for example, [34] for infinite graphs, [2, 7] for hyperbolic spaces, [12, 20] for fractional maximal operators, and [12, 27, 28] for weighted variants. For more developments concerning the boundedness of di ff erent maximal operators on trees of bounded degree, we refer to [18, 24]. We also refer to [19, 21, 22, 23, 30] for various developments in harmonic analysis on trees equipped with flow measures, including the Calder´ on–Zygmund theory, Hardy and BMO spaces, and the boundedness results for Riesz transforms and spectral multipliers associated with the flow Laplacian.
