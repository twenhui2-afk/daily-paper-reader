1. Introduction 

In this article, we study the number of collisions of independent, discrete-time simple ran-dom walks on comb graphs defined over the integer line Z as a base graph. Given a sequence 

H := ( Hx)x∈Z ∈ RZ

Z a vertical segment (called ‘tooth’) of the form {0, 1, ..., ⌊Hx ⌋} (with ⌊·⌋ denoting the integer part). Our interest is both in comb graphs with deterministic, regularly growing tooth profiles, and in tooth profiles that appear as a realization of i.i.d. heavy-tailed random variables. The problem of collisions of random walks on comb graphs goes back at least to [24]. There, it is proved that on a comb graph obtained by attaching infinite teeth to each vertex of Z,two independent random walks starting from the same vertex collide only finitely many times almost surely. Since then, several criteria on finite length tooth profiles (Hx)x∈Z have been developed to distinguish between the (almost sure) occurrence or absence of infinitely many collisions of two or three random walks on Comb( Z, H ), see [5, 8, 11, 12, 23]. Collisions of two or multiple walks have furthermore been investigated on comb graphs with a radially symmet-ric tooth profile over other planar graphs including Z2 (see [14]), as well as on several classes of random recurrent graphs (see [3, 5, 10, 13, 20, 31]), or in certain random or directionally biased media (see [9, 15, 16, 17, 19]). In a slightly different direction, the spatial distribution of the collisions has been studied extensively in [26, 27, 28] for Z as well as certain random graphs. The graph Comb( Z, H ) in which the tooth lengths (Hx)x∈Z are a realization of a random i.i.d. (bi-infinite) sequence is of special significance, since the horizontal coordinates of each simple random walk on the comb may be viewed as a random walk on Z with ‘trans-parent traps’. To that effect, the random walk on Comb( Z, H ) has also received attention in the context of scaling limits of trapped walks, see, e.g., [6, 7]. Finally, we mention that the problem of collisions on comb graphs has appeared in the physics literature (see, e.g., [1, 29] and references therein), as a natural model to study diffusion-limited reactions. Remark 3.8. (1) We compare our proof of the infinite collision property of Comb( Z, H (γ))

for γ ∈ (1 , 2] to the proof for γ > 2 in [11]. We have set up a similar argument of defining strikes and estimating the number of collisions using them. However, our key improvement is to show that, when a strike takes place, the height of the walk which is not on the horizontal axis is essentially uniformly distributed over the tooth. This allows us to avoid the worst-case scenario used in [11], which uses a bound in which the second walk is at the top of the tooth when the other strikes. 16 UMBERTO DE AMBROGGIO, JENSON NG, MAXIMILIAN NITZSCHNER, AND CARLO SCALI 

(2) We briefly comment on the proof of the finite collision property of Comb( Z, H (γ)) for

In essence, our proof shows that this logarithmic term is not an artifact of the proof of the upper bound in the latter reference, but actually correctly quantifies the number of collisions. 

4. Phase transition for the number of collisions on random combs 

In this Section, we consider the comb graph Comb( Z, H ω) with an i.i.d. random height assignment (Hωz )z∈Z defined on some probability space (Ω , G, P), where the height distribution satisfies (1.7) for some γ ∈ (0 , 1] . Our main result is the following. 

Theorem 4.1. The following statements hold for P-a.e. ω ∈ Ω.(1) If γ > 1/3 then Comb( Z, H ω ) has the infinite collision property. (2) If γ < 1/3 then Comb( Z, H ω ) has the finite collision property. 

The two statements in Theorem 4.1 are established separately. Concerning Theorem 4.1-

(2) , we will state and prove a stronger result in Theorem 4.16 implying the latter, which only requires a lower bound on the tail distribution of H0 (see (4.137); a similar statement with an additional technical condition appeared in [23]). The argument in this regime follows from a slightly modified first moment method. The proof of Theorem 4.1-(1) concerning the infinite collision property in the regime γ ∈

(1 /3, 1] is more challenging, and constitutes the principal part of the present Section. We will set up a relatively standard second moment method counting collisions between certain Markovian times. However, both the lower bound for the first moment and the upper bound for the second moment will require uniform estimates over large disks. This substantially increases the technical aspects of the proof and is the main reason behind the absence of the boundary case γ = 1 /3 in our analysis, which we conjecture to fall into the regime of Theorem 4.1-(1) .We outline the ‘collision mechanism’ on Comb( Z, H ω ) effective in the regime γ ∈ (1 /3, 1] 

that gives rise to the infinite collision property in Theorem 4.1-(1) . To establish the infinite collision property we will restrict our attention to the collisions on increasingly large segments of the horizontal axis Z (viewed as a subset of Comb( Z, H ω )). This is perhaps surprising as this ostensibly neglects a considerable amount of vertices that may potentially contribute to the total number of collisions. In fact, we show that the bulk of the local time of a simple random walk on Comb( Z, H ω ) is spent ‘far away from the backbone’, since such vertices constitute most of the volume of the graph (visible by the walk). However, collisions that occur far away from the backbone are highly correlated and hence not suitable for a second moment method. Informally, our estimates suggest that the number of collisions occurring at the typical height are not the main contribution to the infinite collision property. The collision mechanism is therefore not that one random walk gets trapped in a long tooth and then ‘waits’ for the other (as in [15, 16, 17]), but that when both walks move between long excursions their paths typically intersect on the horizontal axis. This is associated to the fact that it is extremely unlikely for two walks to choose the same long tooth for a very long excursion. At the same time, long excursions are the driving force behind the slow movement of the random walk on Comb( Z, H ω ). These observations are related to the scaling limit of the walk projected onto the horizontal axis derived in [6]. Indeed, the Fractional Kinetics limit observed there encodes the fact that traps are transparent (in the sense of [6, Section 3]) and their location PHASE TRANSITION FOR COLLISIONS ON COMBS 17

We now focus on the more challenging case γ = 1 . Since |B(0 , n )| is the sum of n i.i.d. random variables with tail P(H > t ) ∼ Ct −1, one expects that |B(0 , n )| ≈ n log( n). There, the underlying graph is a wedge comb with logarithmic growth profile, and three independent random walks collide infinitely many times almost surely. Despite the fact that the heat kernels for the wedge comb Comb( Z, J (1) ) with growth profile J(1) (k) = log( |k| ∨ 1) and a typical realization of the random comb Comb( Z, H ω ) with tail parameter γ = 1 turn out to be comparable, we will show that finitely many triple collisions occur for this choice almost surely. The main reason causing this contrasting behavior is that the volumes are achieved with very different geometries in the two cases. This highlights that collisions properties are extremely sensitive to the geometry of graphs, and fine information on the heat kernel does not appear to be sufficient for a qualitative classification. We may also compare our set-up to [13], where the infinite triple collision property is established for a typical realization of the trace of the four-dimensional random walk. Their result is obtained through a very fine analysis of the random graph, its geometry and corresponding heat-kernel estimates. The heat-kernel estimate of order n−1/2 log( n)−1/2 also appears there (whenever a set is good in a suitable sense).
