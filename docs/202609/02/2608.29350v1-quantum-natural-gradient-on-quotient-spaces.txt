# Quantum Natural Gradient on Quotient Spaces 

Zeyu Chen 

Parametrized quantum circuits often contain state-preserving redundancies that make the quantum Fisher information matrix (QFIM) singular even when the physical state manifold is regular. We formulate quantum natural gradient (QNG) on the resulting parameter quotient and prove that, when the prescribed redundancy exhausts the Fisher kernel, the Moore–Penrose update is the minimum-norm horizontal lift of the quotient Riemannian gradient. A circuit-to-orbit transfer principle separates intrinsic state distinguishability from circuit-coordinate distortion and gives the exact condition under which a circuit realizes an intrinsic orbit-QNG direction. Representation theory then yields root-resolved Fisher scales on highest-weight flag orbits and isotropic intrinsic metrics for Slater and fermionic-Gaussian manifolds. On cominuscule embeddings, intrinsic fidelity QNG conserves principal-defect ratios and reduces to one scalar equation; Lie-retracted steps are locally cubic at η = 2, with stability boundary η = 4. For finite-shot implementations, we separate exact gauge removal from regularization of physical soft modes, obtain zero cumulative gauge drift under structural projection, and derive confidence-controlled soft-mode rules. Under depolarization, inverse-Fisher scaling restores deterministic scale only by amplifying fluctuations and therefore cannot recover a lost update signal-to-noise ratio. A redundant Slater/Givens circuit verifies the transfer law and the predicted finite-shot tradeoffs. 

I. INTRODUCTION 

Variational quantum algorithms encode physical states in gate parameters and optimize a state-dependent objective through a classical outer loop [1]. Quantum natural gradient (QNG) replaces Euclidean parameter distance by the pullback of quantum statistical distinguishability, making the infinitesimal physical update invariant under regular reparameterizations [2–7]. The pullback is often singular because several parameter velocities can generate the same state velocity. An exact state-preserving redundancy, a rank-changing family, and a physically weak but nonzero direction all produce small or zero Fisher eigenvalues, yet they require different algorithmic treatments. Pseudoinversion is intrinsic in the first case only after the redundant representatives have been identified as a quotient; hard truncation in the third case can erase genuine descent directions. Existing results characterize several parts of this picture separately. QFIM rank measures effective model dimension and overparametrization, and singular quantum Fisher information governs nonregular estimation problems [8–11]. Dynamical Lie algebras organize reachable sectors and also influence gradient concentration, although barren plateaus concern the objective gradient rather than coordinate redundancy itself [12–15]. Wilson et al. established orbitwise QFIM spectral invariance and the fixed nonzero spectrum of the full-observable pure-state case [16], while quotient-manifold optimization explains how exact group redundancies should be removed [17–19]. These results leave a coordinate-level gap: an intrinsic orbit spectrum does not determine the QFIM seen by a gate circuit, because the circuit differential can restrict and distort the orbit tangent space. The same gap prevents orbit-level dynamics from being transferred automatically to a circuit and makes finite-shot null-space decisions ambiguous. The central question is therefore how a redundant circuit chart, a symmetry-reduced state orbit, and the QNG update fit into one geometry. Three actions play distinct roles. The physical symmetry group K decomposes the Hilbert space into sectors, the parameter-redundancy group Γ identifies coordinates that prepare the same state, and the sector dynamical group Gλ generates the reachable orbit. On a regular stratum they form the chain Θ π

−−→ Q = Θ /Γ ¯ρ

We prove that the circuit QFIM factors into the intrinsic orbit Fisher operator and this state-level differential. The factorization requires only a smooth state map into the orbit, identifies the exact Fisher kernel, and separates three effects that a circuit matrix otherwise conflates: intrinsic distinguishability, incomplete tangent-space coverage, and chart distortion. It also gives the pointwise transfer criterion: an orbit-level QNG direction is realized without projection exactly when it lies in the circuit-reachable tangent space. Surjectivity of the circuit differential guarantees this for every orbit objective, while intrinsic isotropy becomes a constant coordinate rescaling only in a conformal horizontal chart. Representation theory makes the intrinsic factor explicit after the physical sector has been selected [13, 14, 20]. The established full-control spectrum serves as a normalization benchmark; highest-weight roots give the Fisher scales on flag orbits; and minuscule representations produce isotropic Slater and fermionic-Gaussian manifolds. For cominuscule projective embeddings, strongly orthogonal root directions factor the fidelity into principal defects. Their common arXiv:2608.29350v1 [quant-ph] 29 Aug 2026 2Fisher scale forces the continuous defects to contract by one scalar factor, yielding conserved ratios and an exact scalar reduction. The embedding index controls the finite map, while tangent-space coverage of the intrinsic fidelity-QNG direction determines whether a circuit inherits the intrinsic flow. Finite measurements turn the same geometry into a statistical decision problem. Known exact gauges can be projected before Fisher estimation and regularization, whereas unresolved low-curvature directions retain a possible physical gradient and therefore call for soft damping. We derive faithfulness and subspace-recovery certificates, perturbation and descent bounds for known or estimated projectors, and an iterative stationarity guarantee with explicit shot budgets and zero cumulative gauge drift for a fixed structural projector. An exact Schur-complement law treats coupled soft modes, and a pilot-calibrated scalar ridge rule gives a finite-sample bias–variance certificate. The accompanying redundant Slater/Givens experiment tests these predictions at equal total shot budgets on a classically simulable free-fermion model.  

> A. Contributions

The paper makes three connected advances. 1. It gives a coordinate-independent criterion for interpreting singular QNG: a prescribed redundancy produces the quotient Riemannian gradient exactly when its vertical space is the full Fisher kernel. The circuit-to-orbit factorization then determines faithfulness, reachable tangent directions, and the singular-value distortion between orbit and gate coordinates. At critical points the same chart differential acts on the Fisher metric and objective Hessian, leaving the intrinsic QNG linearization unchanged under faithful coordinate distortions. 2. It derives exact intrinsic Fisher geometry and dynamics beyond the full-control benchmark. Highest-weight roots resolve the nonzero spectrum; minuscule Slater and fermionic-Gaussian orbits recover one intrinsic scale; and cominuscule fidelity dynamics reduce to conserved principal-defect ratios and one scalar equation. The finite Lie-retracted map has second-order ratio drift and cubic local convergence at η = 2. Its exact stability boundary is η = 4; the full map remains convergent there, and the rank-one distance decays as t−1/2.3. It turns the exact-gauge versus physical-soft-mode distinction into finite-shot guarantees. The results cover kernel certification, fixed and estimated horizontal projectors, iterative stationarity, coupled soft sectors, scalar regularization, and depolarizing signal-to-noise loss. A 28-parameter Slater/Givens circuit with 16 physical and 12 exact gauge directions verifies the transfer law and the predicted resource–accuracy tradeoffs.  

> B. Related work and scope

Metric-aware optimizers, stochastic Fisher estimators, and randomized measurement protocols reduce the estimation or implementation cost of QNG [21–27]. Rank-based analyses identify effective model dimension [8, 9], and horizontal-gate constructions remove symmetry directions at the ansatz level through homogeneous-space decompositions [28]. The transfer theorem complements these approaches by determining, for any smooth state map into an orbit, how intrinsic Fisher scales and tangent directions appear in a redundant gate chart. Manifold and Grassmann optimization provide computational primitives for the orbit examples studied here [18, 29– 31]. The cubic rank-one retraction at η = 2 shares an order of convergence with Grassmann Rayleigh-quotient iterations, while arising from an embedded fidelity loss rather than a matrix eigenproblem. The coherent-state and Hermitian-symmetric-space geometry used in the pure-state analysis is classical [32–34]; compatible Fisher tensors on mixed-state unitary coadjoint orbits provide the corresponding geometric setting beyond pure states [35]. Our results use this structure to connect orbit geometry to circuit pullbacks, optimization dynamics, and finite-shot stability. Symmetry testing and learning can propose structural candidates upstream [20, 36–39]. The present framework begins with a specified candidate action or subspace and determines whether it is a state-level gauge, how the resulting quotient is represented by the circuit, and how uncertainty should be handled when exact structure cannot be certified. Its conditioning statements remove coordinate and gauge effects while retaining physical excitation gaps, estimator-dependent measurement costs, and decoherence-induced signal loss. All quotient results are stated on Γ-invariant regular strata for which Q = Θ /Γ is smooth.  

> II. FROM REDUNDANT CIRCUIT COORDINATES TO QUOTIENT STATE ORBITS

A singular circuit QFIM becomes an intrinsic optimization metric only after physical sector reduction is separated from parameter redundancy. The physical symmetry selects the active Hilbert-space sector, the redundancy action 3removes equivalent parameter representatives, and the circuit differential transfers quotient velocities to the reachable state orbit. The pullback mechanism connecting these three levels controls both the Fisher kernel and the pseudoinverse update. Standard background on quantum Fisher information, natural gradients, smooth group actions, and quotient tensors is collected in Appendix A [4, 17–19, 40].  

> A. Parametric models and symmetries

Variational optimization over parametrized quantum circuits separates naturally into two ingredients: a map from parameters to quantum states and a scalar objective defined on those states. The state map determines the Fisher geometry and the redundancies of the parametrization, while the objective selects the direction of optimization within that geometry. Thus the quotient is determined by the represented state family rather than by the particular loss optimized on it. Let H be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space. We work locally on an open parameter set Θ ⊂ Rp equipped with its standard Euclidean inner product; orthogonality, pseudoinverses, and horizontal complements refer to this background structure unless stated otherwise. A parameter vector θ ∈ Θ specifies a unitary circuit U (θ) ∈ U (H) and an induced quantum state 

ρ(θ) = U (θ)ρ0U (θ)†, (2) with a fixed reference state ρ0. For concreteness, we write the objective in expectation-value form, 

L(θ) = Tr( O ρ (θ)) , (3) where O = O† is a target observable. All quotient and pseudoinverse statements apply to any smooth loss that depends on the parameters only through the represented quantum state. Within this state-space formulation, physical symmetries and parameter redundancies act at different levels. We use K for physical symmetries acting on the Hilbert space and Γ for redundancy groups acting on the parameter manifold. Physical symmetries such as conserved charges, permutation symmetry, or fixed-momentum constraints first reduce the effective Hilbert-space sector. Independently, a chosen parametrization of the resulting variational family may contain smooth redundancies: different parameter values can induce the same represented quantum state. Γ describes this second structure and defines the parameter quotient. Thus K appears in representation-theoretic reductions and commutant ansätze, whereas Γ appears in the quotient Θ /Γ, the vertical tangent space Vθ, and the gauge-drift discussion. We use the state-level equivalence ρ(g · θ) = ρ(θ); a measurement-level quotient would instead identify only the statistics of a fixed measurement model.

> IV. QUOTIENT-QNG LANDSCAPES AND DYNAMICS

The orbit Fisher operator fixes how QNG measures motion, while the objective Hessian fixes which physical directions contract. Their interaction determines critical conditioning, convergence of parameter representatives, and the exact dynamics available on homogeneous orbits. Exact redundancies first appear as Hessian zero modes; after they are removed on a slice, maximal-flat and full-control geometries yield closed optimization laws.  

> A. Gauge-fixed critical geometry

The pseudoinverse lift is intrinsically first order, but the same redundancy reappears in the Hessian at a critical point. The Hessian is flat along every redundancy orbit and therefore has infinite ambient condition number. A transverse slice removes these zero modes, and retraction to that slice turns convergence on the quotient into convergence of the 14 stored parameter representative. Let g be a nondegenerate background Riemannian metric used to define the Hessian operator and the orthogonal slice; it is distinct from the generally degenerate QFIM tensor F . The redundancy action is assumed to be isometric with respect to g, a condition available for compact Γ by averaging.

> V. FINITE-SHOT QUOTIENT STABILITY AND NUMERICAL VALIDATION

Finite measurements turn a small Fisher eigenvalue into two coupled costs: pseudoinverse amplification and uncertainty in the subspace on which the update is formed. The decisive information is structural. A known exact gauge can be projected out before estimation, whereas a statistically unresolved low-curvature direction may remain physical and requires soft regularization. The Fisher spectral gap controls both costs and links pointwise kernel certification to iterative stability. Proofs of the finite-shot bounds are collected in Appendix E 1.

> VI. CONCLUSION AND OUTLOOK

A singular circuit QFIM becomes interpretable once three layers are separated: state-preserving parameter redundancy, intrinsic distinguishability on the reachable orbit, and distortion introduced by the circuit chart. The factorization 

Fθ = B∗ 

> θ

MρBθ makes this separation checkable directly from the state map. When the prescribed redundancy exhausts the Fisher kernel, the Moore–Penrose step is the minimum-norm horizontal lift of the quotient gradient. Failure of faithfulness instead identifies additional state-level nonidentifiability. An orbit-level QNG update is realized exactly when its intrinsic QNG direction lies in the circuit-reachable tangent subspace; otherwise the circuit implements its metric projection onto that subspace. The conformal-chart criterion further locates exactly when intrinsic isotropy survives as a constant coordinate rescaling. This layered geometry clarifies which optimization effects are intrinsic. Highest-weight roots determine the orbit Fisher scales, and minuscule representations collapse them to one scale on Slater and fermionic-Gaussian manifolds. On cominuscule embeddings, the shared maximal-flat scale reduces the continuous fidelity flow to one scalar contraction with conserved principal-defect ratios. Finite Lie-retracted steps break those invariants at second order and attain cubic rank-one convergence at η = 2. At the exact boundary η = 4, the general map remains convergent; in rank one, the distance decays as t−1/2. Coordinate distortion cancels from the faithful local QNG linearization, whereas physical excitation and population gaps remain in the intrinsic objective operator. The same separation gives a concrete finite-shot rule: project only analytically known or statistically certified exact gauges, and retain unresolved directions through calibrated soft regularization. The Fisher gap controls update amplification, kernel recovery, projector leakage, and iterative stationarity; a fixed structural projector also prevents cumulative gauge drift exactly. The Schur-complement and independent-pilot results quantify the cost of coupled soft modes without treating them as redundant. Under depolarization, inverse-Fisher scaling amplifies the mean and fluctuations together and therefore leaves the update signal-to-noise loss unresolved. The Slater/Givens experiment verifies these structural conclusions on one classically simulable instance and establishes a reproducible resource–accuracy tradeoff; scaling and hardware behavior remain open empirical questions. Natural extensions are algebraic criteria for integrable fidelity dynamics beyond homogeneous cominuscule embeddings, end-to-end confidence guarantees for learned redundancy projectors, correlated-noise versions of the mixed-state analysis, and adaptive tests that resolve physical soft directions before regularization.

> Appendix D: Mixed-state Fisher geometry and depolarizing noise

Pure-state sectors carry the Fubini–Study geometry, whereas mixed-state unitary orbits carry the symmetric-logarithmic-derivative (SLD) quantum Fisher metric [4, 67]. This distinction matters for thermal-state preparation, noise-robust quantum machine learning, and dissipative variational protocols. The SLD QFIM remains exactly solvable on unitary orbits, but its scales are set by population gaps rather than by projective-space isotropy. Nearly degenerate populations consequently create both geometric and statistical fragility. 41
