# 1 Introduction 

Vaught’s conjecture (VC), stated by Robert Vaught [20], is the statement that the number I(T , ω ) of non-isomorphic countable models of a complete countable first-order theory T is either at most countable or continuum. The full conjecture follows from its restriction to the class of partial orders (see [4], p. 231) and it was confirmed for the theories of linear orders (Matatyahu Rubin [15]), model-theoretic trees (John Steel [19]), reticles (James Schmerl [18]), Boolean algebras (Paul Iverson [5]). Using the concept exploited in [6]–[11] we deal with the following question: If VC is confirmed for the structures from a class C, is it true for the structures belonging to its closure ⟨C⟩ , obtained by some model-theoretic constructions? Clearly, the same question can be asked for the “sharp” version of the conjecture, VC ♯, saying that I(T , ω ) ∈ { 1, c}. For example, using Rubin’s results from [15], VC ♯ was confirmed in [8] for the relational structures definable by quantifier free formulas in labeled linear orders, and in [9] for the partial orders from the closure ⟨C lo ⟩ ˙∪Π of the class of linear orders under finite direct products and disjoint unions. On the basis of Steel’s results from [19] VC was confirmed in [11] for the partial orders from the closure ⟨C rt ⟩ ˙∪Π, where Crt is the class of rooted trees. Generally speaking, if C is a class of partial orders, ⟨C⟩ Π its closure under finite direct products and isomorphism and ⟨⟨C⟩ Π⟩Σ the closure of ⟨C⟩ Π under finite lexicographic sums, then each partial order from ⟨⟨C⟩ Π⟩Σ is isomorphic to one of the form X = P

For a language L by Mod L, Form L and Sent L we denote the class of L-structures, the set of first-order L-formulas and the set of L-sentences. In particular, Lb denotes the language with one binary relational symbol; working with partial orders we will take Lb = ⟨≤⟩ . Isomorphism and elementary equivalence are denoted by ∼= and ≡. I(T , ω ) := | Mod( T , ω )/ ∼= | is the number of non-isomorphic countable models of a complete theory T ; if T has a finite model, X, then, for convenience, we define
