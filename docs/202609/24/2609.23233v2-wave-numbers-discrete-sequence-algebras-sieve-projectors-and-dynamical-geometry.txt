# 1 Introduction and Physical Context 

1.1 Physical Importance and Ubiquity of Discrete Wave Numbers 

Discrete complex exponential sequences of the canonical form: 

wpf, g qr ms “ exp `2πi pf m ` gq˘, m P Z, (1) lie at the structural intersection of modern mathematical physics, discrete geometry, and harmonic analysis. In condensed matter physics and lattice field theory, they represent Bloch-Floquet crys-tal momentum eigenstates, describing tight-binding electron transport, lattice phonon excitations, and propagation modes on periodic spatial structures [2]. In quantum kinematics, as originally recognized by Weyl [16] and systematically developed by Schwinger [10, 11], discrete plane waves and their position duals constitute the fundamental canonical bases for quantum mechanics on finite-dimensional configuration spaces (qudits) [14]. In discrete signal processing and algorithmic number theory, these sequences form the kernel of the discrete Fourier transform and harmonic analysis on finite cyclic groups Z{nZ [13]. 1arXiv:2609.23233v2 [math.NT] 23 Sep 2026 1.2 The Classical Paradigms and Focus of Prior Analyses 

Despite their pervasive appearance across disciplines, existing mathematical treatments of discrete wave sequences have predominantly operated within three distinct analytical paradigms: 1. The Linear Functional Analysis Paradigm: In traditional harmonic analysis, discrete exponentials are treated almost exclusively as passive coordinate kernels for expanding gen-eral square-summable ℓ2pZq or bounded ℓ8pZq sequences. The primary focus has been on analytical convergence, spectral measures, and operator bounds, rather than investigating the internal algebraic closures formed by the wave sequences themselves under non-linear operations. 2. The Fixed-Period Paradigm: In algorithmic discrete Fourier analysis and quantum in-formation theory, analyses are almost universally formulated on a fixed, pre-selected cyclic lattice Z{nZ for a single integer n [13, 14]. While computationally convenient, fixing n in ad-vance truncates the global number-theoretic structure and obscures the rich inductive tower connecting all rational frequencies Q{Z across the divisibility poset of the natural numbers. 3. The Ergodic and Almost-Periodic Paradigm: In the classical theory of Bohr [4] and Besicovitch [3], linear combinations of exponentials with incommensurate frequencies are examined via topological completions and ergodic translations on compact group compact-ifications. While foundational for quasicrystals and Harper-type spectral models [2], this framework bypasses the discrete finitary arithmetic, treating sequences analytically rather than as exact elements of an algebraic number system. 

1.3 The Foundational Void and the Unique Contribution of this Work 

What has remained absent from the literature is a rigorous, constructive, and unified algebraic closure theory of discrete wave sequences that treats wave numbers as an autonomous, self-contained mathematical number system. 7.1 Adjoining Element-Wise Linear Addition and the Group Algebra 

The polynomial closure space Gpoly is strictly a multiplicative group of unimodular sequences sat-isfying |urms| “ 1 for all lattice sites m P Z.
