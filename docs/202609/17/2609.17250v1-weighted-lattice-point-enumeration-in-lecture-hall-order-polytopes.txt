WEIGHTED LATTICE POINT ENUMERATION IN LECTURE HALL ORDER POLYTOPES    

> KATHARINA JOCHEMKO AND KRISHNA MENON
> Abstract. We consider weighted lattice point enumeration in lecture hall order polytopes. We observe that these half-open polytopes are closed under weight-lifting for a large class of polynomial weights, including monomials and weights given by binomial coefficients. For rooted trees, we prove that the corresponding (weighted) h∗-polynomial is real-rooted thereby complementing previous results of Savage-Visontai and Brändén-Leander.

1. Introduction and results 

Given a partially ordered set P = ([ d], ⪯) on [d] := {1, . . . , d }, Brändén and Leander [8] intro-duced s-lecture hall P -partitions thereby simultaneously generalizing the classical concept of a 

P -partition developed by Stanley [16] and s-lecture hall partitions pioneered by Bousquet-Mélou and Eriksson [3]. For s : [ d] → N, a map f : [ d] → Z is called a s-lecture hall P -partition (or 

(P, s )-partition for short) if (i) f (i)/s (i) ≤ f (j)/s (j) whenever i ≺ j, and (ii) f (i)/s (i) < f (j)/s (j) whenever i ≺ j and i > j ,where ≤ denotes the usual total order on the real numbers. The corresponding s-lecture hall order polytope is defined as 

O(P, s ) = {f ∈ Rd : f is a (P, s )-partition and 0 ≤ f (i)/s (i) ≤ 1 for all i ∈ [d]} .

Observe that O(P, s ) is the intersection of finitely many closed and open half-spaces and thus in general half-open. If s is the all-one map then we obtain the (half-open) order polytope O(P )

studied by Stanley [15]. In this note we investigate structural and arithmetic aspects of weighted lattice point enumeration in s-lecture hall order polytopes. By a classical theorem of Ehrhart, given any lattice polytope 

Q ⊂ Rd, the number of lattice points in nonnegative integer dilates nQ = {nq : q ∈ Q}, n ≥ 0,is given by a polynomial EQ in the dilation factor, that is EQ(n) = |nQ ∩ Rd| for integers 

n ≥ 0. The polynomial EQ is called the Ehrhart polynomial of Q. The polynomiality extends beyond simple counting of lattice points to weighted counting if the weights are given by a polynomial [12, 1]. More precisely, for a polynomial function w : Rd → R, there is a polynomial 

EQ(w; n) in n, the weighted Ehrhart polynomial , such that 

EQ(w; n) = X 

> x∈nP ∩Zd

w(x) for integers n ≥ 0 .

A natural question is to identify situations in which the weighted Ehrhart polynomial is in fact equal to the (unweighted) Ehrhart polynomial of a (higher dimensional) lattice polytope. De Loera et al [9] showed that for a large class of natural weights w, which include monomials and certain binomial coefficients, and any lattice polytope of the form Q = {x : Ax = b, x ≥ 0} there exists a polytope Q∗, called the weight lifting polytope , such that EQ(w; n) = E Q∗ (1; n) = EQ∗ (n).

A classical theorem by Stanley [14] guarantees that the coefficients of h∗ are nonnegative in-tegers. Of ongoing particular interest is the investigation of other families of inequalities, such unimodality and log-concavity conditions on the coefficient sequence [10]. Both these properties follow automatically if the h∗-polynomial is real-rooted, see, e.g., [7]. For order polytopes the real-rootedness was conjectured by the Neggers-Stanley conjecture. While generally disproven [4, 17], the conjecture is true for order polytopes of series-parallel posets due to results by Wagner [18]. A natural question is whether this can be extended to s-lecture hall order polytopes. Com-pare also [8, Section 5]. Savage and Visontai [13] proved real-rootedness of the h∗-polynomial of O(P, s ) for any nonnegative sequence s in case that the poset P is a chain. To prove this result they used an interpretation of the h∗-polynomial in terms of ascent statistics over gen-eralized inversion sequences as well as interlacing polynomials, thereby pioneering the method within Ehrhart theory. For general s-lecture hall order polytopes, Brändén and Leander [8] gave an interpretation of the h∗-polynomials as generating polynomial of the descent statistics over so-called s-colored permutations, generalizing previous results on P -Eulerian polynomials of Stanley [16]. Using this interpretation they were able to prove real-rootedness for O(P, s ) if P is an ordinal sum of antichains and s is constant on every antichain in the sum thereby generalizing the results in [13]. Continuing the investigations for series-parallel posets, we show that the 

s-lecture hall order polytope of rooted trees is always real-rooted. Here and in the following we always assume the rooted tree to be hanging. 

Theorem 1.2. Let P be a rooted tree on [d] and s : [ d] → N. Then the h∗-polynomial of O(P, s )

is real-rooted. 

For the proof we adapt the interlacing techniques used by Savage and Visontai [13] and Brändén and Leander [8]. Analogous to the (unweighted) h∗-polynomial, the weighted h∗-polynomial of a lattice polytope can be defined as the numerator of the generating function of the weighted Ehrhart polynomial [2]. 3

Furthermore, from the proof of Proposition 1.1, it follows that P ∗ can be chosen to be a rooted tree for any rooted tree P . Theorem 1.2 thus also holds in the more general weighted setting of Proposition 1.1. 

Corollary 1.3. Let P be a rooted tree on [d], s : [ d] → N and let w be any of the weights in Proposition 1.1. Then the weighted h∗-polynomial of O(P, s ) is real-rooted.

We conclude by remarking on interpretations, variants and further consequences of the above results. We start by noting that combinatorial interpretations can be given for the coefficients of the weighted h∗-polynomials we have considered in this paper. Given any weight function as in Proposition 1.1 or Proposition 2.4, the weighted h∗-polynomial agrees with the usual h∗-polynomial of the weight lifting polytope described in the proof of these propositions. This weight lifting polytope is a s-lecture hall order polytope and [8, Corollary 3.7] gives a general expression for the h∗-polynomial of such polytopes. Theorem 1.2 can be formulated also for duals of rooted hanging trees: If P is the dual of a rooted hanging tree on [d] and s : P → Z+ a labeling of its elements, then the lattice points in dilates of OP,s are in bijection with the lattice points in dilates of OP ∗,s ∗ ; here, P ∗ is the rooted tree defined by i ≺P ∗ j if and only if d − i + 1 ≻P d − j + 1 and s∗ is the map defined by 

s∗(i) = s(d − i + 1) . By considering the map on Rd that sends x(i) to ns (i) − x(i) for all i ∈ [d],it can then be seen that EO(P,s )(n) = E O(P ∗,s ∗)(n) for all n ≥ 1. See also [8, Theorem 3.9]. Finally, since taking disjoint unions of posets corresponds to taking the Hadamard product of the Ehrhart series of the corresponding s-lecture hall order polytopes, Theorem 1.2 carries over also to rooted forests by [19, Theorem 0.1]. In particular, the unit cube in Rd can be viewed as 

O(A, s ) where A is the antichain on [d] (which is a forest) and s(i) = 1 for all i ∈ [d]. This shows that the weighted h∗-polynomial of the cube associated to any weights of the form described in Propositions 1.1 and 2.4 are real-rooted. This complements the results for unit cubes in [11, Section 4.6] that were obtained by the first author together with Jesús De Loera, Alexey Garber, Sofía Garzón Mora and Josephine Yu.
