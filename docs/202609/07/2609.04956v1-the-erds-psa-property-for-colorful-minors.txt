# The Erdős-Pósa Property for Colorful Minors ∗Evangelos Protopapas †,‡ Dimitrios M. Thilikos §,¶ Sebastian Wiederrecht ‖,∗∗ 

Abstract 

A colorful graph is a pair ( G, χ ) where G is a graph and χ assigns to each vertex of G a finite set of colors. The colorful minor relation enhances the minor relation by merging color sets along contractions and by allowing the removal of colors; it generalizes rooted minors and models problems on graphs with several, possibly overlapping, annotated vertex sets. A graph has the Erdős-Pósa property for minors if and only if it is planar, by a classical theorem of Robertson and Seymour. In this work we determine, for the colorful minor relation, exactly which colorful graphs have the Erdős-Pósa property. Our characterization takes three equivalent forms. The first is structural : the colorful graphs with the property are those that can be drawn with all their colored vertices on one face and whose colors are, in a precise sense, laid out along that face without interleaving. The second is given by an obstruction set : they are those excluding every member of an explicit infinite family O, of which only O(|I|4) members have colors that are a subset of 

I, for every finite set I of colors. The third is grid-like : they are exactly the colorful minors of unions of particular families of segregated grids, the colorful analogues of the grids that drive the classical proof. 

Keywords: Graph Minors, Colorful Minors, Annotated Graphs, Rooted Minors, Erdős-Pósa property, Structural Graph Theory, Obstruction sets.

Packing and covering are among the oldest opposing questions in combinatorics and the theorems that tie them together are among the most useful. This paper is about one such tie, in a setting where the objects being packed and covered are not merely subgraphs but subgraphs anchored to prescribed parts of the host. A graph may come with a set of terminals to be connected, a set of faces to be covered, a set of vertices that a cycle must visit; in each case the structures one wants to find are constrained not only in shape but in where and how they attach. Colorful graphs are a way of capturing all such constraints at once: each vertex carries a finite set of colors, several annotated sets may overlap and a pattern is found in a host only if every color it prescribes is traced back. The question we settle is which patterns, in this generality, admit a packing-covering duality. 

# 1.1 The Erdős-Pósa property 

A family of subgraphs of a graph may be viewed from two perspectives: one may look for many copies of its members that are pairwise disjoint, a packing , or for few vertices that meet all such copies, a covering . A covering is at least as large as a packing and the family is said to have the Erdős-Pósa property when the reverse inequality holds up to a function, that is, when a bound on the packing number implies a bound on the covering number. The name comes from the theorem of Erdős and Pósa [2], later sharpened by Simonovits [16], asserting that a graph either contains k pairwise vertex-disjoint cycles or has a set of O(k log k) vertices meeting all of its cycles. Since a cycle is a K3-minor model, the natural generalization is to ask, for a fixed graph H, when the H-minor models of a graph enjoy the same duality. Robertson and Seymour answered this in the fifth paper of the Graph Minors series [15]: the models of H have the Erdős-Pósa property if and only if H is planar. The two directions of their answer are of very different natures. The positive one is a consequence of the Grid Theorem, as a graph of large treewidth contains a large grid and hence many disjoint copies of any fixed planar H, while a graph of small treewidth can be treated along a tree decomposition. The negative one is a construction: for non-planar H one embeds H in a surface of minimum Euler genus and superimposes many copies of the resulting drawing, so that the copies interlock and no small vertex set can destroy them all. Both mechanisms reappear, in a colorful form, in the present paper. The property has since been studied in a large number of variants (see [14] for an overview). Among the directions closest to this paper, the half-integral relaxation, in which a packing may use every vertex twice, was conjectured by Thomas to hold for every H and was confirmed by Liu [7], who proved it even for topological minors; the threshold at which half-integrality becomes necessary has begun to be delineated by Paul, Protopapas, Thilikos, and Wiederrecht [10, 11]. 

# 1.2 Annotated versions 

The variant that this paper generalizes asks for models that are anchored: a set of vertices of the host is distinguished and the minor models are required to interact with it. Kakimura, Kawarabayashi, and Marx [5] proved that cycles through a prescribed vertex set have the Erdős-Pósa property, and Pontecorvi and Wollan [12] gave a version with a bound independent of the prescribed set. For minors rather than cycles, Kwon and Marx [6] studied the property for minor models with prescribed vertex sets, and Bruhn, Joos, and Schaudt [1] characterized the 2-connected labeled minors that have it. All of these are instances of a single setting, the colorful minor relation introduced by Protopapas, Thilikos, and Wiederrecht [13]. A colorful graph carries, at each of its vertices, a finite set of colors. A colorful minor must trace every color of the pattern back to the host and the annotated settings above are the cases in which a single color is used. The purpose of this paper is to determine, in the colorful minor setting, exactly which patterns have the Erdős-Pósa property. 31.3 Colorful graphs

41.5 The Erdős-Pósa property 

Let ( H, ψ ) and ( G, χ ) be colorful graphs and let k ∈ N. 

> •

A packing of (H, ψ ) in (G, χ ) of size k is a family ( G1, . . . , G k) of pairwise vertex-disjoint subgraphs of 

G such that ( H, ψ ) is a colorful minor of ( Gi, χ ) for every i ∈ [k]. Note that a packing is an indexed family and not a set of subgraphs; the distinction only matters when V (H) = ∅, in which case the subgraph of G with no vertices is vertex-disjoint from itself and ( G, χ ) has packings of ( H, ψ ) of every size.  

> •

A covering of (H, ψ ) in (G, χ ) of size k is a set S ⊆ V (G) such that |S| ≤ k and ( G − S, χ ) does not contain ( H, ψ ) as a colorful minor. We say that the colorful graph ( H, ψ ) has the Erdős-Pósa property [2], in short the EP-property , if there is a function f : N → N such that, for every k ∈ N and every colorful graph ( G, χ ), if ( G, χ ) has no packing of (H, ψ ) of size k, then ( G, χ ) has a covering of ( H, ψ ) of size at most f (k). We refer to the function f as the gap of the EP-property for ( H, ψ ). We also write pack H,ψ (G, χ ) for the maximum size of a packing of ( H, ψ )in ( G, χ ) and cover H,ψ (G, χ ) for the minimum size of a covering of ( H, ψ ) in ( G, χ ). In these terms, ( H, ψ )has the EP-property if and only if there is a function f : N → N such that cover H,ψ (G, χ ) ≤ f (pack H,ψ (G, χ )) for every colorful graph ( G, χ ). In this paper we give a complete characterization of the colorful graphs that have the Erdős-Pósa property. For ∅-colorful graphs, i.e., for graphs without colors, such a characterization is provided by the result of Robertson and Seymour [15] asserting that an ∅-colorful graph has the EP-property if and only if it is planar. Our characterization extends this result to all colorful graphs. As we will see, in the presence of colors the boundary changes in subtle ways that are worth highlighting. 

# 2 Basic definitions and statement of the main result 

Domains and their obstructions. For every set Z of colorful graphs and every I ⊆fin N≥1, we define 

Z| I as the restriction of Z to its colorful graphs whose palette is a subset of I, i.e., Z| I := {(G, χ ) | (G, χ ) ∈Z and χ(G) ⊆ I}.In this paper we study classes of colorful graphs that are closed under colorful minors, i.e., they contain all colorful minors of their members. For simplicity, we refer to every such class as a domain . Notice that if Z is a domain then so is Z| I , for every I ⊆fin N≥1.Given some domain D, we use obs (D) as the set of all colorful graphs that do not belong in D and whose proper colorful minors belong in D. We refer to the colorful graphs in obs (D) as obstructions of D and to the set obs (D) as the obstruction set of D. Notice that obs (D) is not necessarily a finite set, however, given that we assumed that each colorful graph carries a palette of a finite number of colors, this set is always countable. For an example of an infinite obstruction, if D is the domain of all ∅-colorful graphs, i.e., the set of all graphs without colors, then obs (D) = {(K1, χ i) | i ∈ N} where χi assigns to the unique vertex in K1 the color i. On the other side, if D is the domain of all colorful graphs, then obs (D) is finite as it is the empty set. The above imply that obs (D) characterizes D completely, in the sense that D consists precisely of the colorful graphs that contain no member of obs (D) as a colorful minor.

# 2.5 The structural input: torso treewidth 

It is easy to verify that, for every I ⊆fin N≥1, the I-torso treewidth of ( I, k )-segregated grids is lower bounded by an increasing function of k (see [13]). It was proved in [13] that the converse also holds, in the sense that if all ( I, k )-segregated grids are excluded as colorful minors then the I-torso treewidth is upper bounded. The structural ingredient that we import from [13] is the following. 

Proposition 2.4 ([13]) . There exists a function sg : N2 → N such that, for every I ⊆fin N≥1 and k ∈ N≥1, if an I-colorful graph ( G, χ ) has I-torso treewidth ≥ sg (|I|, k ), then ( G, χ ) contains as a colorful minor some (I, k )-segregated grid. Given that the I-torso treewidth of ( I, k )-segregated grids is unbounded as k grows, Proposition 2.4 can be seen as a min-max duality between I-torso treewidth and segregated grids. Clearly, for I = ∅ this is the celebrated grid theorem [15]. For the case |I| = 1 , the I-torso treewidth of ( G, χ ) has been independently defined by Jansen and Swennenhuis [4] and by Hodor, La, Micek, and Rambaud [3] under different names. When |I| = 1, Proposition 2.4 also follows from the results in [8] and [3]. 83 Crucial colorful graphs and their obstructions

# 4.4 The general case 

We now lift Theorem 4.5 to crucial colorful graphs with an arbitrary number of components. The additional difficulty is that a copy of a disconnected pattern may be dispersed around the host: distinct components of (H, ψ ) may be realized in distinct components of ( G, χ ), so removing a set of vertices after whose deletion no single component contains all of ( H, ψ ) — which is what the machinery of the connected case provides — does not yet prevent copies from being recombined out of pieces lying in different components. The proof is by induction on the number r of components of H and revolves around the pieces of ( H, ψ ), the sub-patterns formed by the groups of a partition of its components. After a first deletion as above, every surviving copy of (H, ψ ) is split by such a partition into at least two pieces, each realized in its own component of the host. The heart of the argument is based on the interleaving between packings and coverings for these pieces. If every piece that could still occur is contained in many components of the host, then k pairwise disjoint copies of ( H, ψ ) can be assembled out of disjoint copies of complementary pieces, which is impossible in the absence 23 of a packing. Hence some piece is contained in few components and, inside each of them, the induction hypothesis either produces k disjoint copies of that piece in that single component — copies which serve the assembly of copies of ( H, ψ ) just as well — or a small covering that eliminates the piece from that component. Treating one piece per round and never revisiting a treated piece, the process examines fewer than 2 r pieces in total and, once no copy of ( H, ψ ) survives, the accumulated deletions form the desired covering. 

Theorem 4.6. Every crucial colorful graph has the Erdős-Pósa property.

# 5 The lower bound 

In this section we prove the remaining implication of Theorem 2.1, from i) to ii): a colorful graph that has the Erdős-Pósa property is crucial. Equivalently, and this is the form in which we argue, a colorful graph that is not crucial does not have the Erdős-Pósa property. Refuting the Erdős-Pósa property of a colorful graph ( H, ψ ) means exhibiting hosts in which packing and covering are as far apart as one wishes: a bound c, depending on ( H, ψ ) alone and colorful graphs of arbitrarily large covering number in which no more than c copies of ( H, ψ ) fit disjointly. All our hosts are built from the pattern itself, by the following two-step recipe. First, ( H, ψ ) is drawn in a surface in such a way that its colored vertices all lie on the boundary of a single hole. Second, 2 k copies of that drawing are superimposed and the crossings between different copies are turned into vertices. The superposition contains 2 k copies of (H, ψ ), no vertex of which is used by more than two of them, so its covering number grows with k; the whole difficulty is to show that its packing number does not. That is where non-cruciality enters: each of the four ways in which a colorful graph can fail to be crucial forces the copies of ( H, ψ ) inside the superposition to compete for a resource that the surface makes scarce. For a non-color-facial pattern the scarce resource is Euler genus, since every disjoint copy consumes a fixed amount of it. For the remaining three failures the pattern is color-facial, the surface is a disk, and the scarce resource is the boundary: disjoint copies of the 28 pattern occupy pairwise different portions of the boundary and a disk admits only linearly many pairwise non-crossing objects attached to its boundary. For a pattern that is not single-component bicolored the copies must additionally interleave two linkages of different colors and the scarce resource is the width of the region in which the two linkages cross. One warning about the shape of the argument is in order, as it dictates the organization below. Non-cruciality is detected through Lemma 3.16, which supplies a member ( Z, ζ ) of O that is a colorful minor of ( H, ψ ),

and the geometry above is really geometry of the small graph ( Z, ζ ). It is therefore tempting to refute the Erdős-Pósa property of ( Z, ζ ) and to transfer the refutation to ( H, ψ ). This does not work and the reason is worth recording. Containment of ( Z, ζ ) in ( H, ψ ) makes copies of ( H, ψ ) harder to find and easier to destroy, so it moves the two parameters in the same direction: 

pack H,ψ (G, χ ) ≤ pack Z,ζ (G, χ ) and cover H,ψ (G, χ ) ≤ cover Z,ζ (G, χ ) (9) for every colorful graph ( G, χ ). Only the first of these is of use to us: it lets an upper bound on the packing number of the obstruction be inherited by the pattern. The second points the wrong way, so a lower bound on the covering number of the obstruction says nothing about the pattern and the class of colorful graphs with the Erdős-Pósa property cannot be shown to be closed under colorful minors by this route; that it is closed is Corollary 2.2, which we obtain only as a consequence of Theorem 2.1. Accordingly, every covering bound below is proved for the pattern ( H, ψ ) directly and the obstruction ( Z, ζ ) is used only where its shape is genuinely needed, namely in the packing bounds. 

# 5.1 Preliminaries for the lower bound 

Surfaces. A surface is a compact connected 2-manifold without boundary and the Euler genus eg (Σ) of a surface Σ is 2 − χE(Σ) , where χE denotes the Euler characteristic; the sphere is the unique surface of Euler genus 0 . The Euler genus eg (G) of a graph G is the minimum Euler genus of a surface in which G embeds, so that eg (G) = 0 if and only if G is planar. A single-boundaried surface is a surface from which the interior of a closed disk has been removed; the boundary of the removed disk is its boundary . We write ∆ for a closed disk, which is the single-boundaried surface obtained from the sphere and we use the term disk for both. The following is a classical additivity property of the Euler genus; it is stated in [9, Theorem 4.4.3], where it is attributed to Stahl and Beineke and to Miller and we use it in the following iterated form. It says that non-planar pieces glued along a single vertex accumulate genus, so that a surface of bounded genus can host only boundedly many of them.

Societies. A society is a pair ( G, Ω) , where G is a graph and Ω is a cyclic ordering of a subset V (Ω) of 

V (G). A segment of Ω is a set of vertices of V (Ω) that are consecutive in Ω . The depth of ( G, Ω) is the maximum size of a transaction in ( G, Ω) . Intuitively, the depth measures how much traffic can be routed across the society from one side of the cyclic ordering to the other; the last case of the proof below bounds a packing number by exhibiting the members of a packing as such traffic.

Proof. By the transitivity of the colorful minor relation, every subgraph of G that contains ( H, ψ ) as a colorful minor also contains ( Z, ζ ). Observation 5.3. A colorful graph ( H, ψ ) does not have the Erdős-Pósa property if and only if there is some c ∈ N such that, for every n ∈ N, there is a colorful graph ( G, χ ) with pack H,ψ (G, χ ) ≤ c and 

cover H,ψ (G, χ ) ≥ n. 

Proof. If such a c exists, then no function f can satisfy cover H,ψ ≤ f ◦ pack H,ψ on all colorful graphs, as this would force f (c) ≥ n for every n. Conversely, if no such c exists, then for every c ∈ N the supremum of 

cover H,ψ (G, χ ) over all colorful graphs ( G, χ ) with pack H,ψ (G, χ ) ≤ c is finite and the function mapping c to this supremum is a gap for ( H, ψ ).

Non-crossing families in a disk. Three of the cases below reduce to the same planar counting, which we now isolate. The point is that a disk can accommodate only linearly many pairwise disjoint objects, each of which reaches at least three prescribed stretches of its boundary; the bound follows from Euler’s formula applied to the incidences between the objects and the stretches. 

Lemma 5.4. Let a ∈ N≥3, let ∆ be a closed disk and let A1, . . . , A n be pairwise disjoint arcs of the boundary of ∆ . Let D1, . . . , D ℓ, where ℓ ≥ 1, be pairwise disjoint connected sets, each of which is drawn in ∆ , meets the boundary of ∆ only in points of A1 ∪ . . . ∪ An, and meets at least a of the arcs A1, . . . , A n. Then 

ℓ · (a − 2) ≤ 2n − 4. (10) 

Proof. Let ∆ ′ be the closed disk complementary to ∆ in the sphere, so that ∆ and ∆ ′ meet exactly in their common boundary. For every j ∈ [n], we place a point wj in the interior of ∆ ′ and, for every i ∈ [ℓ] such that Di meets Aj , we choose a point zij ∈ Di ∩ Aj and we join wj to zij by an arc whose interior lies in the interior of ∆ ′. For a fixed j, the points zij all lie on the arc Aj , so the arcs joining them to wj may be drawn pairwise disjoint apart from wj and inside an arbitrarily small neighborhood of Aj in ∆ ′. As A1, . . . , A n are pairwise disjoint, these n neighborhoods may be chosen pairwise disjoint as well. Together with the sets 

D1, . . . , D ℓ, which are pairwise disjoint and lie in ∆ , we thus obtain a family of objects drawn on the sphere no two of which cross. Contracting each Di to a single point ui, which preserves planarity as Di is connected, we obtain a plane graph B with vertex set {u1, . . . , u ℓ} ∪ { w1, . . . , w n} in which ui is adjacent to wj if and only if Di meets Aj .

The graph B is simple and bipartite, it has ℓ + n vertices and every ui has degree at least a, so it has at least 

aℓ edges. As n ≥ a ≥ 3, we have ℓ + n ≥ 3, and a simple bipartite planar graph on m ≥ 3 vertices has at most 2 m − 4 edges. Hence aℓ ≤ 2( ℓ + n) − 4, which is (10). We shall only use Lemma 5.4 for a = 3 , where it gives ℓ ≤ 2n − 4.

# 5.2 The k-multiplication 

We now describe the operation that produces our hosts. The intuition is that of overlaying transparencies: one drawing of the pattern in a single-boundaried surface is copied 2 k times, the copies are placed in general position so that any two of them meet only in finitely many points and each such meeting point is turned into a vertex. The result contains 2 k subdivided copies of the pattern and the general position guarantees that no vertex serves more than two of them, so that few vertices cannot destroy all of them. 30 k-multiplication. Let ( G, χ ) be a colorful graph and let Σ − be a single-boundaried surface in which G

is drawn in such a way that all vertices carrying colors lie on the boundary of Σ −. The drawing is allowed to have crossings, in which case we require that no edge crosses itself, that no two edges cross more than once, and that no crossing point lies on the boundary. We refer to the crossing points of this drawing as the old crossing points, as further crossings will be created below. The k-multiplication of (G, χ ) in Σ− is the colorful graph ( Gk, χ k) obtained as follows. We take k drawings of G in Σ −, each of them a copy of the given one, chosen so that only finitely many points of Σ − belong to more than one of them, so that no point belongs to more than two of them, and so that any two distinct copies meet only in interior points of edges; the points belonging to two copies are the new crossing points. We insist, moreover, that no new crossing point lies on the boundary of Σ − and that the k copies of a vertex of G drawn on the boundary of Σ − are drawn consecutively on it. Finally, we replace every new crossing point by a vertex of degree 4 carrying no color and we let every vertex coming from a copy of ( G, χ ) inherit its palette. The old crossing points are not replaced by vertices, so that each old crossing of G gives rise to k crossings in the drawing of Gk. In particular, if the given drawing of G has no crossings, then Gk is drawn in Σ − without crossings. 

Claim 5.4.1 . Let ( G, χ ) be drawn in Σ − as above, let ( F, φ ) be a colorful minor of ( G, χ ), let k ∈ N≥1, and let ( G2k, χ 2k) be the 2 k-multiplication of ( G, χ ) in Σ −. Then cover F,φ (G2k, χ 2k) ≥ k. 

Proof of claim. The graph G2k contains subgraphs G1, . . . , G 2k, where Gi consists of the i-th copy of G

together with the vertices that replaced the new crossing points lying on it, so that Gi is a subdivision of a copy of G. A vertex of G2k coming from a copy of G lies in exactly one of G1, . . . , G 2k, and a vertex replacing a new crossing point lies in exactly two of them, so no vertex of G2k lies in more than two of G1, . . . , G 2k.

The vertices that replaced new crossing points carry no color, so contracting them back into a neighbor shows that ( Gi, χ 2k) contains ( G, χ ), and hence also ( F, φ ), as a colorful minor; we fix a realization Di of ( F, φ )in ( Gi, χ 2k) for every i ∈ [2 k]. Now let S ⊆ V (G2k) with |S| ≤ k − 1. Every vertex of S belongs to at most two of G1, . . . , G 2k and therefore meets at most two of D1, . . . , D 2k, so S meets at most 2( k − 1) < 2k of them and some Di is disjoint from S. Hence ( G2k − S, χ 2k) contains ( F, φ ) as a colorful minor and S is not a covering, which proves that cover F,φ (G2k, χ 2k) ≥ k. ⊣

# 6 Beyond a single colorful graph 

We conclude by observing that Theorem 2.1 does not extend to the exclusion of more than one colorful graph. Given a finite set F of colorful graphs, a packing of F in (G, χ ) of size k is a family ( G1, . . . , G k) of pairwise vertex-disjoint subgraphs of G such that, for every i ∈ [k], some member of F is a colorful minor of ( Gi, χ ),

and a covering of F in (G, χ ) of size k is a set S ⊆ V (G) with |S| ≤ k such that no member of F is a colorful minor of ( G − S, χ ). Accordingly, F has the Erdős-Pósa property if there is a function f : N → N such that, for every k ∈ N, every colorful graph has a packing of F of size k or a covering of F of size at most f (k). For 

F = {(H, ψ )}, this is precisely the Erdős-Pósa property of ( H, ψ ).

# 6.1 Cruciality fails for many colorful graphs 

The next proposition shows that no analogue of Theorem 2.1 can hold for finite sets of colorful graphs: the set that it provides contains a crucial colorful graph and even one whose palette is that of all its members and still fails to have the Erdős-Pósa property. 

Proposition 6.1. There is a finite set F of colorful graphs, all of palette {1}, that contains a crucial colorful graph and does not have the Erdős-Pósa property. 

Proof. Let F1 := ( K3, ρ {1}), let F2 be the disjoint union ( K1, ρ {1}) ⊔ (K5, ρ ∅), and let F := {F1, F 2}. Both members have palette {1} and F1 is crucial: it is outerplanar, hence color-facial, it has three vertices only and carries a single color, so it is color-segmented and it is connected with a single color, so it is component-wise and single-component bicolored. We now prove that F does not have the Erdős-Pósa property. By the result of Robertson and Seymour [15], the colorful graph ( K5, ρ ∅) does not have the Erdős-Pósa property. Hence, by Observation 5.3, there is some k0 ∈ N such that, for every n ∈ N, there is a graph Hn without colors with pack K5,ρ ∅ (Hn) ≤ k0 and 

cover K5,ρ ∅ (Hn) ≥ n. Let ( Gn, χ n) be the disjoint union of Hn and of n isolated vertices, each carrying the color 1 .

First, ( Gn, χ n) contains no model of F1: every vertex carrying the color 1 is isolated, so a connected set containing one is a single vertex without neighbors, while a model of F1 requires three pairwise adjacent branch sets, each carrying the color 1 . Second, every subgraph of ( Gn, χ n) that contains a member of F

contains F2 and hence a model of K5, which lies in Hn, as the isolated colored vertices have no edges; consequently, every packing of F in ( Gn, χ n) has size at most pack K5,ρ ∅ (Hn) ≤ k0. Third, if a set S ⊆ V (Gn)leaves some colored vertex and some model of K5 in Hn − S, then a model of F2 survives in ( Gn − S, χ n); hence every covering of F either contains all n colored vertices or meets every model of K5 in Hn, so it has size at least min {n, cover K5,ρ ∅ (Hn)} ≥ n. Therefore, for k := k0 + 1 , the colorful graphs ( Gn, χ n) have no packing of F of size k and no covering of F of size smaller than n, so no gap f (k) exists and F does not have the Erdős-Pósa property. 

Excluding an antichain. A set F may always be assumed to be an antichain for the colorful minor relation: replacing F by the set of its ≤-minimal members changes neither its packings nor its coverings, as every 40 member contains a minimal one. For |F| = 1 , the Erdős-Pósa is delineated by Theorem 2.1. Proposition 6.1 shows that the general case cannot be read in the same way: the presence of a crucial member, even one whose palette is that of the whole set, is not sufficient. What is intriguing is that the two members it provides are individually as harmless as can be — the first is crucial and the second becomes crucial once its uncolored component is deleted — and that it is only their interaction that destroys the property. A criterion for antichains must therefore be relational : it has to compare the members with one another and no condition inspecting them separately can express it.

# 6.2 One color is already enough to be open 

The difficulty is not created by the number of colors. Call a colorful graph annotated if its palette is a subset of {1}, so that every vertex either carries the color 1 or carries no color at all; this is the setting of rooted minors and a colorful graph of this kind is crucial exactly when it is color-facial. Even for pairs of annotated colorful graphs the question of Problem 6.2 is open and the following pair of pairs shows how fine the boundary is; see Figure 16. Write A := ( K3, ρ {1}) ⊔ (Γ , ρ ∅), where Γ denotes the (3 × 3)-grid and observe that A is crucial. Let 

B1 := ( C4, ρ {1}) ⊔ (K5, ρ ∅) and B2 := ( P4, ρ {1}) ⊔ (K5, ρ ∅).

The pairs {A, B 1} and {A, B 2} differ in the single edge that closes the path into a cycle and they fall on opposite sides of the boundary: {A, B 1} satisfies the Erdős-Pósa property, while {A, B 2} does not. The reason for the difference is relational and it is visible in the figure. Merging two adjacent branch sets of the rainbow C4 yields a rainbow triangle, so the colored part of A is a colorful minor of the colored part of B1,

and the two members of the first pair are linked; in the second pair no link of either kind is available, as a triangle is not a minor of a path and K5 is not a minor of P4. The pair of Proposition 6.1 is of the second kind: its crucial member is the colored part of A, its other member is B2 with the colored component shrunk to a single vertex and no link is available there either. In particular, no analogue of Corollary 2.2 can be 41 expected for sets: as B2 is obtained from B1 by deleting one edge of the rainbow C4, it is a colorful minor of B1, so replacing a member of {A, B 1} by a colorful minor of it destroys the property. The failure for 

{A, B 2} is unconditional, while the property for {A, B 1} rests on the hypothesis on A mentioned above, so this last statement is conditional on that hypothesis. No condition inspecting A, B 1, and B2 one at a time can distinguish the two pairs, since the member that changes, from B1 to B2, is on its own neither crucial nor far from being so in either case. 

Problem 6.2. Characterize the finite sets of colorful graphs that have the Erdős-Pósa property. By the discussion above, the first case to settle is that of sets with a disconnected member and it is already open for pairs of annotated graphs.
