# 1 Introduction 

The algebra U + 

> q

(B2) was originally defined within the general framework of quantum envelop-ing algebras developed by Drinfeld [9] and Jimbo [14] in the 1980s, as part of the theory of quantum groups arising from the study of quantum integrable systems and the Yang-Baxter equation. Its significance lies in the fact that it constitutes a fundamental example of a rank-

2 quantum algebra (with two simple roots), associated to the complex simple Lie algebra g of type B2. In particular U + 

> q

(B2) is the positive part of the quantum group Uq(g), generated by the Chevalley generators E1, E2 corresponding to the simple roots of the root system of type B2.For the case where q is not a root of unity, this algebra has been studied in detail by Launois [15], who explicitly described its prime and primitive spectra, computed its automorphism group, and obtained quantum analogues of the Weyl algebra as simple quotients. Additionally, in [1] some results related to Hopf algebra automorphism are also developed. Recently, some prop-erties have been studied in the case where the parameter q is a root of unity. In particular, Bera and Mukherjee [5] proved that U + 

> q

(B2) is a PI algebra when q is a primitive m-th root of unity with m ≥ 5, computed its center, and classified its simple modules up to isomorphism. In 2024, Kenneth Chan, Jason Gaddis, Robert Won, and James J. Zhang introduced the notion of the ozone group of an algebra A (see [6]), denoted by Oz( A), and defined by

Subsequently, the first author and Suárez [12] studied their relationship with double Ore extensions and proved that every graded skew PBW extension in two variables over an Artin–Schelter regular algebra is again Artin–Schelter regular. 11 Future work 

A natural direction for future work is to extend the study of ozone groups to other families of quantum algebras at roots of unity. In recent years, Sanu Bera, Snehashis Mukherjee, and Sug-ata Mandal, together with other collaborators, have investigated several such families, study-ing properties such as the PI condition, PI degree, the existence of central and normal elements, and the structure of their simple modules. These families include the two-parameter quantum Heisenberg algebras Hp,q , quantized Weyl algebras, the bialgebra M (p, q ), and the Dipper– Donkin and reflection equation quantum matrix algebras [3, 4, 21, 22]. These results provide part of the structural information that naturally arises in the study of ozone groups, and it would therefore be interesting to determine this invariant for those families for which it is not yet known.
