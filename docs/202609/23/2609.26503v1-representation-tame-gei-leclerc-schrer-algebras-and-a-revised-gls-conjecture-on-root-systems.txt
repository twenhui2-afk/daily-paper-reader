Dedicated to Professor Yanan Lin on the occasion of his 70th birthday 

Abstract. Using Galois covering theory and equivariant techniques, we classify all connected representation-tame Geiß–Leclerc–Schr¨ oer (GLS) algebras in terms of their defining triples ( C, D, Ω). We then study the GLS algebras H(gCD n) with minimal symmetrizers, where n ≥ 2 and gCD 2 = eB2. By realizing these algebras as basic algebras of Z2-skew group algebras of the string algebras H( eC2n−2), we introduce extended strings and extended bands to parameterize the connected components of the Auslander–Reiten quivers of H(gCD n) and determine their shapes. We further classify the indecomposable 

τ -locally free modules over representation-tame GLS algebras of affine type and show that their rank vectors form precisely the set of positive roots together with explicitly described non-root vectors in the positive cone of the root lattice. This yields a precise revision of the GLS conjecture for representation-tame GLS algebras of affine type.

1. Introduction 

Given a symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix C, a symmetrizer D of C, and an orientation Ω, Geiß, Leclerc, and Schr¨ oer introduced a finite-dimensional 1-Iwanaga– Gorenstein algebra 

H(C, D, Ω) ,

now commonly called a GLS algebra; see [21, 22, 23, 24, 25]. These algebras extend several features of the representation theory of quivers, and provide a natural connection among representation theory, Lie theory, and cluster algebras. By Drozd’s trichotomy, every finite-dimensional algebra over an algebraically closed field is representation-finite, tame, or wild; see [17, 18]. The representation type of 

H(C, D, Ω) depends on the Cartan matrix, the symmetrizer, and, in some cases, the orientation. The representation-finite GLS algebras were classified in [22]. Our first main result completes the tame side of the classification. The proof uses explicit Galois coverings of GLS algebras and convex hypercritical subcategories in these coverings as obstructions to tameness; see [31, 39] for hypercritical algebras and [28, 32] for the covering-theoretic criteria used below. 

Theorem A (Theorem 7.3) . Let C be a connected symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix, D a symmetrizer of C, and Ω an orientation. Then H(C, D, Ω) is representation-tame if and only if one of the following conditions holds: 

(1) C is of Dynkin type A2, and D = diag(4 , 4) ;

(2) C is of Dynkin type A4, D = diag(2 , 2, 2, 2) , and Ω is a linear orientation; 

(3) C is of affine type eAn, eCn, eDn, gCD n, eE6, eE7, eE8 or eB2, and D is minimal. 

The simply-laced affine cases in Theorem A are hereditary, while the GLS algebras of type eCn with minimal symmetrizer were studied in [26]. Thus the new Auslander–Reiten and rank-vector analysis is concentrated on the remaining nonsimply-laced affine family. We use the convention gCD 2 = eB2

and consider H(gCD n) for all n ≥ 2, with minimal symmetrizer. For the orientation fixed in Sections 3–5,

The structural link with string algebras is provided by the observation that H(gCD n)is a basic algebra of the Z2-skew group algebra of H( eC2n−2). The latter is a string algebra, whereas H(gCD n) itself is not. Motivated by the string and band combina-torics of H( eC2n−2), we introduce extended strings and extended bands . Equivariantization then allows us to transport the relevant Auslander–Reiten information from H( eC2n−2) to 

H(gCD n) and to determine the connected components of its Auslander–Reiten quiver. 

Theorem B (Theorem 3.6) . The Auslander–Reiten quiver of the GLS algebra H(gCD n)

consists of the following components: 

(1) one component containing all indecomposable preprojective modules and all inde-composable preinjective modules; 

(2) components of type ZD∞, indexed by 1-ex-string pairs; 

(3) components of type ZA∞∞, indexed by non-symmetric full ex-strings; 

(4) one stable tube of rank n − 1;

(5) stable tubes of rank 2, indexed by 2-ex-string pairs; 

(6) homogeneous tubes parametrized by ex-band data and the corresponding scalar pa-rameters. 

The indexing in this statement is understood modulo the equivalence relations specified precisely in Theorem 3.6. We next turn to the root–module correspondence for GLS algebras. The relevant class is that of indecomposable τ -locally free modules, and the corresponding invariant is the rank vector rather than the ordinary dimension vector. In Dynkin type, Geiß, Leclerc, and Schr¨ oer proved that the rank map induces a canonical bijection between the isomorphism classes of indecomposable τ -locally free modules and the positive roots of the simple Lie algebra g(C) [22]. They subsequently formulated the following conjecture for arbitrary symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrices; see [23, Conjecture 5.3]. 

GLS Conjecture. There exists a canonical bijection between the set of positive roots of the Kac–Moody Lie algebra g(C) attached to C and the set of rank vectors of indecom-posable τ -locally free H(C, D, Ω) -modules. 

Identifying each simple root αi with the standard basis vector corresponding to vertex 

i, the GLS conjecture can be written as 

rank( M ) | M is indecomposable and τ -locally free = ∆ +,

where ∆ + denotes the set of positive roots of g(C). The conjecture holds for affine type 

eCn with minimal symmetrizer [26]. Lin and Su proved that, for an arbitrary affine GLS algebra, every positive root is the rank vector of an indecomposable τ -locally free module, but they also constructed affine examples with rank vectors that are not roots; see [29, Theorems 2–3]. Thus the failure of the original conjecture in affine type lies in the converse inclusion: additional rank vectors may occur. We determine all additional non-root rank vectors in the representation-tame affine cases. The simply-laced affine cases and type eCn are already known, so the remaining family is gCD n with n ≥ 2.
