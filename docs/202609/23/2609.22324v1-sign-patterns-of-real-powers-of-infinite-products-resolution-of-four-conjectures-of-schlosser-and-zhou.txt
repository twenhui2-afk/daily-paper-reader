We resolve four of their conjectures completely, in each case extending partial results already in the literature: those for the G¨ ollnitz–Gordon product Q8 =(q, q 7; q8)∞/(q3, q 5; q8)∞ (Conjecture 21), for Q12 = ( q, q 11 ; q12 )∞/(q5, q 7; q12 )∞ (Conjecture 24), and for the Borwein products G7 and G11 (Conjectures 20 and 23). 

• Conjecture 23 is true. 

• Conjecture 21 fails exactly on [ β, 8/3), where β ≈ 2.66448. The threshold 8 /3 is sharp, and the first counterexamples occur near n ≈ 7.6 · 10 5.

• Conjecture 24 fails exactly on ( δ1, 0), where δ1 = −0.64411 . . . is a root of the 22nd coefficient polynomial. 

• Conjecture 20 fails exactly on ( δc, 5), where δc = 4 .8735075853867342634 . . . is a root of the 897th coefficient polynomial. All other stated ranges are proved. Parts of Conjecture 21 were settled independently by He and Li; what is new here is the range near δ = −1, where their threshold diverges. Each failure has the same mechanism: a leading circle-method amplitude vanishes, and a secondary term with the wrong sign overtakes it. The proofs are self-contained apart from classical facts. The cusp analysis is exact, via finite orbits of Weil representations; the Hardy–Ramanujan–Rademacher expansion is made fully explicit; and certified computations in exact and ball arithmetic handle the finite ranges and the degenerate regimes.

The mechanism of failure. In all three failures a leading amplitude of the circle method vanishes, and a secondary term with the wrong sign takes over. 

• Q8. At n ≡ 2, 6 (mod 8) the leading term vanishes identically. There the sign is decided by a correction term at the cusps 3 /8, 5/8 and by the leading term at the cusps of denominator 16. Their growth rates coincide exactly at δ = 8 /3, and below 8 /3 the second, which has the wrong sign at n ≡ 10 , 14 (mod 16), wins. 

• G7. At n ≡ 1 (mod 7) the leading amplitude vanishes at δ∗ = 4 .87350758538675508 . . . .Just below δ∗, the leading term at denominator 14 (with the wrong sign at n ≡ 1 (mod 14)) wins on a window of n, and that window first becomes nonempty at n = 897. 

• Q12 . The failure on ( δ1, 0) is a small-coefficient phenomenon: cδ(22) changes sign at δ1 and keeps the wrong sign on ( δ1, 0).
